# Mary of Magdala at the tomb

First Mary of Magdala comes to the tomb and finding the stone rolled back runs to tell Peter and another disciple what has happened. In speaking to Peter, Mary refers to Jesus as 'Lord,' a title which John reserves for the post-resurrection confession of Jesus. The two disciples go to the tomb to confirm Mary's testimony, but also note details that indicate that the body was not stolen in haste, for the burial cloths are all left neatly (20:5-7). The third step is the response of the other disciple, generally understood to be the Beloved Disciple who is founder of the Johannine community. He ran to the tomb with Peter but showed no urgency to rush inside. When he enters he expresses the mature faith of the early Church in the resurrection of Jesus.
From Mary's human response- someone must have stolen the body - to Peter's perplexed examination of the possible proofs, the narrative concludes in a response that is not based on weight of evidence but on faith. The early Church expresses briefly its journey to faith; the tomb was empty, authoritative people confirmed it, on this alone we formulate our faith.
