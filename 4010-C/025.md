# Peter's speech in the house of Cornelius

First Reading (Acts 10:34, 37-41). This reading is part of Peter's speech in the house of Cornelius, which led to the baptism of the Roman centurion and his whole household. It is one of those early, kerygmatic passages which scholars have recognized in the speeches which Luke has preserved for us in the Acts of the Apostles. Here, in the space of a few short, terse verses the Easter message is recounted, following the usual pattern of all these speeches : (i) a brief survey of the career of Jesus from the baptism by John to his death; (ii) the appearances of the risen one to the special witnesses and the commission to them to testify; (iii) the call to repentance and the message of reconciliation offered by God; (iv) the testimony of the scriptures to the events that have taken place among them.
It will be noted that in this schematic presentation there is a frankness and candour about Peter's approach. He makes no attempt to reason with his hearers or to convince them of the truth of his message, beyond mentioning his own role as a duly appointed witness and showing that what God has done in Christ is part of his plan for mankind revealed in the scriptures. This is not because of any naive optimism on the part of Peter, but rather because of the nature of the message itself. It is gospel, that is 'good news and so it does not have to be argued. It is presupposed that man is vitally interested in hearing such an important message. Because of this the preachers of the Easter message retain the same note of urgency that was so characteristic of Jesus' preaching about the kingdom of God : 'the time is fulfilled; the kingdom of God is at hand' (Mark 1:15). The content too is essentially the same, except that the forgiveness and reconciliation of Jesus' message about the kingdom is now concentrated in the message about what God has done for him and for mankind through him. It is a message of joy and peace, a real Easter message.
