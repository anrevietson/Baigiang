# Jeremiah and the false prophets

First Reading (Jer. 28 : 1-17). Jeremiah's clashes with his own prophetical colleagues, the court-prophets (those whom we, rather loosely, would term the 'false prophets') were probably among his hardest battles. We can gather this from the treatise against the prophets (23 :9-40) and from his dramatic encounter with Hananiak (chapter twenty-eight); his message was entirely different from that of his colleagues. Our passage points to a problem that must have exercised the people of Israel : which prophet should one heed? Hananiah is called a nabi ('prophet') and spoke in the name of God (28 :2) and performed symbolic prophetic actions. He may have been deluded (confusing his own mind with Yahweh's - Ezek. 13 :2 f.), though Jeremiah would seem to suggest that he deliberately deceived the people (Jer. 28 : 15). In either case, the hearers of Hananiah and Jeremiah could not discriminate between them merely from their words and formulas. So Jeremiah gives two criteria : (a) the fulfilment of his prediction (28 :9, 16 f.); (b) the conformity of his teaching to the traditional doctrine (28 :7 f.) . Deuteronomy, too, gives these criteria : one is the fulfilment of prophecy (Deut. 18 :21 f.); the other and more important criterion is the doctrine and life of the prophet (13 : 1-5)-these should be in the line of pure Yahwism.
