# God's impartial judgement

First Reading (Rom. 2 :1-11). After showing that the pagans, even without the revelation of the Mosaic law, stand guilty before God, Paul now turns to consider that other section of humanity, the Jews (2 :1-3 :20). The mere fact of possessing the law will not exempt the Jews from the impending judgement which Paul, along with many of his contemporaries, expected God to deliver to the whole world. Each man will be judged by what he is in himself, not by the banner which he waves. Paul has already suggested that a man s righteousness comes only from God who makes him, the sinner, righteous. But here he is intent on showing that all men are on an equal footing before God, that the Jew, therefore, has no cause for boasting in the mere fact that he is of a particular race.
