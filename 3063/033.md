# The betrayal by Judas

The Gospel (Mt. 26:14-25). While the agreed price for the betrayal (thirty shekels) is the compensation to be paid for a slave accidentally killed (Exod. 21 :32), Matthew sees in the sum the fulfilment of Zechariah 11:12. The extreme economy of Matthews description of preparation for the Passover meal serves to under-line the serene assurance of Jesus. The phrase, 'My time is near', indicating his death, again makes clear that Jesus foresaw and accepted what lay in store for him. The foretelling of the treachery of Judas emphasises this same foreknowledge and thus the free choice of Jesus. The betrayal has its mysterious place in God's plan which the prophecies of scripture had revealed. Only Matthew has Jesus speak directly to Judas. The strong semitic tone of verse twenty-four is meant to render more pointed the fact that Jesus does know who his betrayer is.
