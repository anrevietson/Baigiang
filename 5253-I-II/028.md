# A manual for missionaries

Gospel (Lk 10:1-12,17-20). This gospel reading of Luke is probably from a kind of 'manual for missioners' from the early years of the Church. It gives an insight into the theology and the spirituality that guided them. In this section of Luke the guidelines are given as spoken by Jesus to seventy-two (or seventy according to some texts of Luke) 'others', that is (apparently) other than the Twelve. The substance of what is said to these 'others' here was also spoken by Jesus to the Twelve according to Lk 9:1-6. The actual number (72 or 70) need not worry us; they symbolise the universality of the mission. These are sent in pairs. This was a practice in the early Church (recall Paul and Barnabas, first reading; but also Paul and Silas; Peter and John; Barnabas and Mark; Judas and Silas - all mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles). The mission to which they are sent is of great import. More help is needed, and for this fervent prayer to God is required. Everything about them in this mission should bespeak the urgency of their task. They are not to be encumbered by unnecessary possessions, nor are they to waste time in conversation. (The original significance of some of the details in question, however, is uncertain -e.g. no saluting, light clothing). The central purpose of their mission must remain clear: to preach the kingdom of God, the urgency of repentance, and the consequences of refusal to believe. The narrative on Paul and Barnabas in the second reading today is a good illustration of what is intended. These missioners are to remain humble if successful. Their sole cause for rejoicing is that they have been faithful to the charge entrusted to them.
