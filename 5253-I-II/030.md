# The sending of the seventy-two

Gospel (Lk 10:1 - 12,17-20). In Luke 9:1-6 Jesus sends out the Twelve to proclaim the Kingdom and to heal. Now in chapter 10 he sends out 70 (or 72) others with a similar mission. These 'others' are not the Twelve:
Jesus is involving the wider circle of disciples in the task of preparing for the Kingdom. The actual number (70 or 72) need not worry us: it symbolizes the universality of the missionary team in which all disciples play a role. Hence the prayer for labourers to join in the harvest.
At the heart of the mission is the message of peace. The missionaries are sent out like lambs among wolves: an image with two different aspects. It highlights the dangers to be encountered from a hostile reception. But it also points to the new era of peace which the Christian message proclaims. This is the era when the lamb will lie down with the wolf (Is 11:6) in fulfilment of the messianic prophecy of peace and reconciliation. The missionaries are agents of peace by their presence and their words of greeting. It is perhaps incongruous that they are to greet no one on the road yet speak words of peace when they arrive at a house. Such behaviour emphasises the urgency of the mission: the missionary is not to be deflected from the task by the distractions of social niceties. Their attention must be on the destination of the proclamation. Those who are receptive to the Kingdom must hear the message of peace: others must be passed by. The urgency of the proclamation is highlighted by the public rejection of those who will not listen.
