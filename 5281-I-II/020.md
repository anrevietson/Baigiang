# Paul's greeting to the Romans

First Reading (Rom. 1:1-7). [For an introduction to the Epistle to the Romans see Appendix III.J As most of the addresses and greetings of Paul's letters, these opening verses present a virtual synthesis of the whole work.
Having insisted on his apostolic vocation and authority, Paul proceeds to describe succinctly his 'Gospel' or understanding of the Christian situation. This is the 'Good News' that the promises of the Old Testament have been fulfilled in Christ. Paul is at pains throughout Romans to illustrate this point. Paul's Gospel is centred on the person of Christ, whom he considers in two phases. The first phase is Jesus' earthly existence, the second is his existence beyond the death barrier. As if t6 satisfy common Jewish messianic expectations, Jesus was a scion of David, but his true messiahship was a spiritual rather than a carnal one. Jesus was n6t the Messiah during his earthly existence, he only became Christ, the Son of God in the messianic sense, at the resurrection by the power of the Spirit. This1 'becoming' of Christ is important not only christologically but also anthropologically. Since the Christian is essentially, according to Paul, in Christ, one with him, he can only become himself in so far as the dynamic structure of Christ's existence is realized within him, in other words, in so far as he passes from the state of the 'flesh' to that of the 'Spirit'. A major part of Romans is concerned with just this transference which, curiously enough, Paul sees in two stages. On the one hand, it is only by a complete assimilation to Christ, through actual death and resurrection, that the Christian Will attain perfect fulfilment. As Christ's so the Christian's true destiny is situated beyond the structures of the present World. On the other hand, the fact that Christ has already passed into this new kind of existence, not just as an individual but also as incorporating the Whole of the new humanity, as the New Adam, entails that the Christian, already united with Christ by faith, already enjoys this newness in some initial way. As Paul sees it, the vocation of the Christian is to lead the life of the risen Christ Within the structures of this present world. This is the mainspring of Paul's moral theology.
Originating in the commission of the risen Christ, the essence of the apostolate is to generate faith by the preaching of the Gospel. This faith is not the mere intellectual acceptance of truth but the responsive commitment of the whole pers6n to God addressing himself to men in Christ. Hence it is called 'obedience', but an obedience consisting not in the servile execution of laws, rules and regulations, but in 'faith', that is, trust, confidence in, dependence upon God, who alone has the initiative in the work of salvation.
