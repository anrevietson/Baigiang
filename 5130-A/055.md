# Baptised into Christ's death

Second Reading (Rom. 6 :3-4,' 8-11). Paul concluded his development of the love of God manifested in Christ at the' end of chapter five by insisting in the transcendence' of God's grace over all Sin. The objection is, therefore, obvious': 'Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound?' (Rom. 6 :1). Paul now has to show that the superabundance' of God's grace over all 'sin, 'far from being an encouragement' to sin more, actually 'excludes sin This is because the' point, as it were, 'at which the Christian' is united with God's saving grace' is the sacrament of Baptism which 'is a real communion with the passion; death and resurrection of Christ. This is not a matter simply of benefiting from the merits of Christ.' The Christian, at Baptism, is really inserted into, shares in Christ's saving mystery. In other words, he is destined to live, suffer, die and rise with Christ. As Paul sees it, salvation is essentially a matter of solidarity-communion with Christ.
Thus sin is incompatible with the Christians existence in and with Christ. Indeed his present sharing in Christ's saving mystery involves the renunciation of everything in his life that smacks of sin Baptism is the beginning of a whole life of repentance and conversion, the journey away from all that is not of God towards God, a pilgrimage which will end only with the resurrection which will be the final rupture from sin and complete union with God through Christ. Nevertheless, this risen life begins already in this present life in so far as a person dies to sin and lives only for God in Christ, in leading a life in conformity with God's grace.
