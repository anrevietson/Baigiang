# The eternal priest

Second Reading (Heb. 7 :23-28). The reading from Hebrews last Sunday showed that a high priest must be able to sympathise with the weakness of the people, and that his election to the office must come from God. In today's reading the author argues for the superiority of Jesus' priesthood to that of the Old Testament because of the permanency of his priesthood. Verse 16 if. have already stated that Jesus is priest 'by the power of an indestructible life' and that his priesthood will last for all time. The Aaronic priests ministered only for the course of their lifetime, even though on their death the priesthood was passed on. Even though in a certain sense the Old Testament high priesthood is perpetual (since a successor was always at hand to take the place of the one who had died), it was always subject to the imperfection of change. Jesus' priesthood, on the other hand, is permanent and untransferable (aparabaton). In a certain sense his work of redemption is not completed with his death on the cross but continues in his exalted state at the right hand of the Father 'always interceding for us' (v.25). The theme of the intercessory role of the exalted Jesus is mentioned also by Paul in his letter to the Romans: 'Christ Jesus who died, yes, who was raised from the dead, who is at the right hand of God, who indeed intercedes for us' (Rom. 8 :34). Jesus' act of redemption, then, begun on earth, continues in heaven. But the superiority of Jesus' priesthood does not rest solely on the fact of its permanence it is superior mainly in the holder of this priesthood, Jesus himself. The Aaronic priests were themselves guilty of sin - but Jesus is the spotless one who, although he was faced with the temptations that confront all men, never yielded to them: he is the holy one, free from all stain and sin. Although he came to save sinners, exercised a forgiving ministry to sinners, befriended sinners, his own sinless life on earth and his sharing the throne of the divinity (v.26) puts him in a class well above that of sinful men. Therefore, he does not need to offer sacrifice for his own sins, as did the Aaronic priests. The author's statement that the high priest offered daily sacrifice for sin is best explained as a mistake In any case the author wants to emphasise the efficacy of the one sufficient (ephapax) sacrifice of Jesus who offered himself. Verse 28 concludes the section by emphasising that the new order of things has as priest, not the sinful and transitory characters of the Old Testament, but the spotless Son who has been made perfect forever.
