# High priest of the new covenant

Second Reading (Heb 7:23-28). Jesus is high priest of the new covenant-bond between humankind and God. The old Jerusalem temple and its ritual priesthood have been swept away in the storm of the anti-Roman rebellion. But the author of Hebrews assures his Jewish-Christian readers that nothing of permanent value was thereby lost; the temple era had anyway been superseded by a new covenant, with Jesus as the supreme and permanent priest-mediator.
The heavenly intercession of the exalted Christ makes his one sacrifice (on the cross) permanently available to those who draw near to God through him. A similar link is drawn in Romans 8:34 between Christ's death, his exaltation to God's right hand, and his intercession for us. This idea is basic to the Catholic understanding of Mass as offering us contact with, and access to, the sacrificial death of Jesus.
Verses 26 to 28 praise the unique high-priestly majesty of Jesus in rhapsodic terms, reminiscent of what was earlier said of the mysterious Melchisedech (7:3). As Melchisedech's dignity made him superior to even the great patriarch Abraham, so the status of Jesus puts him above all the priestly caste in Israel. On the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:11) the priest first offered sacrifice for his own sins; but this concept is perhaps implicit in all the other sin-offerings. So while it goes beyond the letter of Leviticus to say that the former high-priests offered sacrifice daily 'for their own sins', it is a way of saying that their human failings made them unequal to Jesus in intercessory power with God.
By contrast, our 'holy, blameless and unstained' High Priest is totally effective on behalf of sinners, so that his sacrifice - in which he is also the victim - needed to be performed only once. The adverb 'once for all' (ephapax), which recurs in 9:12 ('he entered once for all into the sanctuary') and in 10:10 ('we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all') is part of the special flavour of this epistle.
