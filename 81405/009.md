# The choice of Matthias

First Reading (Acts 1:15-17, 20-26). This first discourse of Peter in Acts shows the importance of the apostolic office and the qualifications necessary for it. Many think that we have here a retrocession of ideas from a later generation of Christians who wished to have a solid foundation in apostolic witness. The chief duty of the apostle is to witness to the resurrection, but he is also to witness to the whole public life of Jesus. To do this he must have been associated with Jesus from his baptism by John the Baptist until his ascension. (On these. conditions St Paul does not qualify for the title 'apostle'.) The twelve, therefore, provide continuity between the historical life of Jesus and the era of the Church. Matthias, the man chosen to . replace Judas, does not appear again in the New Testament (nor does Barsabbas), so the stress is obviously on making up the number of the twelve. This number indicates that they are the patriarchs and foundation of the new Israel.
Luke notes . that there . were about a hundred and twenty people in the assembly. According to an enactment of the Mishnah the officers of any. community . should number one-tenth of its total number. There was also the law that one hundred and twenty was the minimum number required for a sanhedrin to have jurisdiction and so the election of Matthias is seen as valid and legitimate. The idea of the new community as a brotherhood is very frequent in Acts and in the whole New Testament (about 160 times).
Peter gives a short outline of the fate of Judas (omitted in today's. reading). The sacred writers place great emphasis on the necessity of fulfilling the divine will as revealed in scripture. Luke sees all the events of Jesus' life as well as his passion and death as a necessity following from God's plan of salvation. Thus, too, events in the early Church are seen as a fulfilment of God's plan. The divine' will is made known by the Spirit through the scriptures.
It is Christ who makes the choice of apostle. As he had prayed before choosing the twelve (Luke 6 :12-14) 50 the eleven and the community now pray. The casting of lots to learn God's will had been used in Old Testament times (cf. 1 Sam. 14 :41). This method of learning God's will is not repeated after Pentecost.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 102 :1-2, 11-12, 19-20). God's love is strong enough to blot out all sin We thank him and praise him for his goodness to us.
