# The song of the suffering servant

First Reading (Is 52:13-53:12). The liturgy takes up the song of the suffering servant, so popular with the early Christians to explain the passion of Jesus (cf. 1 Pet 2:21-25, Phil 2:7, Jn 12:38 and the commentary on the first reading of Palm Sunday). The sapling or shoot suggests the promises made to Israel and especially to the Davidic line of kings (Is I l:l,Jer23:5,Ezek 17:6,22;Zech3:8).The servant is rejected by his own brethren, like Jeremiah or Job. His silence is unusual. In the Old Testament, one cried out one's woes (Jer 15:10-12,15-18; Job 19:13-19; Hab 1:2; P55 17 (16), 22( 21), 69 (68). Although the Servant is compared with a lamb, the lamb is not being offered in sacrifice. The saving value of his sufferings seems rather to reflect the experience of the prophets, especially Jeremiah. Through their sufferings and their willing and privileged witness to the sins and sufferings of the people they brought the people back in atonement to God.
