# Our high priest

Second Reading (Heb 4:14-16; 5:7-9). The second part of the reading has been already heard on the Fifth Sunday of Lent (q.v.). The letter to the Hebrews is written to a community of deep Jewish piety, who appear to be suffering opposition from without and loss of faith within. They obviously feel deeply the loss of their link with the Temple in Jerusalem and its priesthood. The author wishes to persuade them that Jesus is their priest and his death on the Cross their sacrifice. Jesus' priesthood was not temporal, transitory and exercised on earth, but is an eternal priesthood exercised in heaven. Jesus can also support them in their difficulties because he too was tempted as they are but without sinning. The 'throne of grace' is a very rich theme. It recalls the mercy-seat on top of the Ark of the Covenant where God received atonement for sins. In medieval and renaissance art, the 'throne of grace' was a representation of the Holy Trinity. The Father holds Jesus on the Cross in his arms and the dove, representing the Holy Spirit, completes the powerful image of God's mercy.
