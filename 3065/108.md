# The Passion according to John

Passion (Jn 18:1-19:42). The Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ according to John approaches the sufferings of Jesus in a different way than the writers of the gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke. John emphasises the divinity of Jesus more than the other writers, and presents Jesus as in complete control of the events of the passion, allowing and arranging things to happen as, and only when, he wishes.
The first part of the narrative shows Jesus aware of what was to happen, and yet allowing it. The guards who come to take him prisoner fall to the ground, when Jesus answers "I am he" to their inquiry for Jesus. "I am he" is a reference to the name of God in the Old Testament, and while this is not an explicit use of the divine name, it is in the context of a theophany and is clearly intended to be understood as an indirect claim by Jesus to divinity.
After the soldiers worship Jesus, he shows his control of the events leading to the passion by directing the soldiers to let his followers go free, and thus he fulfils the prophecy of Scripture. Jesus then proceeds to work a miracle for an injured servant of the high priest.
John says little of Jesus' preliminary questioning by Annas and of the trial by Caiphas. John's community probably had a good number of Jewish converts, and John, sensitive to these members of his community, focuses little on the involvement of the Jews in Jesus' death. More importantly, John understands the whole of Jesus' life and mission as the confrontation of two ways of life: one way which is Jesus', the way of heaven, life, truth and glory; and Satan's, the way of earth, death, lies and ignominy. Jesus' passion is the final confrontation between these two ways of life, and thus John presents it as the confrontation of two kingdoms Jesus' and that world empire represented by Rome.
The trial before Pilate develops as a clarification of Jesus' kingship
he is a king from above, king of truth, whose kingdom is not of this world. While Jesus is hanging on the cross, his universal kingship proclaimed in several languages, he bestows his Spirit on the first Christian community gathered around Calvary the first fruits of his kingship.
