# The Passion according to John

Passion (Jn 18:1-19:42). In the Synoptic passion narratives Jesus', is distinguished by his silence. In the Gospel of John he enters into dialogue with Pilate. But Pilate cannot understand him. It is a Johannine dialogue in which only those who are born of God can understand, a dialogue in which the deep revelation contained in Jesus' words points up the oilier 5 inability to believe. There is a progression in the conflict. The accusations of Jesus' adversaries grow sharper. He is an 'evil-doer' in 1 8 30 but in 19:7 'he has made himself the Son of God'. For John, Jesus is the King of Israel, something even Pilate recognises. But his adversaries in rejecting him are compelled to renounce all kings but Caesar (19 15-22) For John, rejection of Jesus is rejection of God and Jesus' adversaries arc sinning against the light. On the Cross, Jesus is not alone, mocked by his enemies. as in the Synoptics. The women do not stand at a distance as in Mark 15:40-41. Instead his mother and her sister stand by the Cross with the beloved disciple and Jesus speaks to them. The blood and water flowing from Jesus side are important to John as is emphasised in 1 John 5:6. The water recalls Baptism and the gift of the Spirit (cI. the commentary on the Holy Thursday Gospel). The blood represents the death of Jesus. Like other New Testament writers, John is insisting that the Cross is essential to our salvation.
