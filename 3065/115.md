# The glorious victory of the Son

Passion (Jn 18:1-19:42). In John's gospel the passion is depicted as the glorious victory of the Son of God. Jesus is presented as the one who is in complete control. He knows what is going to happen to him. He permits it but strictly according to his agenda. Thus he fulfils the Scriptures, he obeys God's will.
John wrote his Gospel many years after the Easter event. As he narrates the episodes of the passion he is simultaneously reflecting on them in the light of his faith in the resurrection. For him the suffering and death of Jesus are suffused with anticipated Easter glory. By dying on the cross Jesus reaches the pinnacle of his glory. John interprets Jesus' final words 'It is finished' in the sense of 'mission accomplished'-an exultant proclamation that he had accomplished precisely what the Father had wanted him to do.
Jesus had been 'lifted up' not as a condemned criminal but as King. The Kingship of Jesus is a feature of John's passion narrative. In reply to Pilate's questions Jesus makes it clear that his Kingship is not of earthly origin. He came to reign in the hearts of those who accept the truth which he reveals. Pilate had a notice 'Jesus of Nazareth the King of the Jews' affixed to the cross. It was written in Hebrew, Latin and Greek. For John it was a multi-lingual proclamation of the Kingship of Jesus of Nazareth.
The soldier pierced Jesus' side. Blood and water flowed out. John sees great significance in this incident. The soldier's action and what followed fulfilled the Scriptures. More importantly the flowing blood and water denoted the giving of the Spirit. By dying in obedience to his Father's will Jesus had indeed been glorified. From his kingly throne he was giving streams of living water' to all who believed in him. (see Jn 7:38-39).
