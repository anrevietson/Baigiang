# Jesus our high priest

Second Reading (Heb 4:14-16; 5:7-9). Because Jesus - like the Servant - has positively assumed his suffering and death and made these into a sacrifice, he is a priest who not only offers but also suffers. He is, therefore, able to sympathize with all other human beings who suffer, especially those who suffer because of their fidelity to God. He is a High Priest who leads not only by words but also by example. His authority comes from his having been exalted by God from the death which he undertook out of obedience to God, even while, in his prayer, recognizing that God could deliver him from death and asking him to do so. Jesus's suffering was educative, the means by which he expressed and made real in his life his complete obedience to God. In his attitude to suffering and death, however, he sets an example for all other human beings to follow, being indeed the source of salvation, but only to those who 'obey' him, that is, live, suffer and die as he did.
