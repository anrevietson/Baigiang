# Fan into flame the gift of God

Second Reading (2 Tim 1:6-8,13-14). The apostle tells Timothy to fan into a flame the gift he received at his ordination. It was not one of cowardice, but of power. It also includes a sacrifice and control of oneself. He has no need to be ashamed of testifying to our Lord. We know from 1 Corinthians 1:23 that for the Jews a crucified Christ was an insurmountable obstacle, and for Greeks foolishness. A timid person like Timothy could shrink from the scorn incurred by his preaching. Equally he should not be ashamed of his association with the prisoner, Paul, but should rather share in his suffering, which will redound to the advantage of the Gospel. He can be confident that the power of God will be at hand to help him.
The later verses encourage Timothy to keep as a standard the Gospel message which he heard from Paul himself. In his exposition he must show the "faith and love which are ours in Christ Jesus" (NEB). With the help of the Holy Spirit who dwells in them both because of the pastoral ministry to which they were called, he is to keep safe from distortion the teaching entrusted to him.
