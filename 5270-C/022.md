# The primacy of faith

Today's Celebration insists on the primacy of faith in our lives. As we reflect on the readings, we must realize ever more deeply our relationship with our God and one another. In our very act of worship this Sunday we acknowledge that faith in God grants us a fresh vision of our world and our community.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must candidly admit that we have not always valued the precious gift of faith in the proper way. Realizing our shortcomings, we ask for our God's forgiveness.
You call us to an ever closer union. Lord, have mercy.
You challenge us to a more active and generous faith. Christ, have mercy.
You prompt us to show our sisters and brothers love and concern as the daughters and sons of God. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Hab 1 :2-3;2:2-4). The prophet Habakkuk maintains that God does not pay sufficient attention to the plight of the people. God responds that only the genuine believer can recognize his control of events.
Second Reading (2 Tim 1:6-8,13-14). The author urges his community to conform their lives to the Gospel and hand on its teachings in faith and love.
Gospel (Lk 17:5-10). Luke teaches that faith is as dynamic as the growth of the mustard seed, He also adds that church leaders should demonstrate their faith by carrying out the tasks expected of them,

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware that the gift of faith impels us to share our beliefs and convictions with others, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all civil and religious leaders. May their faith urge them to provide for the needs of their people who are indeed daughters and sons of our God.
We pray for all those who nurtured us in the faith. May their example be contagious in our lives as we seek to share that faith with others.
We pray for all those who have not yet received the faith. May we always respect them by honouring their convictions and learning from their goodness.
President: Generous and loving God, you share your life with us through the gift of faith, especially by sending us your Son. Help us to uphold the dignity of our family allegiance in everyday life. By believing in you, may we come to believe more deeply in he dignity of our sisters and brothers. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
By saying "Our Father," we acknowledge that we are a family whose common bond in faith. It is faith that urges us to collaborate in the coming of the kingdom and to do our God's will. It is also such faith that moves us to ask for forgiveness of our sins and to forgive the sins of others.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In our sign of peace we symbolize our commitment to one another. It is precisely the gift of faith that allows us to recognize the special dignity of our sisters and brothers. The sign of peace in church must become a faith-filled reality outside our place of worship.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Through faith we perceive the presence of our Lord in the bread and the wine. We thereby acknowledge that Jesus is our nourishment for the problems of daily living. However, our faith must also demand that we become nourishment for all those who make up the fibre of our lives. To be nourished in faith is also to nourish others in faith.
