# We belong to the Lord

Second Reading (Rom. 14:7-9). This final brief reading from Romans appears only as a gesture towards the final chapters of the letter - it is the only reading from chapters 14-16. The nature of these chapters make them less suitable for Sunday reading.
The questions of living according to the Spirit have already been dealt with in a general Way. Paul comes to a special problem in chapter 14, the issue of a scrupulous conscience. He has already argued this before in 1 Cor. 8-10. Here we have a briefer treatment; perhaps it was a sufficiently practical problem to be helpful to the Romans, yet suitably general to avoid embarrassment.
It might be useful to quote the verse immediately following the three verses of the reading: 'This is also why you should never pass judgment on a brother or treat him with contempt, as some of you have done' (v.10). Though Paul wants to give a more general principle in his 'living and dying for the Lord', we see his direction in the particular problem. Our living and dying does depend on the Lord - the whole letter has blazed with this headline - and So we must live as baptized, live as people bound together in love; our living and dying, that is, our whole existence, is for him and for each other. The first element of this is mutual respect and a desire to help those in need of more mature Christian instruction.
Does Romans deteriorate into mediocrity? Only if Christian living does. It is the pastoral and practical that are of concern to the busy missionary. The problems he meets on the road are the crises which have formed Pauline theology.
The themes of the Mass make this converge on the forgiving of others, on our unwillingness to suspect their motives, and our disregard of anything which hinders their Christian maturity and progress to the love of God.
