# Fraternal correction

First Reading (Ezek. 33:7-9). Fraternal correction is a keynote of the Mass readings today. This reading from Ezekiel underscores the responsibility of the prophet for the conduct of exiles in Babylonia.
The prophet Ezekiel, like Jeremiah, bridges the period of the breakdown of the state of Judah and the first years of the exile. Contrary to Jeremiah, his ministry is exercised entirely outside of the Holy Land. As a young man he experienced the first deportation to Babylon (597 BC), and there he received his prophetic call some four years later.
Ezekiel's ministry falls into two distinct parts. For the first six years his divinely inspired threats bettered even the scathing Jeremiah, but on the fall of the Holy City, which coincided with the death of his dearly-loved wife, he received a new charge - to be watchman over the house of Israel, at the guard to ward off danger and warn the inhabitants of their imminence.
The present reading is taken from a chapter where personal, individual responsibility to God for one's own conduct is preached. The exiles are in desperation, attributing the loss of their homeland to their fathers' sins (link chapter 33 with chapter 18). The exiles must thus be taught a new understanding of moral responsibility. However, this responsibility is not individualistic. The prophet is set the task of instructing others, with the severest penalty for default. The prophet sees for the future that moral renewal and the restoration of Israel must go hand in hand.
