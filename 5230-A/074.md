# Love fulfils the law

Second Reading (Rom. 13:8-10). Living by the Spirit is the theme of Paul's exhortatory section of Romans. The first two chapters of the section (12-13) are concerned with the more general application of this theme.
In Romans, Paul has dealt more systematically with the part of the Holy Spirit in our friendship with God, but his practical conclusion of the operation of the Holy Spirit is similar to his treatment in the first letter to the Corinthians. In the self-same chapters we have the same linking of charity and the Holy Spirit, a fusion constant in theological and spiritual tradition. In Romans the twin themes are bedded among more practical matters; in Corinthians they are developed with a beauty rare even for Paul, but this in itself reflects the different preoccupations of the letters.
We would expect that in Romans Paul would still have the law at the back of his mind. His attack on it confronts only its claim to saving power. Even on its own terms, as the present reading shows, the law points beyond proscription towards that love and regard for neighbour which makes the law a light burden and a joy to fulfil.
In more modern theological expression we might draw parallels between the Holy Spirit as the bond of divine unity, the gift of divine love which seals us in God's loving kindness, and charity which is the bond of Christian unity and which brings the law of Moses to fruition as a law of love and no longer of constraint. All we should owe to one another is the loving concern which is the mark of our possessing the Spirit of God.
A certain thematic unity does arise from today's Mass, for
fraternal correction, true responsibility and common prayer all presuppose the divine gift of charity.
