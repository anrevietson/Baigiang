# God's gifts are irrevocable

First Reading (Rom. 11:29-36). The case of the historical Israel provides Paul with a first-class example of his major thesis. God allows men to fall in order that he may pick them up, to sin in order that he may have mercy on them. Only Paul, being of the Semitic turn of mind, expresses this in terms which emphasize the divine causality.
God's love being redemptive love implies that man is a sinner and yet his very sin enters into the divine plan. God's love and power are more apparent in His allowing man to sin and saving him from his sin than in making him in such a way that he could not or did not sin. Little wonder if, in the presence of this intuition, Paul should emit a hymn in praise of God's wisdom.
