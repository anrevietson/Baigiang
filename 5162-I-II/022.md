# The crossing of the Red Sea

First Reading (Ex. 14:21-15:1). The crossing of the Reed Sea, described in a highly dramatic way, is perhaps the most striking example of Yahweh's extraordinary interventions on behalf of his people. The New Testament sees it as a figure of the saving action of Christ and in particular of baptism (1 Cor. 10:1-2).
The exact location of the Sea of Reeds is uncertain, but the best suggestion seems to be that it was a Continuation of the north-western end of the Red Sea and acted as a link between it and the Bitter Lakes. In the biblical account of the crossing two sources have been rather artificially combined. One of these (P), bypassing all use of natural causes, stresses the extraordinary nature of God's intervention ; God splits the sea in two when Moses stretches out his hand over it, and the Israelites march across - the waves towering like two 'walls on either side. The book of Wisdom even
furthers idealises the picture by speaking of them marching across on a green' grassy path (Wis. 19:7). The second account (J), nearer we can be sure to the actual historical event, shows God's use of natural forces (a strong east wind) for his extraordinary saving intervention.
