# God's glory leaves the Temple

First Reading (Ezek. 9 :1-7; 10 :18-22). Chapter 8 describes idolatrous worship in Jerusalem; then Chapter 9 goes on to the divine punishment that will follow such conduct. Six angels of destruction are bidden to destroy the idolators. Beforehand, the innocent will receive a mark upon their foreheads: a tau, the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet. Thus they will be spared. The Temple, already defiled by idolatrous cult, will be rendered unfit for any cult. Yahweh will abandon Jerusalem; he will mount his chariot and be borne away from a place where he had been dishonoured. We must bear in mind that when Ezekiel speaks of sin, he has in mind less transgressions of the social and moral commandments than cultic offences. For him, the cause of Israel's approaching fall lay in the sphere of the holy. 8he had defiled the sanctuary (5 :1 lY, turned aside to other cults (8 :7-18), and taken idols into her heart (14 :3-11); in a word, she had 'rendered herself unclean' in the sight of Yahweh.
