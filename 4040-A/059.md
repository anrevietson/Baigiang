# Repent and be baptised

First Reading (Acts 2:14a.36-41). Today's reading based on a text of the prophet Joel, shows every member of the assembly prophesying. Even the servants and handmaids who were not considered hona fide members of the community were prophesying. Joel had said that the restoration would expand to every member of the community and that not only the political and religious leaders would be empowered by God in the new age. The coming of the Spirit at Pentecost both fulfilled and transcended the expectations of Joel. The author of Acts shows the Risen Lord receiving the Spirit from the Father and pouring it forth on anyone who believes in the power of the resurrection, whether Jew or Greek, or slave or free.
The passage in Acts attributes the fulfilment of Joel's prophecy to the Jewish audience's admission of a need for forgiveness after the crucifixion of Jesus. Their request for forgiveness reached universal proportions, making it possible for the Spirit to reign in distant places and the hearts of future generations. The three thousand listeners who are reported receiving baptism symbolize the magnitude of the Spirit's new reign as well as the favour that the new Church received in the sight of God and the people. The new an unexpected power of this Spirit is the forgiveness of sin. This is the new reign of the Spirit that the Father unleashed on the world through the undeserved death and unexpected resurrection of Jesus.
