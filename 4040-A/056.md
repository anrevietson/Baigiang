# Peter's Pentecost sermon

First Reading (Acts 2:14, 36-41). This is another excerpt from Peter's Pentecostal sermon. The first paragraph is, in a nutshell, what the whole sermon is about. The Jesus whom the 'whole house of Israel' crucified has, by his resurrection, been constituted Lord and Christ. God has raised and exalted him; indeed the giving of
the Spirit shows this to be so. Jesus as man has taken possession of his divine prerogatives as Lord and Christ. The purpose of the sermon was to awaken in Peter's hearers a sense of guilt and of the need for God and Christ. And so Peter - successfully - called them to repentance and to 'baptism in the name of Jesus Christ'.
The call to repentance (in Greek : metanoia) is no less important for us than for these first Christians. Sin is in built into our human condition. And so if man's sin crucified Jesus then our sins had a part in it. Conversion then is basic to the Christian. It involves much more than doing acts of penance though these are a necessary part of it. It means a complete change of mind and heart, and a turning upside down of our scales of values. It is more than just sorrow for past sins. It is 'a declaration against sin as a constituent part of life and the human condition. It is a radical change of attitude, of hearts, ambitions, ideals' (J. L. McKenzie).
There is in question here something that is part and parcel of our baptism. Our baptism (as Peter assured his hearers) assures us of the forgiveness of our sins. But (as Peter made equally clear) by it one receives the gift of the Holy Spirit. And this is both a call and a help to wage unending war against evil and the sinful aspect of our existence. Repentance then is the steady effort to realise ever more deeply as time goes on what one has become in baptism. Each time a man repents of what he is, whenever one renews his conversion after a fall, he lays hold afresh of the grace and being given him by his baptism.
