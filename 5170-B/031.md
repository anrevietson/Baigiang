# One body, one Spirit

Second Reading (Eph. 4 :1-6). The first part of the letter to the Ephesians (chapters one to three) has a doctrinal emphasis - the breaking down of all the barriers that divide mankind so that all men are united in one community, one people of God, one Church. This is the 'hidden plan' (1:9) of God to be accomplished in Christ when all men would be reconciled with God (2:16). The second part of the letter (chapters four to six) is hortatory, out-lining the immediate and urgent consequences for the daily living of the Christian. Today's reading opens this exhortation and the basic motif is unity. Again it is by a dynamic peace that this unity is constituted (v.3; see last Sunday's second reading, Eph. 3:13-18), a peace which is typified not by any grand gestures but by the very 'ordinary' charities of 'selflessness, gentleness and patience' (v.2). The context is that of simple daily life. But we touch the hem of a great mystery - for it is not just a matter of people living together harmoniously, but of preserving 'the unity of the Spirit' (v.3). The Holy Spirit is the principle of unity for the Church as he is the principle of unity between man and God (cf. Eph. 2:18, 22; 1 Cor. 12:4 if.). Paul underscores his teaching by heaping up expression after expression of this fundamental Christian oneness: .... one body, one Spirit ... one Lord, one faith, one baptism ...' (vv. 4-5). And all of this dilates from 'one God who is Father of all, through all and within all' (v.6; echoes of the basic formula of Jewish faith: 'Hear, 0 Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord'; Deut. 6 :4). Notice how Paul weaves a Trinitarian pattern (one Spirit ... one Lord ... one God who is Father) through these insistent expressions of Christian unity. In the verses which follow today's reading Paul stresses that this unity of men and God has not been achieved as yet. He speaks of 'building up the body of Christ' (v. 12), of coming to unity in our faith' (v.13), of having to live 'by the truth and in love' and so 'grow in all ways into Christ' (v.16). Unity is a dynamic mark of the Church, something to be achieved - this is the vocation we are to live up to (v.1). At a time when Church unity and ecumenism are living issues among Christians, it is as well to be aware of the full dimensions of the question.
