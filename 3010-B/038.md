# Jesus in the desert

The Gospel (Mark 1:12-15). This concise account is complete in itself. One should not attempt to flesh it out by using the fuller accounts of Matthew and Luke. Mark gives us the fact and the -duration of the temptation of Jesus; he offers no explanation as to its precise nature. He simply mentions the two opposing forces -Satan and Jesus. And that is sufficient. For current Jewish belief had it that in the last days the power of evil would be overcome. The scene then, in a nutshell, is a picture of Jesus' redeeming work - as Son of God and bearer of the Spirit he confronts and conquers the power of - Satan. This is what his whole ministry is all about.
It is significant that he enters into the conflict under the impulse of the Spirit. That Spirit which had descended on him in his baptism, and anointed him with power, drove him out into the wilderness. His messianic ministry then was n9t to be a call to a safe haven where he would be protected from the assaults of evil. His anointing with the Spirit was instead a call to wage war against evil. At the same time it was a help in the battle and an assurance of ultimate victory. The Spirit led Jesus to self-giving, and it was in that giving of himself that the revelation of God's saving love lay.
The disciple is not greater than his master. Temptation is the lot of all of us. But for the Christian there is more. It is a challenge to stand up and be counted! There is nothing quite like adversity to reveal a man to himself and to others. Ml of us have to steel our-selves to fight against the 'tangled web of specious alternatives' to our vocation as Christians that presents itself to us from within and without. But like Jesus we have the gift of the Spirit to help us -God never asks us to do something without giving us the strength to accomplish it. Mark presented the conflict between Jesus and Satan then as an indication of the arrival of the messianic era. Hence he tacks on to his account a programmatic summary of Jesus' preaching, emphasising the necessity of personal commitment. The temptations that cross our paths should serve to remind us always of the personal decision inherent in our baptism. 'Repent and 'believe' are still a good summary of what it means to be a Christian.
