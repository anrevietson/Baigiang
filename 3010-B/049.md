# Baptism and the flood

Second Reading (1 Pet. 3 :18-22). Peter is exhorting Gentile Christians to persevere in their faith in the face of severe persecution. He points to Christ as the basis of their hope. Christ may have been put to death 'in the body' i.e. as far as his earthly life was concerned. but that was the beginning rather than the end. For in his resurrection he was 'raised to life in the spirit', and ascending to his Father he proclaimed to all his definitive victory over evil.
Christ's victory is communicated to man in baptism. In it he shares in his resurrection and is therefore assured of eternal salvation and ultimate triumph over all that is evil. Like Noah then, the Christian is saved by passage through water. The salvation of Noah and his family is used as a type of Christian salvation; it symbolically represents and foreshadows the latter. The baptised person then has a firm foundation for hope in time of persecution. Christ has already won the victory over the power of evil. The Christian who thus pledges himself to God 'from a good conscience' thereby professes faith in God's power and goodness, and so can look, without fear or disappointment, to the living Lord for the strength and grace to keep the pledge he has given.
