# The covenant with Noah

First Reading (Gen 9:8-15). There are three things that can usefully be taken into account in the interpretation of a biblical text: (1) the source material or traditions being used by the writer; (2) the manner in which the sacred author moulds these, and the point he is making; (3) the manner in which the sacred writer's text was later used, and its message for us today. The three readings of today's liturgy present an excellent opportunity for the study of these three points. This first reading is the ending of the biblical Flood narrative (Gen 6-8). In all this narrative the biblical author is moulding a variety of earlier flood traditions. In the biblical account we are told that evil had been on the increase since Adam's sin and Cain's murder of Abel. It was as if within the created order itself there was a sinful power opposing God and his divine plan for the world which he had created. By the time of the Flood this evil had reached such a pitch that God is represented as saying that he was sorry he had ever created the human race (Gen 6:6). The flood was sent to destroy this evil age and permit God to begin anew, with Noah and his family (his wife, three sons and their wives; eight in all). God would not allow himself to be defeated by evil. Humanity would have a future, but without any repetition of the flood. In this reading we see him make a solemn covenant with Noah and humanity. God says he will remember this covenant on seeing the rainbow. The rainbow brings to mind rain, water, the flood. After God's promise to Noah it would henceforward recall God's abiding fidelity and love and his concern for his creation's future. God says he will remember his covenant. The real point being put across, of course, is that humans should remember, never forget, recall on seeing the rainbow (or any other appropriate symbol) that God is concerned about them. No power, however destructive, within the world, within society or within the individual, holds sway. The Lord reigns, let the world rejoice. The Lord (Yahweh) has been enthroned as king since the flood.
