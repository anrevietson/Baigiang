# The gift of the Spirit

Gospel (Jn 20:19-23). The brief gospel reading conveys John's understanding of the gift of the Spirit to the Church. On the first day of the week, after the resurrection of Jesus, the Lord appears to the disciples, described as a miraculous event. The greeting of the Lord, 'Peace be with you,' has deep significance, and the disciples immediately recognize the Lord. This is the same Jesus who suffered and died; his wounds verify this fact. But now Jesus is present in a new way, and he commissions the disciples to continue his mission with the words, 'I am sending you.' Then Jesus breathes on them, reminiscent of the first creation in Genesis, and gives them eternal life, reminiscent of Ezekiel and another Son of Man who breathes spirit and life into the dry bones (Ezek 37:3-5). John reminds us that Jesus offers new life in the Spirit to disciples of all ages.
The account also speaks of the forgiveness of sin, an action of God on behalf of the community. The early Church understands that it is to proclaim forgiveness of sin as part of its kerygma or profession of faith. John may associate this power to forgive with baptism or with the sacrament of penance. However, he certainly understands that Jesus overcomes the power of evil and sin through his death and resurrection, and that disciples offer forgiveness of sin in their gospel proclamation. Pentecost celebrates the new life of the Spirit within the Church and inaugurates a new level of being for humankind.
