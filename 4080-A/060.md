# The Pentecost account

First Reading (Acts 2:1-11). This account of Luke's is a combination of two sources, one emphasizing the universality of the Church, and the other showing the impact of the Spirit on the local community. The apostles, gathered together in one room, are filled with the Holy Spirit. The writer's use of wind, sound, and fire, reminds the reader of earlier biblical theophanies, and focuses on the senses of sound and sight. This visible manifestation of the Spirit as giver of gifts, prepares the recipient for proclaiming the Word of God in such a way that all can understand the message. In fact, this filling by the Spirit prepares the Church, symbolized in the apostles, for its mission to all the world. Luke's theme of universal salvation in the Gospel continues in Acts with the development of the Church. Through the Church and its mission, all humankind can know the gift of salvation that the Lord offers.
