# Letters to the Churches

First Reading (Rev. 3:1-6, 14-22). The two letters to Churches in today's reading make considerable use of topical references. Sardis was the ancient capital of Phrygia, but by now shrunk to a shadow of its former glory; John suggests that its Christianity has similarly fallen away from its former eagerness. It was reputedly impregnable, but had twice been captured by surprise attack, so John suggests that its Christians also may be caught unawares by the second coming of Christ.
Laodicea was a wealthy city of the Lykos valley just below the famous hot springs of Pamukkale (whence 'neither cold nor hot') and renowned for its production of eye-ointment (whence the allusion to their inability to see). The letter concludes with an allusion to the Eucharist, where Christ pressingly invites all his faithful to fellowship with himself.
