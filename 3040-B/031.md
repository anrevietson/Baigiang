# Grace, abundant and beyond price

Second Reading (Eph. 2 :4-10). 'Grace, abundant and beyond price' is St Paul's preoccupation in the epistle to the Ephesians. From the start, he talks with enthusiasm about God's gift to men, through Jesus the mediator. 'Grace and peace to you, from God the Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ' he says in the very second verse; and he proceeds to elaborate on the gifts so recently lavished upon the Church: 'Blessed be the Lord and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places . . . He. destined us in love to be his sons (through Jesus Christ), according to the purpose of his will . . . a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him, things both in heaven and on earth' (1:3, 5, 10).
Today's section affirms that this grace is given to the unworthy, for God finds us dead in sin, quite undeserving of help. It is this contrast between our merits and God's gift which shows . how generous the Lord truly is. And so, Paul keeps up the adjectives: 'rich in mercy', 'great love', 'immeasurable riches of his grace . (We may see here a continuation and heightening of the mercy-theme in the first reading.)
Man's part in the process of redemption is to be grateful, trust in Christ, accept the gift with all humility, and begin to act in accordance with our new status - for we are 'created in Christ Jesus, for good works . . . that we should walk in them'. In this brief section we have the kernel of Paul's views on justification.
