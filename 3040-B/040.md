# Salvation, not condemnation

Gospel (Jn 3:14-21). Two themes run through this reading: Jesus came to bring salvation, not judgement or condemnation. And yet, his coming does mean judgement and condemnation for those who reject or do not accept him. In Christ God has spoken. In him he has revealed his saving plan. And since God can only be God, acceptance or rejection of his message in Christ cannot be just a free option for the individual. By reason of the very nature of God, of God's truth and plan for the salvation of the human race, Christ's coming and preaching can only be an event of tremendous consequences. On the one hand there is eternal life, on the other divine judgement because of rejection of the message. In this present reading acceptance and rejection of the message are linked with the hearer's predisposition towards the divine realm, towards what John calls truth and light. We recognise, of course, that John is writing from a perspective and tradition that divide humanity into two classes, characterised by their relationship to truth and light. But even with our awareness that there are many shades of colour, many levels to the human soul and such like, the central message of this Gospel reading stands:
God's message to humanity through Christ calls for an appropriate response. Through Christ the light has shone and judgement is constantly being passed on everything contrary to the light and the truth
