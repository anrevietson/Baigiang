# The Son of Man lifted up

Gospel (Jn 3:14-21). The Fourth Gospel has a way of combining various biblical traditions so that one can simultaneously react to a variety of biblical allusions and connotations. Throughout the Gospel Jesus is spoken of in terms of the Suffering Servant who will be 'lifted up' (Is 52:1 3ff) on the cross, and in glory, and thus draw all people to himself (Jn
12:32), and show his true identity (Jn 8:28) because they will look on' the one thus raised up. The account of his death on the cross is followed by the statement that 'they looked on the one they had pierced' (Jn 19:37) and this brings the theme of the wounded only son from the Book of Zechariah (3:10) into play with its allusions of mourning for the one struck down by his own. All these themes are knit together in the Fourth Gospel and here we have the theme of Moses raising up the serpent in the desert so that all who 'looked on' it would be healed used as another example of the saving death of Jesus. Furthermore the term 'Son of Man' comes laden with allusions to the two great powers of the Son of Man which are the power to issue judgernent/condemnation, and the power 10 offer life. Both are in evidence here, but with particular Johannine perspective. Judgement does not come from outside the person as the sentence of a judge, but is the result of the inner dispositions of the individual who accepts or rejects the light coming in Jesus. And the gift of life comes as a free gift of God's love in sending his only Son. This statement in Jn 3:16 that 'God so loved the world that he sent his only son that those who believe in may not be lost but may have eternal life' is seen often as the central and summary statement of the Fourth Gospel.
