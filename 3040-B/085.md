# The sin of priests and people

First Reading (2 Chr 36:14-16.9-23). The sinfulness of the priests and people brought destruction on the nation, the Temple and the city. This connection between sin and punishment was a common theological point of view in the Old Testament especially in books like Samuel and Kings where Josiah is the only king praised without reservation and the failures of the others are seen in large measure as contributing to the misfortunes of the nation. The idea that God actually used pagan kings as instruments of his wrath and punishment against his people was also a strong Old Testament theme, as for example Isaiah's understanding of God's use of Assyria as the rod of his anger against the people. Here in the reading from Chronicles God is said to have used Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon to punish them just as he later used Cyrus of Persia to set them free. God is seen clearly as God of the nations and God of all history. The perspective of Chronicles differs from Samuel-Kings in that these are books trying to explain the Exile and the devastation it brought in terms of God's punishment, whereas Chronicles pays attention to that perspective but has as its main thrust the idea that God is also the great restorer of the fortunes of his people, and after their time of punishment God emerges as the one in whom they place their hopes for the future restoration at a time when they were de-spirited and even those who had returned to the land of Israel were faced with great difficulties in re-establishing the old order of things. The Books of Chronicles were written to encourage them in the task of restoration.
