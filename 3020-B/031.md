# If God is for us

Second Reading (Rom. 8 :31-34). In the mind of Paul only the Christian has real reason for present assurance and future hope. He has been reconciled to God in Christ, liberated from sin and death, empowered by the Spirit and is destined for glory. The more he thought about it the more convinced he became. For ultimately everything rested on the sure fact of the love of God as shown in the life and mission of Christ Jesus. Our reading is a triumphant celebration of that love.
Commentators have noted the ascending series of rhetorical questions. Using the method and terminology of the law courts Paul leads his readers to an inspiring and inescapable conclusion. There is no doubt that God is on the Christian's side - Christ's sacrifice is eloquent proof of that. 'God did not spare his own Son ...' Anyone who knew his Old Testament would immediately have called to mind the sacrifice of Isaac. For that almost-accomplished offering of Abraham's son, who fittingly carried the sacrificial wood on his back, was looked on as a prefigure-ment of Christ's death. And Abraham's loyal obedience, outstanding though it was, represented only the faintest glimmer of the loving care and concern of God for his people. The fact that God is our judge is no difficulty, for - in that he has already chosen us - he has pronounced in our favour. The only person who could claim the right to condemn us is Christ. And he does the very opposite. Far from being our prosecutor he is our advocate; standing at 'God's right hand' he presents our needs to him and 'pleads for us'.
This passage is then a celebration of assured victory for the Christian. For he shares in the Spirit of Christ, and so his judge is his Father. He therefore participates fully and really in the victory of God's love for man. But only as long as he keeps in mind the words of the French theologian Yves Congar: 'Here below the Christian life consists not so much in being a Christian but in trying to become one as best we can. '
