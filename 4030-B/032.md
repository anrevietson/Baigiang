# Peter's sermon

First Reading (Acts 3 :13-15, 17-19). A kerygmatic sermon by Peter (see on the first reading of Easter Sunday). The occasion is the healing of a lame man, to which there is allusion in the omitted verse 16. 'The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has glorified his servant Jesus.' Continuity with the past and the recalling of God's saving deeds of the past is a primary characteristic of biblical revelation. (Cf. Pascal : 'Give me not the God of the philosophers but the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob!') 'Servant' (pais - both 'son' and 'servant') is an early liturgical title of the risen Jesus; it must be linked with the 'suffering servant' of Isaiah. Peter then makes a bold challenge to his hearers: they have killed the 'author of life', but God has raised him from the dead, after which the kerygma issues in a demand: 'Repent and be converted, that your sins may be forgiven.'
