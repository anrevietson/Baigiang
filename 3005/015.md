# The Son of Man must suffer

The Gospel (Luke 9:22-25). This passage follows Peter's profession of his belief that Jesus is the Messiah in whom the Old Testament hopes are fulfilled. However, lest such a profession should arouse false political hopes in his followers Jesus requests his disciples to remain silent about his messianic mission. He then sets out to correct any false notions the disciples may have had about him and his work. He says that he must suffer. This indicates a divine necessity, an obligation imposed on him by his heavenly Father. Jesus refers to himself as the Son of Man. This title is heard only from his own lips. As used by him it refers both to the humility of his role as Suffering Servant (Is. 52:13-53) and to the glory that would be his at his second coming (Dan. 7:13).
