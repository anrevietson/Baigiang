# Many gifts, one Spirit

Second Reading (1 Cor. 12:3-7, 12-13). In the second reading Paul is in the midst of his passage about the varied gifts of the Spirit at Corinth, diverse ways in which the influence of the Spirit expressed itself in the young and enthusiastic community there. The diversity of these gifts and the extraordinary character of some of them (prophesying and speaking in tongues) gave some cause of dissension in the community ; this leads Paul to stress here the unity in diversity which must be the mark of the Church, but which would be particularly difficult in a community as heterogeneous as Corinth, with its dockers and philosophers, Jews and Greeks, rich and poor.
To explain this unity in diversity Paul makes use of the image of a body. This image was in fact already widely used in the ancient world to express the working of many elements in different ways towards a common end, and had been used of the state and the universe and other ,corporate bodies'. But for Paul the meaning is far deeper, for the image of the body is no mere metaphor but a reality. How real is the physical incorporation of the Christian into Christ's body is shown by two other passages in the same letter :
if a Christian beds with a prostitute he is taking Christ's body and uniting it with her (6:15-16); also, we are made one body by eating the one eucharistic body of Christ (10:16-17). In using the terminology of the body Paul simply means that our union to Christ is real and total, a union of whole persons; not just a ,spiritual' union. But it is the presence of Christ's Spirit in us which makes this union ; it means that we act by his power and in his person. The action can be (and is, in the reading) attributed also to the other persons of the Trinity : 'it is the same God who is working in all of them' and 'these are the work of one and the same Spirit' . So it is the whole Trinity which dwells and works in the Christian by reason of the coming of the Spirit.
