# The birth of the Church

The Gospel (John 20:19-23). The gospel reading represents John's version of the birth of the Church as the first reading does Luke's. It is certainly the passage in John both where mission and where empowering are clearest. The apostles are to continue Christ's mission from his Father, and this mission is said to be, as Jesus' was, one of judgement : they are to forgive or retain sins : but the emphasis is on the forgiveness, which comes first. Christ's mission was to bring men to the light and to the Father ; by their reaction to him, their acceptance or rejection of his message, they are themselves judged, so that in John's gospel there is a continuous process of confrontation with Christ and of self-judgement. By their attitude on meeting Christ men judge themselves, turning either towards or away from the light. This process is to continue in the Church.
It is significant that in John the Church is founded by the Lord when he is risen, for it is the risen Lord who is in the Church ; the sacraments are encounters with him and are an extension of his life into the time of the Church. So while in Matthew the power of the keys is given to the community well before the passion, in John it is given by the risen Lord. When Jesus breathes upon the disciples it shows that a new creation is taking place. Just as God made Adam into a living being by breathing upon him, and in Ezekiel 37 the dead bones of Israel are brought to life by the breath of God, so the life of the Church comes from the breath or the Spirit of Jesus (in both Hebrew and Greek the same word means breath and Spirit). This is the new or eternal life which Jesus came to bring, which plays such a major part in John's gospel, and in this sense everything is already accomplished when Jesus breathes such life into his disciples.
It is, perhaps, strange that the imparting of the Spirit to the Church should be described so differently by the authors of the first and third readings. Was the Spirit given twice? Similarly the sending out of the apostles is described in quite different terms by Matthew (28:19) an4 by John here. The fact that they are described differently serves to throw a theological rather than a historical emphasis on the narrations : there were different versions of these events in the Church ; the unanimity of the tradition insisted only that the mission of the Church to the world was the last legacy of Jesus and that by his Spirit he had given them mighty power to carry this out.
