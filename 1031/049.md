# The oracle of Balaam

First Reading (Num. 24 :2-7, 15-17). The Book of Numbers, so-called - inaccurately - because in it the people are numbered, describes what happened while the Israelites were in the wilderness between Mount Sinai and the entry into the Promised Land. In it the soothsayer Balaam, from the Euphrates valley, is called by Balak, the king of Moab to curse the Israelites, camped east of the Jordan overlooking the plain of Jericho. He feared an attack, and since in the ancient world blessings and curses, once uttered, were regarded as irrevocable and efficacious, he thereby hoped to avert disaster. But Balaam, himself an enemy of the Israelites, was unable to resist the spirit of God, and instead of a curse blessed them repeatedly.
Our reading is comprised of two of these oracles uttered by the prophet. In verses 2-7 he likens Israel to an oasis in the desert, and water was a sign of great prosperity to a nomadic people. He compares the Israelites to cedars, thus indicating their great strength and power. Their king is mightier than Agag, the king of the Amalekites, who was overcome by Saul (1 Sam. 15), and his kingdom will be renowned. In verses 15-17 Balaam takes a rather ecstatic view of the Israel of the future. He speaks words of praise for a royal figure who will give powerful leadership. He describes him under the images of a star and a sceptre, symbols of royal power.
This reading can be applied to king David, but our Christian tradition, following in the footsteps of Jewish interpretation, has applied it to Christ. In him it is completely fulfilled. It is interesting to see that in the gospel of Matthew (2 :2) the star is a sign of the birth of Jesus, the Davidic Messiah-King. We, who live with the reality rather than the dream, should rejoice in our Great Leader, while never ceasing to try to realise that his lordship is a far cry from the nationalistic aspirations of the Jews. It isn't the sort of kingship they expected. Often it isn't the kind we want either.
