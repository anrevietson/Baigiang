# Jesus our brother

Second Reading (Heb. 2 :9-11). This is a short reading which gives a panorama of the entire range of Christ's work. The activities of Christ occupy an intermediate position within the enclosing action of the Father who stands at the beginning and the end of the entire movement (cf. Heb. 2 :10; 5 :5). That the mission of Christ is the expression of a divine initiative and love is an idea that receives due mention in many parts of the New Testament (cf. Rom. 5 :8; Gal. 2:20; John 15:13). This insight receives most vivid expression in the Pauline theme of reconciliation. Far from arresting a divine anger or appeasing a heavenly vindicatory justice, the death of Christ reveals a passionate, pleading love of God which seeks out man and his friendship and reconciliation (cf. 2 Cor. 5 :18-20). Likewise in Christ all things wend their way back to the Father. When everything is brought together under Christ as head (cf. Eph. 1:10), then in the end he will bring back this unified universe to the Father and subject it to him (cf. 1 Cor. 15 :22-25).
The integrating, reconciling activity of Christ is possible because of his solidarity with creation and the human race (cf. Heb. 2 :11) which renders his life vicarious and redemptive. The author of Hebrews shows that the key moment of Christ's salvific work was the moment when he submitted to death and was made perfect through suffering. The perfection here intended is the exaltation of Christ which is the consequence of suffering and the meaning of suffering is located in Jesus' obedience. In Heb. 5 :8 the suffering of Christ is described as an experience of obedience and it is this submission to the will of the Father that gives the orientation to the Passion as described in the Gospel accounts: 'not my will ...' (Mark 14 :36; John 12 :27). Salvation is found in Christ's vicarious will to allow his life to be shaped by the word of God. This experience of Jesus was not worked out in placid tranquillity but in the welter and storm of temptation to do otherwise. St Matthew gives the backdrop to his public life in that opening scene in the desert (Mt. 4 :1-11). Jesus is one who has come to these cross-roads situations of human life and while tempted has preserved a steady unflinching commitment to the Father. His death is that final agonising choice between the last idol which is life itself and the divine purpose.
