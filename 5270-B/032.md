# Marriage

Marriage is the theme of today's Mass. The United Nations declared 1994 to be the International Year of the Family. To mark the beginning of the year the Pope declared, 'The family is a community founded on the indissoluble marriage of a man and a woman, who with their love blend their existence into one and open themselves to the gift of life.'
The ideal of Christian marriage brings up a whole series of emotions which we register as we celebrate. We pray that we may be enlightened by God's word, and encouraged by our celebration.
In the first reading the origins of marriage - man leaves father and mother to become one flesh with woman - are placed firmly within God's plan. In the Gospel reading, Jesus, under testing questioning from some Pharisees, refers back to the Book of Genesis in arguing for the ideal of the indissolubility of marriage. In religious terms the unbreakable bond between husband and wife is a sign on earth of God's ever-faithful love.

PENITENTIAL RITE
Each of us is the unique fruit of God's plan, and of distinctive human relationships. We bring before God our experience of ourselves, especially as we consider our relationships with our parents, our partners or friends, and our children, if we have them.
Lord Jesus, your love for us surpasses all our hopes and desires. Lord, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, forgive us our failure to love others as you love us. Christ, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, lead us in the way of salvation. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Gen 2:18-24). This reflects on the incompleteness of being alone: the single man delights in seeing the first woman, who is bone from his bone, and flesh from his flesh. That is how the author attempts to explain, albeit from a man's perspective, the great attraction woman holds for man, and the strength of the mutual affinity.
Second Reading (Heb 2:9-11). Jesus Christ achieved salvation for us through his suffering.
Gospel (Mk 10:2-6). Jesus presents marriage as the bond established by God, which should not be broken.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President At the heart of our faith is the love of God and of our neighbour. We pray for the gift of God's love that we may love others better.
Response: Lord Jesus, help us to love one another.
Reader(s): For all the people of the world.
For the Church in its ministry to the world through the proclamation of the Gospel.
For all who exercise leadership in the Church.
For the leaders of nations.
For all who are married, and who prepare for marriage.
For all who are happily married.
For those who are disillusioned in marriage, and for those who are separated and divorced.
We commend to God's care those who have died, those who are ill, and each other.
President: Lord God, you give us so much in your wonderful creation. Give us also a spirit of reverence, that we may cherish your world, and love each other. We make this prayer through Jesus who because he submitted to suffering and death is now crowned with glory and splendour for ever and ever.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Let us pray in joyful hope for the flowering of God's rule on earth, as Jesus taught us to pray.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
We are bonded together as sisters and brothers, and are sanctified by Jesus. Let us offer each other the gift of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
In our Mass we bring before our minds the redeeming suffering and death of Jesus, and his being crowned in glory. Our minds and hearts are filled with grace, and we are given a foretaste of heaven.
Our relationships, too, have a past, a present and a future. We ask Jesus, who triumphed through suffering, to heal the wounds we inflict upon each other, and to help us make our relationships signs of our future glory in heaven.
