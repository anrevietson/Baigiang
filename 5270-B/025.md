# The creation of woman

First Reading (Gen. 2 :18-24). This reading traces the broadly social nature of man from his brotherhood with all created being to that basic social relationship which is the family unit. The fall will centre its effects precisely in this area disharmony in the universe, strife, conflict and lack of communication among man ' s own kind (cf. Gen. 11 :7-9).
The first creation account makes man the explicit master of all things (cf. Gen. 1:26). The same idea is found in Gen. 2 :18-20. The wild beasts and the birds find meaning in relationship to man. They bear names imposed by him and so have real significance only in his world. It is important to remember that this mastery is found in the context of brotherhood. It is not good for man to be alone. It is not sufficient therefore to relate non-spiritual creation to man in terms of utility; it was made for man's use and benefit. We must also include the ideas of respect, responsibility and love. In a world in which women were given a very subservient role, the full equality of the sexes is given clear affirmation. It is utterly absurd to interpret this passage in terms of the domination of man Over women. The whole point of it is their absolute equality -'bone of my bone and flesh of my flesh' .
An element of joy wells up in man's and woman's discovery of one another. Here at last is the social relationship that their whole nature desires. It is a lasting union and monogamous. This is clear from the text with its note of permanence and unity. There is no hint in Gen. 2 24 that this situation is not designed to last and that more than two individuals are involved. This relationship involves a blending of their whole lives and personalities - 'two in one flesh'.
