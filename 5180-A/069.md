# Come to the waters

First Reading (Is 55:1-3). This passage imitates atypical literary genre running through all major world religions in which a god or goddess invites humans to a banquet. First the deity offers food and then it offers a life of fellowship with the godhead. Whoever lacks understanding or is inexperienced in religious affairs is advised to leave aside a life of folly in order to eat and drink at God's table and to gain spiritual insight.
This text in second Isaiah was written in the sixth century B.C. when the exiles returned from Babylon to Israel. The return to the Promised Land symbolised an outpouring of divine blessings, an abundance of salvation for all people. Whereas prophets and kings formerly witnessed to the covenantal bond with God, the entire nation now bears witness to the bond between God and humankind. This bond is often expressed in terms of need, hunger and searching. Love for God is expressed as an intentional search for the Wisdom that brings one close to God and the things of God.
