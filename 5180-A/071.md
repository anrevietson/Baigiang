# The nature of the Eucharist

The Holy Scriptures that are read to us today give us a profound teaching on the nature of the Eucharist that we gather to celebrate. We pray that we may be encouraged by this celebration to live out the Eucharist more fully in our lives.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We ask the Lord for forgiveness today for our lack of devotion to the
Eucharist we celebrate, for our lack of preparation, attention and
thanksgiving; and for forgiveness for not recognising the presence of
Christ in others.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 55:1-3). The prophet describes the relationship that is possible with the Lord as being like a rich banquet.
Second Reading (Rom 8:35.37-39). This is a hymn to the absolute power of the love of God which triumphs over all.
Gospel (Mt 14:13-21). The miraculous feeding of the five thousand is nuanced by Matthew precisely to point to the miracle of the Eucharist.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: The Psalm today exclaims: 'The eyes of all creatures look to you, and you give them their food in due time.' We now ask the Father to feed his Church and his world.
Reader(s): For the Church, that we may live out more fully in our lives the Eucharist we celebrate.
For all governments, that they may work for a greater redistribution of the world's resources.
For all who suffer from starvation, that aid may quickly reach them.
For our Parish, that we may appreciate more deeply the miracle of the Eucharist.
President: Father, hear our prayers, and enrich our world with your gifts. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Let us ask the Father to nourish us and all people with our daily bread:

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
As we prepare break the Bread of Life, let us recognise Christ in each other.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Lord we thank you for the gift of the Holy Eucharist. We pray that just as you fed the multitude so that all had enough to eat and there was an abundance left, so may we be led to share all we have and are.
