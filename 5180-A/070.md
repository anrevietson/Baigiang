# Reconciled to God

Second Reading (Rom 8:35,37-39). Every Christian who has been reconciled to God by the death and resurrection of Jesus experiences a peace that trials, distress and persecution cannot disturb. Reconciliation replaces all forms of estrangements, which are symbolised by the sword, dangers and hunger. The reference to the heights and depths may indicate the proximity or distance of stars which could influence the fate of people; the sphere of grace won by Christ's death and resurrection remains unshaken even in the face of such adverse powers.
The result of the peace which the Christian experiences in the face of difficulties, engenders the additional gift of hope; God's glory shining through apparent defeat. Because the believer is convinced of God's powerful hand in all events, the tribulations of life engender a certain strength of character which reinforces the hope, a hope that emanates from the presence of God within the person. Nothing can separate the believer from such presence.
