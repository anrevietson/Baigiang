# Come to the waters

First Reading (Is. 55 :1-3). The passage is an exhortation to exiles returning from captivity ma Babylon. In chapter fifty-four a picture is drawn of the new Jerusalem and its future is described. The vision put before us is that of a messianic, eschatological era. A common feature of this era is the messianic banquet (Is. 25 :6; 65 :11-11; Ps. 23 :5), and our passage is an invitation to this banquet. The banquet is open to all, the only requirement for admission being a 'thirst' for God. The universalism of the passage is striking in view of its historical context where we might have expected the prophet's main concern to be purely nationalistic. The invitation to the banquet is ultimately an invitation to life,' to life with all its fullness, whose source is the living God (cf. Ps. 42 :2).
Responsorial Psalm : The verses are taken from Psalm 145, a liturgical hymn of the community at worship, which because of verse fifteen ('the eyes of all creatures' etc.) was sung in the early Church at the time of the midday meal. It is in God's providence for his creatures, the practical expression of his compassion and tenderness, that the true greatness of God lies.
