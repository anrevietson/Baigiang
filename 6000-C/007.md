# The visit to the Christ Child

Gospel (Mt 2:1-12). Much has been written about the familiar story of the visit to the Christ Child of the Magi. Christian tradition later on numbered and named the Magi, and even transformed them into kings. In Matthew's presentation of the Christ Child and the world's reaction to him, biblical figures such as Joseph, Moses and Baalam are recalled and ancient Jewish traditions are re-worked.
The story tells of wise men (astrologers) from the east Babylonia or
Arabia) who came to pay homage to the 'King of the Jews', and they come to Bethlehem. The glory of the Lord shines not in Jerusalem, as in the first reading, but in Bethlehem: the goal of these Gentiles is not the capital of Judaism but the city of David. Matthew's story is the story of a divinely guided pilgrimage that reaches its goal, of a search for truth that is rewarded, of an attempt at deception that is frustrated. It is a story of acceptance and rejection. The wisdom of the Gentile world pays homage to the 'King of the Jews' while King Herod and the wise men of the Jews reject the testimony of their scriptures. It is a story that will be repeated in the gospel ('And many will come from east and west... 'Mt 8:11-12). The cross casts its shadow here. The title given by the Magi is the title that will be placed over the cross at Christ's crucifixion. The universal outreach of Matthew's gospel is anticipated in Matthew's birth story as it is in Luke's infancy narrative, though somewhat differently.
