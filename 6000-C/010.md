# Awe and wonder

Gospel (Mt 2:1 - 12). This passage reflects the awe and wonder that human beings experience when they come face to face with God through his incarnate Son. It also provides us with one of the rare pieces of chronology to be found in Matthew's gospel. The event recounted is placed in the reign of King Herod (37-4 B.C.), though the exact date cannot be fixed with certainty. The term 'magi' was originally applied to the learned priestly caste of the Persians, though later on it was applied loosely to anyone skilled in occult knowledge or power. Mention of the 'star' tells us that they were called 'magi' because of their knowledge of astrology. The heavens, like Matthew's genealogy (chapter 1), show that this child is the Messiah king. In their quest for this king the magi consult king Herod, who, in turn, consults his religious experts as to where this king may be found. Their reply is supported by a quotation from the prophet Micah (5:1-3): Bethlehem, the city of David, from whose lineage the promised Messiah would come. However, the account is concerned not only with the messiahship of Jesus but also by his being adored by the Gentiles. The fact that Gentiles and not Jews are the first to recognize the Christ is deliberately affirmed elsewhere in Matthew's gospel and indeed, several times he points to the indifference of the Jews, even when informed, to the Messiah.
