# The anointing at Bethany

The Gospel (John 12:1-11). Jesus had been marked down for death by the Jewish authorities : 'from that day of the raising of Lazarus on which they took counsel how to put him to death' (11:52) - and the narrative of the anointing at Bethany follows with dramatic appropriateness. The anointing is explicitly associated with his burial by Jesus himself (12:7). Symbolically, then, we are to understand that he is already dead (11:53) and buried (12:7). In this context, the triumphal entry into Jerusalem, which follows immediately (12:12-15), must have the signification of his triumph, his glorification. Mary has come to understand that Jesus is 'the resurrection and the life' (11:25). Jesus' words in 12:8 put the
continuing Christian obligation of service to the poor in proper perspective. Mary's anointing of the deathless dying one is a recognition of the real nature of the Son of God; and recognition of the person and stature of Christ inspires and gives meaning to the service of the poor.
