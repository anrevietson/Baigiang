# The grain of wheat

Gospel (Jn 12:20-33). Verses 27-28 contain elements found in the Synoptic accounts of the Agony in the Garden. Jesus' soul is troubled and he voices the prayer: 'Father, save me from this hour' but accepts his destiny. John transforms what was a very private event at Gethsemane into a public manifestation of Jesus' union with the Father. Thus the prayer of the agony is turned into a question spoken before the crowd and the prayer Jesus actually makes is, 'Father, glorify your name'. This is followed by a divine manifestation as a voice from heaven answers in the affirmative. The divine witness to Jesus leads to a judgement on the world that rejects him (cf. Jn 3:18-21). The 'prince of this world' is Satan, but John uses Satan only as a foil for Jesus' victory or to account for Judas's betrayal (13:2,27; 14:30; 16:11). The 'grain of wheat' also occurs in I Cor 15:36-38 with reference to the nature of the resurrected body. In the Synoptics; 'hating one's life' refers to the acceptance of opposition and conflict that is involved in being a disciple.
