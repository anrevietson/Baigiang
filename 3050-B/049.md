# The new covenant

First Reading (Jer 31:32-34). Today's four scripture texts give us a rounded summary of the biblical doctrine of the new covenant, the covenant written on human hearts. This first reading gives the well-known prophecy of Jeremiah on this covenant. The prophecy found in Jer 31:31-34 is but the culmination of a rather long process of biblical reflection. The earlier prophets preached the demands of God's law, of Israel's election and what was required by this. They reminded God's people of the dire national consequences of disobedience to the demands of the covenant. A change of approach can be detected in Jeremiah who pondered on the weakness and waywardness of the human heart (Jer 17:9). Only God could fathom it. The book of Deuteronomy, which seems to represent a literature current in Jeremiah's day, speaks of the need of openness of the heart to God, of circumcision of the heart (Deut 10:18; 30:6). Through exile, and in its aftermath, God would 'circumcise' the hearts of his people, so that they could love him with all their heart (Deut 30:6). In the present passage from the book of Jeremiah it is recognised that in a new order God must take the initiative. He must give the people a new inner being, a new heart and a new spirit. This same theme is taken up by the prophet Ezekiel Ezek 36:24-29), through whom God promises that he will cleanse his people internally, remove from them the heart of stone and give in its stead a heart of flesh.
Jeremiah in his prophecy does not specify when this new order, this new covenant, will become a reality. He presumably would have regarded it as coming soon rather than later. We tend to link fulfilment of the prophecy with Christ's death and resurrection. In so doing we very probably unduly limit the intent of the original. The psalmist in today's Responsorial Psalm (Ps 50/51) seems to consider that the living out of this promise was also something for his own day. He prayed that God would create for him a new heart and a new spirit, a humble and contrite heart.
