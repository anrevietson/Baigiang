# Christ the high priest

Second Reading (Heb. 5 :1-9). Of all the New Testament writings, only the Epistle to the Hebrews deals explicitly with the priesthood of Christ. Nowhere else is Jesus called hiereus (priest) or archiereus (high-priest), although the doctrine of his mediator-function between God and man is found in various ways throughout the Gospels and Epistles. Perhaps the very word 'priest' had too close an association with the corrupt, politics-ridden priesthood of Jerusalem, for Paul or the evangelists to use it of Jesus. St Peter calls the whole Christian community a 'holy priesthood', offering spiritual sacrifice to God (1 Pet. 2 :5-6); and the Apocalypse (1:6; 5 :10; 20 :5) likewise sees the Christian calling and holiness as a real
priesthood. But only in Hebrews is it clarified what 'priest' means; how Jesus Christ is the one essential priest; and how he surpasses the centuries-old priesthood of the Mosaic law. Heb. 6 :1-9 is a key-text in this clarification.
In Heb. 4 :14 we are told that Jesus is our 'great high priest, who has passed through the heavens' (i.e. he stands in the presence of God, in a position of real efficacy). Yet he is not too far away to understand our needs, since 'in every respect he has been tempted as we are, but without sinning'. This combination of nearness to God and sympathy with men makes him the perfect priest, far beyond the humanly imperfect priesthood of Aaron's sons. Our text for today enlarges on the superiority of Christ's priesthood to theirs. The Aaronic priests (Levites), being ordinary men, had sins of their own to expiate, even as they offered sacrifices for the sins of their people. Jesus, however, is more than a mere man, but is the 'Son of God', appointed as the eternal highpriest. He learned solidarity with us sinners when, 'in the days of his flesh' he suffered human afflictions, and learned the experience of suffering obedience. That patient obedience perfected his humanity, and made him truly a redeeming, compassionate priest, for all who put their trust in him.
