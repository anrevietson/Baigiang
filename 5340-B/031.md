# A hymn to the glorified Christ

Second Reading (Rev. 1:5-8). This exultant hymn in praise of the glorified Christ is not only a beautiful prayer but also a remarkable summary of Christological belief and a very fruitful source of meditation. The author whose aim was to encourage his readers in the face of sufferings and persecutions which the Romans were soon to inflict on them, points to Jesus as the model of all those who have to suffer for their beliefs. Jesus is 'the faithful witness', for not only did he bear witness to truth during his ministry (see today's Gospel and 1 Tim. 6 :13) but he crowned his preaching by laying down his life in order to remain faithful to the mission given to him by his Father. The Christian too may be called on to suffer and even to die for his beliefs. Yet he need not fear because Jesus has overcome even death, and by becoming 'the first-born from the dead' (cf. 1 Cor. 15 :20; Col. 1:18) he has given his followers a guarantee that they too will conquer death and share in the glory of their risen Lord.
Through death Jesus has entered into the glory of the Father and he has been established as 'the ruler of the kings of the earth' . He has been given that universal dominion, unlimited by time or space, that had been promised to the Davidic Messiah (cf. Ps. 89 :28). But he exerclses his kingship not by forcing people into subjection but by loving them and by winning their love. He who once proved his love by sacrificing his life in order to wash away our sins (cf. John 13:1; Gal. 2:20; Eph. 5:2 etc.) continues to love us now although he is our King and Judge. He does not treat us as slaves. Rather has he given us a new dignity by granting us the privileges of the old people of God and making us 'a line of kings, priests to serve his God and Father' (cf. Ex. 19 :6; 1 Pet. 2 :9). As Christians we are privileged to be able to worship and serve our glorified Lord, who although he is mighty and majestic is still loving and gentle.
The enemies of Christ (those who pierced him') will submit to him when he comes in glory and power on the clouds (cf. first reading) and all those who persecuted Christ or his Church will have compunction for their wickedness. They will have to recognize him as the final Judge. He is the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. He began history and he will control it to the end. He it is who will judge those who have not joined the faithful Christians in honouring their King and in singing the hymn 'to him be glory and power for ever and ever. Amen.'
