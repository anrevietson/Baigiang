# Jesus examined by Pilate

The Gospel (John 18 :33-37). Jn describing this dramatic scene where Jesus is privately examined by Pilate John is interested in the spiritual significance of the confrontation between the humble Galilean and the representative of mighty Rome. The kingdom of Christ would always be tried and harassed by 'the world' and here was the founder himself being tried by the Roman leader in whom we may see symbolised the power and the wickedness which John usually associates with the term 'world' (cf. John 1:10). By asserting that his kingdom is not of this world (cf. John 8 :23; 15 :19; 17 :14, 16) Jesus made clear the non-political and non-seditious nature of his claim to kingship. His mission was to give witness to the truth and to call men to submit to the demands of divine truth as revealed by him and to recognise God as the all-important King and Judge. Jesus does not challenge Pilate's right to govern tiny Palestine but he does assert that all who are morally sincere must accept his teaching and become members of the kingdom he has come to found. The dilemma in which Pilate found himself when confronted with Jesus has its own lesson for us. The Roman leader was convinced that the accusation of treason brought against Jesus was false. He could say 'I find no crime in him' (v.39). Yet he did not wish to offend the powerful accusers of the gentle Jesus. He thought that by appealing to the custom of releasing a prisoner at Passover he could save the accused without incurring the wrath of the accusers (v.39). But his little plan didn't work and because he failed to follow his conscience he was unable to save the innocent prisoner. The case shows that there are no half-measures for the followers of Jesus. One must be for him or against him. Christ the King calls for total and enthusiastic submission to divine truth. He who does not clearly choose Jesus and his cause rather than the world and its interests is not worthy of the kingdom of God.
