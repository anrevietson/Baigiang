# Christ the King

Today's celebration is the solemnity of Christ the King. While we are conscious of Jesus' royal stature, the liturgy also reminds us of our own royalty that stems from our baptism and calls us to a life of service.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must candidly admit that we have not always lived up to the implications of our royal status. Aware of these shortcomings, we seek our God's forgiveness.
You make us members of your family in baptism and call us to demonstrate our unique status in daily life. Lord, have mercy.
You ask us to be aware of the special dignity of our sisters and brothers and enable us to see that they too are your sons and daughters. Christ, have mercy.
You bid us to show allegiance to your Word in all segments of our lives and help us to recognize that loyalty to that Word takes precedence over all other allegiances. Lord, have mercy.
Generous and loving God, we glory in the realization that we share in your royal dignity. Aid us to lead trusting lives on the basis of that dignity. Help us also to serve your entire family and thus witness to your gifts to us.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Dan 7:13-14). The author of Daniel exhorts his audience to remain faithful during a time of persecution. He assures them that in 'one like a son of man' triumph will be the reward for their perseverance.
Second Reading (Rev 1:5-8). The author of Revelation urges his audience to cling to their faith during a period of persecution. The basis for their hope is Jesus, the exalted one, who is now the ruler of the kings of the earth.
Gospel (Jn 18:33-37). John presents a Jesus whose kingship is non-political and non-national. Indeed his royalty focuses on truth, i.e., the revelation of God's Word.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Realizing that we are called to demonstrate our royal dignity in all situations of life, we address these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all heads of state. May they exhibit their high calling by dedication and service to their people.
We pray for all the leaders in our Church. May they reveal their sense of leadership by looking, first and foremost, to the needs of their sisters and brothers.
We pray for all the baptized. May they give evidence of their lofty vocation by concern for and dedication to all humanity.
We pray for all the deceased who reflected their royalty in unflagging devotion to their world. May their example become truly infectious for us today.
President: Father, in your Son you have revealed the ultimate meaning of royal dignity. Enable us to take that model and lead lives of service whereby others become the beneficiaries of our time and talent. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we are to reflect on 'thy kingdom come.' We are reminded that our God's plan for humanity is still in the process of being realized. Hence we are urged to play our part in fulfilling that plan.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Worship calls to mind in a very special way the unique dignity of our sisters and brothers. The sign of peace, therefore, challenges us to recall that dignity not only during worship but also in all the other segments of daily living.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Eucharist vividly recalls the self-giving of Jesus. It is only against that background that we can fully appreciate the kingship of Jesus. In turn, the bread and the wine are both the challenge and the means for living out the implications of our own royal status. To be son and daughter of God and to be brother and sister of Jesus mean that we must be servants to all of humanity, particularly those who are hurting the most.
