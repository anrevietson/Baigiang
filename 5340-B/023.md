# Jesus before Pilate

Gospel (Jn 18:33-37). Had the Jews the power to execute Jesus they would stone him. In the verses immediately preceding today's Gospel Jesus is brought from the house of Caiphas to the Praetorium in an attempt to use Pilate to do their work. The great manipulation begins, the crowd minders orchestrate the emotions of the people to the level of hysteria. Pilate attempts to shift responsibility but fails. He invokes the custom of releasing a prisoner at the Passover in an effort to release Jesus. Well tutored, the crowd rejects the ploy. Thinking that a scourging might satisfy them, Pilate orders it. It is not enough.
It is against this background that we are invited in today's Gospel to witness the conversation (rather than trial) between Pilate and Jesus. By contrast to the emotional scenes outside, there is a sense of being in another place. Though he is scourged and bleeding, weakened and in pain, the overwhelming impression is of the dignity of Jesus. It is he who commands the conversation, leaving Pilate in no doubt that his kingdom is not of this world. Both fence with words, both know the end line:
political expediency requires a death.
