# Daniel's vision

First Reading (Dan 7:13-14). During the persecution by Antiochus IV Epiphanes, around 165 B.C., the author of Daniel took up the question facing faithful Jews: Would the forces of evil ultimately triumph or would God's people finally have the upper band? In response to this issue the author pictures four beasts that refer respectively to the pagan empires of the Neo-Babylonians, the Medes, the Persians, and the Greeks (see Dan 7:2-7). With regard to the Greeks the 'little horn' Qan 7:8) is none other than the persecutor himself, viz., Antiochus IV Epiphanes.
At this juncture the author depicts a royal audience where God, the Ancient One, is flanked by numerous angels. In this royal setting the fourth beast is killed. Next, "one like a son of man," i.e., a human being, appears before God. He is granted dominion, glory, and kingdom in view of the persecution he has endured. He is also identified with the "saints of the Most High" ([)an 7:18). He is an individual who represents a larger group, viz., those faithful Jews who have persevered despite pain and torture. In the "one like a son of man" the audience can recognise itself and find courage to remain loyal to their covenant with God.
