# Thức dậy và sống ngày cứu độ

**Tác giả:** Chưa xác định

Second Reading (Romans 13 :11-14). The Old Testament writers presumed that when God's plan reached its climax, this would be the end of time; 'the time' would have come, the great 'day of the Lord' would mark the close of history. The early Christians, St Paul included, shared this view at first; but they quickly realised that although in Christ God's definitive work of salvation has been achieved, nevertheless it has to be realised in the life of each individual Christian. So we can both say 'The Day has come' and 'We wait for the Day'. But since the coming of our Lord, our hope is not just a desire; our hope is a confidence and a certainty based on the fact of his coming and the life it has brought us. Paul ends his letter to the Romans (as he does most of his letters) with an exhortation; and here he bases his exhortation on this dual aspect of salvation - that it is something already achieved and yet something to be achieved. And as he does so, other ideas connected with 'the Day of the Lord' occur to him Day - day-light - dawn - a new day, a new start - a time to be up and doing - a time for work and for 'day-light' works, not for night-time, dark works; the night is the time of ignorance, the dark is the time for shameful works. But he ends (slipping into yet another metaphor as he does so : 'Day - work - action - battle - armour') by reminding them once more that our salvation is a reality; Christ for us is not just a memory and a model; he is our life, it is in his strength that we fight.
