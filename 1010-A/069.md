# Đức Kitô quy tụ muôn dân trong hòa bình

**Tác giả:** Chưa xác định

First Reading (Isaiah 2 :1-5). One of the key concepts of the Old Testament was the idea of 'covenant' - God's loving initiative of allying himself with men, and in doing so, to sow the seeds of harmony between men. A symbol of the covenant was the temple, where God 'dwelt' with his people, and which was therefore also the focal point of the national unity. And in the temple was enshrined another factor in the covenant-relationship - the Law, God's guidance for his people, helping them to find peace, justice and unity. The prophet (possibly secon4-Isaiah; in any case, hardly the author of the rest of these early chapters) looks forward to a time when the covenant between God and Israel will be extended to all mankind; where the temple will be the focal point for all men, not for Israel only - he describes this with poetic hyperbole as a miraculous enlargement of the hill on which the Temple stood, so that it becomes a mountain which towers up for all the world to see. Then Israel's precious gift of God's word will be, not their exclusive possession but theirs to teach to others. Following God's law, men will then live in peace with each other. But if Israel is to play her role of 'light of the nations', she must first follow God's law herself: 'House of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord'.
The prophet's yearning was fulfilled in Christ. In him the covenant was made perfect; God dwelt amongst us; he was in reality all that the temple stood for, his Risen Body replaces the temple building (John 2 :21). We do not rely on a written law to show us God's will, but Christ is our light, our way, the truth. His law is that men should love one another, and loving one another, live in peace and harmony. He thus laid the foundations of the kingdom of God, in which all men live as brothers. But the work is not yet completed; and, as eagerly as the prophet of old, we pray for his light to be made manifest to all the world.
