# The flight into Egypt

Gospel (Mt 2:13-15.19-23). On what level or levels should we read this passage about the flight into Egypt? The story is so carefully constructed that we might wonder about the logic of Matthew's imagination in telling it in this particular way. Perhaps the last line gives a clue:
In this way the words spoken through the prophets were to be fulfilled. The first hearers of the gospel would have been familiar with the story of Moses leading his people out of Israel. The same Exodus idea is taken up here, but transformed to suggest that we have in Jesus both continuity and discontinuity with the tradition. So we have Moses fleeing from Egypt (Ex.2: 15), and returning to his people there (Ex 4:20), (an exact verbal echo in Mt 2:20) whereas Jesus escapes to Egypt and returns 'out of Egypt' to Israel, to fulfill the words of Hosea: I called my son out of Egypt. So for Matthew Jesus is a new and greater Moses - a new Israel.
On a personal note I find it regrettable that verses 16-18 are omitted from the lectionary. They contain a very moving text from Jeremiah, which Matthew uses to describe the women mourning the death of their children at the hands of Herod:
A voice is heard in Ramah, lamenting and weeping bitterly:
it is Rachel weeping for her children, refusing to be comforted because they are no more. (Jer 31:15; Mt 2:18)
