# The flight into Egypt

Gospel (Mt 2:13-15,19-23). If the earlier readings are marked by the traditional wisdom of family life, then the Gospel story might be read in a different key. Traditional wisdom, by its nature, tends to support what is solid, to speak in terms of the ideal. The Gospel of Matthew tells the story of a family uprooted by political terror, of the slaughter of the innocent and the flight of a carpenter and his young family into exile. There are preludes of the passion story here. There are also echoes of the past, of the story of a people who knew exile and slavery in Egypt. The rescue of the child from the wicked king also parallels the rescue of Moses from the slaughter decreed by Pharaoh. The figure of Joseph is an almost forgotten one in the Christian story: in Matthew's account he is the representative of the just man of Israel, the obedient servant of the Lord. He discerns and obeys the word of the Lord revealed in dreams, like Joseph the dreaming son of Jacob, who was a favourite figure in the Wisdom traditions of Israel.
The readings of the liturgy as seem to hint at once at both the value and limits of traditional wisdom in family life: it can offer a certain security but it will be tested by the unexpected, and the new, where the Word of God can also be discerned.
