# The Christian household

Second Reading (Col. 3 :12-21). Only the last section of this reading deals with the home and family. The greater part is concerned with general rules for Christian behaviour. The terms used to describe Christian community life are those used to describe Israel in the Old Testament - chosen, saints, beloved. Christians comprise the new Israel of the new Covenant. Since they have experienced God's love they should be characterised by gratitude and rejoicing. Because Christians have experienced God's love they should also practice compassion, kindness, patience, 'humility and forgiveness. Possibly Paul has the Our Father in mind here. Love completes and binds together all the virtues. It unites all in one body with Christ at the head. Obligations are mutual. Christians encourage one another by words and songs in the limrgical assembly. To call on the name of the Lord was so characteristic of the Christians that it even identified them (cf. Acts 9 :14, 21).
In the final section of the reading the more important virtues in the home are listed; love and gentleness are prescribed for all. Wholehearted fulfilment of ones duties in the family is practical obedience to the Lord. The criterion for all conduct in the family is 'what will please the Lord.'
