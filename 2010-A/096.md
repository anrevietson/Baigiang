# The ethics of the household

Second Reading (Col 3:12-21). Colossians places the ethics of the narrower circle of the household within its treatment of the demands of life in the Church, the household of God. Believers are instructed to clothe themselves in the fundamental Christian virtues as though they were a new set of clothes with love as final fastening; this may well have echoes of the baptismal liturgy. Yet it is not merely an external change -Christ's peace and his message must become part of the inner dynamism of a life which manifests itself in joyful and grateful worship. It is this larger setting on which to reflect on family and its values which commends Colossians in today's liturgy - its fundamental values are to nourish life in the family-church. In the light of this ideal picture of the fundamental Christian attitudes, it is possible that its practical conclusions about life in the family may sound flat and rather trite particularly true of the word to wives). These belong to the standard 'household codes' of Greek and Jewish ethics of the period. They were adopted into New Testament literature as embodying the good sense of the culture, but were lightly christianised, as here, with a reference to pleasing the Lord as their new motivation.
