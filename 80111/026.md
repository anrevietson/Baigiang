# The vision of the redeemed

First Reading (Rev 7:2-4,9-14). The call of the book of the Revelations is for persecuted Christians to persevere in faith even to the point of death since, in the light of Christian experience, death is but a door leading to new life. Those, then, who remained steadfast would live forever in the Kingdom of God.
One of the questions arising in this liturgical year is whom the author means in referring to the one hundred and forty-four thousand. It seems likely that the author has in mind those Christians who were still alive and struggling when the end came, those who had persevered in the faith. The other "group beyond counting" may then refer to the dead, to those believers already in heaven.
Whatever the symbolism, the passage expresses the hope of all people who labour and struggle with living the faith whether that means contending with persecutions or just the daily ups and downs of a dedicated Christian life. In the end, we all hope to be in the ranks of the saints. We hope and pray that our labours will be remembered by God, and that we too will stand before the Throne and the Lamb.
