# The vision of the redeemed

First Reading (Rev 7:2-4.9-14). The Book of Revelation is usually attributed to John the Apostle, writing from exile on the island of Patmos to which he has been banished by the Roman authorities. John addresses the Seven Churches of Asia, in other words, the totality of Churches. This is a book destined for liturgical reading. John presents himself as part of a group persecuted for its faith. He endeavours to strengthen the faith of this group by revealing to them the meaning of the oppression they are presently enduring and the glorious goal of their suffering.
This book is rich in apocalyptic symbolism. In Verses 1-8, John seeks to show how the Church on earth is preserved by God. The protecting angel appears in the east, the source of light and the place of Paradise. The Messiah was expected from the east. Whatever was marked by the seal (an image inspired by Ezekiel 9) belonged to the Lord and was under the Lord's protection. Whoever bears 'the seal of the living God' will thus be God's property, ultimately secure in the midst of persecution. This does not preclude the Christians' escape from persecution or death but assures them of God's strength to help them persevere. The symbolic number of those 'sealed' suggests a multitude of the Elect whose real number is known only to God.
In Verses 9-14, John envisages the multitudes of Christians, victorious over persecution, appearing in full glory. This is the Church in heaven glorifying God. The palms are signs of the victory and thanksgiving of the Elect that, having been washed in 'the blood of the Lamb' (sharing the salvific power of the death of Christ through their own endurance of persecution to the end), their fidelity is now being rewarded.
