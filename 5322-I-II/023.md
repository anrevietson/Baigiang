# The souls of the just

First Reading (Wis. 2 :23-3 :9). Life, the writer of Wisdom knows, is the reward of virtue. But what kind of life? Here he tells us : it is life lived with God after death.
Because he is made in the image of God, and because the living God of the Old Testament is the very opposite of death, man (says the book of Wisdom) can gain immortality. This (3 :4) is the first time the noun 'immortality' occurs in the Old Testament. What the writer has in mind is not bodily immortality, i.e., the resurrection of the body What he is expressing is the carefully-nurtured hope that the virtuous man will not lose his intimate friendship with God after his death. For 'the souls of the virtuous are in the hands of God' (3 :1).
The writer is interested not in physical death - he knows that comes to man because be is of the earth. His concern is with spiritual death - the only real death because it cuts one off from God. Satan is its agent, and it is sin alone that can cause it. Sufferings and trials are a testing of one's belief in God's goodness. For 'only in winter can one tell which trees are evergreen'.
