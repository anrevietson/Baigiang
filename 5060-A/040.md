# Fulfilling the law

Gospel (Mat. 5:17-37). Matthew was concerned that the members of the church to whom he was writing his gospel understand the true relationship between the Gospel and the Law. Since he wrote to a Jewish Christian community that now was excluded from the synagogue, he had to help them understand that they were now the true Israel. Matthew believed what God had done in and for Israel was good. The Law was the expression of God's will and a revelation of God. Matthew therefore taught that Jesus did not come to annul or terminate the Law and Prophets. Law and prophets was seen here as the whole of scripture and God's revelation. Jesus, rather, came to affirm the Law and also build upon it. The Law was not perfect in itself and Jesus came to bring it to perfection.
In the verses which follow, Matthew shows how this it true. Our reading today give us four of the six examples that are found in this chapter. They deal with anger, adultery, divorce and oaths. The statement of the Law is contrasted with the teaching of Jesus. Jesus is shown to have brought each a deeper meaning and a greater insight. The demand is more intense. This means that the disciples of Jesus have to make a greater commitment.
