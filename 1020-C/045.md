# Dating the ministry of Jesus

Gospel (Lk 3:1-6). Luke begins his account of the public ministry of Jesus by dating it as precisely as he can, a historical perspective which characterizes his two-volume work. The preaching of John is a call for repentance which will lead to a forgiveness of sins. Like the other Synoptics he links the ministry of the Baptist with the prophet of the exiles (Is 40). However, he alone adds the theme of universal salvation, of major importance in his Gospel: 'And all mankind shall see the salvation of God'.
The words of the prophet-poet comforting the people of Israel in the Babylonian exile resound through Advent, 'Hear ye! there is one who cries out, "In the wilderness prepare a way for the Lord, make straight in the desert a highway for our God".' We prepare a way for the Lord through the penance of openness. We take the barriers down. We give the Lord access to our lives.
I keep a copy of 'The Light of the World' in my living room. The original was painted by Holman Hunt in 1854. It portrays the Christ, crowned with thorns, carrying a lantern, knocking at a closed door. When the artist showed the picture to his friends, one of them pointed out that he had put no handle on the door. Holman Hunt replied: 'We must open to the Light- the handle is on the inside'. If we remove the obstacles which would hinder the action of God, if we open the door to his risen Son, then he will come and make his home within us through his Holy Spirit (see Jn 14:23). 'Listen! I am standing at the door, knocking; if you hear my voice and open the door, I will come in to you, and you with me' (Rev 3:20). Saint Ambrose declares: 'Blessed therefore, is that man at whose door Christ knocks. Our door is faith which, if strong, fortifies the whole house. It is through this door that Christ comes in'. In Advent, we ask the Lord to deepen our faith, to strengthen our hope, to enkindle our love. Thus, in the present coming (the in-between Advent), he will be our rest and our consolation.
We may feel a little uncomfortable at the idea of Advent penance. It is an admission that we are not self-sufficient, that we need to open ourselves to the powerful presence of God. We need to give up a little, to give way a little, to give over a little. We need to open our door to the poor Christ that he may join at our Christmas table. Our Lord was not born in a palace, or even a semi-detached. He was born in the stable area underneath the house of his relatives. He was not laid in a cradle of silk but rather on a manger of wood. He had no Mercedes official car. He walked the roads of Palestine as an itinerant preacher. He did not die a much publicized death, mourned by millions, He died a criminal's death on a cross mourned by his mother, the loyal women from Galilee, and one male disciple. 'The birds of the air have nests', he had said, 'but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head' (Lk 9:58). Our Father God wished to ensure that when his Son became man, he would identify with the deprived.
A good proportion of our Christmas sufficiency should go to the poor. At Christmas we have a large family. We are all Christmas people. If we merely look after our friends, if we withdraw into the unity of our own family comfort, we are missing the message of Advent. 'All mankind shall see the salvation of God'. That includes the hungry, the homeless, the disabled, the deprived. We are challenged to bring that divine salvation to them through our healing word and work.
Such a healing ministry requires that increase of love for each other of which St Paul spoke to the Philippians. For Christian love is a sharing of life together with others with a deep respect for them, a continued concern for their needs, a growing understanding of ways to help them in those needs, and a ready appreciation of others who come to help us in our own need. It is a love which continues to challenge us towards the more rather than the less.
We pray in the words of St. Richard of Chichester: 'Lord Jesus, as we begin this new year in the Church, help us to see you more clearly, to love you more dearly, to follow you more nearly'
