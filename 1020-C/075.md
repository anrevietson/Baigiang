# The Book of Baruch

First Reading (Bar 5:1-9). The Book of Baruch, a collection of several different passages, supposedly directed towards the exiles in Babylon, was really put together in the Greek language several hundred years later. In fact, a writer of the Greek period is holding out to the Jewish people scattered throughout the Mediterranean world (the Diaspora) the promise of divine assistance. The verses read in today's liturgy are part of a concluding poem which begins with the city of Jerusalem personified as speaking to its people in exile. They are reminded of their sinful past, for which they have been punished. They are promised divine intervention on their behalf in the future. Now the poet addresses Jerusalem and encourages the City to throw off her mourning garment and put on the garment of light which comes from her Lord. The forsaken city on the holy mount will see her children from the West and the East return to her in safety, protected by their God who will flatten the mountains and fructify the forests before them.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps 125). A song of deliverance from exile. Just as the parched torrent-beds of Southern Israel are filled with flooding streams from the winter rains, so the life of the people also is renewed by the Creator-Saviour.
