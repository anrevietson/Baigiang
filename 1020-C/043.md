# The public ministry begins

Gospel (Lk 3:1-6). Having completed the infancy narrative, Luke now begins his narrative of the public ministry of Jesus. He sets this ministry in the context of the ministry of John the Baptist, which he is careful to date with reference to the Roman Emperor of the day and the local political and religious authorities of the day. In this way, Luke emphasises that the life and ministry of Jesus happened on the stage of world history and was destined to shape significantly the course of that history. The aspect of John's ministry which is highlighted in this reading is his preaching which called for repentance with a view to receiving forgiveness for sin. That this is not simply a vague call but has concrete implications for particular groups in the society of that time, like tax-collectors and soldiers, is clear from the following verses (3:10-14). In linking the ministry of John the Baptist to the mission of Deutero-Isaiah to the exiles, it is only Luke among the evangelists who extends the quotation from Isaiah 40 to include the statement 'and all mankind will see the salvation of God'. Thus the quality of universalism which so characterises the ministry of Jesus in Luke is indicated at the very beginning of his public ministry. In Luke, Jesus is God's salvation, sent to both Gentiles and the people of Israel (2:30-32). He is the Son of Man who has come to seek and to save the lost (19:10), whoever they are.
