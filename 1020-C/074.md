# Jerusalem, put on the robe of glory

First Reading (Bar 5:1-9). This reading is part of the prophet's word to the city of Jerusalem (4:30-5:9) in response to Jerusalem's own lament to her inhabitants scattered as exiles in Babylon (4:5-29). Jerusalem spoke as a widow, a woman bereaved of her children, dressed in mourning robes. The prophet now calls on Jerusalem to change her dress because of God's gracious initiative towards her. If she were to look towards the east, in the direction of Babylon, she would see the result of this initiative, her own children coming back to her, carried on thrones. God has flattened the hills and filled the valleys to create the shortest possible route between Babylon and Jerusalem. God's mercy and saving justice will accompany them as escorts on this journey. The book was actually written later than the Babylonian exile, most likely in the Greek period. The author makes use of the historical setting of the Babylonian exile to send a message of hope to the Jews of his own day who are living far from home.
