# A living hope

Second Reading (1 Pet. 1:3-9). This year the second reading for the Sundays after Easter is taken from the first letter of St Peter. For a commentary on this letter see SCRIPTURE IN CHURCH, no.1, pp.128-134. The spiritual rebirth of Christians and the practical obligations which this entails are the main topics of today's lesson. The author praises and thanks God for the new and supernatural life which he has bestowed on Christians through the resurrection of Christ. God communicates this new life to the soul of the believer at baptism 'by means of the cleansing water of rebirth and by renewing us with the Holy Spirit' (Tit. 3 :5). However, the new life which becomes a reality at baptism is never quite perfected and never fully secure in this life. The pilgrim Christian is fallible and weak and he can lose or spoil his inheritance. Yet he does not lose hope for he is assured that God himself will defend the inheritance of divine life which he has bestowed until it is made secure in heaven where it cannot be devastated by enemies nor defiled by sins.
Because he believes that God will enable him to reach a perfect and lasting salvation the Christian is confident that he will be able to overcome the many trials and difficulties that he is sure to meet on his journey to final union with his Saviour. Indeed one of the paradoxes of the Christian life is that the believer can rejoice in trials and difficulties because he is sure that they will actually bring him to salvation (cf. Mat. 5:11 f.; Acts 14:22). Since Christ achieved man's salvation through suffering the believer considers it a privilege and a joy to share in his Lord's suffering. The Christian's love for a Lord whom he has not seen and his conviction of the reality of a salvation that is invisible (cf. Heb. 11:1) lead him to accept a way of life that is humanly unattractive and help him to find joy in things that are naturally repugnant.
The follower of Christ as described in this reading is a man who believes that he has received a new life through Jesus Christ as a gift from God. This belief leads him to hope that this life will be perfected in the world to come. His belief and hope lead him to love Jesus Christ who is the source of his salvation and they enable him to bear with joy all the trials that fidelity to Christ entails
