# Jesus appears to the disciples

Gospel (Jn 20:19-31). This selection from John's gospel closely follows the Easter Sunday readings particularly in its theme of belief, omitting only John 20:10-18. The Risen Lord appears to his disciples and sends them forth with the power of his Spirit. However, Thomas, one of the Twelve, absent on this occasion, demands a sign before he will believe. The unit ends with a summary indicating the purpose of the signs, that of believing in Jesus. Let us examine the two major events.
In the first, disciples fearfully gather behind closed doors, their fear specified as fear of the Jews (Jn 19:38). Jesus appears, using the usual greeting of peace, and the disciples are filled with joy because of his presence. Jesus sends them forth just as the Father sent him and he endows them for their mission. John notes that Jesus breathes on them, reminiscent of the first creation in Genesis 2:7 when God breathed his Spirit into a living being. The Spirit of Jesus in his followers inaugurates a new creation, and will, in fact, also give them eternal life. This episode also alludes to the vision in the valley of the dry bones from Ezekiel, a reading we had during Lent. "I will cause breath (Spirit) to enter you, and you shall live" (Ezek 37:3-5), indicating new life for the people. Furthermore, this gift of the Spirit, given after Jesus' glorification, is a power entrusted to the Church to preserve new life through forgiveness of sin. John's episode also includes forceful imagery so characteristic of the gospel:
fear to joy; seclusion to mission; absence to presence; mere existence to new life. Underlying this unit is the conviction that faith in the Risen Lord accounts for the transformation indicative of the new creation.
In the second part of the reading, the focus is on Thomas, an emphasis only in the Fourth Gospel (Jn 1 1:16;14:5;20:24-28;21:2). Thomas seems to encompass courage, ignorance, doubt and faith in the successive gospel portrayals. Likewise, Thomas furthers the theme of belief since he conveys the inadequacy of his faith by asking for a sign. Belief in Jesus because of the signs he performs is only a starting point for faith. Signs point to Jesus, and in this gospel, we are asked to believe in him and so have life in his name.
However, Thomas' doubt becomes faith as he confesses "My Lord and my God." For a skilful writer like the author of this gospel, the confession forms an inclusion with John 1:1 "and the w6rd was God." Both of these statements indicate the level of the community's faith in Jesus. This language also reminds us that we can address Jesus in the same language that Israel addressed Yahweh.
The only beatitude in the gospel refers to those who believe in Jesus without the signs, a reference to all Christians who believe on the testimony of others. This episode, as the initial gospel, challenges us to believe in Jesus and to experience the consequences of that belief life in his name.
