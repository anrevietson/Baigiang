# A key subject in our lives

In today's Mass we are invited to consider a key subject in our lives. As we celebrate God's gifts in this Eucharist, we are challenged to question our attitude to wealth and poverty, to leisure and to the needs of others.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We are all affected by the pressures of consumerism and the promotion of a life of leisure. We need God's help to enable us to achieve a balance between what is necessary and sufficient for our well-being and what is in excess.
You call us to show concern for one another. Lord, have mercy.
You challenge our ambition and our greed. Christ, have mercy.
You teach us to be generous and responsible. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Am 6:1,4-7). The self-indulgent use of wealth that shows no concern for the sufferings of the poor is bound to lead to the disintegration of society.
Second Reading (1 Tim 6:11-16). The love of money is the root of all evils. People who long to be rich get trapped in foolish ambitions.
Gospel (Lk 16:19-31). Responsibility goes hand-in-hand with wealth. Constant concern for others is the only safeguard against the blindness and hardness of heart that can befall those who are preoccupied with growing rich.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Our personal talents and all the world's resources are God's gift to us. He wills that we use his gifts in a manner that shows concern for others. We ask God's guidance and help in this responsibility.
Response: Lord, hear our prayer.
Reader(s):For the Church: that she may continually show concern and support for those who are neglected and downtrodden.
For those who make decisions about aid and trade with poorer countries.
For those who legislate regarding welfare in our own country: that they may act with compassion, equity and concern.
For ourselves: that we may live up to the principles the Gospel teaches us about generosity and concern for others.
For those who feel outcast and deprived: that they may persevere in their claims to a life of dignity and respect. For the dying, and those who have died: that they may experience your steadfast love and experience your comfort through the ministry of your people.
President: Father, we have heard your words concerning riches and wealth, concerning the poor and the downtrodden. We want to do your will. Help us to be alert to the needs of others, and generous in reaching out to them. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Remembering that we are called to be brothers and sisters in the world-wide family of God our Father, let us pray in the words Jesus taught us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
As a sign of our concern for one another and for all who need us in any way, let us exchange a sign of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Jesus said: "Ask and you shall receive, seek and you shall find, knock and the door shall be opened to you". He is concerned about us and he supplies all our needs. He also said: "When I was hungry you gave me food, when I was sick and in prison you visited me... "Our communion with him is a time for asking and receiving, and for offering ourselves to him through those who need our concern, near at hand or afar.
