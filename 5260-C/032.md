# Woe to the complacent

First Reading (Amos 6:1.4-7). The prophet Amos admonishes the rulers of Judah and southern Israel for their failure to recognize the poor in accordance with the mandates of the Torah. Theologically Amos directs his message toward a united kingdom of Israel and Judah, although politically the land is already divided. The prophet chides the rulers for their self-indulgence, not because luxury is evil but because their apathy has permitted the demise of the House of Joseph, which entails the northernmost reaches of Israel.
A later editor may have completed the final portion of today's passage because historically the deportation of the Israelites occurs after the time of Amos' preaching. The passage states how fitting it is that those who were the first to indulge in the bounty of Israel are the first to be deported. The theological point is the correct re-ordering of the society: the proud are stripped of their rank and independence. Those who ignored the basic necessities for survival and basic rights of others reap the effects of their demoralizing behaviour.
