# The worship of idols

First Reading (1 Cor. 10:14-22). The issue involved in the 'worship of idols' is the participation of Christians at the sacred meals of pagans at which meats offered to the pagan gods were eaten. The kernel of Paul's argument is that to eat the victim of a sacrifice is to enter into communion with the god. when, in this context, he speaks of the Eucharist, he clearly presupposes that the Eucharistic bread and Wine are the body and blood of Christ offered in sacrifice to God. The 'cup of blessing' is the technical term for the cup of wine (the third cup) over which the thanksgiving after the Passover meal has been said; the expression 'breaking of brea4' is a New Testament technical expression for the Eucharist. Through eating the bread and drinking the cup, Christians are united with Christ in an intimate fellowship (Koinonia) because the Eucharist is his body and blood. Paul affirms that if the Christian community can form the 'body of Christ' in the wider sense, it is primarily because it partakes of the Eucharistic body of the Lord.
