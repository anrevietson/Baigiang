# The call of Isaiah

First Reading (Is. 6 :1-8). Today we read of the call of the prophet Isaiah. Isaiah was called by God during a visit to the temple 'the year king Uzziah (of Judah) died', i.e. 742 BC. His vision is of a greater king, Yahweh king of Israel, whose glory (represented by the train of his royal robes) fills the whole temple. lie is surrounded by heavenly beings, whom Isaiah calls seraphim, 'the burning ones'. Like the statues in front of Mesopotamian and other eastern temples, they had six wings. The divine presence is represented by smoke (cf. Is. 4 :5; cp. Ex. 40 :34; 1 Kings 8 :10
- God's presence indicated by a cloud). At the presence of God the entire temple trembled. God revealed himself to Isaiah above all as the all-holy One. Holiness in the Bible means separation, being removed from sin and the things of this world. This vision of the holy One of Israel makes Isaiah acutely conscious of his own sin and of the sinfulness of his people. God in his holiness and glory is a devouring fire. Before becoming a messenger of God's holiness Isaiah himself must be made holy. This God does, by the symbolic action of having Isaiah's lips touched by burning coals from the fire at the altar of incense in the temple. Today's Gospel reading will recount the call of some of the successors of the prophets by Jesus.
The Responsorial Psalm is a song of thanksgiving sung in the temple, leading us to recall the vision of the prophet Isaiah.
