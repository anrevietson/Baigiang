# The Servant Song

First Reading (Is. 49:3, 5-6). This reading is one of the well-known Servant Songs. It is the Servant himself speaks and tells us of God's message to him. Before his birth he was chosen and set aside by God for a predetermined purpose, viz. to bring Israel, God's people, back to him, to lead them from sin and infidelity to a life worthy of their vocation. But, even this did not exhaust the Servant's mission. This was to go beyond Israel to the pagans, to all humanity in fact. The Servant would bring the light of revelation to the pagans and bring redemption to all men, to the very ends of the earth.
In this reading we have one of the most universalist passages of the entire Old Testament. The exile among the pagans of Babylon was the occasion in which God brought Israels prophets to a realisation of the fact that redemption was not only for the Jews. God loved all his creatures. Scholars argue endlessly as to the identity of the Servant in the mind of the author of this section of the book of Isaiah. We know that in point of fact the prophecy was fulfilled in the person of Christ. That this was so was seen by Simeon when Christ was presented in the temple' (Luke 2:23-32, read on Dec.29).
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 39) expresses the sentiments of Christ as he entered this world (cf. Heb. 10 :6-10). He came, as servant of the Lord, to do God's will and through his obedience to bring salvation to all men.
