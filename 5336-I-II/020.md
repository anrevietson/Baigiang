# The two witnesses

First Reading (Rev. 11:4-12). In the interpretation of the two witnesses or olive trees, authorities are divided. Obviously it is a question of triumph after persecution, and the two figures are described in terms of those great Old Testament leaders Moses (water into blood, and plagues) and Elijah (locking up the heavens). They are killed, but the incompleteness of this is shown by the number 3j, which is regarded as the antithesis of 7, or completeness. Does the author refer to Peter and Paul, martyred in Rome the 'great city', or to the remnants of the Jewish-Christian Church killed in AD 70 in Jerusalem, the city 'in which the Lord was crucified'? In either case they surely stand for the Church as a whole which is to be taken up to heaven after the period of persecution.
