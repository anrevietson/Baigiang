# The temptations of Jesus

Gospel (Mt 4:1-11). This passage follows the account of the baptism of Jesus and the coming of the Spirit upon him with the voice from heaven (the voice of the Father) declaring him to be the beloved Son. The Spirit guides Jesus throughout his public life. Jesus prepares for his mission by a period of prayer and fasting. At the end of this Satan tempts him, just as the first man and woman were tempted but failed and just as the Israelites were tempted in the desert during the journey to the promised land. Jesus uses in reply to Satan words form Deuteronomy (Deut 8:3; 6:16; 6:13) which were used in reference to the exodus and the trials of the Israelites in the desert. Satan twice says in this episode: 'If you are the Son of God.... 'In Scripture (e.g. Ex 4:23) Israel is called God's son. But Jesus is the Son of God in a unique way, as the Baptism account indicated. The temptations might be seen as exam-
pies of how we are tempted. All are based on various ways of sinning against the great commandment to love God with all one's heart, soul and might. Jesus, rejecting all these temptations, showed himself a faithful son and a true Israelite. He was tempted to take the crown without the cross but rejected it.
