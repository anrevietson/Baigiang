# Adam and Christ

Second Reading (Rom. 5:12-19). The second reading from today's Mass is taken from a letter of St Paul to Christians who were living in the permissive society of first-century Rome. The apostle sketches in briefly for them the sequel to man's first rebellion against God. Far from learning by bitter experience that his happiness is bound up with God's plans for him, man continues to rebel, heaping sin upon sin. And all the while, death stalks through the world, a grim reminder of his lapse from God's favour. Cheated of his birthright from the beginning, burdened with the
generations, man thus becomes acutely weakness within him - a weakness that re-enter the paradise of God's love and other words, his crying need for the gift
The gift when it comes is like all beyond human expectation : for God God's help, when it comes, follows the

And of all God's ways with men : it is simple, easy to understand, and it goes right to the root of the trouble. Man's failures lay in the pride of his desire to be like God, in the disobedience of his rebellion against God's plans, in his neglect to return God love for love. When God's Son comes, therefore, he comes as a new Adam, a new man representing and embodying within himself the whole human race. He comes in the humility of his earthly mission to counteract man's former pride; he comes in perfect obedience to the Father's plans, to counteract man's former rebellion; he comes with love to counteract man's former ingratitude.
Following on Christ's humble, devoted obedience, every son of man has now an unfailing source of strength that he can call upon. At baptism he is re-instated in the paradise of God's intimate friendship and love, he has implanted in him the mind and heart of Christ. And as he faces the challenges of everyday living, he is fortified from within by the outlook and attitude of God's own Son.
