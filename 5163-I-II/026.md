# Manna in the desert

First Reading (Ex. 16:1-5, 9-15). God's extraordinary providence for his starving people in the desert is described. The poetic description of the manna (bread from heaven) makes it a wonderfully suitable figure of the' Blessed Eucharist, the spiritual food that sustains us on our way to the heavenly homeland. The biblical description of the manna has convinced scholars that it was the sweet edible substance still found in the Sinai peninsula., It is produced by, insects from the tamarisk tree, and dropping to the ground hardens in the cool night air of the desert. It has to be collected early as it melts when the sun gets hot. The abundant quantity of this so opportunely available, points to an extraordinary intervention of God as' the Bible seems to suggest. The quail are small birds of the partridge 'family, which are known to be fairly easy to capture when they visit the Sinai peninsula as they annually migrate from Mica in the springtime, or return in the autumn.
