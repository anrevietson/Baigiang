# The early community

First Reading (Acts 4 :32-35). This is one of the three 'greater summaries' in Acts (ends of chapters three, four and five). The summary is a characteristic technique of Luke for building up a picture of progress in the Church (sometimes used also in the Gospel; cf. Luke 4 :40 ff., where he extends Mark 3 :11 f.). One usually finds a triple scheme: (i) scene; (ii) spread of the Gospel word; (iii) consequent response or attitude of the brethren.
Here the scene is omitted. The spread of the message comes in v.33, which actually breaks the context; and the consequent attitude is that 'all had one mind and one soul'. This leads to an idyllic depiction of the Christians sharing their p6ssessions, some-times described as 'Christian communism'. However, one notices that they still held ownership of their property. Moreover, that the picture is a bit idealistic is apparent from the following story of Ananias (chapter five). The essential message is the importance of concrete response to the message of faith; and this is also true of the story of Ananias, but by way of contrast.
