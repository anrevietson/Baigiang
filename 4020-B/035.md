# The community of believers

First Reading (Acts 4:32-35). As Luke writes Acts of Apostles, he pauses from time to time to summarize various aspects of the life of the early Church and its mission. One such summary focuses on the life of sharing experienced within the community life of the early Church. Luke's summary statements are easily identifiable in Greek, and are not intended to be a description of a single historical event, but more a description of what was typical in the life of the early Church - so summary statements do not refer to a single event but describe what was a common practice in the early community. Today's reading comes from one of Luke's summaries, describing the life of intense sharing practised by early believers. Focused especially on the teachings of the apostles, the breaking of bread, prayer, and community spirit (Acts 2:4245), their sharing in faith overflowed to sharing of their material goods too, so that there was no needy person in the community. By this strong emphasis on the quality of their sharing, Luke hopes to persuade readers of Acts that Christianity leads to ideal forms of community sharing. Greeks who may read Luke's work would be aware of the ideal form of communal sharing found in Plato's Republic, while Jews who read it would know that Moses foretold the coming of the ideal community of God in which no one would be in need Deut 15:4). Luke insists that the post-Easter Christian sharing authenticates that this is the community of God. Poverty does not consist in abnegation but in sharing with others so as to remove need.
