# Paul's greeting to the Romans

Second Reading (Rom 1:1-7). In this address to the people of Rome, Paul gives a longer opening than usual. His letter is written in anticipation of his visit and was probably written in Corinth between 54 and 59 A.D. It seems that Paul's own status as an apostle had been challenged and he plans to go to Jerusalem to clarify matters, though he has some doubts as to his reception there ~om 15:30-32). Perhaps this is why in the first verses here he stresses his call as apostle. Later he talks of the growing difficulties between Jewish and non-Jewish Christians and as he tells of his specific call to the pagan nations, he preaches tolerance to all.
Paul talks of the human and the divine nature of Jesus which made him the Messiah and in the risen Lord. Paul has received his mission to all non-Jewish nations, including his call to the Romans.
