# Elijah has already come

The Gospel (Mat. 17:10-13). The disciples, aware that Jesus is the Messiah (16:16), have met him in his glory at the Transfiguration (17:1-7). The messianic era is present. But they had a small problem. The prophets had it that Elijah would return as the precursor of this new era; it was to be a sign of its coming. Why hadn't he come? Jesus sets their doubt at rest by telling them that the prophecy had been fulfilled in the person and mission of John the Baptist, though it was not recognised. For the prophecy was that the mission of Elijah - a purifying, reforming crusade would be repeated. So John the Baptist came to prepare the way of the Lord and perfect his people in anticipation of the coming of the kingdom. He was cruelly put to death for his trouble, and so too, hints Jesus, would himself.
The disciples needed to learn that to follow Christ meant suffering and sacrifice. For he was not to be an all-conquering warrior-king, but the Suffering Servant who would die on a cross. Discipleship still involves that same process of learning and unlearning.
