# Zechariah's prophecy

Gospel (Lk 1:67-79). Like his wife Elizabeth, Zechariah is filled with the Spirit and prophesies about his son. As far as theological format, the opening of Zechariah's Benedictus is similar to the prayers from the Jewish circumcision ceremony. The canticle then diverges to foretell the future role of John the B Baptist as precursor of Jesus, which in tum leads into a third movement about the mission of Jesus. The prayer is addressed to the God of Israel and the entire canticle seems to remain directed to the Jewish community. The reference to God's visitation' is a common biblical expression denoting either God's blessing or punishment on the people. The brief mention of David might lead one to believe the coming messiah will be a military figure but the author of the gospel is quick to present Jesus as a peacemaker in the scene that directly follows. Through Jesus, God will conquer the enemies of Israel by bringing them peace, through the forgiveness of sin. The echo of Malachi's prophecy repeats the theme of reconciliation. This is the peace proclaimed by the angels: 'on earth to those with whom he is pleased!'
