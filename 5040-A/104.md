# The humble of the land

First Reading (Zeph 2:3; 3:12-13)0. The reading is composed of two segments of the writings of a prophet who lived before the exile during the reign of the king Josiah, when Jerusalem was recovering from the excesses of the evil king Manasseh. Consequently his preaching, following in the line of Amos and Isaiah, looks forward to a great Day of the Lord, when retribution will be meted out to the evil of the earth. To him is due the credit of fashioning the phrase that figured in our funeral liturgy until recently, dies israel, which occurs in our reading today. But God's work of justice is not one of total destruction; it also involves a gathering of the little ones of the earth. The first section of this reading exhorts the poor, humble people of the land to grow in their sense of dependence on the Lord, while the second section promises them salvation. The poor and humble will be left in the land as a blessed remnant- another theme that Zephaniah shares with Isaiah. This remnant are faithful to the name of Yahweh in a country where the cult of the Assyrian astral gods had flourished, a cult which mingled with the worship of the Canaanite god Baal and the Ammonite god Milcom. thus the poor and humble of the earth is not a purely social description of the faithful ones; the element of a heroic dependence on Yahweh in the midst of poverty is also present. The material element is an occasion of demonstrating the utter fidelity of the poor of Yahweh.
