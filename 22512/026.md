# The shepherds at Bethlehem

The Gospel (Luke 2 :15-20) continues where the Gospel of the Midnight Mass ended. The angels had departed and the shepherds hasten to see the great event of the birth of their saviour. As Mary had hastened to visit Elizabeth so the shepherds now hasten to see the child. They believed the message and soon found the facts were as the angel had described them. They spread the message and naturally people were astonished when told of a divine manifestation, especially to shepherds, who, because of their nomadic life, were not very observant of religious practices. That there were people in the vicinity indicates that the place of Christ's birth was not far from the village, perhaps in it. In fact it is in keeping with the positioning of the birthplace behind an ordinary guest-room. Mary had to exercise faith all through her life, particularly in the infancy years, but she often thought about the mysterious events concerning her child. Luke is possibly hint-hinting here that Mary is the ultimate source of his material in these first two chapters, but he would have got it indirectly. Joy and praise of God are themes running through Luke's Gospel and the Acts. The source of joy is the fact that salvation has come in Jesus Christ. God had fulfilled his promises and salvation is not for some only but is offered to the whole world.
