# The kindness and love of God

Second Reading (Titus .3 :4-7). In: the incarnation, in Jesus Christ, the love of God for mankind has been revealed. Before Christ came all were in the same state, ignorant, misled, and enslaved by passions. But God freely and without any merit on our part sent his Son to cleanse and renew us (cf. Rom. 3 :21-26; Eph. 2 :1-10). This reading expresses a constant teaching of Paul, that the call to salvation is a gratuitous act of God, independent of our deeds. We are now saved through faith in Jesus Christ, not through works of the Law. Faith, of course, must be expresse4 through works of charity but it is God who saves us. The salvation won by Christ is applied to the individual through baptism which is a rebirth (cf. John 3 :5) and an interior renewal in the holy Spirit. The term 'rebirth' was drawn from the Pythagorean doctrine trine of transmigration of souls. Christian preaching used it to express the new life in Christ of the Christian. For St. Paul, the Holy Spirit is the law of the New Covenant, written on the hearts of the Christians and enabling them to carry out what he urges. The idea of the Spirit being poured over us is a prophetic one (cf. Joel 3 :1); it signifies the abundance of messianic blessings which were to come in the eschatological era. This era has now begun with the coming of Christ. Note the reference to the Three Divine Persons in connection with baptism and the spiritual life. The hope of the Christian is expressed in v.7 (cf. Gal. 4 :7). Jesus is heir by nature; through baptism we become one with Jesus and so heirs along with him.
