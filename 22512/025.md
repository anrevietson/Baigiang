# Your saviour comes

First Reading (Is. 62 :11-12). The three readings of the Dawn Mass put before our minds the effects of Christ's birth on us. In this first reading we have the concluding verses of a hymn on the glorious restoration of Jerusalem. The message is for the daughter of Zion. This was a poetic personification of Jerusalem, built on Mount Zion, and was used especially by the prophets in oracles regarding the restoration of Jerusalem. An exhortation to rejoice generally accompanies this title. The passage, written after the return from the Babylonian exile, speaks of the glorious coming of the Saviour. This coming is presented as a glorious triumphal pro cession, the trophies of victory being carried in procession before the king, as was the practice among rulers of the time. Salvation comes to Jerusalem first, but the whole world is affected. As in several of the readings today there is a note of universality, the proclamation of the message to the ends of the earth. The four symbolic names used refer to the inhabitants of Jerusalem as a whole and to the city. They are a 'holy people', set apart for God (1 Pet. 2 :9 refers this title to the Christian community) They, from all peoples, have been redeemed by God. That people should say of the Israelites that God had abandoned them was one of their greatest humiliations. The last two titles signify that Jerusalem will not be like a divorced woman or an abandoned woman but a wife anxiously sought after and desired.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. .97(96): 1, 6, 11-12) is from a hymn honouring Yahweh's kingship. The upright and just of all nations (note again the universal reference) are protected by God and rejoice; but the wicked remain in darkness.
