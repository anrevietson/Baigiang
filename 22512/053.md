# The Christmas morning

As we gather in the early morning, children are excitedly opening their presents. How delightful it is to be the object of so much generosity! And we think of the meaning of the feast: God's great generosity to all of us, embodied in the Babe of Bethlehem.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
To the one who is born this day to redeem us, we offer our contrite hearts. When we refused you room at the inn, you made room for us in your
heart: Lord, have mercy.
When we cowered in darkness, you came to us as the healing dawn:
Christ, have mercy.
When we are still too fearful to reach out to you, you bend down to embrace us: Lord, have mercy.

INTRODUCTION TO THE READINGS
First Reading (Is 62:11-12). The Lord comes like a nervous young lover hoping to impress. When this is just the lover you hoped for, what joy!
Second Reading (Tit 3:4-7). It's not my nice behaviour that impressed God - he's hooked on me, no matter what I've done. And in his love he wants to share everything he's got, for ever.
Gospel (Lk 2:15-20). To the mother who has been busy with her new-born baby come the shepherds with their wonderful story of heaven opened and angel choirs. We and Mary hear the same good news, together.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: God our Saviour has revealed such kindness and love in Christ that nothing is too much for us to ask. Therefore our prayer is full of confidence.
Reader(s): For the Pope and our bishop, who care for God's people.
For those who bear civil authority for the service of the nation.
For parents who love their children, and for those who are separated from them.
For those who give presents and those who receive them.
For refugees and exiles who long for their homes and for their friends.
For absent members of our families.
For those who have come home for Christmas.
For all of us; by celebrating Christmas may we become more loving, more generous and more sensitive to the deprivations of others.
President: Gentle and loving God, may this Christmas Day, as it comes again, bring us the assurance of your care for us and the confidence to model ourselves on the Christ-Child. Hear and answer our prayers for his sake who with you lives and reigns for ever and ever.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
When the Lord of glory became our brother we were made children of
God. This is what emboldens us to say.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
The Incarnation is the great Sign of Peace, since it unites heaven and
earth with a bond that can never be broken. Let us share the Peace of
Christ with one another.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
How silently, how silently the wondrous gift is given! So God imparts to human hearts the blessings of his heaven. No ear may hear his coming; but in this world of sin, where meek souls will receive him, still the dear Christ enters in.
O holy Child of Bethlehem, descend to us, we pray; cast out our sin, and enter in:
be born in us today. We hear the Christmas angels the great glad tidings tell; O come to us, abide with us, our Lord Emmanuel.
Bishop Philip Brooks, 1835-1893
