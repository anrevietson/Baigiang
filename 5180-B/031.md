# Work for the food that endures

The Gospel (John 6:24-35). Today's Gospel follows almost immediately on that of last Sunday (6:1-15). Excited by the miracle (sign) of the miraculous feeding the people come running for more. But it is only 'more of the same, please'. They have seen the sign but are blind to its significance. There follows a long discourse (in a typically Johannine style - by questions, answers, misunderstandings, objections) in which Jesus attempts to explain the meaning of the miracle of the loaves, and indeed of all that he is doing and saying (6:26-66). The argument revolves around the metaphor of food (bread). And the food in question is the 'food that endures to eternal life' (v.27) which Jesus is offering them. The concept is as yet too dense for his hearers, and they shift their ground - what miracle (work) can he do comparable to that worked by Moses in the desert when he 'gave them bread from heaven to eat?' (v.31). Jesus' reply (vv. 32-33) is compact and complex. Firstly, it was God and not Moses who fed the Israelites in the desert. Secondly, that bread was 'bread from heaven' in only a relative sense - it was 'food that cannot last' (see v.27; also Ex. 16:19-21) - but it did point to a future heavenly bread. And this is Jesus' third point -the true bread is the 'bread of God ... which comes down from heaven and gives life to the world' (v.33). This is the fulfilment foreshadowed by the manna in the desert - and the concept is a shattering one. Awe-struck, the crowd breathes: 'Lord, give us that bread always' (v.34). And the laconic reply of Jesus has the deceptiveness of a slow-burning fuse: 'I am the bread of life' (v. 35). ('I am ...' egs eimi - a phrase redolent of Yahweh's identification of himself to Moses: 'I am who I am', cf. Ex. 3:14; 20:2. Compare John 8:12; 10:7, 11 - this is a typical Johannine usage.) Jesus' description of himself as the bread of life causes consternation in his hearers (6:41 if.) and eventually it becomes the touchstone of belief in him (6:59 if.). It is, of course, a metaphor. But there is more than a mere figure of speech involved, for this is the realm of sacramental reality. The full meaning of Jesus' reply can be found only in the context of the Eucharist, just as the full meaning of the Eucharist must be sought in the full depths of the mystery of Christ - who he is, what he has done and is doing for us.
