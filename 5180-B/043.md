# The people's complaint

First Reading (Ex 16:2-4.12-15). The account in this reading of the people's complaint that they have not enough to eat after their escape from slavery is a complex blending and editing of traditions, a development of an earlier story found in Numbers 11. The manna with which God fed them is still gathered and eaten by natives of the region who consider it a delicacy. Nonetheless, for us to place too much emphasis on what manna is and where it comes from would be to miss the point, just as the people in the Gospel story missed the point about the food Jesus miraculously provided. This reading taken in the context of today's liturgy is a parable. God will send real food to nourish the people; the real food will come in the person of Christ and it will be a food that satisfies not just the body but our hearts and souls as well. Rather, the manna is a promise and a sign of things to come, of a food that will satisfy our deeper longings. But, as the people asked Moses, 'What is this?', so we too often wander through life without noticing and harvesting the food Christ came to give and still provides.
