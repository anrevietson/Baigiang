# The new self

Second Reading (Eph. 4:17, 20-24). In the first three chapters of the letter to the Ephesians, Paul has outlined the 'hidden plan' of God, stressing the unifying nature and role of the Church. Chapter 4:1-16 (see second reading for last Sunday) highlighted the radical effects of this plan of God on the daily life and lifestyle of the members of the Christian community. In today's second reading, Paul seems to backtrack a little, as if he had just remembered that his readers are newcomers to the Church community. For them, there is not only a contrast between what should and should not be done, between right and wrong; they would be even more keenly aware of the contrast between this new way of life and their old ways, pagan ways (see also 2:11 if.). Paul urges them 'not to go on living the aimless kind of life that pagans live' (4:17). 'You must give up your old way of life ...' (4:22). Not just a matter of growing up (cf. 4:14-16) but of taking on new ways. And so Paul brings in one of his favourite ideas - the contrast between the 'old man' (v.22) and the 'new man' (v.24 - translated 'old self' and 'new self' in the Jerusalem Bible). For Paul, the 'old man' signifies Adam and the fallen lot of sinful men, the descendants of Adam, while the 'new man' is, of course, Christ and those who have been incorporated into Christ in his Church (cf. Rom. 5:12 if.; 2 Cor. 5:17; Eph. 2:15; 4:13). Such is the revolutionary nature of Christian salvation - renewal in the Spirit (v. 23), re-creation (v.24). So by means of the 'old' and 'new' contrast Paul manages to stress the definitive and lasting character of Christian salvation, while through the imagery of growth he reminds us that this very salvation is still in the process of being worked out in us.
