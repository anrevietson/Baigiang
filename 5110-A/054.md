# Jesus the shepherd

Gospel (Mt 9:36-10:8). Matthew, in symbolic form, presents Jesus as the shepherd. While John had Jesus call himself the Good Shepherd, Jesus, in Matthew, acts like a shepherd., He has pity at sight of the crowd for as Matthew says, "They were lying prostrate from exhaustion, like sheep without a shepherd".
Jesus uses another image from nature and tells his disciples that the harvest is good and they must be the labourers who gather the harvest. We then are told that Jesus selected twelve to be his closest followers. They are called apostles which means the ones who are sent forth; Matthew has Jesus then send them on mission. They were to be witnesses of Jesus. They are given his own power to heal and to exorcise, thus to continue what Jesus is presented as doing within the Gospel. They were to be shepherds and harvesters. These twelve are selected from the general group of disciples for their special role. The number twelve is certainly symbolic of the twelve tribes of Israel. They thus may constitute and symbolise the New Israel with, in Matthew's mind, the role that Israel was called to live in Exodus. Written to a Jewish Christian community, the Gospel saw the role of the Church in this light.
