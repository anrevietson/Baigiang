# A kingdom of priests

First Reading (Ex. 19 :2-6). This passage represents the promise of the covenant made by Yahweh to the Israelites upon their arrival at Mount Sinai. It was this covenant, modelled upon the well-known vassal treaties of the Ancient Middle-East, that forged the Israelites into God's people. Until this time they were a very loosely-related agglomeration of tribes, claiming descendence from one common ancestor, Abraham, through the Twelve Patriarchs. they became united as a people precisely by their common union with their unique God, Yahweh, who communicated himself to them in the covenant. Being a treaty or cone tract, the covenant implies an agreement by both parties: Yahweh and the Israelites. Yahweh's role is infinitely more than an agreement to terms stipulated by another, since he takes the initiative in 'choosing' the Israelites. But he has chosen them within the structure of an agreement. They will be his 'chosen' people only in so far as they keep their side of the pact, consisting in obedience to the torah.
The promise of the covenant also expresses the role and mission of Yahweh's people. His 'choice' of Israel from among the nations is not exclusive but inclusive. In choosing Israel he does not reject or exclude the other nations, rather he chooses Israel for the other nations. As his promise to Abraham (Gen. 12 :1-3), 50 his promise of the covenant is remarkably universalistic. By the covenant, Israel will be a kingdom of priests, not in the sense that it will be ruled by priests or simply in the sense that all its members will be priestly, but in the sense that it will itself be a mediating nation, communicating Yahweh's salvation to the rest of the world. It will be a pilot nation, whose consecration, being set aside, will be the mission to show forth in its life the universal saving plan of God. In regarding Israel all the other nations of the earth (who also belong to, and are 'chosen' by Yahweh) should be able to see the destiny to which Yahweh is calling them.
