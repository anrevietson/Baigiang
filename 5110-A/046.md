# God's work of salvation

Second Reading (Rom. 5 :6-11). In Romans Paul envisages God' work of salvation in Christ in two stages, as it were: the establishment of right relationship with God; the fulfilment of salvation proper. The first stage has already been realised and is associated with God's 'grace' or 'free gift' . the second stage is yet to come and has as its basis God's love. In Romans 5 :5, having resumed his thought on man's righteousness by God's grace, Paul has stressed that God's love is now the principle motive for our hope in final salvation by pointing our that God's love has been poured into our hearts by the gift of the Holy Spirit. In the present passage he continues to illustrate this point.
Christ's death is a sign of God's love for men. The 'judgement' of God upon mankind, which looms very largely in Paul's mind throughout Romans is about to break, but the man who is justified by faith need have no fear because, by very definition, he is justified by God's grace. He already enjoys the 'favour' of his judge. The same love which justified him will also save him. The basically forensic character of righteousness here comes to the fore. The righteous man is the one who is found or pronounced righteous by God in the judgement of the last days. But, since no man is righteous in 'himself before God, God does not pronounce him righteous because he is righteous; rather he is righteous precisely because God declares him such. God's word is justifying and, 'since it always finds man in himself a sinner 'and unrighteous, necessarily salvific. God's righteousness is a saving righteousness. Hence the justified man knows that, as far as God is concerned, he is already saved in hope.
