# The mission of the Twelve

The Gospel (Mat. 9 :36 -10 :8). This scene depicts the apprentice mission given by Jesus to the 'Twelve' during his Galilean ministry in preparation for the universal mission, given after the resurrection ("Mat. 28:18-20). The 'Twelve' represent the Church, the new people of God built upon the twelve Apostles, just as the old people of Israel was 'descended from the twelve Patriarchs.
The Church is here described as essentially apostolic or missionary. As the people of the new covenant, it exists for others, for the world. Its' mission and raison d'etre to communicate Christ to the' world. It is thus endowed with the authority and power of Christ, to perform the same messianic signs as Christ. Its spoken message is 'the same 'as Christ's: 'The kingdom of heaven is at hand.' This is the Good News.
The Church's role in the world is that of a shepherd or guide. In contemplating the Church all men should be able to see God's plan for them. This role of the Church is doubly rooted in Christ's compassion for the world and the' Church's prayer. It is only by realising that its work is the channelling of God's mercy and by the continuous "opening of itself to this work in prayer that the Church will be able' to carry out its mission.
