# God's compassion

The Scriptures which are read today remind us that God, who in love created us and has compassion upon us, wishes to make is possible al-ways for us to come to him. In this liturgy we respond to God who has already reached out to us in Jesus Christ.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
God's compassion for all calls us to share our love with any in need. Sometimes we can become wrapped up in our own concerns or else we go along with prejudice and division. Let us acknowledge our failures in silence and ask for his help.
You are the loving shepherd of your people. Lord, have mercy. You bring us peace through the power of your cross. Christ, have
mercy.
You are light in our darkness. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Jer 23:1-6). God promises to gather his people and provide a leader who will care for them.
Second Reading (Eph 2:13-18). Jesus preaches peace to those near and far and gathers a new community who are reconciled to God and to each other through him
Gospel (Mk 6:30-34). Jesus wishing to withdraw with his disciples to a quiet place nonetheless has compassion on those who come after him and are like sheep without a shepherd.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware of God's concern for all who are in need, with confidence let us pray.
Reader(s): For the whole Church: that our divisions may be healed, and the world looking upon our unity may believe the good news of Christ.
For the work of the United Nations and all who seek to mediate in situations of conflict: that they may have the patience to persevere.
For those who have been forced to flee their homes, have been injured or are bereaved as a result of fighting, that they may not be destroyed by bitterness as a result of their experiences.
For those among us who experience conflict in our families or in our work places: that we may discover new ways of relating to one another.
President: Lord our God, your concern extends to all people. We trust to your care all those for whom we have prayed this day, through Christ our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Today's Epistle reminds us that through Christ we all have access in one Spirit to the Father. Therefore, in confidence we pray.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In the early Christian Church Jews and Gentiles saw ancient divisions disappear through their common relationship to Christ. Let us express our desire to live in unity now and in the future, as we offer each other the sign of Christ's peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
God, we thank you for drawing us to yourself and uniting us with one another through Christ the Lord. May the gift we receive from him renew us and equip us to serve you in the world.
