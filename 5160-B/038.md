# Christ our peace

Second Reading (Eph 2:13-18). This reading presupposes a situation where Jews ('these who were near at hand') and Gentiles ('you who were far away') were united in the Church, living on an equal footing in the Christian fellowship, and sharing in a common redemption. This situation has been brought about by Christ who is 'the peace' between the two groups who had formerly been hostile to one another. Christ's death had terminated the hostility, broken down the barriers, and united the two groups into 'one single new Man', the Church, the body of the redeemed. Thus it can be said that Christ brought about a double reconciliation; he reconciled Jew and Gentile with one another, and he reconciled the whole of humanity with God. The writer sums up this idea in the Trinitarian statement that rounds off today's reading.
As we contemporary Christians read this passage which speaks so eloquently about the unity achieved by the death of Christ we must feel ashamed of the divisions that exist among those who call themselves Christians. We also become aware of the scandalous nature of fueds and factions which cause hostility and division within our own Church.
