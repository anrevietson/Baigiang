# Good and bad shepherds

First Reading (Jer 23:1-6). This passage contrasts negligent 'shepherds', the unworthy kings and nobles of Judah who brought disaster on their land, with the 'virtuous Branch for David', the ideal future king of the Davidic line, whose wise and just reign would restore the security and the well-being of the nation. The prophet predicts doom and disaster for the inept and careless rulers whose failure to live up to their responsibilities brought about the destruction of the nation and the exile of its people.
Beyond disaster, however, the prophet sees hope. The Lord himself will raise up a Davidic king whose wise rule will give new life to the kingdom and new hope and security to its people. The promised king's symbolic name, 'Yahweh our-Saving-Justice'(New JB), is an indication that under Yahweh the expected king will vindicate the rights of the people, and re-establish justice and well-being in the nation.
The reading as a whole is a reminder of the weighty responsibility that lies on the shoulders of those who hold authority and power, whether ecclesiastical or political, and who must make decisions that affect the lives of a community or of a people. It is small wonder that we Christians are urged to pray 'for king and others in authority so that we may be able to live peaceful and quiet lives' (1 Tim 2:1-2).
