# Christ our peace

Second Reading (Eph. 2:13-18). This reading concerns a very concrete instance of the unifying and reconciling force of the Church (see last Sunday's second reading). In the Jewish viewpoint there was a radical division between those who were of the chosen people (the Jews themselves, of course), and the others - all others, the goyim, the 'nations', the gentiles. Paul stresses the difference, the distance between the two peoples (v. 12) 50 as to highlight the most dramatic, most instantly visible sign of God's salvation - the union of Jew and Gentile to 'create one single new man in (Christ)'
(v. 15). It is hardly necessary to remark that this reconciling of Jew and Gentile was not something that had in fact taken place during Paul's own lifetime (and indeed it cannot be said to have ever happened to any significant extent). What Paul is stating here is a matter of principle - and of hope. The breaking down of the barriers between Jew and Gentile is an on-going affair, of the very essence of the Christian ekklessia. The heritage of the Old Testament belongs to the Church, but the Church goes beyond the narrow bounds of that heritage to gather together all men and so 'reconcile them with God' (v. 16). So, reconciliation between Jew and Gentile is characteristic of the Church. The divide between them is (for both Jews and Gentiles) the most radical of all; if this goes, all other divisions fade into insignificance - 'Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female' (Gal. 3:28; cf. Col. 3:10-11). Paul's point here is that it is the Church which is the focus of unity - unification of all men in the Church (and, a fortiori, unity of life, love and witness within the Church!). Throughout the reading Paul typifies this ecclesial unity in one word - peace (vv. 14, 15, 17). Peace is not a negative thing, a mere absence of conflict. Peace is the dynamic reconciliation of men in one community. Paradoxically, this peace is made possible only through violent conflict concentrated in the person of Christ. We are united 'by the blood of Christ' (v.13), 'through the cross' (v.15; cf. Col. 1:20 '... he made peace by his death on the cross'). And this peace among men is the necessary concomitant of the great peace when man is reconciled with God (vv. 16-18).
