# The Council of Jerusalem

First Reading (Acts 15:1-2.22-29). Last week's reading from Acts reported the success of Paul and Barnabas' first missionary journey in opening the door of faith to the Gentiles. Today's first reading appears to be a set-back for the same Gentile Christians. Some self-appointed representatives from Judea visit Antioch and tell the residents they must be circumcised order to be saved. This incident moved Paul and Barnabas to contact the apostles and elders in Jerusalem. The Jerusalem Church decides to send representatives back to Antioch with a letter containing a resolution:
circumcision is not essential to the Christian covenant. This decision separates Christianity from Judaism, yet the Christian still retain the major Jewish customs: to abstain from meat sacrificed to idols, from blood, from the meat of strangled animals and from illicit sexual union.
The resolution of the Jerusalem Church is proclaimed as 'the decision of the Holy Spirit,' emphasising divine initiative. In establishing its identity, the Christian community does not promote its own power or set up arbitrary agendas. The legitimacy of Church power resides in doing the will of God, which is to save all people who want to be saved. The letter organises its plan of action according to Leviticus 17-19, which suggests Christianity is to retain the essence of the Holiness Code: to be holy as God is holy.
