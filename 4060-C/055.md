# My peace I give you

Gospel (Jn 14:23-29). The gospel recaptures many of the themes of today's first and second readings: union with God, the dwelling place of God, and salvation. Jesus tells his disciples to rejoice that he is going to the Father who had sent him into their midst and to wait for the Spirit whom the Father will also send to them. This Spirit will help the disciples to remember what Jesus has taught. This 'remembering' actually enables the disciples to understand the true significance of the words and deeds of Jesus after the resurrection. The Spirit's teaching is not independent of Jesus' revelation of the Father. The apocalyptic rendering of dwelling place' is the presence and peace of God. Jesus makes this peace immediately available to his disciples. This indwelling presence of the Father and Son will remain with the apostles amid Jesus' apparent separation from his disciples on the night before he dies. The peace that Jesus leaves to us is the very presence of the Father, Son and Spirit. This peace, which is God, can be present in the midst of insults, persecution, and even death. Salvation is living deeply in this peace. Salvation is being in God. Jesus tells these things before they come to pass so that the disciples are able to 'see' the kind of peace that no one can take away from them, and enjoy a union with God stronger than death.
