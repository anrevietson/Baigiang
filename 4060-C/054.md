# The New Jerusalem

Second Reading (Rev 21:10-14.22-23). This week's second reading continues the description of the New Jerusalem begun last Sunday. There is a simple view of apocalyptic literature that regards this genre as consolation texts targeted toward people undergoing persecution or some similar social crisis. However, apocalyptic literature can be ideological and propagandistic, interpreting events as a means of persuading people to adopt certain values or life styles. The imagery of this seventh and final vision of the Book of Revelations focuses on the theme of salvation. The symbols in this passage contrast good and evil. The city of Babylon which comes from the regions of the sea monster and land monster represents the anti-Christ and stands in sharp contrast to the new Jerusalem which comes down from heaven. Babylon, a city of darkness, is called a prostitute whereas Jerusalem which is filled with light is described as the bride of the Lamb.
The images of the twelve tribes of Israel and the twelve apostles suggest that the city symbolises people. The use of the number twelve indicates a respect for tradition and gives the impression of 'chosen-ness.' Earlier mention of 144,000 redeemed people, who are called the 'first fruits' of the Lamb and go wherever the Lamb goes, indicates that birth alone is not the criterion for citizenship in the city. The criterion seems to be the ability to follow the Lamb, even to death.
The radiance of the city is the very presence of God, but the fact that God can be seen 'face to face' indicates this is a transcendent, future state of salvation. There is no visible, earthly temple in the vision of Jerusalem. God almighty and the Lamb are its temple. The physical temple is not of ultimate importance for salvation. The image of the city as the Bride of the Lamb symbolises the close relationship between God and believers. The city has no need of sun or moon, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb. Those who follow the Lamb are filled with the radiance of God.
Those who are chosen, do not pick and choose where they will go. They go wherever the Lamb goes, and when they do so the glory of God goes with them and shines forth from them. Union with God in all things is salvation.
