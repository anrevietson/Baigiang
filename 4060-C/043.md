# The farewell discourse

The Gospel (John 14:23-29). Context and content : The whole farewell discourse is dominated by the thought of the forthcoming departure of Jesus. Around this idea John builds a whole theology
- what is called a 'realized eschatology'. It is a reinterpretation of traditional Jewish eschatology. One sees it in the repeated use of phrases such as 'the hour is come', 'Now the Son of Man is glorified' (elsewhere the Son of Man is a coming figure), 'Now is the judgement of the world', 'on that day...', 'I go and I come again', etc. Elsewhere these ideas and expressions belong to the end judgement of the world ; by using them here and applying them to the death and glorification of Jesus, the 'Son of Man', John implies that this Christ-event is the great judgement of God, and that all other judgement of men depends on their stand vis-à-vis of this. So too the 'parousia' or glorious 'coming' of the Messiah; John sees it not as an end-event (except in 5:28 fi) but as something that is for ever happening in the experience of Christians. 'I will not leave you orphans' (14:18); 'on that day you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you' (20). This raises a question from Jude, 'how is it that you will manifest yourself to us and not to the world?' which is answered by the opening words of today's reading (23); there follows a reference to the role of the Spirit (25 fi), and then the final farewell of Jesus (27 ff.).
