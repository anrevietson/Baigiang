# The farewell discourse

The Gospel (John 14 :23-29) (K.C.). The whole farewell discourse is dominated by the thought of the forthcoming departure of Jesus. Around this idea John builds a whole theology - what is called a 'realised eschatology'. It is a reinterpretation of traditional Jewish eschatology. One sees it in the repeated use of phrases such as 'the hour is come', 'Now the Son of Man is glorified' (elsewhere the Son of Man is a coming figure), 'Now is the judgement of the world', 'on that day ...', 'I go and I come again', etc. Elsewhere these ideas and expressions belong to the end-judgement of the world; by using them here and applying them to the death and glorification of Jesus, the 'Son of Man', John implies that this Christ-event is the great judgement of God, and that all other judgement of men depends on their stand vis-à-vis this. So too the 'parousia' or glorious 'coming' of the Messiah; John sees it not as an end-event (except in 5 :28.) but as something that is for ever happening in the experience of Christians. 'I will not leave you orphans' (14:18); 'on that day you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you' (20). This raises a question from Jude, 'how is it that you will manifest yourself to us and not to the world?' which is answered by the opening words of today's reading.
To love Jesus is to 'keep his word', i.e. to respond in all one's life to the revelation of Jesus. If this be the case, then 'my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him' (on this, see Monday of fifth week). This is a personal coming of the Father and the Son, or better, of the Father in the Son. But not only is there a personal coming; the word of Jesus - his revelation also continues (25 f.). It continues through the work of the Holy Spirit, whose word is the word of Jesus, just as the word of Jesus is that of the Father (therefore Father in Son, and Son in Spirit).
Verse twenty-eight gives the basis for this unshakeable peace and joy and confidence - 'because I go to the Father, for the Father is greater than I'. The confidence of Jesus rests on his total subordination to the Father . our confidence rests on our total subordination to Jesus. God reaches down through the Son, through the Spirit, to lift us up through the Spirit, through the Son, to the immutable God.
