# The Church deals with conflict

First Reading (Acts 15:1-2,22-29). Within fifty years of the ascension, the Church had to deal with a serious crisis. Sometime before 49 A.D. the church at Antioch was disturbed by certain visitors from Jerusalem. They were Jewish converts to Christianity. These self-appointed representatives of the mother church could not come to terms with the fact that baptism had superseded circumcision. They took it upon themselves to coerce Gentile converts into accepting circumcision. Paul and Barnabas objected to such high-handed conduct. They understood that the Jewish law was temporary. It was given by God to prepare for the coming of Christ. Christ had come, Henceforth people would be saved not by circumcision but by baptism. In order to resolve the issue a council was convened in Jerusalem. The disciples and elders were open to the guidance of the Spirit. They listened to one another. A decision was made. Gentile converts were not obliged to receive circumcision. However, they were ordered to abstain from certain practices which would have rendered them ritually unclean in the eyes of the Jews. This was to ensure that Jewish and Gentile Christians could celebrate the Eucharist together.
