# A child is born

First Reading (Is 9:1-7). The prophet Isaiah was writing in a time of war and rumours of war when King Ahaz and his people were trembling at the thought of attack 'as the trees of the forest tremble in the wind' (Is
7:2). Into this situation of disaster Isaiah speaks a word of light and of joy:
a child is to be born who will bring peace, a peace that will reverberate down the centuries. He speaks of the birth of a new king who will have superlative qualities of leadership: wisdom, power, a care for justice and for peace. As none of the successive Davidic kings fulfilled all of these qualities, the prophecies were projected into the expectation of a future messianic leader. The early Christians joyously mined the Book of Isaiah to find out more about their Messiah, Jesus.
Second Reading C[it 2:11-14). Living in the Greco-Roman world in which many new philosophies were being bandied around, the author of the Epistle to Titus proclaims that Christianity is the only true philosophy or way of life. God's saving power present in Jesus was not just for Christians; it was for all human beings and through accepting it they would 'fulfil the deepest aspirations of contemporary culture for civic and familial harmony' (New Jerome Biblical Commentary, p.895). The words, 'we must be self-restrained and live good and religious lives here in this present world', have often been narrowed down in preaching to the level of personal piety, whereas in the Epistle they are a call to live responsibly in relation to those around us in society.
