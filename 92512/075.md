# The grace of God has appeared

Second Reading (Titus 2 :11-14). Titus was left by Paul to organise and govern the community in Crete (Tit. 1:5). To help him, Paul sends him in this epistle some instructions. This epistle and the two epistles to Timothy have been grouped together as the 'Pastoral' epistles.
In the incarnation of the Word and in his redemptive work, the grace of God, the source of salvation, has been manifested (cf. 2 Tim. 1 :9-10). Paul encourages simplicity or single-mindedness; God alone should be our end and all that comes between us and God is to be put aside. Salvation means a participation in the life and happiness of God himself. The hope of attaining this when Christ comes in gl6ry (at the Parousia) is a 'blessed hope'. The universal scope of the work of Christ and of the gospel message is noted. According to the reading generally accepted by the Fathers and according to the rules of Greek grammar, v.13 expresses Paul's belief in the divinity of Jesus Christ. Besides, the glorious parousia everywhere pertains to Christ (cf. 1 Tim. 6 :14-15; 2 Tim. 4 :1), and v.14, which speaks of Christ's sacrifice, at the same time speaks of him as having a people for his Very own:
a characteristic of Yahweh in the Old Testament. God had ransomed Israel from Egypt and made them his own people; Jesus ransomed us and made us his own people. As the Israelites were expected to be holy as God is holy, so we are expected to be good as Jesus was good. Paul, seeing Christians as the people of the New Covenant, uses Old Testament terminology relating to the covenant.
