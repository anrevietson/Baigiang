# The people who walked in darkness

First Reading (Is 9:1-7). Though omitted in the Liturgy reading, chapter 9 opens by recalling the humiliation of the northern kingdoms in the campaign and deportation carried out by Tiglath-Pileser III in 732. Isaiah, who looked forward to a successor to Ahaz in whom the promise of the dynasty would be realized (7; 14-15), here describes him and the deliverance his coming would bring about. Whether this is a hymn of thanksgiving or a coronation song is hard to say. While the advent of the new king is of interest to Judah (v.5, 'to us') the first four verses concern the deliverance of the northern kingdom.
'The yoke', 'the bar', and 'the rod' are symbols of Assyrian oppression while the 'day of Midian' alludes to the holy war (Judg 7:15) when victory came through Yahweh. Verse 4: usually booty was forbidden to be taken in a holy war and was burned. Verse 5: that this is the same oracle as 11:1-9 is unlikely. Many would hold that this verse was about the child Hezekiah (though there are chronological difficulties about that) who did not live up to expectations and Isaiah later projects his hopes (11:1-5) to a remoter future. The sense of the liturgy would favour this future connotation. 'Wonder-counsellor' - the new king will have no need for advisers such as those who lead Ahaz astray. 'Eternal Father' describes the quality of his rule. The result of his rule will be peace, and the virtues of justice and integrity will sustain the Davidic throne.
