# The infancy narrative

Gospel (Lk 2:1 - 14). The apparently insoluble chronological conundrums posed by the infancy narratives of Luke and Matthew should not preoccupy the homilist. The mention of Augustus is significant, because
- as the traditional Martyrology text for Christmas stated - he was regarded as having brought peace to the world. He built the magnificent Ara PacisAugustae in Rome, and in Asia Minor his birthday, September 23, was regarded as New Year's Day, and 'the beginning of good news for the whole world' (Priene Inscription). Luke is concerned to indicate where 'good news for the whole people' really comes from. The Davidic descent of Jesus is emphasized to proclaim him as the one who fulfils the prophecies, who will be a shepherd and a king. The announcement to shepherds shows already at work the upheaval occasioned by this birth, which will overthrow some and elevate others in a manner which will give deep offence (the 'sign of contradiction'): shepherds were riff-raff, the very antithesis of the pious Simeon and Anna who figure later in the story. But they were equally the recipients of the good news, and (as St Ambrose says) it was they who evangelized Mary!
