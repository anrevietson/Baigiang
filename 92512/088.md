# The paradoxes of Christmas

Gospel (Lk 2:1-14). The Christmas story as recounted by Luke contains a host of paradoxes. Although Jesus' actual birth takes place in the most abject of circumstances, those of rejection and homelessness, it is nonetheless set in the vastness of the world of its time, in the complexity of the Roman Empire and its grasping need for a more productive tax-system. Jesus' messianic mission, however, will not and cannot be hindered by human scheming. It is therefore not surprising that it is from among the most unassuming and rejected of occupations that the first witnesses to this extraordinary birth are drawn. Humbly born, Jesus is saviour for the humble. The proclamation of the angels is a marvellous synthesis of the Gospel message in its entirety in one simple phrase:
'Today in the town of David a saviour has been born to you; he is Christ the Lord'.
