# The census and the birth

Gospel . (Luke 2 :1-14). By his reference to the decree of Augustine and the census carried out by Quirinius, Luke - if too vaguely for our taste - has fixed the birth of the Saviour of all men on the calendar of world history. Joseph, of the house of David, went from his home in Nazareth to the birthplace of his ancestor (1 Sam. 16 :1). Although Joseph had taken Mary into his home (cf. Mat.1 :24), and she was therefore his wife in the full legal sense, Luke, by referring to her as 'betrothed', delicately hints that Joseph was not the father of Jesus. No indication is given of the time spent in Bethlehem, nor does the text necessarily mean that Joseph had taken his wife on the long journey just when she was about to give birth to her child. The term 'firstborn son' has in mind (or echoes) the law prescribing the consecration of the firstborn male to God (Ex. 13 :12; 34:19; cf. Luke 2 :23); an only son is 'firstborn' in this technical sense. In Luke 7 :22 we learn that one of the signs given to the Baptist whereby he might know that Jesus was indeed the Messiah was that 'the poor have good news preached to them'. So it was that the first announcement of Jesus' birth was made to simple shepherds: these, the poor and humble, despised by the orthodox as non-observers of the law, are granted and accept the revelation which the leaders of Israel will reject. The angelic mess-age is an announcement of good news and joy to all the people of Israel. 'This day' - the long-awaited day of Israel's salvation -has dawned; a new-born child is the Saviour who has brought salvation. In the angels' song of praise it would seem that 'Glory to God' expresses not so much a wish ('let God be glorified') as a statement, a recognition of the significance of the hour, an acknowledgement of the saving act of God. The sense is that, through the birth of the Messiah, God is glorified in heaven (that is, his power and mercy are manifest) and, on earth, the men whom he loves receive the divine blessings of peace, the peace which the Saviour has brought.
