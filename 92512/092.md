# Midnight Mass

INTRODUCTION TO THE MASS
We have come to celebrate birth. We have come not to remember the past but to rejoice in the present: God is here; God is now: God is with us. Hope is born again in us this night.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Into our darkness you bring your light. Lord, have mercy.
Into our conflicts you bring your peace. Christ, have mercy.
Into our pain you bring your healing. Lord, have mercy.

INTRODUCTION TO READINGS
The first reading from Isaiah is a reading to be proclaimed with great joy and excitement. Choose a reader who has such a gift and no further explanations will be necessary! The note of exultation in the
psalm requires that it be sung.
First Reading (Is 9:1-7). Words of hope, faith and power.
Second Reading (Tit 2:11-14). A sober reminder of the need for a life-style which is God-centred.
Gospel (Lk 2:14). The birth of the Saviour is no fable, but occurs at a precise point in history.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: On this special feast let us bring our prayers in joy to the Lord.
Reader(s): For those who bring peace to people caught in conflict
For those who bring food to people suffering in famine.
For those who bring friendship to the lonely and forgotten.
For those who tend and nurture the earth.
President: God, you are with us in all who through their lives witness to your love. We pray for them and for all of us here through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
We have all been made children of God which makes it possible for us to pray.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Sharing the joy of the angels, we extend peace promised on this night.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
'She laid him in a manger' That child laid in the feeding trough becomes the Bread of Life for all.

The Iectionary permits us to choose our readings according to the pastoral needs of the congregation. It seemed to me that the readings from the Dawn Mass were most suitable for a family congregation. 'Dawn' is, therefore, to be interpreted loosely!
However I would strongly recommend that the Gospel should include the section from the Midnight Mass, in other words tell the whole story, Luke 2:1-20. Perhaps the second reading could be omitted if time is a consideration. Because I am imagining a lively group of children I will make additional suggestions which include the non-verbal.

INTRODUCTION
[This should follow an entrance procession which includes as many of the children present as possible. If they have been given some advance notice they will bring bells or tambourines or drums or whatever local custom suggests as appropriate for a joyful celebration.]
You are most welcome to our celebration! Many of you have been awake since very early this morning feeling excited. Share that excitement with us now and fill the church with your greetings - Happy Christmas!

INVITATION TO SORROW
Even at Christmas time we can have feelings of anger or pain or disappointment which block us and make it difficult for us to be open to God's love. Let us remember those feelings and then let them go, and turn to the Lord and ask for forgiveness.

INTRODUCTION TO READINGS
You might consider taking the psalm from the midnight Mass and highlighting the idea of the whole earth rejoicing - with the trees shouting for joy.
Both the first reading Is 62:11-12 and the Gospel Lk 2:15-20 [or 1-20] demand our attention with phrases such as: 'Look, your saviour comes!' and in the Gospel 'Listen' and 'Let us go'. Hopefully, you have a group of children prepared to mime or tell the story very simply.
The Gospel could be introduced by a child calling out 'Let us go the Bethlehem and see what has happened.'

PRAYER OF THE PMFHFUL
President: Christmas is a time for children - they say - but many children in our world will not experience happiness this Christmas. Let us now pray for them.
Reader(s): For children who are hungry because of war or famine.
For children who are homeless, who are refugees, for whom there is no room.
For children who are caught in difficult family situations.
For children who feel they are not wanted.
For children whose childhood has been taken from them.
For all who care for children.
President: Loving God, you know the vulnerability of a child, so with total trust we make these prayers to you: in Jesus Christ our Lord.

THE PROCESSION OF GIFTS
All opportunities for movement should be used. So make this a procession with song and celebration. [See Concluding Rite below)

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
We pray not just with our lips but with our bodies. So let us raise our hands and pray the Our Father together.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Once again we use our bodies to express what we say as we shake hands or hug one another and exchange the greeting of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The Bread we eat comes from the earth.
It does not belong to us.
It belongs to everyone.
So we break it and share it..
The earth we live in does not belong to us.
It is not ours to destroy.
We belong to it, we are part of it.
The bread we eat tells this story.
and makes us one.

CONCLUDING RITE
The Mass might conclude with a procession to the crib. This should not be solemn but lively. Perhaps you will have asked the children during Advent to make cards or greetings or to bring paintings. [We used to invite them to make a star and then we had a procession of stars to the crib. Alternatively this could take place at the entrance or at the Presentation of the Gifts.) Whatever the children bring it
should be simple and joyful. The instruments will be taken out again and lots of carols sung! Then everyone who has come will leave knowing that it is indeed Christmas!
N.B. Leave plenty of time for this celebration. There is nothing more likely to dampen the spirits than the sight of a hovering parish priest with watch in hand bustling everyone out because it is time for the next Mass!

Mass during the Day
INTRODUCTION TOT HE MASS
'The Word was made flesh" What wonder! What joy! Let us begin by sharing this joy and greeting one another.

INVYFATION TO REPENTANCE
Even on this feast we are caught up in our own shadows and darkness, but we trust in God's mercy and ask for forgiveness and light.

INTRODUCTION TO THE READINGS
It will be important to choose readers very carefully so that these beautiful passages will live, and of course the psalm must be sung.
First Reading (Is 52:7-10). A beautiful poem of joy and hope. Second Reading (Heb 1:1-6). The word has been spoken through the
prophets but now God speaks through the Son.
Gospel (Jn 1:1-18) The gospel reading takes the form of a hymn: the Word has always existed and becomes flesh so that we can enter the story of God. Could people be substituted for men?]

PRAYER OP THE FAITHFUL
President: God's Word is made flesh and our words will be heard.
Reader(s): For those who bring light to people struggling in darkness: we pray.
For those who work for justice among the oppressed: we pray.
For those who risk their lives that others might live: we pray.
For all in this community, that we may be faithful witnesses to the Gospel: we pray.
President: This is the day that light overcomes darkness; in that light of Christ we make our prayers.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
'But to all who did accept him he gave power to become children of God.' Let us now pray together.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In this sign of peace, we share the joy of accepting that from God's full-ness we all receive grace in return for grace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The Word is made flesh today, and Christ is poor
and Christ is black and Christ is old
and Christ is Maori and Christ is Indian and Christ is woman and Christ is child.
The Word is made flesh today.
