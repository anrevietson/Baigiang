# The prince of peace

First Reading (Is. 9 :1-7). All the prophets taught that God would some day intervene in a special way and give liberty and happiness to the oppressed Israelites. The oracle from Isaiah which we have in this first reading expresses this hope. It was spoken about the year 732 BC or shortly afterwards when Galileans were deported to Assyria. It tells of an ideal future king (probably to be identified with Jmmanuel of 7 :14). Some scholars think that it was an oracle as part of the ceremony of accession of a Davidic king, since new accession brought rejoicing and renewed hope. The prophecy was fulfilled above all in the coming of Christ to Galilee (cf. Mat. 4 :12-16). The presence of God brings light and happiness. St John contrasts often good and evil as light and darkness. Christ is the light bringing deliverance. Isaiah compares the liberation from Assyrian captivity to the breaking of a yoke and the lifting of a burden (Is. 10 :25-26; 14 :25). God will free the oppressed regions as he did when Gideon defeated the Midianites (cf. Judg. 7 :15-25). Four titles are given to the child who is to be the ideal king. It seems, from the solemn way in which the birth is described and from the titles, that no contemporary king such as Hezekiab is intended. The title 'Wonder-Counsellor' is used of God only. Nowhere else do we find a king called Mighty God (El-gibbor). The child will combine the qualities of the great kings and patriarchs. Peace will characterise his dominion, a peace that has no end. These verses are a fitting choice for the feast which commemorates the occasion when shepherds were surrounded by the glory of the Lord and the angels sang of peace. Yahweh, the Lord of Hosts, would himself ensure the fulfilment of his promise to the people.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 96(95): 1-3, 11-13) is part of a hymn in honour of the sovereignty of God and his coming as judge of the world. Israel first is exhorted to praise God; then all the world is exhorted to rejoice at the saviour's coming.
