# The admission of the Gentiles

First Reading (Acts 10:25-26, 34-35, 44-48). The story in Acts 10 faces the problem of the admission of Gentiles to what was, up to then, an exclusively Jewish community. The issue was whether pagans could be readmitted into the 'holy' community without being obliged to observe the law of Moses. Peter had been divinely directed to the household of Cornelius, a Gentile centurion; Cornelius, too, had been alerted and greeted Peter warmly. At the start of his address Peter acknowledged that he had learned a salutary lesson: God is no respecter of persons. He offers salvation to Jew and Gentile alike, and his Jesus is Lord of humankind. The amazement of the Jewish Christians who had accompanied Peter was due to their realization that 'the gifts of the Spirit had been poured out even on the pagans.' What Luke makes inescapably clear is that the admission of Gentiles at Caesarea was due entirely to the initiative of God. The significance of it was only gradually made clear to the participants.
