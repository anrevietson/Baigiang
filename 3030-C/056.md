# The burning bush

First Reading (Ex 3:1-8.13-15). The compilers of the Lectionary have omitted some verses of the incident of the burning bush, but the resulting pericope has its own inner coherence. Moses is engaged in his ordinary work of shepherding the flocks of Jetliro when the extraordinary bursts in to his everyday routine. The phenomenon of the burning bush that is not consumed attracts his attention. Moses actively pursues his curiosity and from that the initiative is taken further when God speaks to him. Moses searches and Yahweh reveals himself.
Yet as Moses becomes aware of God's presence his reverence deepens. There is a contrast between the awesomeness of Yahweh and his intimate involvement with his people's slavery. The tension between transcendence and immanence is visible in the narrative. The very name 'Yahweh' is variously interpreted. In the Middle Ages it was seen as God's definition of himself as pure Being. A later awareness of the refinements of Hebrew idiom proposes the interpretation: 'lam the one who causes one to be'. The activity of God is implied. But whatever aspect one lays emphasis on, the mystery of God is made visible and it effects a great change in the shepherd Moses as he becomes the agent for the liberation of his people.
