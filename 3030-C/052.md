# The burning bush

First Reading (Exod. 3:1-8, 13-15). On the occasion of the covenant with Abraham, of which we read last Sunday, God told the patriarch that his children would be enslaved in a foreign country, i.e., Egypt. During their enslavement there God remembered his Covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (Exod. 2:23-25) and by reason of his goodness and fidelity to this covenant intervened to save them. Moses, who was destined by God to be the deliverer, one day slew an Egyptian whom he saw beating a Jew. Because of this Moses had to flee for safety from Egypt to Sinai (probably to the southern part of it), where he worked as a shepherd for Jethro, a priest of the Midianite tribe, whose daughter he married. While there he had a vision of God at the sacred mountain of Sinai (also called Horeb). God appeared in the form of a ball of fire within a bush. A new era was now beginning. God was known to the patriarchs under various names, 'God' (El), 'God Eternal' (El Olam), and 'God Almighty' (El Shaddai). He was the God of the patriarchs, the God of the fathers. The sojourn in Egypt had probably brought many of the Jews to forget this their God and turn to idolatry (cf. Jos. 24:14). At the mount of Horeb God would soon make the covenant with Israel by virtue of which they would be his people and he their God. And as their God, as the God of the people of Israel, he would be known to them by a special name:
Yahweh. This name is now revealed to Moses before the exodus from Egypt. What the precise meaning and origin of the name Yahweh (generally translated 'the Lord' in English) is, remains uncertain. For an Israelite, however, it would connote 'being'; Yahweh meant the God who is and the God who causes to be; the God who is eternally; who was, who is and who will be. Above all, however, it means the God who is ever with his people, guiding it towards its destiny. He it was who was with the patriarchs and protected them; who in Moses' day was with his people in Egypt and who will ever be with them bringing the promises to fulfilment. It is as this God who is and causes things to come into existence that Yahweh sends Moses back to Egypt to lead his people to freedom. It will be his name for all generations, for the New Testament as for the Old. In Christ God will again act as the creator God, bringing a new age into existence. Through him he assumes his kingly power and reigns (cf. Apoc. 1:4, 8; 11:17; 15:5).
