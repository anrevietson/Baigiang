# The one sacrifice of Christ

Second Reading (Heb 10:5-10). At this point in his homily, the author contrasts the ineffectiveness of the sacrificial system of the Levitical priesthood with the effectiveness of Christ's sacrifice. Having established that 'the law is only a shadow of the good things that are coming' (10:1) and that it is 'impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins' (10:4), the author focuses on the 'good things that are coming', the priest hood of Christ. His priesthood is distinctive with reference to the Levitical priesthood. His priestly offering was unique in that he offered to God, not anything external to himself, but his very own life. He came to do the will of the one who sent him, even when this meant death on a cross. His priestly offering was unique in another sense, in that it succeeded where the older priesthood failed, accomplishing a reconciliation between God and his people. The blood of Christ has cleansed 'our consciences from acts that lead to death, so that we may serve the living God' (9:14).
