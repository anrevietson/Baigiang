# The sufferings of the Servant

First Reading (Is. 53 :10-11). It is a short lesson on the sufferings of the Servant of God. There are four main texts on this mysterious Servant figure, all from Deutero-Isaiah (Is. 42 :1-4; 49:1-7; 50:4-11 and 52:13 - 53 :12). It is a difficult area of interpretation. Who is this Servant of God? It was a problem even in the New Testament times (cf. Acts 8 :34).
The question is: are we dealing in these passages with an individual personality Or a collective one? Some parts of the Servant hymns appear to identify the 'Servant' with the whole of Israel (cf. Is. 41:8; 49 :3). Others see him as an individual (cf. Is. 53). It is not necessary to give an exclusive commitment to any one of these ideas. The Servant is not always the collectivity or not always an individual. Both blend together in the idea of an individual representing the majority which is common in the Old Testament. According to the principle of representation, the people can stand for the entire human family, the remnant for the people and eventually Christ for all. The Servant, therefore, is at one and the same time an individual and Israel.
The most important characteristic of the Servant of Yahweh is that of vicarious representation accomplished in suffering. In consequence, Isaiah 53 is a very vivid anticipation of the Passion of Christ.
