# Servant of all

The Gospel (Mark 10 :35-45). In his recorded sayings, Christ has very little to say about the structures of his Church. However, he is most insistent on the way this leadership should not be exercised. There is no place in his community for ambitious, power-hungry men. Those called to office within the Church are not to act as domineering masters. Their office is described as less a series of rights and more a. responsibility and an obligation (cf. 1 Cor. 9 :16-17). Jesus outlines the relationship between leaders and their communities in terms of service. Using this word, he evokes the pattern of his own life as Servant of God - a selfless kenosis unto death. . This is . hammered home by word and action in John 13 :14-15 where the washing of the feet is a sign, a symbolic gesture .of Christ's role in which he associates his disciples. The pattern and nature of Church Teadership is found therefore in the work of Christ. It is a ministry like Christ's which is a service of reconciliation and unity. As Christ died to bring together into unity the scattered children of God (John 11 :51), so the Christian minister struggles at this service of unification. He celebrates the mysteries of communion, the Eucharist and penance; he spends his talents and abilities to make peace, to harmonise and integrate the manifold forms of Christian vocation.
The ransom or redemption idea is found in the last verse of the reading. As used in the secular language of the ancient world it is related to the buying back of a slave or prisoner of war. It involves a price paid to someone for liberty. The idea is not quite the same in the Bible. The exodus is described as a redeeming work of God in Ex. 6 :6-7. However, redemption is used in a very metaphorical sense. There is no question of a payment made to anyone. Redemption has taken the meaning of liberation and the end of the process is a new ownership of the people. They cease to be the property of the Egyptians and become that of God. In Deut. 7 :6-8 redemption is a work of God motivated by his love; the Israelites are liberated 'to be his very own people'. The New Testament follows in this tradition. Christians pass into the possession of Christ (cf. 1 Cor. 7 :23; Gal. 4 :5). They are redeemed to the freedom of Sons of God.
In the Old and New Testaments, there is no trace of the patristic idea of a ransom paid to the devil. The use of the term 'redeem' has the limited meaning of deliverance and liberation to the free companionship of God.
