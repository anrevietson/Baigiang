# The Word of life

Like the Fourth Gospel 1 John begins with the solemn introduction which sums up the book. There is an obvious connection between the two introductions. In both the Word has a prominent place. He was '1n the beginning' (John 1:1) or 'since the beginning' (1 John 1:1); he 'had life in him' (John 1:4) or he 'is life' (1 John 1:1); he was 'with God' (John 1:1) or with the Father (1 John 1:2); he was 'made flesh' (John 1:14) or 'was made visible' (1 John 1:2); certain witnesses had seen his glory (John 1:14) or had heard, seen and touched him (1 John . 1:1, 3). However, 1 John 1:1-4 does not personify the word as clearly as does John 1:1 f. 'The word of life' (1 John 1:1) could be taken to mean the whole revelation of God to man, a revelation which tells of life and is a source of life. This revelation was begun by the prophets but fully proclaimed by Christ who is the Word of God. This Word appeared in human form on earth and became the object of the apostles' sense experience. This direct personal experience is the basis for the writer's strong faith in Christ. He wishes others to share this faith so that they too might experience an intimate union with God which would become the basis of their union among themselves.
