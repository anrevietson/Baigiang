# The centrality of fidelity

Today's celebration insists on the centrality of fidelity. As people who gather to celebrate the Eucharist we are reminded of our sense of commitment to our God and to our community.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must frankly admit that we have not always lived up to our baptismal promises and their call to fidelity. Recognizing these shortcomings, we beseech our God for forgiveness.
You call us through Baptism to a life of dedication and service; we implore your grace to enable us to answer that call daily. Lord, have mercy.
You bid us to conduct ourselves as daughters and sons in your community of faith; we ask your aid that we may live up to our family name. Christ, have mercy.
You sent us forth into the situations of daily life to reveal your presence and concern, and enable us to communicate that presence and concern by wholehearted service to you and to our community. Lord, have mercy.
Generous and loving God, we find consolation in the fact that you never cease to be involved in our problems and needs. Renew in us that spirit of commitment by which we may properly respond to your call and thus become involved in the problems and needs of your entire family.

HEADINGS FOR READING
First Reading (Josh 24:1-2.15-17.18). Joshua gathers the tribes of Israel at Shechem and urges them to renew their covenant with Yahweh. Allegiance to this God automatically excludes worship of all other gods.
Second Reading (Eph 5:21-32). The author lists the obligations of husbands and wives. The key notion in the whole passage is loyalty that is at the heart of submissiveness.
Gospel (Jn 6:60-69). The author presents different reactions to Jesus' words: murmuring, unwillingness to believe, and complete loyalty. He is also reminding his audience of their own experiences of betrayal and lack of perseverance.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware that we are called to lead committed lives, we address these petitions to the Father through the Son.
Reader(s): For all civil and ecclesiastical leaders. May they fulfil their promise of service to their people on a daily basis.
For all believers who have pledged to build up the kingdom of God. May they lead lives of dedication to their community and thus make Christ more truly present in our midst.
For all married people who have assumed the obligation of mutual support and love. May their marriages never cease to remind us of Christ's unflagging love and devotion.
For all whose lives were a constant source of edification and encouragement. May their example continue to influence us both now and forever.
President: Father, in giving us your Son, you indicate that you are ever involved in the needs of your Son's sisters and brothers. In your concern heed our petitions so that, like your Son, we may grow in commitment and dedication to others. We ask this through the same Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we are reminded of our faults and the reality of temptation. We are, therefore, urged to seek our Father's help so that we may lead lives characterized by loyalty and service.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Our sign of peace implies our willingness to work for peace in our community. Fundamentally this gesture evokes loyalty, dedication, and concern. To offer the sign of peace is to re-commit ourselves to these values.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
To share the bread and the cup is to share the needs of the community. Eucharist is that powerful symbol that seeks to energize our sense of commitment. Having celebrated Christ's self-giving, we are called upon to make that presence felt in the anxieties and problems of others. To that degree Eucharist is the sacrament par excellence of undivided loyalty and on-going service.
