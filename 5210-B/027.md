# The domestic virtues

Second Reading (Eph. 5 :21-32). In this passage St Paul begins a discussion of the domestic virtues of three groups of Christians wives and husbands, children and parents, slaves and masters. All are taught to show reverence to one another because of the reverence they owe to Christ. Our model always is Christ in his self-sacrificing love. Paul presents Christ's love for the Church as a model for married love. This is unique in the New Testament. When husband and wife make their lives a sacrifice of love through subjection and love, or mutual self-giving, then their married love will be a visible sign that they are imitating and sharing the invisible action of Christ. Paul makes an analogy between the oriental custom of bathing, anointing and presenting the spotless maiden to her husband and the care with which Christ cleanses and enriches the Church by baptism and the other sacraments so that his spouse will be pure and holy in his sight. The husband must love his wife as he loves his own body.
Genesis 2 :24 is quoted, but Paul hastens to point out that this is no new doctrine. Rather it is a question of realising now the meaning that has lain hidden in Genesis for so long. The text of Genesis, which expresses the sacred nature of marriage, foreshadowed in the bond of husband and wife the union of Christ and his spouse, the Church. The love of husband and wife is a total and exclusive love which unites them and makes them one.
