# Paul as an example

Second Reading (Phil 3:17-4:1). St. Paul has no hesitation in putting himself forward as an example of Christian life and virtue (see 1 Thess 1:6; II Thess 3:7-9; I Cor 4:16). Imitation of Christ gave him a great sense of confidence about his own example. All Christians who adapt his way of behaving (of walking) can influence others in the community. The quality of life in the Church depends intimately on the example of good people who take Christ as their inspiration and model.
Paul is saddened by the conduct of some people who were enemies of the cross of Christ. The community may have been bothered by Judaisers who saw no merit in the cross. Perhaps, the unrest was caused by those who were opposed to the basic spirit of the gospel. Paul makes clear that they are destined to be lost. Their focus is on what is earthly and external.
Christians, on the other hand, already enjoy a citizenship which is destined for full realisation in heaven. It is the glorified Christ who will complete the work of salvation by transforming the weak, earthly, dethrone bodies of people into bodies of glory modelled on his own exalted state. As Christ passed from weakness to glory, so will his followers be glorified and come to live in perfect community of life with God. He will transform his people in virtue of the supreme power he has since his exaltation to the right hand of God.
Paul concludes by again encouraging his readers to remain faithful. He describes the Philippians as his joy and crown. He may be anticipating the Parousia, the Day of the Lord, when his converts will be received into glory as the fruit of his own ministry.
