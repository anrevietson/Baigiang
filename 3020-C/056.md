# God's guarantee to Abraham

First Reading (Gen 15:5-12,17-18). In this reading God gives Abraham a solemn guarantee for the future. Abraham's days for having a family were over. Yet God assures him that his descendants would be as numerous as the stars. Despite the paradox Abraham believed. He took God's providence seriously and trusted himself confidently to God and to his promise. His faith was strong and sustained and the Lord counted this as making him justified. By his continuing surrender of faith Abraham acquired the credentials of the right relationship with God. He responds to grace with faith and so becomes a good person in God's eyes.
God also reveals himself to Abraham in order to assure him and his descendants of a land in which to settle in the future. The promise is ritualised in a ceremony of covenant making. Animals were slain and divided up, leaving an intervening passage. Those who were party to the covenant passed through the divided animals, thus invoking the fate of the slain animals on themselves. They said in effect: 'May we die in the same way if we fail to live up to our obligations'. In this passage, only God places himself under obligation to grant the promised land. Represented by fire he passed between the halves and solemnised the covenant.
