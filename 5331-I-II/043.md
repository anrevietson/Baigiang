# The opening of Revelation

First Reading (Rev. 1:1-4, 2:1-5). The greeting which opens the book already shows its character and how it is to be read. It is in the genre of apocalyptic literature, and so an inspired interpretation of the course of events by the use of symbols drawn from the Old Testament. One of the main purposes of an apocalypse is to encourage the community in difficulties, and all the divergent analyses of this book accept that it was written during a time of persecution.
There follows one of the seven letters to particular Churches in Asia Minor with which the book begins before going on to the properly apocalyptic part; Ephesus, being the capital of the Roman province of Asia, has pride of place among them. We know from the Pauline epistles that there had been a good deal of difficulty for the Christians of this area from heretical teachers who claimed to be teaching true Christianity. Though the author praises their constancy, he also reproaches them with lack of love; is this a loss of charity among the brethren or of zeal for Christianity?
