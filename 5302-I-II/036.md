# Christ's self-sacrificing love

First Reading (Eph 5:21-33). Christ's self-sacrificing life provides a principle of reverence for all our relationships between husbands and wives, parents and children, employer and employee. Because Christ's presence now fills the entire universe, the devotion we show to one another is directed to the Christ in all people and in all events. The writer uses customs from the Jewish wedding ceremony to describe this reverential love. The ceremonial bath of the bride before her marriage is compared to the baptismal bath of the regeneration in which the words of the baptismal formula and profession of faith purify the believer. According to the Jewish custom there was an interval between the betrothal and the presentation of the bride to her husband and a delay between the contract of baptism and the presentation of the bride to Christ at the second coming. The author quotes the passage in Genesis alluding to marriage to illustrate how the bond between husband and wife foreshadowed the union of Christ with his bride the Church.
