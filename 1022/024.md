# The Book of Consolation

First Reading (Is. 40 :1-11). This extract is from a section of the writing known as the Book of Consolation, comprising chapters 40-50 of the Book of Isaiah. It is not regarded as the work of the eighth-century prophet Isaiah, and its historical background is of a time about two centuries later. Jerusalem has been taken, the Exile is a fact of life. The tone of our excerpt is one of comfort and consolation for the exiles in Babylon. Their safe and triumphant ant return is prophesied and it will be accomplished by the power of God himself. It is to be a kind of repetition of the Exodus (v. 3). The arrogant (symbolised by the mountains in v.4) will be laid low. True the people of themselves are helpless (vv. 6-8), but the all-powerful God will not be put off. Hence, they should be encouraged (vv. 9-11). Indeed all mankind will see the glory and majestic mercy of their God.
Our God is a God of power and majesty; but he is also a God of mercy. In verse 11 there is a beautiful portrayal of him in this role. It is a timeless picture and a consoling one - he is the Good Shepherd (cf. Psalm 23) who has compassion for his flock and looks after their every need.
