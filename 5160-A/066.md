# Sighs too deep for words

Second Reading (Rom 8:26-27). Some scholars believe that these verses refer to glossolalia, the gift of speaking in tongues, which Paul discusses at length in 1 Cor 14. But one can understand Paul's words without reference to these extraordinary manifestations of the presence of the Spirit.
Although prayer is the most basic of Christian duties (cf. Mt 7:7-li; 1 Thess 5:17; Eph 6:18) Paul proclaims in our present reading that we weak human beings do not know how to pray as we ought. We who are often guided by our natural inclinations, and whose vision is often limited to things of this world (cf. Rom 8:5), do not know how to address God as we should. But the Spirit who has poured the love of God into our hearts (Rom 5:5), who has raised us to the status of children of God (8:14-15), and who continues to dwell in us (v.9), comes to our assistance in our prayer. The Spirit overcomes our weakness, enlightens our blind spots, and even prays on our behalf, transforming our inadequate groanings into prayers that are pleasing to God.
