# God's mercy

First Reading (Wis 12:13,16-19). Chapter 12 of the Book of Wisdom contains four brief exchanges with God on the ways of providence. Just before the section we have today, there is a text which shows us the honesty of the Bible. It is a challenge to God, because he seems to have hated the Canaanites for their magical rights of idolatry and cannibalism. Yet he had eliminated them only gradually because he gave them an opportunity for repentance. The fourth conversation with God describes how he treated the Egyptians during the times of the exodus. Likewise, we learn the lessons of experience too late in life. Sinners in fact are punished by their own sinful ways. In our present text the writer insists that God's providence is a challenge to upright people in their turn to show loving kindness and patience to others. The passage is a very fine teaching on the noble restraints of power. We have to learn from our God that to be just and to be patient and kind are the virtues of the strong, of the courageous, of the God-like. What we have to be particularly careful of is never to allow power to become blind rage; instead to exercise clemency to be kind to non believers and offer them hope for forgiveness. As one commentator put it. Judaism had gradually arrived at the notion of a God who shows his strength more by leniency than by punishment. They had, through many tribulations, grown to overcome the primitive picture of an angry God who punishes sin without pity. They had learnt to concentrate on the gentleness of God in judgement as a sign of strength and a sign of total control without fear of rivalry or competition.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps 85). This is a supplication for help with a short hymn of praise in the beginning which forms the bulk of our psalm for today. The Psalmist in the midst of his problems reminds himself of the goodness and forgiving nature of God, and that God listens to people. This is a psalm of hope that all nations will come to adore God, to appreciate his marvellous deeds. It is a reminder that God is slow to anger and that God is one who turns and takes pity on those who are sinners.
