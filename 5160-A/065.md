# God's patience

Today's readings remind us of God's universal power and of his justice. but also of his patience and mercy. In this celebration we come to worship God in his majesty and glory, and to entrust ourselves to his generous mercy.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Our Responsorial Psalm proclaims that our God is a God of mercy and compassion. Trusting in those divine qualities we call to mind our sins and ask pardon for them.
You have created all things, and you show your concern for all you creatures. Lord, have mercy.
You judge your people with mildness and leniency. Christ, have mercy.
You are patient with sinners, allowing them time to repent. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Wis 12:13.16-19). The power of our God is unlimited, his justice unquestionable, and his concern for his creatures all-embracing. He is patient and merciful towards those who sin against him.
Second Reading (Rom 8:26-27). When we cannot find suitable words to address God in prayer the Spirit expresses our pleas in words that are pleasing to God.
Gospel (Mt 13:24-43). The Kingdom of God on earth is made up of saints and sinners. On the Day of Judgement, when the Lord will separate those two groups, he will reward the virtuous and punish the sinners.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Our first reading today assures us that the power of our God is unlimited, and that the divine concern reaches to all creatures.
Gathered together to worship this God of power and concern we offer him our prayers with confidence.
Reader(s): We pray for the Church throughout the world. May the Lord confirm committed believers in their loyalty and dedication, and may he lead the lax and the wavering to renewed fervour and to more generous commitment.
We pray for the pope and for the leaders of Christian communities, that by word and example they may guide and support their people in their service of God and in their striving to do his will.
We pray that the Spirit of God may support all believers in their weakness, assist them in their prayer, and give them the strength to bear witness to their faith in all their actions.
We pray for the weak and straying members of the Church, that the God who is patient and forgiving may lead them to true repentance and forgive them all their sins.
We pray for our departed brothers and sisters, that God who is rich in mercy and compassion, may forgive them their failings and reward them for their good deeds.
President: Lord, our God, your concern extends to all your creatures, and your compassion is without limit. Hear those prayers which we offer to you with confidence, through Christ our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Aware of the fact that we are all members of a Church that is composed of saints and sinners, and that all of us fall short of the holiness to which we are called, let us offer each other a sign of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The Kingdom of God on earth is a field where wheat and weeds grow side by side. Saints and sinners, the virtuous and the less committed, the enthusiastic and the lax, form the Mystical Body of Christ, and they are all embraced in the care and concern of God. May those who participate in this Sunday liturgy be strengthened in their commitment, and challenged to respond ever more generously to their call to holiness. May those who neglect their duty to worship be guided by the Spirit who can lead them into a deeper union with Christ and into an appreciation of the fellowship of the believers. May all experience the graciousness of our God who is rich in mercy and compassion.
