# The Spirit intercedes

Second Reading (Rom 8:26-27). Paul has few illusions about the sinfulness and weakness of human nature and yet he is above all a messenger of hope for people who feel frustrated and enslaved by the failures of their lives. He insists that the Spirit stands by to assist us in our weakness. His message here is similar to John's view of the Spirit as the advocate or the defender of God's people and the synoptic view that promises the Spirit's aid to the community in times of persecution. What Paul is saying is that human beings are not left to their own resources, to the inadequacies of their own prayers, to carry their burdens alone.
The Spirit's intercession is authorised and encouraged by God himself who knows us in the very depth of our hearts. For Paul the heart of prayer is to realise that God is Abba, a much more loving father, a much more intimate friend than we could ever have in this world. Yet he knows that we do not know what to ask for, and even if we did, we don't know how to put it into words. He invites us to relax secure in the love and care which the heavenly Father lavishes upon each of us, through the Spirit.
