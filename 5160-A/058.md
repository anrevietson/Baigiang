# God's might and mercy

First Reading (Wis. 12 :13, 16-19). The main idea in this passage is that God's might is the source of his mercy. The power of the creator is the ground of his leniency towards sinful creatures. The context provides examples of that mercy: God's forbearance with the Egyptians (11:15-20) and the Canaanites (12 :2-11). The punishment inflicted by God on these people for their sins and perversions was not an arbitrary display of power. It was meted out in moderation, by stages, with a view to moving the guilty to repentance God's mercy is held out as an example to man me his dealings with his fellow-men. It has taught a lesson - how the virtuous man must be kindly to his fellow-men. And it is intended to give hope to man in his waywardness, the hope that lies in the offer of repentance.
The Responsorial Psalm continues the theme of God's mercy and compassion. It appeals to the long-suffering of God, an attribute which the Bible considers to be typically divine and which St Paul regards as one of the hallmarks of Christian love (1 Cor. 13 :4).
