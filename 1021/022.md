# The paralytic forgiven

The Gospel (Luke 5 :17-26). Jesus' declaration that the sins of the paralytic were forgiven must be correctly understood. Contemporary belief inextricably linked together sin and suffering. It was held that a man suffered because he had sinned. Now the forgiveness of sin was regarded as the prerogative of God; even the Messiah could not do it. Consequently, the Pharisees were outraged at Jesus' statement. But his subsequent healing of the paralytic proved that he was not a blasphemer, for God would not grant the power of healing to such a man. And so his claim to forgive sins was also true. Jesus' action was carried out under the 'Power of the Lord'. The forgiveness of sins where we would expect a physical cure clearly indicates that his struggle was against Satan and the kingdom of evil. Suffering, sorrow, death, and evil are the antitheses of the kingdom of God which Jesus proclaimed. Above all sin is the barrier to the kingdom. For us, it should be a source of great joy that Jesus brought with him to earth the very source of forgiveness itself. Like the people who witnessed the miraculous cure of the paralytic we should be filled with astonishment and awe and full of the praise of God, who does 'strange things'.
