# The return from exile

First Reading (Is. 35 :1-10). This is a prophecy of the return from Exile by the Chosen People. It is a triumphant cry of liberation and restoration. . There is present exultant joy and real confidence, and the imagery of the passage evokes the picture of a second Exodus. God will come to his people in their exile; the desert will be turned into a kind of paradise - the luxuriance of Lebanon, Mt. Carmel and the valley of Sharon along the Mediterranean Sea will be bestowed upon it. The sick and the unfortunate will be healed and prepared for the journey home. A highway will be opened through the desert - a 'holy way' - which will lead the people of God back . to Zion where only the just will live. There is, of course, hyperbole and poetry in the passage, and in fact, the homecorning to . Jerusalem was far . removed from this idealistic picture. But the prophecy and the ideal are none the less real and relevant. If there are no dreams then man will perish. We must keep alive our faith an4 our hope in the future. It is of the essence of a Christian that he looks forward to the future and the perfect fulfilment of Isaiah's prophecy in the heavenly Jerusalem. There is a reason and a meaning for everything under the sun even if it is a little beyond our understanding. We are a pilgrim people on the way to that moment of understanding, when 'joy and gladness' will be ours and 'sorrow and lament will be ended' .
