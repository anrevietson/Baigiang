# Creation groaning

Second Reading (Rom 8:18-23). Paul, despite his enthusiasm and his excitement about the victory of Christ, is very honest about the problems of our world. There are sufferings, there is a lot of futility, corruption, groaning and agony in our world. All this despite our eager awaiting, our hope and even the presence of the Spirit. Paul's key word is hope. Our hope is not just in some remote future but we see signs of it as we look around in our world, as we see beginnings of freedom, as we see intimations of the glory of the children of God.
In a typically Jewish vision Paul sees people as environmentally(s see us, as one with creation. It is not just how we use creation or how we develop it. We are all part of one great unity. Each of us is called to be God's representative in his marvellous creation, which we are only beginning to understand.
Our purpose is to reflect God in our world, and help creation reveal and show its true potential which we are only beginning to grasp. Paul insists that we cannot set ourselves free but that God is, and God will set us free if we but co-operate with him.
