# Examining our lives

As we begin our celebration of thanksgiving to the Lord let us gather our thoughts and examine our lives, our struggle to live the Christian life. On reflection perhaps there is much failure that comes to mind. The Eucharist helps us to put things in perspective and to measure our own lives against the model life of Jesus of Nazareth who showed us how to face problems and above all to experience his encouraging forgiveness.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
The gospel of today is the well-known story of the sower going out to sow. We are warned that people listen but really do not understand the message of the Kingdom. They see it but do not really perceive it. These words could be written for ourselves. We need to ask are our ears dull of hearing and have we shut our eyes for fear they should see, hear and understand and be converted and healed by the Lord.
We reflect for a few moments on the week so quickly gone by. Let us reflect honestly in the presence of an encouraging and forgiving God, seeking mercy for the times we have heard the word of God without proper understanding and let it be carried away from our lives.
We admit also the times we have received the word on rocky soil and though welcoming it with joy, have not let it take root or let it last; when trials and difficulties came our way we have often failed in our promises.
And we acknowledge the times when the worries of the world and the lure of riches have choked the word so that we have achieved nothing of value.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is. 55:10-11). Isaiah has a great confidence in the power of God to work all unto good, and he reminds us that God will carry out his will in our lives. His words are a great source of confidence to all of us despite the many failures of our lives.
Second Reading (Rom 8:18-23). Though there always will be suffering in this world yet it cannot be compared with the glory which is waiting for every one of us. With the whole of creation we should be people looking forward to God revealing the glory of his sons and daughters. We can identify with his picture of people groaning inwardly as we are waiting for that full freedom that comes in the heavenly Kingdom.
Gospel (Mt 13:1-23). The Gospel invites us to use our imagination. Farmers in Palestine sowed before ploughing. Imagine all the various
things that happen, all the difficulties that come up, even though one does one's best to sow seeds properly. The Gospel honestly portrays people as
we know them, who do not really understand, do not perceive, whose hearts have grown coarse, who close their eyes. Yet it is a gospel of hope that there will be a great harvest in their lives.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As we listen to Jesus' great parable we realise the many problems and difficulties that we, and others too, face in responding to his
message, in producing his harvest. And so we call upon the Lord to help our community be a community of God's people producing the harvest of love and justice and peace that he wants in our lives.
Response: Lord, continue to sow your seed in our world.
Reader(s): For the whole world: that the Lord will continue to sow the seed of his Word and give people confidence that one day there will be the great harvest.
For the leaders of our Church, particularly for our pope and our bishop, for our parish priest too: that they may be an example of the struggle to
respond to God's word.
For ourselves: that we will struggle to produce godly fruit in our lives.
For all those who have a sense of failure in their lives, who see the difficulties as they struggle to live the Christian life and sometimes are overwhelmed by them. We pray that they may not lose hope but may trust in the great power of the Lord.
For all the missionaries everywhere, that they may be faithful, to Christ and to his great mission in our world.
For those who have died: that they may experience already the great harvest of the Lord there are some in particular who have died recently whom we would like to commend to the Lord's great harvest.
President: 0 Lord, we thank you for continuing to sow your seeds, your opportunities in our lives. Help us to realise that from those to whom have been given much, more will be expected. Help us to be open, yet to be afraid of temptation, not to let the cares of the world or the lure of riches blind us, or dull our senses. Help us to see you at work in our lives and in those around us. We ask this in the name of your Son, who promised to be with us forever, Jesus Christ, who is our Lord for ever and ever.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In the gospel the Lord reminds us that the mysteries of heaven are revealed to us and that they are not as clear to others. Yet for us the Kingdom is not just a gift, but also a task to be fulfilled in our lives. And so let us pray the prayer of Jesus for the coming of the Kingdom that we will play our part and that he can work through our lives and that we will do his will on earth.
Thus we can pray with confidence, the words that Jesus taught us.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
There are many signs of peace in our world today. Yet there are many counter-signs. The gospel invites us to play our part, to take the opportunities that God has given us. Now he gives us an opportunity to turn to each other and in all sincerity to wish each other the peace which is the gift of a very generous God.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Lord we have so much to thank you for in our lives. We thank you for our Church here, for the peace that we experience here as we sit and reflect together and reach out to each other.
We thank you for the different gifts that you give to each of us, the different opportunities that you make available to us. Help us to be supportive of one another, especially in our failures and in our difficulties. Help us to play our part in witnessing to and in helping those less fortunate than ourselves to realise the wonderful gifts of your Kingdom which you make available to each of us each day of our lives.
Give us strength to sow the seeds of love, and justice and peace in a world which so badly needs to grow in understanding the mysteries of your Kingdom.
