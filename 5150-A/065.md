# The hope of creation

Second Reading (Rom. 8 :18-23). Today's reading from Romans is about hope. Life in the Spirit is a life lived in hope. The Spirit gives life; it is he too who gives hope.
Hope is the basis for Christian consolation and encouragement in suffering. 'We even exult in our afflictions,' Paul says,5 'because we know that affliction produces endurance and endurance tried character, and tried character hope' (Rom. 5 :3-4). 'Endurance" is a favourite word of St Paul's. It suggests the ability to look beyond the present suffering and see its meaning in the future. The Christian, therefore, is not only enabled to put up with suffering; he is able to see through it and find its meaning. And this is hope. ' Christian hope is sure, because its ultimate basis is God's love for man. 'Our hope is not one that can put us to shame, for God's love has already been proved out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us' (Rom. 5 :5).
Hope has a cosmic dimension. If the universe shared man's bondage to evil powers and was subject to 'futility' and 'thraldom to decay, it now shares man's hopes. It too is waiting for something. The world and man are both locked in the same struggle, waiting with eager longing, with the groans of a woman in labour ' for the revelation of the glorious freedom of the children of God. Creation is on man's side; it was first designed in view of man. Its groaning will cease when man's cry for justice and freedom has been heeded, when all will be free with the freedom of children of God.
