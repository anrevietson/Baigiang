# It was reckoned to him

First Reading (Rom. 4 :20-25). Paul, in typical rabbinical style, fastens on the phrase 'it was reckoned to him' in order to emphasize that the Christian's righteousness, typified by Abraham's, is not earned or deserved but is a pure gift or 'grace' of God. For Paul a man is not declared righteous by God because he is righteous, rather he is righteous because he is declared so by God. This does not imply that the righteousness in question is merely imputed and extrinsic. For God's word is creative. If God says that a man is righteous then3 by that very fact, he is Or becomes righteous.
The Christian's faith is not just a general trust and confidence in God, however profound. It has as its definite 'content' the death and resurrection of Christ, the essence of the Kerygma. But it is not Simply a faith in af act, it is faith in a person who raised Christ from the dead. Faith is itself already a share in Christ's passion, death and resurrection. It is a death to self and a reliance upon God who alone can bring the human self to fulfilment. Christ's death and resurrection are indeed facts and events, but they are also, as Paul has intimated in 1:3-4, the pattern of all authentic human existence.
