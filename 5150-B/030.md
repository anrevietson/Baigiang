# The call of Amos

First Reading (Amos 7:12-15). Amos is the earliest of the classic prophets whose teachings have come down to us in written form. We know very little about him, but much of his ministry as a prophet seems to have centered at Bethel, the well-known cultic centre near the then Israel-Judah border. He seems to have been active during the reigns of Uzziah, King of Israel, and Jeroboam, king of Judah, between 786 and 742 B.C. This seems to have been a time of great prosperity in Israel accompanied by falling social and religious standards characterised by widespread corruption. This forms the background for understanding the prophet's favourite themes, namely, a very pessimistic judgement of history; a call for social justice and a stance against the oppression of the disadvantaged; a call for genuine religion and a stance against hypocritical show; and finally a call for a change by taking God's word seriously in a genuine living of the covenant. The text before us today is the heart of a slightly longer biographical passage. According to this biographical note, Amos was not a professional prophet nor a member of a prophetic guild. Instead, he was a man involved in the livestock and fruit trade who was called by God to speak out on the key covenant issues of the time. In the text we are given an account of a row between Amos and Amaziah, the priest of Bethel who is contemptuous of Amos and his ministry. In the course of answering Amaziah, Amos tells the story of his call to be a prophet and his determination to be true to it before prophesying a bad end for both Amaziah and Israel. Amos teaches commitment to vocational demands in the face of opposition.
