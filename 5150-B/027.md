# Amos and the priest of Bethel

First Reading (Amos 7:12-15). In the context of the book of Amos this is a biographical insertion which breaks the sequence of visions in chapters seven to nine, visions dramatically suggesting the punishment in store for Israel and the royal house of Jeroboam. The passage underlines a fundamental aspect of Amos' life as a prophet. Amos, a native of Tekoa in Judah, is warned off by the local priest from prophesying at Bethel (the national shrine of the kingdom of Israel, vv. 12-13). The gist of Amos' reply is that he has no choice in the matter. To Amaziah's gibing suggestion that he earn his bread at home Amos answers that he is not one of the professional prophets, the so-called 'sons of the prophets'. These appear to have been groups of (court? cultic?) prophets who earned their livelihood by 'prophesying', sometimes but not always with a bias in favour of the source of their income (cf. 2 Kgs. 2:3; 1 Kgs. 20:35 ff., 22:6 ff.). Amos insists he is not one of these. In fact, left to himself, he wouldn't have been a prophet at all - the choice was not his but God's. And so he finds himself, a shepherd and agricultural labourer, away in a foreign country proclaiming God's judgement upon it and its people. And this is the kernel of the matter - Amos has been sent to prophesy: he is at the service of the one who sent him. 'The Lord Yahweh speaks: who can refuse to prophesy?' (Amos 3:8). This condition of being at the service of God is typical of the prophet. He is a carrier of another's message, not his own. The constant refrain is: 'Thus says the Lord
'The word of the Lord came to me saying...'. Never does the prophet say: 'Listen, Israel, to my message' (contrast with Christ's 'But I say to you . .' !). The prophet is one who is sent, and his own feelings or aptitude for the task don't seem to count. Though he considers himself 'a man of unclean lips' Isaiah is willing to be sent (cf. Is. 6:4-9); Jeremiah, on the other hand, is called, is reluctant to go, and complains that God has 'seduced' him, 'overpowered' him (Jer. 1:6 ff., 20:7-9) 50 that he must proclaim his word. Either way, the prophet is sent. This is the imperative of his mission. And this is Amos' self-justification to Amaziah - this is his mission; he cannot do otherwise.
