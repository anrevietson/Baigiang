# How salvation was achieved

Second Reading (Heb 9:24-28). This text offers a classic interpretation of the process by which salvation from sin is achieved: it was through the vicarious sacrificial act of Jesus, who is both High Priest and Victim, and who carried our sins upon himself. Our author stresses the once-for-all and heavenly quality of this sacrifice, contrasting it with the repeated ritual earthly sacrifices of Judaism. The essential difference is that the Jewish priests made their offerings in an earthly sanctuary, while the one, perfect sacrifice was presented in the heavenly sanctuary, which Jesus has entered through his death on the cross. Christians who had been raised in Jewish piety, but who now found themselves excluded from any contact with their long-cherished Jerusalem temple, would have found this a great consolation. Their gain far outweighed their loss. Jesus, the supreme Mediator, is a far more effective remedy for sin than countless ritual Days of Atonement.
