# The superior sacrifice of Christ

Second Reading (Heb. 9 :24-28). The central theme of this passage is the superiority of the sacrifice of Jesus to that of the Old Testament sacrifice. The sanctuary that he enters is not the man-made construction of the earthly temple but is heaven itself. Because Jesus is spotless he can freely enter into the divine presence, and he has no need to offer sacrifice on his own behalf, but Only on behalf of us. When the high priest entered the sanctuary of the temple on the Day of Atonement every year, with blood which was not his own, he made his sacrifice to God, and emerged from the sanctuary with the certainty that the ceremony would have to be repeated again and again as the years passed. Not so with the sacrifice of Jesus: he entered the holy place of heaven once and for all, and his sacrifice had an enduring efficacy. If his sacrifice did not have this perjuring character, Jesus would have had to undergo the death of the cross many times 'since the foundation of the world'. Like the rest of mankind, Jesus died but once, but this unique act transcends the limits of time in its efficacy. The phrase 'the end of the age' is best understood as the time of fulfilment which was inaugurated with the coming of Jesus. The purpose of Jesus' coming is stated to be 'to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself' (v.26). The language of v.28 recalls that of the Fourth Servant Song of Isaiah 53 :12, 10 : the Greek verb, anapherein, means both 'to take away' and 'to bear'. The reference to the second coming of Christ is probably suggested by the imagery of the Day of Atonement ceremony: the Israelites, who were on the outside while the high priest was in the sanctuary, waited anxiously for his reappearance which they welcomed as a sign that his sacrifice had been acceptable to God (Sirach 50 :5-10). The author of the epistle pictures the reappearance of Jesus as the sign that those 'who are eagerly waiting for him' have been cleansed from their sin, and that their salvation is now at hand.
