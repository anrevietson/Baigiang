# Solomon's prayer

The first reading (1 Kings 8:41-43) forms part of Solomon's prayer at the dedication of the temple he had built. But in the course of its transmission to us this prayer, as many important theological passages in the Old Testament, underwent a process of development. These passages were considered to be God's word to contemporary man, not only at the time they were written but always ; and so the editors of the period re-read there in the light of contemporary thought about God; thus they became a vehicle of developing revelation. These particular verses seem to have been added to the prayer after the Babylonian exile, at a time when Israel was becoming more and more conscious that her religious heritage and her messianic destiny were intended not for herself alone but for all nations. In one way this can now be seen to have been one of the chief purposes of the destruction of Jerusalem and the exile, for they transformed the chosen people from being a petty amalgam of tribes turned in upon itself, into being a race conscious of the part it had to play in the community of nations . Israel was brought to a realisation that she had a mission, namely to bring salvation - which was, to be sure, directed in the first place to herself - also to other nations. Men were to be persuaded by the amazing protection shown to Israel in the course of her history, by the miraculous presentation and cherishing of Israel by God's 'name', his 'mighty hand and outstretched arm', that Yahweh was the true God.
