# Zechariah and Elizabeth

Gospel (Lk. 1:5-25). The passage opens with a testimony concerning Zechariah and Elizabth's priestly origins and their faithful observance of the law. They depict the best of Israel, the remnant awaiting news of the Messiah. Zechariah goes to the Temple to bum incense but is interrupted by an angel who foretells the birth of a son. The angel strikes Zechariah dumb so he cannot announce the news yet. Zechariah is not being punished for wondering how God would use a humble person like him, for Mary asks Gabriel the very same question. Instead, Gabriel is silencing Zechariah, the same way the prophet Daniel was silenced before his proclamation about the Son of Man. The state of muteness allows the character of Zechariah to repeat a message important enough to be proclaimed more than once. The child's name 'John' is a proclamation that 'Yahweh has shown favour' and the circumstances of John's birth from Elizabeth show that God is faithful to the covenant even in the most unlikely situations. Zechariah's inability to speak did not allow him to finish the Temple liturgy. The worship service was suspended for a reconstitution of the Israelite assembly, including a shift in the way God's promise of salvation will ultimately be fulfilled.
