# God, the redeemer of Israel

First Reading (Is. 41:13-20). God is portrayed as the redeemer of Israel in this reading. This has the technical meaning of the Hebrew word goel, and involves the twin ideas of close kinship with a resultant right and obligation to help. To reinforce this idea Israelis described in terms of endearment 'poor worm and 'puny mite'. The great event that revealed God as the go'el of Israel was the Exodus from Egypt. Now the deliverance of his people from Babylon and exile will reveal him to be still so. In fact more so for this new exodus is in many ways more marvellous than the first (see vv. 17ff.). For God will not only save his people in this new liberation; he will indeed transform them and their world. Such signs of God's favour could not leave his people indifferent to him. The same is true for us, for his work is ultimately for the whole world that all men may see that he and he alone is God.
