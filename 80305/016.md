# James the Less

In contrast to Philip, who is mentioned several times playing an active part in the Gospel, James the son of Alphaeus (not of Zebedee, John's brother) hardly features at all. Still he is mentioned by St. Paul as having been privileged with a special appearance of Jesus to him after the resurrection (1 Cor 15:18) that is, if it is the same James in question. Maybe this shows a special affection of Jesus for this quiet, unassuming man working away in the background. At any rate, James must have had a special attachment to his Master. Philip, for his part, appears in the Gospel as someone who brings other people to Jesus, as he brought Nathaniel (Jn 1:45) and some Greeks (Jn 12:21). Jesus' question to Philip about the feeding of the crowd that came to hear him (Jn 6:5) suggests that he recognised the concern of Philip for other people. His answer to Philip's question in Jn 14:6-14, however, sums up the heart of our Christian faith: God is in Jesus and Jesus is in God. Through him we therefore have great power to continue his mission of healing in this world.
