# No prophet is accepted in his own country

Gospel (Lk 4:24-30). Jesus reminds his own people, the Jews, of the message inherent in the first lesson read today. He is sent by the Father to heal all peoples, pagans as well as Jews.
We are all very proud of the country we come from. We are all proud of the town we live in. And rightly so. When we hear that they have better things elsewhere we are reluctant to believe it unless we are desperate. Naaman, was an inhabitant of Aram, and a general in the army but he was suffering from a disease that was despised as much as AIDS is today - Leprosy. He left home with all the presents he had so that he could buy his cure. He expected to suffer or to be asked to do something very difficult but all he was asked to do was to bathe in the river seven times. He was indignant since he had rivers far better than the Jordan at home. He was persuaded to do it and was cured.
Jesus was not well received when he spoke to his own people. They intended throwing him over a cliff. We must listen to the message of Jesus and realise that it is not in foreign places or in foreign people that we will find the answers to life's mysteries but rather amongst the ordinary people we meet every day.
