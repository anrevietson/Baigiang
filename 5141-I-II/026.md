# Jacob's dream at Bethel

First Reading (Gen. 28:10-22). God shows his approval of Jacob's decision to seek a wife for himself among his' own people. (Esau is reproved by implication.) Jacob's vision Is used as propaganda for the Bethel sanctuary. Men are seen as they are, warts and all, in Genesis. Lionel Johnson celebrates this vision in his poem on Charing Cross. It shows contact between heaven and earth under the image of a ladder suggested by the staged towers of Babylonia. Some Fathers understood it as a symbol of God's provident' care continuously exercised through angels. Others see it as a type of the incarnation, God's bridge thrown down from heaven; Jesus 'refers to it (John 1:51; cf. John 14:6). Jacob prays that God may be with him as we do at Mass asking protection and blessing.
