# Ostentatious officials and the poor widow

On the one side are a group of ostentatious officials, experts in the Mosaic law, but all too fond of parading their own importance and their formal works of piety. Their concerns are egotistical rather than truly religious. (It is sobering to wonder whether any of this indictment could apply to those of us who belong to the Christian ordained ministry, as well as to the Scribes.) Jesus makes the scathing comment that they 'devour widow's houses'. As lawyers, they might serve as trustees for the property of the deceased, and cynically exploit such situations to their own advantage.
In stark contrast is the widow's unobtrusive but reckless gesture of throwing in her last coins as an offering for the upkeep of the temple. Jesus notes her gesture and underlines its value. Such dedication and generosity are exactly the qualities most pleasing to God, and which indeed will lead Jesus himself to his sacrificial death on the cross. The small monetary value for her coppers (explained by Mark as equal to a quadrans - a Roman coin, familiar to Mark's Roman readers) does not matter. Paradoxically, she put in more than anybody else, since her giving was at real cost to herself.
