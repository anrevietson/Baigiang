# Divisions at Corinth

Second Reading (1 Cor 1:10-13.17). Paul is facing a potentially dangerous situation in Corinth with various groups within the Church. Perhaps these groups were different house churches that had evolved varying theologies and were in tension with each other rather than in outright heretical division. It seems that the differences have not reached that stage as yet. The exhortation to be united again is couched in terms of putting things back in their proper place once more. What might have been the areas of difference? Apollos was an Alexandrian convert and would have been familiar with the philosophy of the great Egyptian city. Possibly his group was interested in the rhetoric rejected by Paul so fiercely. Cephas was the obvious head of a mission to the Jewish people and possibly his partisans were of the opinion that Christians should obey the ritual laws of the Torah, a tendency we know to have been very widespread in Paul's world. The phrase 'I am for Christ' may be best interpreted as Paul's own stance or that of a scribe; (it does not occur in the listing of groups at Corinth given by Clement in his letter).
Paul's answer is grounded in three of the great fundamentals of his gospel, the centrality of Christ, the power of the cross and the sacrament of Baptism. Christ has not been divided, neither the cosmic risen Christ nor the Body which is Christ. The cross has a power that cannot be trammelled with the shackles of philosophy or oratory. Baptism is the sacrament that immerses the Christian in the paschal mystery of the one Christ. How can there be tension much less division?
