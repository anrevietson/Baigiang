# Jesus begins his ministry in Galilee

The Gospel (Mat. 4 :12-23). Today we begin the semi-consecutive reading of the First Gospel which will continue for the remaining Sundays of the year. In the passage read today Matthew begins the narrative proper of Christ's public life, which commences only after the public ministry of John, the Precursor, had ended through imprisonment. Before this Jesus had been in Judea. For Matthew, Jesus' withdrawal from Judea for Galilee was no mere accident. He sees in it the fulfilment of the prophecy we have read in the first lesson today. This same prophecy is also seen by Matthew as a fitting introduction to the entire ministry of Jesus. He is the light and joy of the world, both of Jew and Gentile. It is a light that shows up sin and calls on man to turn from it to a life in accord with God's law.
Jesus opens his nulustry with the same words as the Baptist did (Mat. 3 :2) 'Repent; for the kingdom of heaven is close at hand' (or 'upon you'). The kingdom of heaven will be the subject matter of Christ's preaching. The expression is one rich in meaning. It signifies God's rule on earth, his reign in men's hearts. It means the fulfilment of the promises, the fullness of blessings; in short the new order of things to come, an order already present in part in the person of Jesus. The kingdom is not of the material order of things, it is something to be received in faith. Hence repentance is required to come into possession of it; repentance in the true biblical sense of the word - turning aside from a life of sin to a life in accord with God's law, from a self-centred existence to one of obedience to God's call
Jesus came to preach the kingdom and to inaugurate it. But since his work would continue after he had visibly passed from this world he also chose apostles who would continue to proclaim the good news of the kingdom and show it a present reality through their ministry of healing.
We may well be surprised by the abrupt nature of the call of the Apostles and the alacrity of their response as these are narrated by Matthew (and also by Mark and Luke). Matthew's account gives us a theological schematization. In actual fact things did not happen so abruptly. We know from John's Gospel (1:40-42) that both Andrew and his brother Simon (Peter) had already made the acquaintance of Jesus in Judea while the Baptist was still preaching repentance to the people. Matthew's account of the events is interested in the theological essence of the Apostles' vocation and in the significance of their call for the Church. In the interests of this many details are omitted. The call of the Apostles and of the first disciples became the model of all discipleship in the Church.
It is Jesus, not the disciple, who takes the initiative in this call. The essential words are: 'Come, follow me.' To follow Jesus means to become his disciple. And this meant abandoning one's former way of life. The Gospels note the willingness of the response. The Apostles were called to he with Jesus as companions, to be later sent out to proclaim the Gospel (cf. Mark 3 :14). The visible call of these first Apostles and disciples by Jesus was but the beginning of a continuing process. The proclamation of the Gospel was to continue; even in Jesus' own life the harvest was great and the labourers few. He himself exhorted his followers to pray the Lord of the harvest to send labourers to continue the work. The risen saviour continued to call men and women to abandon their earlier pursuits and follow him in order to proclaim the mystery of the kingdom 'of heaven. And in exhorting men and women to follow Jesus by giving themselves to the priesthood, to the religious life and missionary apostolate, the Church has put the call of the first Apostles before them. In so doing she has been faithful to the original intent of the early tradition and the evangelists who have so schematised the narrative as to have the living Christ speak to men of all generations through their words.
