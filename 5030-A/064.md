# The ministry in Galilee

Gospel (Mt 4:12-23). The section comes after the description of the ministry of John the Baptist and his baptism of Jesus to fulfil all righteousness, a description followed by the account of the temptations. The pericope is composed of three parts -12-17, the preaching of Jesus as the fulfilment of the scriptures; 18-22, the call of the disciples; 23 a summary of the ministry Of Jesus. The first and the third parts are strongly Mattheaen in their theology and composition, having no parallel in Mark. In fact Matthew uses a similar summary at 9.35 to bracket off chapter 5 to 9, the Sermon on the Mount and the chapters of the miracles, showing Jesus in action preaching, teaching and healing. Thus the ending of this pericope opens out the way for what will follow. The first part of the pericope interprets Jesus' ministry as the light of the people of Zebulun and Naphtali. There are various echoes of the Isaiah text in the description of Jesus moving to Capernaum. It is by the sea, as mentioned in Isaiah, although the sea Isaiah meant was the Mediterranean; Capernaum is in Galilee and in the Old Testament territory of Naphtali. Matthew has stretched the attitudes of geography to take his fundamental theological point that Jesus is the fulfilment of ~ Old Testament plan of God, witnessed in the scriptures.
The culmination of the plan is that the kingdom is at hand, a symbol that shows that God is offering himself in a definitively new way and that people must change their hearts and repent to accept this offer of God in Jesus Messiah, the Son of God. The offer of God is made explicit in the description of the summary in verse 23. Jesus summons men to work with him in his ministry in calling the disciples. They will be given a definite charge in Chapter 10 - to share in Jesus' work of preaching and healing. After Jesus has demonstrated his own power he will communicate it to the disciples. Thus this pericope is very fundamental for the later development of the story and theology of Matthew's gospel.
