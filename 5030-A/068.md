# The people in darkness have seen a great light

First Reading (Is. 8 :23 - 9 :3). This reading is the opening section of a longer prophecy on the birth of a son and heir to the throne of David. (The entire prophecy is read in the first Mass for Christmas Day.). We shall understand the prophecy better by situating it in what would appear to be its original historical context. Northern Israel (or the area later known as Galilee) bordered on pagan territory and was exposed to pagan influence. It was probably partly pagan even when it was part of the kingdom of Israel. The region was given as territory to the tribes of Zabulun and Naphtali and was thus part of the sacred heritage of Israel. Some of the 'mighty deeds of the Lord' in favour of his people were worked there. Not very far to the South, Gideon was raised up by God as a redeemer for Israel in the days of the Judges and his defeat of the invading and marauding Midianites is recounted at length (chapters six to eight) in the Book of Judges. In 'the days of Midian' God showed his favour towards his people. Matters looked different when towards 732 BC the Assyrian armies overran the area, annexed it to the pagan Assyrian Empire and deported its population. The darkness of paganism seems to have returned and the area could be described as a 'province of the nations' or 'pagan'. (The Hebrew word galil, rendered 'province', can also be taken as a proper name 'Galilee'. This is how the Greek translation, used in Matthew 4 :15, understands it.)
It was in such circumstances that Isaias made his prophecy of a glorious future for these regions. In prophetic vision he sees their liberation already an accomplished fact. Salvation and the light of divine revelation will again dawn for the oppressed and humiliated people and bring with them the joy and jubilation, like that known to the ancients in the division of spoil after battle or at the harvest festivals after the crops had been' saved. The oppressor ' s yoke of bondage will be broken by Israel's redeemer, as it was by Gideon in the days of the Judges. The prophecy (in a passage not included in today's reading) goes on to say that the promised redeemer will be the son of David. This magnificent prophecy On the light to shine on 'Galilee of the Gentiles' was fulfilled by Christ, as we shall see in today's Gospel reading.
In the Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 26) Israel, the Church and each one of us, express faith, hope and confidence in God, our light and our help.
