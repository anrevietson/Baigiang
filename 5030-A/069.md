# Divisions at Corinth

Second Reading (1 Cor. 1:10-13, 17). As was remarked last Sunday, Corinth had a mixed, cosmopolitan population with no great sense of unity. It was a faction loving city. It did not take very long for these human traits to manifest themselves within the early Christian community there. Some time after Paul had preached Christ there and founded the Church, Apollos, a convert Jew renowned for his learning and eloquence, came to the city (Acts 18 :24-28). It is possible that Peter ('Cephas') also visited Corinth; in any event the early converts would have known of him and of the special position he enjoyed among the Apostles. Some trouble makers may have been extolling Peter's position to the detriment of that of Paul himself. The Christians of Corinth, still very young in the faith, got confused and banded themselves into groups around the persons of Paul, Apollos, Cephas and Christ. There was no question of heresy or schism; yet, the unity of the Church was in danger and the factions showed up a serious misunderstanding of the faith and of the nature of the Church. News of the sad state of affairs was brought to Paul in Ephesus by men connected by a certain Chloe 2 they may have been some kind of commercial travellers for the firm or business of Chloe.
The personality cult, centred about Paul, Apollos, and Peter, had put Christ in the last place, whereas he should be first and central in their thoughts and Christian lives. The Corinthians were considering the Church in too human a fashion with the result that preachers and theologians were Occupying the place of Christ. Those faithful to the founder of the Church at Corinth favoured Paul; Christians impressed by learning and eloquence would opt for Apollos, while those standing for the primacy or some other privilege of Peter would group themselves around the name of Cephas. (Not that Apollos or Peter were in any way responsible for or favoured such factions. Paul points out their error.) The Church is Christ's Church; it is Christ alone who has redeemed man and it is in the name of Christ and into Christ all are baptised. All others, apostles, doctors, theologians, etc., are but servants of Christ; their mission is to lead men to him. The error of the Corinthians and Paul's solution for it have a very timely lesson for us today. When Christ ceases to enjoy a genuinely central role in the Church, factions and divisions begin. The human element then takes over and with it brings division.
Some will assert the role of authority (~I stand by Peter'), others the freedom of theological research (~I stand for Apollos'), others the fidelity to the traditions of their founder or elders. One and all forgot the central role of Christ, the sole source of unity in the Church. Through him all else is held in harmony. The Church's security cannot be guaranteed either by authority, tradition or theology. It is not a human institution or a human philosophy. It is a way of salvation, a salvation won by Christ on the cross. And its life is authentic only when the person of the crucified and risen Saviour is at its very centre.
