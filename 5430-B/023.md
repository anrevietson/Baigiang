# Christ, mediator of a new covenant

Second Reading (Heb 9:11-15). The Letter to the Hebrews is more a sermon than a letter, with theological explanations and exhortations throughout its three sections (Heb 1:1-4,13; 4:14-10:31;l0:32-12:29). Central to its message is Christ as high priest and mediator of the new covenant because of his sacrificial death Reflecting on Old Testament themes of priestly ministry, covenant and sanctuary ritual enables the reader to understand the message and its centring on Christ.
This New Testament writer recalls rituals associated with the Day of Atonement (Lev 16), the sacrifice of the heifer, a sin offering (Num 19), and the sacrificial ratification of the Sinai covenant (Ex 24:3-8) In all these rituals, the shedding of blood is a central image, preparing the way for application to the sacrificial death of Jesus. Christ becomes the perfect sacrifice and the mediator of the new covenant, bringing people forgiveness of sin and fullness of life. His death brings salvation, creates a new order, and establishes a new covenant, all clearly seen as fulfilment of the Old Testament. Within the second section of Hebrews (4:14-10:30), the writer challenges readers to draw close to Christ, the high priest, and to hold fast to their confession of faith. While Hebrews focuses on the death of Jesus, with no reference to resurrection or Eucharist, the sacrificial sealing of a new relationship between God and humanity points to its celebration in the Lord's Supper.
