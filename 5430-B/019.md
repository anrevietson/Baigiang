# The blood of Christ

Second Reading (Heb. 9 :11-15). The earlier part of the chapter from which today's reading is taken describes the Israelite tabernacle and its division into the outer sanctuary (the Holy Place) and the inner sanctuary (the Holy of Holies). The priests performed their liturgical services in the Holy Place every day, but no one entered the Holy of Holies except the High Priest, and he could do so only on the annual Day of Atonement (cf. Lev. 16:1-14). The fact that the High Priest alone could enter the inner sanctuary where God was considered to be pre-eminently present indicates that the priests of the old dispensation did not have access to God
At this point today's reading takes up the story and asserts that Christ who is a High Priest far superior to those of the Old Testament has actually entered into the presence of God in his heavenly abode. He can enter the divine presence not because he has offered the blood of animals in expiation for sin, but because he has shed his own blood for the redemption of men. The blood of such a victim is infinitely pleasing to God and has the effect of blotting out forever the sins of men. For this reason the sacrifice of Christ does not have to be repeated annually as had the sacrifices of old. The shedding of Christ's blood took place 'once for all' and its effect of purifying the consciences of men is lasting. The Jewish sacrifices produced only ritual cleanness, and that temporarily. The superiority of the new order of things is obvious.
The new covenant which has Christ as its mediator and which was sealed in his blood both fulfils and abrogates the old covenant. The old economy is obsolete. It is replaced by the newer and richer covenant which had been promised by Jeremiah (Jer. 31:31-34) and brought into being by the High Priest whose sacrifice could give life and grace to all who belonged to the new covenant people.
