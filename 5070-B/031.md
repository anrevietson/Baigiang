# I am doing a new thing

First Reading (Is. 43:18-19, 21-22, 24b-25). Today's Old Testament reading consists of a series of verses chosen from a hymn found in the second part of the book of Isaiah. The Lord proclaims himself creator, redeemer, saviour, king. His people, Israel, have been themselves created as witness to this powerful God (cf. Deut. 4:6-8). Unfortunately, they spend their time looking back to former glories, the first exodus, and their subsequent entry into the promised land. They failed their maker, thinking that they could keep their possessions through a merely formal sacrifice, rather than sincere living. Now, they languish in exile.
Our passage speaks of a new deliverance from that place of exile. They shall not recall the old 'way out'. A new way will be opened out to them by the Lord. The 'rivers in the desert' emphasize the fertility of this 'new thing' done by Yahweh. We might note a similar passage in Isaiah 41:17-20: 'I will open the rivers on the bare heights ...
The last stage of fulfilment, of course, will be the Eucharist. God will remember his people through the celebration of the Mass (cf. Luke 22:19; 1 Cor. 11:24-26).
The people were 'formed' by the Lord that they might gIorify him through a praiseworthy life. We ourselves are exhorted similarly in 1 Peter 2:9. The reaction of the Israelites to the second 'desert purification' (exile) was one of complaint. Such complaints demonstrate that they are weary of the Lord, as they would be of a slave-master. They are not prepared to admit their sins, and they seek a restoration to happiness without recourse to Yahweh. Yet, despite their attitude, the Lord initiates the era of forgiveness out of his goodness. Pardon for sins is a free gift of God.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 40) outlines the 'Confiteor' attitude of the true Israelite, often lacking in the exiled people. 'Heal me, for I have sinned against thee!' The prayer is that of a sick man, conscious of the evil forces that surround him.
