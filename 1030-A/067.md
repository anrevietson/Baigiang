# Are you the one who is to come?

The Gospel (Matthew 11:2-11). John the Baptist came, after Israel had waited {or many centuries for the coming of the Messiah; he stirred them from their lethargy and apathy and roused them to imminent expectation. But the Messiah he preached was one who would bring God's judgement on the world, 'whose winnowing fan was in his hand, who would burn the chaff with unquenchable fire'. Our Lord's unfailing gentleness and mercy was something of a shock to him; until Jesus reminds him that the Messiah comes 'not to condemn but to save'; that healing, forgiveness and redemption are the real work of God's judgement. Our Lord then bears glowing tribute to the personal greatness of John; but this very greatness serves only to bring out even more clearly the radical transformation of the human situation which the gospel calls 'the kingdom of God'. (It is of course this last point that the evangelist is stressing; he is not at all suggesting that John is excluded from the kingdom.) The Christian lives in the power of this new life; even greater faith and hope than John's can be expected of us
