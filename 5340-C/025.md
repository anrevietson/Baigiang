# God our shepherd

God is to us as a shepherd, a kind and supportive friend. He is the source of peace and happiness.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We proclaim our faith in Christ's strength as we say.
You are the Good Shepherd, leading us to everlasting life. Lord, have mercy.
You made peace by your death on the cross. Christ, have mercy.
You promise us a home in heaven. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (2 Sam 5:1-3). King David, the shepherd of his nation, points to Christ, the universal king and shepherd of all peoples.
Second Reading (Col.1: 12-20). Paul presents a triumphant vision of the wonder of Christ with special emphasis on peace and reconciliation.
Gospel (Lk 23:35-43). The criminal on the Cross utters the memorable words. 'Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom'.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Jesus holds out a vision that takes us beyond imagination. There is a place beyond the famines, the mountains of arms, our own private pains and worries.
Reader(s): We pray that the Christian community may be a presence in the world of peace and reconciliation.
We pray that the healing power of Christ the King may spread throughout our whole environment so that all creation may be safe and protected.
We pray for peace among nations and communities so that we may live our short lives in contentment and quiet.
We pray that the end of our lives may find us with all the blessed in paradise.
President: We offer these prayers for our whole world. May it be a safe place to live in. May all species be protected for the greater glory of God.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Let us pray that God's kingdom of justice, peace and love may come now.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
We turn to one another in peace so that Christ's kingdom may be truly manifest in our lives.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The themes of this mass are a recipe for human contentment. All we want is peace and hope for the future. It is what Christ offers as he lights up our hearts.
