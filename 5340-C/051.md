# The privilege and dignity of Christians

Second Reading (Col. 1:11-20). In this reading Paul reminds Christians of the privilege and dignity that is theirs. Through baptism into Christ they have been taken from the. power of sin and darkness into a life of grace and divine revelation. In Christ their sins are forgiven, and the power of sin over them is broken. They thus obtain that true freedom which consists in the power to do what is right. These thoughts lead Paul to give us a hymn on the place of Christ in creation, and to put before us the basis of his universal kingship, or 'headship' as Paul puts it. Christ is head of all created beings. He is God's Son and God's very image. Adam, the first man, was created in the image of God and as such made lord of created beings on earth (Gen. 1:26, 28; Ps. 8; Heb. 1:1-3; 2 :5-9). Christ, the second and the true Adam, is the very image of God in' his humanity as well as his divinity. All things, visible and invisible, material and spiritual (Thrones, etc.), were created through and for him. Created for him, because in the new order of redemption he is head of the Church which is his body As its head he gave his life for his Church, he restored friendship between humanity and God. MI he did he did as bead of the Church and of the new humanity. He has in himself the plentitude of divine power (the probable meaning of the word rendered 'perfection' in the JB), a power to be conveyed to those who believe in him. In God's plan of redemption his mission Is to restore all things to God. He is head of all creation, inanimate as well as animate or rational. In other w6rds, God in Christ and through Christ, has willed to restore mankind to that original state it had before the fall, a state in which man is reconciled with himself, with his fellow-men, with inanimate creation and with God. But all this he did in the first instance through the cross. And the nature of the kingdom of Christ is understood only through the cross. Christ is king as the Suffering Servant, as the servant of others. His kingdom comes in men's hearts and in the world under the shadow of the cross, when men put to death in themselves the sources of disunity and when they live together through love.
