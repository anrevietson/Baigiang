# David anointed king

First Reading (2 Sam. 5 :1-3). David had already been anointed king of Judah by his own people (2 Sam. 2 :4) at Hebron. Today's reading recalls how David's kingship was extended to include the tribes of the northern kingdom of Israel, at the request of the elders of Israel. The anointing of David as king of Judah and Israel was an external sign that David was now God's representative in governing his chosen people. The rite was not merely declaring externally what was already true as an interior reality; it also constituted this reality (after his anointing by Samuel. David is said to have been seized by the spirit of Yahweh from that day forward (cf. 1 Sam. 16 :13). The term 'Anointed One' (Messiah) became a technical term for the future liberator of the people. The Israelites expected from David and his many successors a form of government that would be a reflection of the divine will. However, the nineteen anointed ones who reigned in the North, and the nineteen in the South, gave little hope to the people that divine government would ever come from their lineage. Hence the idea grew that the future liberator of the people would be a truly human and Davidic figure, but at the same time Son of God 'coming on the clouds of heaven'. Jesus was the fulfilment of this expectation, but not quite in the way Judaism saw things. He was not the fire-brand liberator of his people, but the one who was anointed (made Christos, Lord) only after he had given himself up to death on a cross (Phil. 2:5-11).
