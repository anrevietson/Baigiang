# Paul's service of the Gospel

First Reading (1 Cor. 9:16-19, 22-27). Paul had always made a point of not accepting financial support from his converts, while fully acknowledging his right to support. This was not only shrewd policy; as he explains here, it sprang from the inner compulsion he felt to preach the Gospel. In the same spirit he has made himself all things to all men, to win them for Christ. Paul has much to say of Christian freedom; here we can see how he understands it. His very freedom can lead to the renunciation of rights and can also lead to the acceptance of regulations and restrictions where that can help the apostolate. Nor has the apostle any illusions about the demands of the apostolate. If he speaks of pommelling and subduing his body, he is not referring to ascetic practices. Rather, he is considering the austerities and hardships of his life as an apostle : he must drive himself and spend himself in the service of the Gospel.
