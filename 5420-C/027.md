# Peace with God

The second reading (Rom. 5 :1-5) gives one of those magnificent statements we find so often in Paul's letters, where the riches of the multiple relationship of the Christian to God is shown. God is the origin of salvation; it is with him that we are reconciled and at peace; it is to his glory that we look forward; it is his love for us which is at the origin of our movement towards himself. Though Paul gives us no theoretical treatise or even passage on Trinitarian theology, we can see the distinct roles which the different persons play in his thought from the fact that there is no certain passage in his writings where he calls Christ 'God'. He would not, of course, at any stage deny the divinity of 'the Lord Jesus Christ', but while he looks on God as the origin, he looks on Jesus primarily as the means of salvation through whom we are brought to God.
In Paul the Spirit is always thought of primarily as dwelling in Christians, the means by which God dwells in and so works in men. It is the Spirit of God which was promised to men in the Old Testament, as the spirit of holiness which would be poured out in the last times; it is the communication of God and the source of divine power in virtue of which men can love God and their neighbour with the same love as that with which God loves them, and do the works of God, whether it be healing, teaching or preaching God's word, The Spirit is therefore necessarily God in so far as he is in man. The Spirit is the completion of the grand whole of salvation from God through Christ and all in the Spirit.
