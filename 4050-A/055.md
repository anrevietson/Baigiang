# A royal priesthood

Second Reading (1 Pet. 2:4-9). This passage brings out the fact that the community of Christians is the new Israel or the new temple where God is present. Jesus Christ glorified is the life-giving
corner-stone of this building; on him all depend for cohesion and stability; through contact with him Christians are living stones. Here, as in his sermons in Acts, Peter recalls that Jesus was rejected by men but raised up by God. Christ risen is endowed with new life which he can now share with those who believe in him. ('He was raised to life in the Spirit', 1 Pet. 3:18). The Christian community is the new temple where God is worshipped in spirit and in truth. They form a spiritual house; that which binds them to Christ and to one another is the Spirit and the Spirit is the gift of the risen Christ. The idea of temple leads to the idea of priesthood. By Baptism Christians are called to share in the priesthood of Christ, to offer the sacrifice of a life based on Christian faith, especially the sacrifice of obedience to God's will, to a life of prayer and good works which give God glory. Offered in union with Jesus Christ and his sacrifice they are acceptable to God
The Old Testament quotations show the necessity of faith. It is through faith, not through race, that we are united with Jesus Christ. Moreover, men cannot be indifferent to Christ. They must accept him or refuse to accept him. For those who do accept him, he is a solid foundation ensuring salvation; for unbelievers he is a stumbling block. By their rejection of him they condemn themselves. Peter takes titles applied to Israel in the past (cf. Exod. 19:5-6 and Is. 43:20-21) and applies them to Christians because they are the new Israel. To them now is given the role of bearing witness to the great works of God. They are a chosen race. God has taken the initiative (cf. John 6:44). They are a royal priesthood. By baptism Christians are debuted to the cultic service of God in his kingdom established by Jesus Christ. They are a consecrated nation, set apart for the service of God in their whole lives. They are a people for possession - God's property as it were - and with the duty of obeying and serving him. Without Christ men we in darkness, subject to sin and evil and unable to overcome it. Christ has brought them light and life, made good living possible for them. For this they should praise and thank God.
