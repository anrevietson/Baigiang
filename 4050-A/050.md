# I am the way, the truth and the life

Gospel (Jn 14:1-12). This part of Jesus' farewell discourse catches up the atmosphere of Jesus' imminent departure. The troubled hearts of the disciples suggest the subsequent conflict between the powers of evil ('the world') and believers at the departure of Jesus. Although the first three verses may originally have reflected Jesus' return to take the disciples to heaven ('My Father's house'), it was later reread to focus on the union between the disciples and Jesus/God. 'My Father's house' thus becomes the body of Jesus (see Jn 2:19-22; 8:35).
As the way, Jesus is the unique means of salvation. As the truth, he reveals the Father. As the life, he communicates the life he shares with the Father.
The verses following this statement by Jesus develop its implications. To know Jesus, i.e. to acknowledge him in a covenantal way, is to know and acknowledge the Father. To see Jesus is to see the Father. Reflecting Deut 18:18, the author presents Jesus as the emissary of the Father. His words and deeds demonstrate the intimate bond between the Father and himself.
Finally the author links with the present office of the believer. To believe in Jesus is to be empowered to do the works of Jesus. To be united with Jesus and the Father is to share their power.
