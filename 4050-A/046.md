# Let not your hearts be troubled

The Gospel (John 14:1-12). Three things that Jesus has said disturb the disciples - the predictions that one of them would betray him and that Peter would disown him and the statement that he was about to leave them. His words in chapter fourteen aim at strengthening their faith and giving them courage. They must have a confident faith in God and in Jesus himself. As in other places
Jesus puts himself on the same level as the Father in the common work of salvation (ct John 10:30). In the only other reference to my Father's house' in John's Gospel (2:16) the temple in Jerusalem is meant but here something else is intended. The 'Father's house' is wherever God is and whoever is with God is in his house. The many rooms or dwelling-places may indicate simply that in heaven there is room for all, or they may refer to the many members of the Church who go to make up God's spiritual house (cf. second reading above). Jesus promises to return. This promise keeps the Christians' hope alive. Jesus probably refers to his second coming, the parousia, but through his Spirit he is already with his Church so that Christians already enjoy in a way that presence which they will enjoy fully at the second coming.
When Jesus speaks of a 'way' the disciples take it literally, still not understanding him despite the time he has spent in their company. He clarifies for them what he has said. He himself is the way by which alone we can come to know the Father. As the truth he reveals the Father and the Father's love for men in his own person and work. As the life he communicates to men the life which he receives from the Father and which constitutes him as Son. Verse six combines the most fundamental ideas of John's Gospel.
Philip's request implies that he was thinking of some marvellous manifestation of God such as the theophanies mentioned in the Old Testament, and it shows also the disciples' failure to realise that in Christ they have all that can be known of the Father. But in this life we can know the unity of Father and Son only by faith. The 'work' of Jesus includes his whole life and activity, his teaching and miracles.
Those who accept him, who form the Church, will continue the work of Christ, bringing salvation to men. His work was confined to Israel and (with a few exceptions) to the Jews, but the Church is to continue its salvific mission throughout the world. Before it can do this, however, Jesus must go to the Father, he must be glorified so as to send the Holy Spirit.
