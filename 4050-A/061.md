# The first jarring note

First Reading (Acts 6:1-7). Luke mentions the first jarring note in the Jerusalem community, suggesting that the Church Will not be limited to the Jews of Palestine. The problem was the inequitable distribution of food between the Hellenist widows and the Hebrew widows. The former spoke only Greek while the latter spoke a Semitic language besides Greek. To resolve the impasse, the Twelve assembled the community and decided to choose assistants.
Though these assistants are not called 'deacons,' their office is 'deaconing,' i.e., service. It is worth noting that Luke nowhere describes these assistants as providing help for the poor. Instead, men such as Stephen and Philip, are soon seen to be preachers/debaters. In any event the imposition of hands acknowledges the acceptance of the new office in the Jerusalem community. The passage ends with one of Luke's minor summaries, here demonstrating the acceptance of the faith by many of the Jewish priests.
