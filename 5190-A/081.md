# Paul's anguish for Israel

Second Reading (Rom 9:1 -5).Towards the end of the previous chapter, Paul wrote passionately about God's love invincibly bestowed. He is now diverted by a pressing personal concern. Why did the corporate body of Judaism, Jewish religion as a whole, reject Christ?! In this passage, Paul affirms the special situation of Judaism and contents himself with a vehement statement that he is not indifferent to the situation of his kinsmen. Further on he states that Israelis not created by blood and soil but by the promise of God and so lives within the limits of God's freedom. This is Israel of the faith. The present rejection is part of God's plan (Rom 11:1-2). Their refusal to accept the Gospel is temporary and while it lasts it enables the Christian mission to turn to the Gentiles (Rom 11:11). He foresees that they will eventually believe and this will bring a transition from life to death (Rom 11:15). Finally, Paul concludes several chapters of argument on the position of the Jews with a statement of his belief that the tangled problems of existence and history find their solution not in human terms but in divine wisdom (Rom 11:33).
