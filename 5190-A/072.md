# Paul and Israel

Second Reading (Rom 9:1-5). Among the themes in Paul's letter to the Romans are the issues of the Law, justification, and the place of the Jews in salvation. Jesus came to move people beyond the strict confines of the law to a new freedom found through faith. Throughout the letter it is faith in Christ that is the keystone to salvation.
But Paul's faith in Christ does not excuse him from his deep concern for the Jewish people nor does it make them his enemy. Following his conversion and subsequent persecution by the Jews, it would have been easy for Paul to turn his back on them and simply consider them dead because they had rejected not only him but Jesus. However, Paul's faith calls him to continual love.
Indeed, that is why Paul says "I could even wish to be separated from Christ for the sake of my brothers". Faith for Paul is not a private affair that releases us from the obligation to love or serve. Faith opens us to friendship with all people.
