# Adam and Christ

First Reading (Rom. 5 :12, '15, 17-21). Having shown in the first part of the doctrinal section of his letter (1:17-4 :25) that the Christian is righteous only by God's gift, Paul, in the second part (5 :1-11:36), deepens this insight by insisting that the Christ-event was a work of God's love. He announces his positive thesis in 5 :1-5, develops the antithesis in 5 :6-7 :25 and returns to his thesis in 8 :1-39, chapters 9-11 being his scriptural justification for the whole of the second part.
The positive experience of liberation in Christ has as its necessary negative counterpart a previous state of slavery: man has been liberated by Christ; therefore, before and without Christ, he was and is a slave. It is the positive experience that reveals its own privation and not the experience of the privation which calls for its healing.
Paul sees man before and without Christ as the victim of a threefold slavery: death (5 :12-21), self (6 :1-23) and the law (7 :1-25). His awareness that it is in the one man Jesus that all men receive the gift of life allows him to draw a parallel between Adam and Christ. He understands mankind's doleful incorporation in Adam in the light of its present incorporation in Christ, and not vice Versa, although he appeals to the story of Gen. 3 as a point of comparison. He lays great stress on the superabundance of God's gift in Christ over the consequences of Adam's fault.
