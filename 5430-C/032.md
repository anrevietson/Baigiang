# The offering of Melchizedek

First Reading (Gen. 14 :18-20). The offering of Melchizedek has always in the Church been considered to be a type of that of Christ. In Psalm 110 the Messiah is promised to be a priest after the order of Melchizidek, for Melchizedek was the priest-king of Jerusalem, the city which was later taken over by David to be his own possession; at least at first he and his sons retained the right to function as priests, so that the Psalm in claiming this priesthood for the Messiah ]is asserting his Davidic descent and return to the full Davidic role as the perfect king after the model of David. There are two other elements which invite us to compare Melchizedek to Christ, namely that Melchizedek appears in the Bible without 'father or mother or ancestry, and his life has no beginning or ending; he is like the Son of God' (Heb 7 :3), and secondly that his offering is bread and wine, as at Jesus' last supper
The Responsorial Psalm is the well-known Sunday Vespers psalm, which sings of the Messiah king and of his priesthood 'like Melchizedek's'
