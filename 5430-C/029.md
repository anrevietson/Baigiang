# The miracle of the loaves and fishes

The Gospel (Luke 9:11-17). The miracle of the loaves and fishes was certainly seen by the evangelists and the early Church as a 'foretaste of the Eucharist; it is to this miracle that John' attaches his great Eucharist discourse on the bread of life. It is the meal of the Messiah with his companions when he provides for them abundance of food. But for a fuller understanding of its significance it is essential to understand also the backward references : Moses 'already 'had provided' bread for his people in the desert (as here, 'in a lonely place'). The narrative is, however, consciously modelled on that of 2 Kings 4:42-44, when Elisha in a very similar way miraculously distributes bread among his followers ; Jesus is shown to be in the same prophetic tradition, but he works the miracle by his own power rather than by prayer to God, and the miracle is far greater; it is also a sign of the plenty promised for the messianic times. a
