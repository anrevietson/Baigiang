# Eating together

Eating with someone is usually a sign of companionship and friendship. The feast of Corpus Christi reminds us that Jesus showed friendship and companionship with the marginalised of his society by eating with them.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
You offer us the gift of friendship with you. Lord, have mercy.
You call us to share that friendship with one another. Christ, have mercy.
You summon us to serve the week and poor of our society. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Gen 14:18-20). Abraham, our ancestor in faith, shares the bread and wine of friendship with Melchizedek, king of peace.
Second Reading (1 Cor 11:23-26). St. Paul gives an account of the last meal of Jesus with his disciples before he died.
Gospel (Lk 9:11-17). Jesus' ministry of preaching and curing people is brought to fulfilment by the sharing of food.

PRAYER OF THE faithful
President: As we come together to share in the table of the Eucharist, let us place our petitions before our God.
Response: 0 Lord, hear us we pray: 0 Lord, give us your love.
Reader(s): For the leaders of the Church: that they may break the bread of God's word in a new way for us all today.
For those in positions of authority in society: that they may examine how best to use the world's resources for the benefit of those who are most in need.
For the harvest: that we may receive with gratitude the fruits of the earth.
For all Christians: that we may have the same sense of sharing that Jesus had when he broke bread with the poorest in his society.
President: As we bring our gifts to the altar, we thank you, 0 God, for your faithfulness and love towards us in Jesus Christ, your Son.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Give to all who need it their daily bread as we pray.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Instead of giving each other a handshake today, let us think for a moment how closely linked the giving of peace is with the giving of food.

COMMUNION REELECTION
Bakerwoman God,
I am your living bread,

I am your low, soft, and being-shaped loaf.
I am your rising
bread, well-kneaded

pair of knuckles,

Put me in fire,
Bakerwoman God,
put me in your own bright fire.
I am warm, warm as you from the fire.
I am white and gold, soft and hard,
brown and round.
I am warm from fire.
Break me, Bakerwoman God.
I am broken under your caring Word.
Drop me in your special juice in pieces.
Drop me in your blood.
Drunken me in the great red flood.
Self-giving chalice swallow me.
My skin shines in the divine wine.
My face is cup-covered and I drown.
I fall up in a red pool in a gold world where your warm sunskin hand is there
to catch and hold me.
Bakerwoman God, remake.
