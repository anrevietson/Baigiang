# The offering of Melchizedek

First Reading (Gen. 14:18-20). The offering of Melchizedek has always in the Church been considered to be a type of that of Christ. In Psalm 110 the Messiah is promised to be a priest after the sower of Melchizidek, for Melchizedek was the priest-king of Jerusalem, the city which was later taken over by David to be his own p05-sion ; at least at first he and his sons retained the right to function as priests, so that the Psalm in claiming this priesthood for the Messiah is asserting his Davidic descent and return to the full Davidic role as the perfect king after the model of David. The letter to, the Hebrews sees further significance in the prophecy because the" priesthood of Melchizedek owes nothing to the line of Aaron, of which it is completely independent; the author also interprets the a fact that Abraham offers tithes to Melchizidek (the action implying recognition of a superior) as, implying the superiority of Melchizidek's priesthood because the ancestor of Aaron does him 'homage in this way. There are two other elements which invite us to compare Melchizedek to Christ, namely that Melchizedek appears in the Bible without 'father or mother or ancestry, and his life has no beginning or ending; he is like the Son of God' '(Heb. 7 :3), and secondly that his Offering is bread and wine, as at Jesus' last supper. But the incident of Melchizedek and its under' standing in the Bible are so enigmatic and remote from our way of reasoning that it is difficult to see in it more than a fortuitous resemblance to the Eucharist which can be used only in an accommodated sense.
