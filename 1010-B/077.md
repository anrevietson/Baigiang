# Xin Chúa xé trời và cứu dân khỏi tội lỗi

**Tác giả:** Chưa xác định

**Chủ đề:** Chú giải Isaia về lưu đày, thống hối và hy vọng cuộc xuất hành cứu độ; tiếng Anh.

First Reading (Is. 63:16-17; 64:1, 3-8). This is taken from a long psalm (Is. 63:7-64:12) which was composed sometime during Israel's exile in Babylon (587-537 BC) or immediately after the exiles' return to their homeland in Palestine. In this psalm Israel reflects on God's loving mercy towards her in the past. He is her Father; he has created and moulded her history. He in person it was who led his people like a shepherd from Egypt at the first exodus. And despite all this the nation abandoned him, but to her own great loss. Jerusalem the holy city where God dwelt was in ruins. Israel declares its sin. It has become as an unclean, polluted, thing. Having cut itself off from its divine life-giving source the nation has become like a heap of withered leaves, scattered abroad in exile by the wind. The psalmist pleads with God to intervene again as he did of yore, and bring about a new exodus of salvation. He begs of him to rend apart the heavens where he dwells and intervene. (The very mention of this divine intervention has the psalmist make use of the traditional imagery; cf. Pss. 143 [144] :5;17[18]:8-1O; 50[51]:3.) This psalm which prays for the advent of the messianic redemption is one from which the traditional liturgy of Advent has borrowed rather heavily. Today at the opening of the Advent season it shows us Israel's expectation of the new exodus to come in messianic times. This age was inaugurated long after this psalm was written, some five hundred years later. And even though the Messiah has come we too can make this prayer ours as we still look forward to that final coming of Christ when sin will be destroyed for ever. God's kingdom, even now, is ever coming through the conquest of sin and Christians, through a life in conformity with Christ's teaching, can hasten his final coming; cf. 2 Peter 3:12 (Second Sunday of Advent).
