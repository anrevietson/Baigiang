# Mary, uniquely graced by God

Today's feast celebrates Mary, uniquely graced by God. With her, we too are called to wholeness, to openness, and to generosity in recognizing and responding to the Word of God in our lives.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We know that Christ came for us because of our need of him, because we are sinners. Let us pause and ask for his forgiveness before we celebrate our Eucharist together.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Gen 3:9-15,20). In the aftermath of temptation and fall, all is not lost. Our abuse of freedom often exposes us to suffering and pain. But there is still a promise of hope, symbolised in the mysterious offspring of an unnamed woman.
Second Reading (Eph 1:3-6,11-12). God's plan for us has its origins before the creation of the world. This plan, made possible through Christ, calls us to a radical holiness, to live in love in his presence.
Gospel (Lk 1:26-38). Mary's extraordinary wholeness and holiness is shown in her capacity to respond totally to God coming into her life. This can be seen especially in the angel's greeting, 'Rejoice, O graced one' and in her reply, 'Let it happen to me according to your word'.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Let us turn to God our Father and place our needs before him in the spirit of Mary, woman of faith.
Response: Lord, we ask you, hear our prayer.
Reader(s): We pray for those who lead the people of God, especially for N. our pope and N. our bishop, that they will be faithful in proclaiming Jesus, the Word of God.
We remember those who are oppressed in any way, especially those who suffer the consequences of war, injustice and exploitation.
We pray that human hearts will turn to thoughts of peace, and that bitterness and anger will give way to forgiveness and healing.
Let us pray for our departed, asking that they be received into God's home, just as Christ once made his home with us, through the generosity of Mary, his mother and ours.
President: Blessed are you, Lord God, for you have given us knowledge of your love by sending us your only Son, born of the Virgin Mary. We ask you to hear these Our prayers, and answer them through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
We bless God's holy name for the gift of his Son, praying as Christ has taught us to do.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Let us pray to our heavenly Father, asking that the forgiveness which Christ has brought may be ours, and that we may be generous in giving
that same forgiveness to others.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The Word of God is a tree of life,
which offers you fruit from every side.
It is like the rock which burst forth in the desert becoming spiritual drink to all who were thirsting.
Should we discover one of its riches let us not think we have found all that is there. Enriched by its strength, let us not imagine we have impoverished it. Rather, let us give thanks for its greatness and recognise that we are unequal to it.
Rejoice that you have been satiated and do not be upset that it is richer than you. The thirsty rejoice because they can drink but are not upset if unable to render the source dry. The well can conquer your thirst, but your thirst cannot conquer the well.
Adapted from the writings of St Eplirem (d. 373)
