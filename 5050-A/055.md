# Share your bread with the hungry

First Reading (Is. 58 :7-10). This passage is taken from a discourse (Is. 58 :1-14) of some unidentified prophet who preached to the Jews shortly after the return from the exile. Legalism and ritual practices such as fasting were then beginning to become prominent in Israel and the Jews thought they could win God's favour by the external observances of religion. Through the prophet God makes reapply and seeks to interiorise their religion. External ritual observance without interior religion and moral behaviour is useless in God's sight. By fasting, rich and poor alike took on themselves the common state of hunger. Fasting, then, by its nature denoted solidarity. What God wants is that this solidarity be real, its genuine character expressed by the rich sharing their goods with the poor, by helping the homeless, and by bringing oppression to an end. In this the light of the messianic age will be seen and redemption will come to rich and poor alike. By such behaviour will believers have God with them ('I am here'); then he will answer when they pray.
The present reading is chosen to match the Gospel in which Christ called his followers the light of the world. The entire passage of the book of Isaiah from which the reading is taken, however, is in the spirit of the Sermon on the Mount, both stressing interior religion.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 111) speaks of the upright man as a light for others through his good works, especially those of consideration for others.
