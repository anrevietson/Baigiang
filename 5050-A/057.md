# A poem on fasting

First Reading (Is 58: 7-10). Today's reading is the middle of a poem on fasting addressed to the Israelites during post-exilic days. The passage stresses the need for personal integrity in life and worship. Clothing the naked and sheltering the oppressed and homeless are key components for the kind of authentic fasting that leads to the credible witness value of the worshipping community. The Jews particularly turned to works of charity and concern for the poor during times of bereavement and times of national sorrow. In the past their collective fast commemorated the siege of Jerusalem, the breach in walls, assassination and other forms of turmoil; the fasting described in today's reading targets the discouragement of the post-exilic Jews and tries to focus their attention on problems of people who are suffering without the support of a community like their own.
The reference to sharing bread is akin to the liturgical concept of breaking bread. The need to share bread with the poor makes the eschatological deliverance of the people coincide with compassionate acts of charity. Only the rich can fast in this way but as they share the lot of the poor there is a resulting solidarity, uniting all people. The effects of sincere fasting allow God to fill all the material and spiritual needs of the world because the poor share their emptiness with those who had not been empty enough to be filled with God.
