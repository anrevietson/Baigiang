# God has been waiting

Welcome to today's Eucharist! If we can believe the gospel reading for today, God has been waiting and watching for us. We gather in thanks for the love and compassion God so generously offers us.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Now we open our hearts and present ourselves to God without pretence;, we are people who have sinned but our God is full of mercy and forgiveness:
You came to welcome sinners. Lord, have mercy.
You wash away all guilt and shame. Christ, have mercy.
You bid us to open our hearts to God's peace. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Ex 32:7-11.13-14). Though the people of Israel sin and deserve God's wrath, Moses intercedes for them. What begins as a story of a people's sinfulness is really a story of God's forgiveness.
Second Reading (1 Tim 1:12-17). Personal experience has taught the author of this letter one thing for certain: Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners. This reading should give hope to even the worst of sinners. God is ever ready to show mercy.
Gospel (Lk 15:1-32). The familiarity of this reading should not cause us to lose sight of its message and meaning. Christ came to welcome sinners. God also waits and watches with love for us to return home. We need not fear God's wrath. It is only our guilt and pride that keeps us separated from God.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: We rise now to answer God's call. We set aside our judgments of others and empty our hearts of the desire to see people get what we feel they deserve. In humility and compassion we open our hearts in prayer for the whole of our Christian family.
Reader(s): For our Church and civil leaders: that they may be effective witnesses of Christ's values and way of life: let us pray to the Lord.
For an increase of peace throughout our world, a peace made possible only by our forgiving one another: let us pray to the Lord.
For the healing of those who feel they are beyond forgiveness, that they may open their hearts to the miracle of God's mercy and love: let us pray to the Lord.
For those who are ill, for those who feel overwhelmed by life, for those who struggle with depression and despair, for those who seek employment and homes, for those without any sense of human worth and dignity: let us pray to the Lord.
For those who have died and for those who grieve their loss: let us pray to the Lord.
President: Merciful God, we ask you to receive our prayers and in answering them to give us the grace to become your instruments of peace and forgiveness. We offer these prayers with the willingness to change our lives in the example of Christ Jesus our Lord forever and ever.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Let us pray this prayer carefully and realise that we are asking God to forgive us in the measure that we ourselves are willing to forgive others.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Now we offer each other a sign of our unity, a sign of our desire to live in peace and love.

COMMUNION MEDITATION
Lord God, we are so ready to dwell on the vision of our sin and guilt. Strengthen our sight, purify our vision to see you as you wait and watch. Let us see the look in your eyes of longing for our return. Let no fear of punishment or disappointment hinder our coming home to you. And, Lord, let us be sources of encouragement and hope. Enable us to make the words of your forgiveness become flesh in our world through our own willingness to forgive.
