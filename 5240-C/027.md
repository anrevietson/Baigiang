# The golden calf

First Reading (Exod. 32 :7-11, 13-14). Chapters 32-34, after a long priestly insertion on the cult of Israel (25-31), take up again the Yahwists narrative of the events at Mt. Sinai (cf. ch. 24). While Moses is conversing with God on the mountain, the people conspire with Aaron to make themselves gods; 'as for this Moses who brought us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him' (verse 1). So the people made themselves a golden calf, 'sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play' (verse 6). Many commentators think that the narrative is here playing on the historical defection of the northern kingdom of Israel under Jeroboam, when semi-pagan cult was established at Dan and Bethal (1 Kings 12 :28).
The reading of the Mass takes up from this context (verse 7). God informs Moses on the mountain of what the people have done. 'Your' people, he calls them as if these no longer belonged to him; and in verse 10 there is a hint that he can make himself another people. Then Moses turns to God and intercedes for the people (11-13; the power of intercession is also a frequent theme), reminding God of what he has done in the past and of the promise given to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. In the end, God himself 'repents' of the evil he had contemplated for them.
Application: idolatry is a besetting sin of mankind, if not the idolatry of the pagans then that of the market-place: business, pleasure, materialism, the mammon of iniquity.
