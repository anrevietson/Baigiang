# The faith of Abraham

Second Reading (Heb 11:8.11-12.17-19). The author of Hebrews had begun this section on faith by stating that faith consists in being sure of what we hope for and certain of what we do not see. He goes on to give examples of such faith in the history of Israel, the most significant being Abraham. Despite moments of doubt and questioning, Abraham trusted God's word of promise, remaining certain of what he did not see. He did not live to receive the things promised; he 'only saw them and welcomed them from a distance' (11:13). The greatest test to his faith in God's promise was when he felt called to sacrifice his son Isaac to God. He showed himself willing to do what God appeared to be asking, even though the death of Isaac would mean the death of the promise. Although God did not, in fact, ask Abraham to make this sacrifice, the experience revealed Abraham's faith in a God who had power to bring life out of death.
