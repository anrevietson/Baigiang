# The call of Abraham

First Reading (Gen 15:1-6,21:1-3). In the first of these two passages, the author describes the call of Abram in terms similar to that of a prophet. Abram is worried that a slave born in his household will become his heir. Yahweh re-assures him that his very own issue will be his heir. Abram believes the divine promise, and his confidence places him in a right relationship with Yahweh. The second passage describes the birth of Isaac in the old age of his parents, the fulfilment of the promise made to the man now called Abraham by God.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps 105): The congregation of Israel are summoned to acknowledge the reality and wonderful deeds of a God faithful to the chosen people. 'Yahweh is our God, mindful of the covenant forever'.
Second Reading Heb 11:8,11-12,7-19). 'We must study the old to understand the new'. The author of this work interprets the Old Testament in the light of New Testament events, and exhorts the readers to persevere in faith and hope. This passage quotes the example of Abraham from many biblical' pioneers in the faith, Three instances are cited: the journey in faith from his homeland to a promised land; his belief in the divine assurance of a son in his old age; his readiness to sacrifice that son at the divine command, trusting in a God who can raise from the dead.
