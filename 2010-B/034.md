# The presentation in the temple

The Gospel (Luke 2:22-40). The Gospel narrates the presentation of the child Jesus in the temple. Two salient themes should be noticed: the obedience of the Holy Family to the law of God given to Israel through Moses, and the altogether exceptional nature of Jesus and his mission. Because Mary and Joseph were God-fearing they observed the law of God concerning a first-born child. Luke lays stress on this observance of the law on their part. The law in question is to be found in Leviticus 12:1-8. The offering Mary and Joseph bring is that of the poor and an indication that they could not afford the regular offering which was a year-old lamb and a young pigeon or turtledove.To ordinary eyes Mary and Joseph were but two ordinary Jews. But this situation was no ordinary one. The Spirit was already active, revealing to certain pious people - and to Mary and Joseph as well - who this child was. There was then a rather general expectation of the Messiah, the Anointed of the Lord, among Jews even though they must have differed not a little on the nature and mission of the Messiah. To most he would be a warring monarch come to destroy Roman power and restore the kingdom of David. Other pious Jews most probably had a more spiritual concept of the Christ. Simeon sees in him the Servant of the Lord spoken of in the Book of Isaiah. His role was to bring salvation to the pagans, to be the light of the world (cf. Is. 52:10; 42:6; 46:13; 49:6). Simeon praised God for the honour of having lived to see the Messiah and tells God that he can now die in peace. Anna, too, sees in Jesus the promised Saviour and speaks of the mission of the child to those who awaited the long-promised deliverance. Mary, too, receives a revelation of the mission of her Son from Simeon. Her Son would later say (Luke 12:51-53) that his coming would cause dissension among men. His person would make men take sides, for him or against him. And Mary, his mother, was intimately related to the mystery of her Son. She would suffer with him and learn from his life and teaching what this mystery really was.
The message of all this for the feast we celebrate today is that the Holy Family lived in obedience to the law of God as revealed to Israel of old and as it was being revealed anew through Jesus; they were faithful to the valid and genuine traditions of the past and open to the light of the new age that was dawning. They lived with their traditions and the mystery of Christ, a mystery to be learned through faith. And in this, of course, the family of Nazareth is the model for Christian families today.
