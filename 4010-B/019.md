# Peter's witness

First Reading (Acts 10:34,37-43). The different books of the New Testament show a progressive understanding of who Jesus is in the light of his death and resurrection. This culminates in the theologies of Paul and John. In the Acts of the Apostles, Luke has included speeches in which some of the earliest formulations of the Christian message about Jesus are preserved. This address of Peterto the household of the Gentile, Cornelius, is one of these speeches. The account of Jesus ministry is simple: Jesus was anointed with the Holy Spirit and with power and went about doing good and curing all who had fallen into the power of the devil. They' killed him but God raised him to life. There is an emphasis, due no doubt to Luke, on the witnesses to the Resurrection. They were few, but God chose them and gave them convincing experience of Jesus' presence, as he ate and drank with them (cf Lk 24:43, Acts 1:4). The message about Jesus is to be proclaimed to the people. But Christ as judge is not specially emphasised in Luke's thought. It seems to be characteristic of the early witness to the Gospel and is good news rather than bad.
