# Hidden with Christ in God

Second Reading (Col. 3 :1-4). We have been raised to a new life with Christ, we are hid with Christ in God, and we await his coming. The context of this is not in the immediately-preceding verses but in 2 :12 f. Here (as in Rom. 6 :5 if.) there is a realistic interpretation of baptism, which the late Dom Odo Casel and many others think to be influenced by the ancient mystery-cults. Death and resurrection with Christ are thought of in terms of a ritual re-enactment. At any rate, baptism commits one to the new life of resurrection.
Alternative Second Reading (1 Cor. 5 :6-8). Due to the fact, in all likelihood, that he was writing his letter at Easter time, Paul draws an analogy between the Easter event and the feast of the 'unleavened bread' (mazzoth-azymem). Originally this was probably a distinctive feature from that of the paschal lamb (the one possibly Canaanite and agricultural by origin, the other Israelite and nomadic). Both the bread and the lamb were sacrifices of the spring first-fruits; historicization through association with the commemoration of the exodus probably came later. As a first4ruits the bread had to be unprofound, pure and uncontaminated, there-fore free from any leavening agent. During the week beginning with the Passover no leavened bread was used. Paul applies the lesson to Christian life. For him, as for John (19 :26 if.) and the Apocalypse, Christ himself is the sacrificial paschal lamb. Therefore tyou are now unleavened'; from now on there must be no impurity in your lives.
