# The new leaven

Second Reading (1 Cor 5:6-8). As so often, Paul is speaking in the context of Christians living their new life and in this chapter is facing severe scandal in the Corinthian community. He may have written the letter around Paschal time (cf 16:8). At any rate the feast of unleavened bread prompts him to write this passage. Jewish families searched the house to get rid of last year's leaven in order to celebrate the Passover with unleavened bread and begin the year with new leaven. So the Corinthians should take care to rid their lives of the old influence of sin and celebrate the feast with sincerity and truth. Leaven is an image several times repeated in the New Testament, sometimes with positive overtones (Mt 13:33; Lk 13:20-21), sometimes with negative (Mk 8:15; Gal 5:9).
Alternative First Reading (Col 3:1-4). Here Paul is confident enough to speak of Christians as already risen with Christ and sharing his risen life, though this life is still hidden and waiting to be fully revealed when Christ is revealed in his glory. The contrast this time is not with sin but with rules and regulations about material things, prohibitions about this or that, devotions, self-abasement, bodily asceticism, people seeing visions or demanding worship of angels (vv. 16-23). The Christian is genuinely liberated by sharing in the resurrection to live a new life in the Spirit and is not compelled in a superstitious or psychologically compul sive way by rules and regulations, devotions, asceticism, or visions. Rather these things are there to be of service and should be used insofar as they are helpful to the Christian community and serve to build up the body of Christ.
