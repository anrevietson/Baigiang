# The ambiguity of the empty tomb

Gospel (Jn 20:1-9). As in Matthew's account, so too in John, the ambiguity of the empty tomb is pointed out (v.2). Of itself it does not prove the Resurrection but raises the question about the body of Jesus. Together with the appearances, it forcefully points to the reality of Jesus' Resurrection. The resurrection is not merely a spiritual or psychological event and if Jesus' risen body does not simply return to the materially conditioned life he has left, the new life of the Resurrection has nonetheless its bodily aspect. This Gospel recounts the finding of the empty tomb It is complemented in the rest of the chapter by three appearances of the risen Jesus, one to Mary Magdalene, one to the community of the disciple without Thomas and one with Thomas, who insists on the bodily aspect if the Resurrection is to be real. Thomas believes because he sees (and touches) but Jesus blesses those who have not seen and yet believe (v.29). One who sees only the empty tomb and the linen cloths but already believes is the beloved disciple (v.9). In Chapter 21, he is again ahead of Peter in recognising the risen Lord (21:7).
