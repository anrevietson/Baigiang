# The empty tomb

The Gospel (John 20 :1-9). In the Gospel reading two episodes are linked. (They do not fit well together; cf. v.2 with v.11 and 17 f. with 8.) The first (Mary's visit to the tomb) has some basis in the Synoptic tradition, in which, however, several women are mentioned; the second, the race of Peter and the beloved disciple, is proper to John. In all the Gospels there is some emphasis on the fact that the women were the first to discover the empty grave and that the disciples were incredulous when they heard of it.
The race of the two disciples is almost certainly symbolic. Peter, setting out first, probably represents the Jewish church, which was the first to believe. But the Jewish community was soon overtaken by the Greeks, who came in greater numbers (equals the Disciple). Finding the tomb empty, though the cloths are still there, 'they believed' (i.e. in the resurrection). Hitherto they had not 'known' (equals understood) the scripture that he must rise from the dead.
