# Chồi công chính của Đavít và Giêrusalem mới

**Tác giả:** Chưa xác định

**Chủ đề:** Lời hứa cứu độ của Giêrêmia và lòng trông cậy của dân Chúa; tiếng Anh.

First Reading (Jer. 33 :14-16). (See SCRIPTURE IN CHURCH, no.3, pp.473-478.) It is likely that this passage, significantly missing in the LXX, is a later, adapted version of an authentic oracle of the prophet already given in Jer. 23 :5-6. There, as a conclusion to his oracles against the kings of Judah (21:11 . 23 :8), Jeremiah gives three messianic oracles (23 :1-4, 5-6, 7-8); the second of these looks to a personal Messiah: 'Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, when I will raise up for David a righteous branch, and he shall reign as king and deal wisely, and shall execute justice and righteousness in the land' (23 :5). The term 'branch', derived from Is. 11:1, designates the Messiah (cf. Zech. 3 :8, 6 :12). The new reign will be marked, to an eminent degree, by wisdom, justice and righteousness and, in the days of the new king the reunited land will again know peace: 'In his days Judah will be saved, and Israel will dwell securely' (v.6). The name of the Messiah is 'Yahweh is our righteousness' in deliberate contrast to the last pathetic king of Judah, Zedekiah - a living contradiction of the meaning of his own name ('my righteousness is Yahweh'). In the adapted oracle the New Jerusalem has taken the place of Israel and is now called by the new name of the messianic king (33 : 16). This hopeful oracle does find place in the 'Book of Consolation' (Jer. 30-33).
The prophet takes his stand on God's word: a time must and will come when his promise to his people will be accomplished. The day will dawn when the Son of David will stand among his people and when the New Jerusalem will stand forth as the true city of God. The oracle fittingly introduces the season of Advent, of preparation. For Jesus is the 'root of David' (Apoc. 5 :5), who will inaugurate the new Jerusalem, the veritable kingdom of God (21:9-27).
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 24 :4-5, 8-9, 10-14) stresses the goodness and righteousness of the Lord. The psalmist prays that the Lord will enlighten and guide him.
