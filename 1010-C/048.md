# Canh thức đón Con Người trong vinh quang

**Tác giả:** Chưa xác định

**Chủ đề:** Chú giải khải huyền Luca, hy vọng giải thoát và cầu nguyện giữa thử thách; tiếng Anh.

Gospel (Luke 21 :25-28, 34-36). The three synoptists have each an 'eschatological' or 'apocalyptic' discourse: Mt. 24 :1-51; M. 13 :1-37; Lk. 21 :5-36. Luke alone, as well, treats of the parousia; the glorious return of Jesus, by itself (17:22-37). In chapter twenty-one he makes a clear distinction between the destruction of Jerusalem (21:5-24) and the End (21:25-36). In short, he handles two distinct themes: one historical - the destruction of Jerusalem and the victory of the gospel; the other eschatological - the end of this age and the parousia of the Son of Man. He does so because he is conscious of the delay of the parousia (no longer sharing the earliest expectation that it was around the corner) and because he is convinced that Christians must adjust to a long period of waiting and persecution. The two passages that make up our reading concern the coming of the Son of Man and a call to watchfulness.
Luke's apocalyptic description (21:25-26) is couched in Old Testament language; it has been influenced especially by is. 13 :10 and Ps. ,65 :8. Cosmic signs and distress on earth are the stock accompaniment of a divine intervention and especially of the divine judgement of mankind. The cloud which accompanies Old Testament theophanies not only veils the glory of' God (Hab. 3 :4; Ps. 18 :11) but also manifests it (Ex. 34 :5). The cloud of the parousia will reveal Jesus' hitherto hidden glory, which is the glory of God. He comes in the cloud, the vehicle of God, to effect the divine work of judgement and redemption. We must not forget that all this is apocalyptic imagery. The parousia really means that there is a goal to the divine plan working itself out in history, a plan that is accomplished in and through the incarnate Son, the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end (Apoc. 22 :13).
Christians need not fear: the' cosmic events which 'will terrify the nations will indicate to the followers of Christ that the time of persecution is ending - their 'redemption' (a Pauline word) is drawing near. They have been liberated, set free from sin, by Christ. But they must not be presumptuous (2,1:34-36). Christians must be constantly on their guard against dissipation and absorption in worldly affairs (cf. 8 :14; 12 :22; 17 :26-30): the end will come suddenly, and upon all men without distinction. Vigilance and prayer (cf. 18 :1) will win for them strength to support the danger s and temptations of the last trials and will enable them to stand (among the redeemed) in the presence of the Son of Man come in his glory. The parousia may be delayed, but it will eventually involve us all. How, one lives here and now determines how one will 'stand before the Son of Man'. These words of Luke, apparently so remote, are not at all without reference to our day-to-day lives. For trials can strike us suddenly, at any time; and the need of prayer is always with us.
