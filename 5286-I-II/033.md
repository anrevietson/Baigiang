# Paul's prayer for the believers

First Reading (Eph 1:15-23). Paul completes his opening greeting to the new believers with a blessing from the Spirit akin to the Irish greeting 'Bail 6 Dhia oraibh!'('Success, blessing from God be upon you') - that they may be brought to full knowledge of God who was and is and is to come. He prays for the eyes of the mind to be opened to the hope that underlies God's call, which is exemplified in the power of Christ as head of the Church, 'his body, the fullness of him who fills the whole creation'.
The psalm of the day muses at the amazing care that God has for man, for the power with which he has endowed us, and for which we should be humble and responsible.
Ignatius of Antioch, celebrated today, who was also called Theophorus ('bearer of God in his heart'), was regarded as a type of Christian humility. He was one of the first bishops of Antioch, martyred during the reign of Trajan in Rome by being thrown to wild beasts. 'Nothing matters', he said. 'whether fire, or the cross, or the assault of wild beasts, or the tearing asunder of my bones, or the breaking of my limbs, if I do but gain Christ.'
