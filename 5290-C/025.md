# Persevering prayer

The first reading (Exodus 17 :8-13) is an apt illustration from the history of the Chosen People of both 'the need to pray continually and never lose heart', and of the efficacy of such prayer. Israel was in the early stages of her 'training' in the wilderness, the 'testing of her inmost heart . . . to make her understand that man lives on everything that comes from the mouth of God' (see Deut. 8 :2-6). She had hardly finished celebrating her miraculous deliverance from Egypt by the overthrow of Pharaoh's pursuing armies (Ex. 15), when (chapters 16-17) she had to face different, but equally terrifying threats to her very existence - the need for food and drink. These basic needs are met by God's miraculous provision of quails, manna, and water from the rock. But the moral trauma which Israel's faith-relationship with God had suffered ('Is God with us, or not?': 17 :7) was grievous enough to be commemorated in the name of the place: Massah and Meribah. And now, with the sudden onslaught from the hostile Amalekites, an even swifter annihilation of the whole people seemed inevitable. God, it would seem, was not prepared to intervene miraculously for his people this time, as he had done on the former occasions. And Moses now steps forward to assume that full prophetic role of intercessor which would be increasingly his (cp. Ex. 32 :11 f; Num. 14 :13 f; etc.). The raised hands are a traditional gesture of prayer (Pss. 28 :2; 44 :21; 63 :5; etc.); but its naively concrete expression in today's passage would remain in Israel's traditions an unforgettable reminder - in the face of any disaster - of its efficacy with God 'Israel's guard . . . who made heaven and earth' (Responsorial Psalm).
