# Moses' hands raised in prayer

First Reading (Ex 17:8-13). Here we have an incident from the desert wandering of Israel that is not prepared for in the previous narrative of Israel's apostasy at Massah and Meribah (Ex 17:1-7). Two of the characters of the story appear without introduction in the text, Joshua and Hur. Aaron is known from the earlier sections of the book of Exodus, but the Amalakites are equally un-introduced. But like the other characters they will figure again in the Hexateuch and their role seems to be that of enemy of Israel right to the time of Saul and David (1 Sam 15). In this story the focus falls on Moses and his curious activity of raising his hands, whether with his charismatic rod or without it. Despite early and consistent understanding of this activity as alluding to Moses' prayer, there is no reference to such in the text. Possibly his activity was viewed as quasi-magical. This passage is very elusive for the reader trying to find a definite meaning to the story. Possibly it is to explain the phenomenon of the stone on which Moses sat.
