# Moses' hands raised in prayer

First Reading (Exod. 17 :8-13). On the way up from Egypt towards the promised land, the Israelites were attacked in the desert by the people of Amalek. This is the first of many battles to be fought by the Hebrews, God's chosen people. It is symbolic of the spiritual struggles with which people of all ages have to reckon - including our own battles against selfishness and sin. Amalek is defeated, but due to God's help more than to sheer force of arms; the determining feature, stronger than tactics and weapons, is the prayer of Moses. With his arms upraised in appeal to God, this great man of faith is a model of prayer for all ages.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 120) expresses the trust that gives meaning to prayer: the trust that God never sleeps, but is always alert to the needs of his faithful people. He is thought of as 'above', 'in the mountain-tops', 'in the heavens', yet at the same time, always close at hand ('at your right side he stands') able to give instant protection against all dangers. This profound paradox of the majesty and intimacy of God, is repeated in the response:
'Our help is in the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth.'
