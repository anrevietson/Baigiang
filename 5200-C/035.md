# The martyrdom of Jeremiah

First Reading (Jer. 38 :4-6, 8-10). This passage is part of the story of the 'Martyrdom of Jeremiah' (Jer. 36-45) which we owe to Baruch, Jeremiah's faithful scribe. The scene is set in the declining years of Judah's power, at a time, in fact, when that power was about to be quenched forever. Jeremiah saw that the taking of his beloved Jerusalem by the . Babylonians was inevitable (37 :8, 17; 38 :3). He urged the king to surrender to the invading army (38 :17) so that lives might be spared and they are saved from total destruction. Some of Jeremiah's hearers deplored what they regarded as his defeatist attitude and in order to silence the prophet they had him thrown into a muddy well. Rejected by his fellow-countrymen and apparently abandoned by the God in whose name he had spoken, the prophet owed his rescue from the well to the compassion of a Cushite (Ethiopian) courtier.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 39 :2-4, 18) is a hymn of thanksgiving for deliverance from a desperate situation such as that described in the first reading. Its tone is one of joyful trust in the Lord who rescues his own from their every trial.
