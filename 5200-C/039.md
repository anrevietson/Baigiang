# Jeremiah rejected

First Reading (Jer 38:4-6.8-10). For proclaiming the word of God Jeremiah meets with rejection and suffering. The period is about 586 BC. The armies of Babylon are preparing for an assault on Jerusalem, an assault which would mark the final collapse of Judah as a kingdom. Jeremiah, inspired by God, counsels surrender so that lives would be spared and destruction averted. The authorities reacted strongly. Such defeatist counsel was demoralising, an act of treason. The man would have to be silenced by abandoning him in a well of mud. Fortunately, through the intervention of a sympathetic Ethiopian, Jeremiah was released.
The passion of Jeremiah foreshadowed the passion of Jesus. Like Jesus, Jeremiah, by preaching the word of God, brought suffering and rejection upon himself. Like Jesus he was abandoned to his enemies.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps 39:2-4.18). A hymn of thanksgiving for deliverance from some desperate situation. The 'deadly pit' of verse 2 recalls the muddy well out of which Jeremiah was taken in the first reading. Coupled with the psalmist's prayer of thanksgiving is his spirit of trust in God.
