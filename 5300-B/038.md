# The priesthood of Christ

Second Reading (Heb. 5 :1-6). It is one of the major aims of the author of the epistle to show the superiority of Jesus' high priesthood to that of the priesthood of the Old Testament. But it was first of all necessary to show that Jesus did have the qualities required of a high priest. It is first of all necessary that the priest be sympathetically sensitive (metriopathein) to the condition of weakness which all men experience. Since Jesus was tempted in all things, as we are, he is compassionate towards our weakness (cf. 4 :15). The high priest could never assume a self-righteous posture in the fact of the weakness of those whom he represents in those matters for which he was responsible to God. Perhaps the writer was not unaware of the unworthy ministry of those high priests who had been given the office since the assassination of the high priest Onias III in 171 BC. The high priest was responsible for offering sacrifice 'for the person who commits an error, when he sins unwittingly' (Num. 15 :28), and since he himself was clothed (perikeitai) in weakness he offered sacrifice first of all for his own sins, and then for those of the community. Jesus, on the other hand, had no need to offer sacrifice for his own sins, since he was 'holy, blameless, unstained, separated from sinners (7 :26; cf. 7 :27). Since Jesus was subject to the temptations that all men suffer he was able to sympathise with the people, but it was because he never succumbed to those temptations that he was able to gain help and salvation for them. It was necessary for the high priest to be called to the office by God, as Aaron was (v.4): Aaron, the first high priest of Israel, was divinely appointed to his office (Exod. 28 :1 ff.) as were his successors (Num. 20 : 23 ff.). It was to sustain his argument that Jesus is the great high priest that the author advances evidence of Jesus' divine call. He does this by applying Ps. 2 :7 to Jesus, as he had already done by implication in 1:5, and he clinches the argument by quoting Ps. 110 :4 which mentions priesthood explicitly. The author, then, shows that Jesus has the two main qualities of high priesthood: a compassionate understanding of the weakness of the people for whom he intercedes, and a divine calling to the office. In subsequent chapters the author proves the superiority of Jesus' priesthood to that of Levi (chapter 7), and the superiority of Jesus' sacrifice to that of the Old Testament sacrifices (chapters 8 ff.).
