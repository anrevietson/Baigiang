# God so loved the world

The Gospel (John 3 :16-18). The theme in this short passage is that faith in Christ as the Son of God is Slavic for man. The Father has given his only Son to the world out of love. It is the Father therefore who initiates the work of man's redemption. John underlines the fact that Christ is the object of all the love of the Father when he refers to Jesus as his 'only' or 'only begotten' Son. The Father loves the world, then, because of his Son. Verse seventeen develops this idea. God's love is shown by sending his Son on a mission of salvation. It is not to judge, i.e., to condemn, that Jesus is sent. The mission of Christ has for its purpose to save man. One condition must be fulfilled on the part of man - he must believe in Jesus as the only begotten Son of God. He must accept Jesus totally for what he is. Failing this, man will be condemned. In fact, according to John, by refusing to believe in Christ as the Son of God, man is already condemned. The evangelist sees eternal life beginning on earth. Faith in Jesus is already salvation. The final judgement will be based on one's position vis-à-vis Jesus and one's acceptance or not of him as the Son of the Father.
In this passage, we see John's understanding of the part played by the Father and Son in man's life. The Father initiates the work of saving mankind out of love. He sends his only Son as' the Saviour. Later on in the Gospel, the evangelist speaks of the Spirit as the; Advocate who will remain with the disciples. Today's pass-age illustrates how God shares with man his love which he fully bestows' on his Son. In fact,. the mutual love of the Father and the Son is the Holy Spirit who is given to us.
