# God manifests himself

The first reading (Exodus 34 :4-6, 8-9) treats of God as he manifests himself to his chosen people. It is placed in the context of the renewal of the covenant after the incident of the worship of the golden calf. This action on Israel's part had ruptured the alliance between God and his people. Moses is at Sinai to receive for a second time the commandments of God. On the mountain, Yahweh appears in the form of a cloud, one of the Old Testament symbols for the divine presence.
God proclaims his name, Yahweh. For the Hebrews, to know the name of a person was not merely to have a word for distinguishing one person from another; it told something of the nature and function of the person in question. The divine name Yahweh, meaning 'I am who I am', or better, perhaps, 'I will be present to whom I will be present' is at the same time a revelation of Yahweh's intention to the present in human history as a Saviour and a veiling of the full reality which is the Godhead. The following words elaborate on the nature of Yahweh. He is 'God of tenderness and compassion, slow to anger, rich in kindness and fidelity'. Moses, taking the part of a mediator, begs God to stay with his people and accompany them on their journey despite their faults and sinfulness.
At this point of revelation, it is the unity and unique character of God which is stressed and the fact that there was a special covenant relationship between Yahweh and Israel. We have to wait f6r the incarnation before the triune character of God is revealed.
