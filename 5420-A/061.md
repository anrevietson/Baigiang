# The God of love and peace

Second Reading (2 Cor 13:11-13). As is clear from today's first reading, the dominant characteristic of Israel's Lord is that he is a God who wants to be, who is, "with" his people. For his part, Paul reminds Christians that this "God of love and peace" will be with them in so far as they are united and live in peace with one another. And, at the very end of his letter, he refers to the "three" with whom Christians are intimately related: the Lord Jesus Christ, God, the Holy Spirit. With each of the "three" is associated a "divine" characteristic: grace, love, fellowship. Paul is here expressing the essential "threeness" of God and the involvement of the Christian in the divine life. It is by the grace of "free gift", revealed in Jesus, that we are saved; this salvation consists in the union with God (the Father) that is love; and it is the Holy Spirit who communicates the divine gifts to us, empowering us to lead the divine life. The "three" are indeed spoken of as persons, although less as what they are in themselves than as what they are to us: Lord, God (Father) and Holy Spirit, that is, the Leader, the Source or Origin, the Communicator.
