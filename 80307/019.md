# The risen Messiah

Scripture taught that the Messiah whom rise from the dead (Ps 16:9-10), and the early Church insisted that without the resurrection their faith was empty (1 Cor 15:14). Our readings today remind us of the centrality of this belief and at the same time focus on the nature of faith. For the author of our gospel reading, faith is not based on having seen miracles, even though they can support our faith (Jn 3:2); nor on the persuasiveness of Jesus' preaching, for some listened and still did not believe (Jn 2:23-25), nor on verifiable evidence, even if it can be found (Jn 20:24-29). Believing is an active commitment of receiving and knowing Jesus (Jn 1:12; 10:38) and acknowledging him as the one sent by the Father (Jn 3:16-18), cherishing his Word (Jn 8:31; 15:7) and being time to his Word (Jn 8:51). Believing is a gift of the Father (Jn 6:3740) who draws individuals to Jesus (Jn 6:44.65) and helps them persevere in their commitment (Jn 10:29). Believing is a personal choice to respond in obedience and love to the Lord's challenge (Jn 3:36; 14:15,21,23) and in witnessing to the Word of the Lord (Jn 15:27; 20:21). Believing is central to John and his gospel contains only one beatitude: 'Blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe' (20:29).
Today's gospel reminds us that faith is not a human response, like that of Mary of Magdala, nor is it based on proofs, but it is an active commitment without evidence or proof, other than the tradition of witnesses from the early Church. Thus, Cornelius, the first representative of the Gentile Church, accepts the words of the witnesses.
Today's celebration is filled with enthusiasm, praise, and rejoicing. The responsorial psalm, the Easter alleluia, and the sequence set the tone for the whole liturgy. The Lord's resurrection reminds us of the intense interest that God has in human history. Not a distant God but involved in world history, sending the Son to die for us and to raise us up with him. Brought back to true life with Christ (Col 3:1), let us dedicate ourselves individually and as a parish to reaffirm our faith and find ways to make it visible in our parish, family, and social life.
