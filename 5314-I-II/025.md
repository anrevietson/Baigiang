# We belong to the Lord

First Reading (Rom. 14:7-12). In 14:1-15 :13 Paul affords a particular example of this brotherly love, an example which doubtless reflects the composition of the Roman church: Christians of both Jewish and Gentile origin, and the inevitable clash of mentalities which this involved. Here it would appear to be basically a question of the Jewish alimentary laws which were revered even by Jewish converts, while despised by their Gentile counterparts. Although the whole of Paul's doctrinal argument has tended towards making such regulations irrelevant and obsolete, he still exhorts the pagan sector of the Roman Church in particular to respect the innate scruples of their Jewish neighbours and not to allow mere differences of custom to form a barrier between them. For no Christian lives for himself alone but for others, submitting to the idiosyncrasies of others, in imitation of Christ who assumed the total human situation for others.
