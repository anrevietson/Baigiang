# Paul's joy in prison

Second Reading (Phil 4:4-7). Despite the fact that Paul writes this letter from prison, his joy as he writes is very much in evidence. He has learned to be content whatever the circumstances because he knows he can do everything through the risen Lord who gives him strength (4:13). History is the fruit of the risen Lord's presence in his life. As he has learned to rejoice in his affliction, he calls on the Philippians to rejoice despite the fact that they are going through the same struggle as Paul (1:30). The Lord's return is near, when he will complete the good work he has begun in their lives. In the meantime, joy rather than deep anxiety should characterise their waiting. They can rejoice as a people about whom God cares, and who, therefore, can approach God with confidence, asking for his help when they need it. Such is to be their confidence in God that even their prayer of petition should be shot through with thanksgiving. Those who relate to God in this way will experience the very peace of God himself, a peace beyond human understanding.
