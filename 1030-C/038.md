# The ethics of the Baptist's preaching

Gospel (Lk 3:10-18). Luke goes beyond the other evangelists in giving us the ethical dimension of the preaching of John. This is the good fruit which every tree is expected to produce (3:9). John advocates care for the needy and the sharing of resources, an ideal realised, according to Luke, in the life of the early Church (Acts 2:4445). The professional conduct of the tax collectors and the soldier is to be marked by honesty, equity, the avoidance of intimidation. In the secondary part of the reading, John clearly distinguishes himself from Jesus. The latter is more powerful than John and his baptism, into the Holy Spirit and with fire, is more powerful than John's water baptism. John also identifies Jesus as a win ower, a judge who sorts out wheat from chaff. This latter element of John's portrait of Jesus is not fully in keeping with Luke's presentation of Jesus in the Gospel as the Saviour of the lost. In this context, we can understand John's later question from his prison addressed to Jesus: 'Are you the one who was to come or should we expect someone else?' (7:20).
