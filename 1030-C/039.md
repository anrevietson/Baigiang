# Gaudete Sunday

The joy of Advent rings forth in this Sunday's Liturgy. We call it Gaudete 'Rejoice' Sunday. It is joy in God, our Father, who calls us to a future with him. He asks us in this, our earthly life, to follow the way of his Son, Jesus. John the Baptist, the prophet of Advent, tells us in today's Gospel, how we can do this: 'If anyone has two coats', he says, 'he must share with the person who has none, and the one with something to eat must do the same'. We get many opportunities before Christmas of helping the deprived of our country and world. We should avail of them.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We begin our Mass by calling to mind for a moment in silence our failures to share where we found need in our daily lives.
Lord Jesus, you brought good news to the poor. Lord, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you gave sight to the blind. Christ, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you let the oppressed go free, Lord have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Zeph 3:14-18): Joy and renewal are the gifts of God to those who are open to his will.
Second Reading (Phil 4:4-7). Followers of Jesus are marked with the gifts of joy and gentleness, with a spirit of thanksgiving which leads to inner peace.
Gospel (Lk 3:10-18): John the Baptist preaches a message of social justice, and prepares us for the coming of the Messiah, who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As we prepare to celebrate the coming of Christ among us, we bring the needs of our Church, our World, and ourselves to our Father God, Creator and Saviour.
Reader(s): For all who follow the healing role of Jesus Christ in our society, especially for doctors, nurses, social workers, helpers. May their loving kindness be rewarded by a continuing joy and contentment in their lives.
For the deprived of our world, especially the hungry, the homeless, the elderly who are lonely. May they experience the mercy of God through the goodness of other people who serve their needs.
For the deprived of our world, especially the hungry, the homeless, elderly who are lonely. May they experience the mercy of God through the goodness of other people who serve their needs.
For those who are bereaved among us, and particularly those who have lost their loved ones in the course of the past year. May they receive the gift of Christmas peace through the concern and understanding of those who grieve with them.
For all those who seek employment in our country. May they be enabled to preserve their human dignity at this present difficult time, and to find suitable work in the future.
For the grace of final perseverance. May all of us gathered in prayer at this Advent Mass remain steadfast to the faith we have received, constant in our hope of heavenly life, and fervent in following the way of divine love.
President: God, our Father, we present all our needs to you through the intercession of Mary, who stood at the wood of the manger, and again at the wood of the cross, and is now mother of the Church, and pray that, attentive to the Word of God, as she always was, your will may be done through each one of us. Enabled by the Holy Spirit, we make our prayer through Jesus Christ, our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
The Lord, our God, is in our midst. He exults with joy over us. Let us turn to him in the prayer that Jesus, his Son, taught us to say.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Let us exchange with one another that peace of God, which is so much greater than we can understand, which we have received in Christ Jesus.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
You will find that charity is a heavy burden to carry, heavier than the kettle of soup and the basket of bread. But you must keep your gentleness and your smile. Giving soup and bread is not all that the rich can do. The poor are your masters, terribly sensitive, exacting as you will see. But the uglier and dirtier they are, the more unjust and bitter, the more you must give them your love. It is only because of your love - only your love -that the poor will forgive you the bread you give them.
Saint Vincent de Paul
