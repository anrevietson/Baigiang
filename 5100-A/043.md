# Mercy, not sacrifice

First Reading (Hos. 6 :3-6). Hosea prophesied in the kingdom of the north (Israel) during the period 750-730 BC, a time which was marked by increasing insistence on material prosperity and political intrigue. As Amos before him, he inveighed vehemently against the' se two tendencies, especially against the former which involved the oppression of the poor and the miscarriage of justice. In the present passage, after describing an ephemeral conversion of the people in view of Yahweh's judgement (v. 3), he continues to upbraid them for their superficially and formalism. The basic requirement for Yahweh's people was hesed or covenant love. This was essentially faithfulness to Yahweh through a the profound obedience to the torah as its demands were made increasingly explicit through the preaching of the prophets. Yahweh was a God of hesed, of faithfulness to his word, and this was all that he asked of hi5 people. Such a love was comparable to the fidelity expected of a wife a towards her husband in marriage. Hence the comparison of Israel to an unfaithful wife and Yahweh to a faithful, loving and forgiving husband is a dominant motif in Hosea. This also explains why the love of Yahweh is paralleled by the 'knowledge' of Yahweh - the absence of which is responsible for all Israel's ills. 'To know' in the biblical sense is not to have' more intellectual acquaintance' but to have a living experience of a reality, in the case of a person to be 'fully united with that person.
In the absence of this love and knowledge, the whole of Israel's cultic system was useless; worse, the source of even greater condemnation. The prophet's word judges, condemns, punishes in so far' as it interprets historical events, say, the invasion of the Assyrian army, as the right hand of God's wrath.
