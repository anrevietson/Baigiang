# The faith of Abraham

Second Reading (Rom. 4 :18-25). In the whole of chapter four, Paul presents the example of Abraham, the 'father' of the chosen people, in order to show that his thesis of 'justification 'by faith far from running counter to the evidence of the Old Testament actually fulfils it.
Abraham was the type of all those who are 'justified' and he was justified, well before the introduction of the law into Israel's history, by his faith. It follows that it is believers who are the true sons of Abraham. Paul lays great stress, in rabbinical, style, upon the phrase of Genesis 15 :6 ' . . it, was reckoned to him as righteousness', since he wishes to bring out the completely gratuitous character of man's righteousness before God. There is nothing that a man' can do to earn righteousness. He can only accept it as a pure gift of God' made in Christ. To believe is to accept actively this gift of God, to open oneself, as it were, to the action of God through Christ.
In the light a of the Christ-event, Paul sees Abraham's faith as the type not only of all believers in general, but also of the Christian in particular. Abraham believed in God who was able to bring life out of death (the 'death' of his old age, his wife's barren womb and, eventually, Isaac's sacrificial altar). The Christian is justified in believing in God's action in the resurrection of Christ. The resurrection is the object both of Abraham's faith and of the Christian's.
