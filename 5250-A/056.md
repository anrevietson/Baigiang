# God's thoughts are not our thoughts

First Reading (Is 55:6-9). Together with verses 10-11 this passage is the conclusion of Second Isaiah's preaching. Around the middle of the sixth century B.C. this anonymous prophet seeks to energize an exile audience that entertains doubts about their God's capacity to begin anew. Even granted Yahweh's ability to start over, this audience wonders whether their God has the intention to do so (see Is 40:12-31). In this situation the prophet exhorts the exiles to seek Yahweh and to call upon him.
'Thoughts' and 'ways' are the key words in this passage. The thoughts and ways of Israel lack faith in Yahweh and are clearly in contrast to those of Yahweh. 'As high as the heavens' or 'as the heavens are higher' (v.9) is an apt expression for the chasm separating divine and human activities. The thoughts and ways of God are calculated to provide confidence. At the same time the prophet insists that Israel must respect Yahweh's freedom to surprise. Instead of placing limitations on that freedom, Israel should be prepared for the surprises emanating from that freedom.
