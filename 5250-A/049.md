# To live is Christ

Second Reading (Phil. 1:20-24, 27). The city of Philippi in northern Greece, founded by the father of Alexander the Great, was the site of the first Christian church in Europe. St Paul founded the Church there during his second missionary journey, about AD 50. There seems to have been a special bond between Paul and the Christians of Philippi, the majority of whom were converts from paganism. His letter to them is the Warmest and most intimate we have from his pen.
When he wrote this letter he was in prison and his future was quite uncertain. He does not yet know whether his imprisonment will end in death or liberation. But he is not worried. He belongs entirely to Christ arid Christ will be honoured whether Paul lives or dies. His death will honour Christ, for he freely accepts it for Christ's sake; if he is set free he will continue to spread the name and the Gospel of Christ. From his own personal point of view, he would prefer to die in order to be with Christ. For him, to die would mean a gain of life, not a loss. However, if there is still a task to be done on this earth, for the sake of the Philippians he is quite willing to stay 'alive in this body' for a while.
To many quite good Christians, St Paul's words 'To live is Christ' sound a little extravagant. If they sound extravagant to us, is it not because our approach to Christ, our commitment to Christ, is a half-hearted, niggardly thing? A kind of insurance. When Paul says 'To live is Christ' he means life would be dull, flat, boring, if we had not Christ to imitate, to talk about, to be about us like an atmosphere in which we live and move and have our being.
His last word to the Philippians is important for us too: no matter what happens either to Paul or to themselves, let them live lives worthy of the faith they profess.
