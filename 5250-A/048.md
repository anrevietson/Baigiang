# Seek the Lord while he may be found

First Reading (Is. 55:6-9). The background of this chapter is the period near the end of the Babylonian exile, sometime after the middle of the sixth century BC. The chapter is the work of the prophet who has come to be known as Second Isaiah, the herald of consolation and messianic glory. For him the poor and the lowly, rather than the Davidic family, will have the most prominent places in the kingdom of God which is coming.
In today's extract, the prophet insists that Israel (and consequently we) must seek the Lord', which means here, as often in the Bible, turning to God with humility and a real sense of need of him. Underlying the passage are some indications of God's paradoxical way of dealing with us: God is transcendent, yet he is close enough to help us; man is helpless, yet he is expected to act energetically in seeking the Lord; the ways of the Lord are exalted, yet man is required to reach for that way of life.
The chapter forms a natural sequel to that of the new Zion (chapter 54). Having described the glorious condition of the Zion of the future, Yahweh now turns to the exiles and invites them to become partakers in the blessings of the new age. These blessings are described under the figure of food and drink (vv. 1-2) and as the fulfilment of the promises made long ago to David (vv. 3-5). To share them, however, the exiles must be converted. Yahweh is ready to pardon them because he has a purpose to achieve and the power to achieve it.
Responsorial Psalm. When we reflect on what God has done for us, and remember that our lives are borne along on a continuing stream of God's love, we are constrained to praise this loving God of ours.
