# The radical power of God to give

Today's celebration emphasizes the radical power of God to give. As we reflect on the First Reading and the Gospel, we realize that God's generosity absolutely surpasses our purely human calculation. As we gather for worship this Sunday, we are urged to believe in a God of surprises and to imitate this God in our everyday living.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must admit that we have not always believed in a God who surprises us with gifts beyond our capacity to imagine. Aware of our shortcomings, we ask for our God's forgiveness.
You call us to trust in God's ability to change despair into joy. Lord, have mercy.
You turn even our sinfulness into the start of a new life. Christ, have mercy.
You call us to reach out to others. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 55:6-9). This anonymous prophet of the exile addresses an audience that doubts God's capacity to begin anew. He reminds them that God's ways and thoughts utterly surpass purely human calculations.
Second Reading (Phil 1:20-24,27). Faced with the possibility of death, Paul reflects on the pros and cons of his absence, particularly its implications for his community in Philippi.
Gospel (Mt 20:l-~6). In his parable of the workers in the vineyard Matthew insists that the believer must allow God to order the world in God's own way. Because Jesus is good, he has the right to reward others on the basis of that goodness.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware that God's ability to surprise us is also a challenge to effect surprises for others, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all Christian civil and religious leaders. May the example of God's gift-giving be their model in ministering to the needs of their charges.
We pray for all the members of our local faith community. May Jesus, God's greatest gift, be the inspiration for our providing gifts for one another.
We pray for all those, both living and dead, who always sought to change our despair into hope. May their memory give us the energy and willingness to convert pain into joy for our sisters and brothers.
We pray for all those who have never known happiness and peace. May our capacity to surprise become the springboard for their journey from frustration to confidence.
President: Compassionate and loving Giver of gifts, we acknowledge your generosity in history and in our daily lives. Energise us through the example of your Son to become the bearers of gifts and surprises for our world. Enable us to offer others the vision of concern and caring. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we ask God that his Kingdom, his reign of providing for others, become real. In reciting this prayer we implicitly commit ourselves to involvement in this task. To bring about that Kingdom, we must dare to make our gift-giving contagious to the point of surprise.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Through this symbol we announce that we are prepared to make Christ's peace a reality for our sisters and brothers. The absence of peace is hatred, despair, and frustration. If this symbol is to be genuine, we must exercise our capacity to surprise our sisters and brothers so that love, hope, and confidence are the result.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Eucharist is the perfect example of a God of surprises at work. In Eucharist we recall the passion and death of Jesus but press forward quickly to announce the resurrection. It is this model of surprise that must impact our day-to -day living. Eucharist powerfully reminds us that we are empowered to help others from the pain of Good Friday to the joy of Easter Sunday
