# The mystery of growth

Today's celebration focuses on the mystery of growth and God's capacity to be a giver of gifts. We are forcefully reminded that we must allow God to surprise us. In turn, we are challenged to place less emphasis on our human calculations and control and more stress on God's graciousness and concern. God's grace and human growth are not opposites.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must acknowledge that we have often planned our lives without making our God a vital partner in our deliberations. aware of these failures, we humbly approach both God and community, seeking forgiveness.
You call us to be mindful of the needs of sisters and brothers: Lord, have mercy.
You call us to be mindful of the gifts of our sisters and brothers and the grace of our God: Christ, have mercy.
You call us to recognize the image of our God in the needs of community: Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Ezek 17:22-24). The author offers a message of hope to a despondent community. God will restore the dynasty of David and thus create something out of nothing.
Second Reading (2 Cor 5:& 10). Paul insists that the period of separation from the Lord is one of faith, not sight. For Paul the most important thing is to do God's will.
Gospel (Mk 4:26-34). Mark speaks of surprises. One cannot thwart the seed growing by itself. Similarly the mustard seed, despite its smallness, experiences incredible growth.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Realizing that we must effect surprises for others and thus imitate our God, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all religious and civil leaders. May they recognize the needs of their people and respond in unexpected generosity.
We pray for the members of our local faith community. May we imitate our God by turning despair into hope and dejection into new life.
We pray for all those, both living and dead, who reflected our God of surprises by changing our darkness into light.
We pray for all those who depend solely on their own resources and manifest no concern for others. May our manner of life move them to reach beyond their isolated world and embrace hurting sisters and brothers.
President: Gracious and loving God, we acknowledge you as the giver of gifts and architect of surprises. We reflect especially on your transformation on Easter Sunday when the dead Jesus became our risen Lord. Enable us to imitate your capacity to give in all the situations of our daily lives. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer, among other things, we speak of both God's forgiveness and our forgiveness. We recognize that it is relatively easy to ask God for pardon and then anticipate it. however, we also realize that it can be relatively difficult for us to pardon those who have hurt us. In this prayer we are reminded that to offer reconciliation is to imitate a God of surprises

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
The sign of peace we exchange with sisters and brothers becomes genuine only when we take it beyond the confines of our church into the realities of everyday life. To wish someone peace is to be willing to create peace. To that extent our sign of peace is the recognition of our obligation to effect surprises for others.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
In our celebration of Eucharist we accept the self-giving of Jesus in death and the Father's transformation of death into the new life of the resurrection. Eucharist reminds us that we must imitate the style of our God by effecting surprises that change despair and dejection into hope and new life for our sisters and brothers. Ultimately Eucharist is the sacrament of surprises, both divine and human.
