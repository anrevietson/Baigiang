# The cedar of Lebanon

First Reading (Ezek 17:22-24). Today's passage is part of a larger structure, containing a mixture of allegory and concrete historical references. Verses 1-10 are an allegory depicting the debacle of the late seventh century and early sixth century B.C. Nebuchadrezzar, the Babylonian king, will destroy the southern Kingdom of Judah. Verses 11-21 interpret this allegory. Babylon will be God's instrument for punishing Judah and its king, Zedekiah. Finally verses 22-24 are a further allegory that provides hope in the face of overwhelming defeat. It announces the restoration of God's people under a King from the line of David. As in Ezekiel 34:23-24, the exact manner of restoration is not spelled out.
The crest of the cedar (see also Ezek 17:4; Jer 22:6,23) is a future King from the house of David. the Lord will restore the Davidic dynasty in the land of Israel where it will prosper. As a result, the once rejected dynasty will take on its proper royal stature, providing protection to vassal nations ('the shade of its boughs'). The Lord's policy of reversal (high/low, green/ withered) will indeed be recognized. The trees of the field, i.e., the kings of the surrounding nations, will be witnesses to the Lord's saving action. Since the Lord's word cannot but be fulfilled (see Num 23:19), the promise will become a reality.
