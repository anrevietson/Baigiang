# The cedar of Lebanon

First Reading (Ezek 17:22-24). The prophet Ezekiel writes in a vivid and colourful way, binding his book together by use of prophetic words and visions. Dividing the work into two major sections enables the reader to focus on Ezekiel the prophet (chapters 1-39) and Ezekiel the priest (chapters 40-48). Today's reading, part of a larger section of condemnations of Jerusalem (chapters 4-24) offers hope in God's promise to Israel.
Within Ezekiel 17 is the literary use of fable and allegory with the eagle representing the king of Babylon and the cedar the king of Israel. The tender shoot becomes the symbol of the messianic king from the line of David. Ezekiel presents the allegory in verses 1-10, interprets it in verses 11-21, and follows it with the promise we read in this liturgy.
God promises growth, restoration and fruitfulness to his people. The imagery of 'every kind of bird', reminiscent of the story of the flood, will be used by Mark in the gospel parable when again the branches of the tree will provide shelter and shade. The short reading ends with a prophetic word 'I, the Lord, have spoken'; he will accomplish his word. Growth and restoration is the promise of Yahweh for the community, and, in faith, Israel knows that such renewal is the work of the Lord.
