# Israel's humble beginnings

First Reading (Deut. 26 :4-10). Israel's beginnings were humble. Her traditions traced her origins back to Abraham and to his son and grandson, Isaac and Jacob. Her real origins as a people stem from Jacob who for this reason had his name changed to Israel (cf. Gen. 32 :28). The Bible calls him a wandering Aramaean by reason of his journey to Syria and his descent into Egypt. With his children and grandchildren, few in number - seventy persons in all (Ex. 1:5) - he entered Egypt when his life was threatened with destruction through famine. But Jacob was no longer an individual: he was Israel, the nation. (Note how Deuteronomy 26 :5 passes from the individual Jacob to the nation Israel.) And from this small group came forth the nation that would possess Canaan, the land of promise. Israel in her liturgy tried to combat the temptation ever present to her people of turning from the true God Yahweh to the local Baals. So when the Jews went on pilgrimage with the first fruits of their farms (probably at Pentecost) they were given a credo, a profession of faith, to recite. It is that found in today's reading. This creed is really a summary of her early history, from the patriarchs to the first possession of the land promised to Abraham. The readings of the following Sundays will spell out individual items of the credo in greater detail. We may note the concrete form this profession of faith takes. It looks on God as the God who acts, God whose fidelity to the promises is seen in history.
