# The trial of Jesus and of Israel

Gospel (Lk 4:1 - 13). The temptations of Jesus in this story are paralleled to the trial of Israel, the son of God in the foundation days of its history. Jesus appears as a great prophetic figure 'filled with the Holy Spirit'. His desert experience resembles that of the nation in earlier times. He is put to the test three times and each time he proves his loyalty as Son of God. He was tempted to walk away from the plan and mission given to him by his Father, but he refused to comply with Satan's wishes. When prompted to satisfy personal desire he reminded the tempter, in the words of Deuteronomy, that there is more to life than physical sustenance. God's word nourishes a quality of life.
Jesus was also enticed to experience the thrill of imperial power by worshipping the false values embodied by the tempter. Jesus refused to
be diverted from the God to whom he was committed and the plan for life he had offered his people.
Luke locates the final temptation in Jerusalem because of his interest in that city. Jesus was tempted to take part in a piece of dramatic sensationalism by thrusting himself from the temple parapet. Again he declines. He was not prepared to put God to the test by calling for a remarkable intervention to rescue him.
The passage concludes with the reminder that the power of evil would make one final effort, in the shadow of the cross, to shake Jesus' faith in his Father and divert him from fulfilling God's purpose.
