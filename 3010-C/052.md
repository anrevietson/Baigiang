# Confessing Jesus as Lord

Second Reading (Rom. 10:8-13). In his credo, given in the first reading, the Jew professed belief in the God who acts, in the Lord of history who called his people into being and guided its destinies towards its ultimate goal which was to be none other than the coming of his Son and the call of the non-Jewish world into the one true faith. Such a faith in the living God could look to the past and find there evidence of God's fidelity. It was, however, a faith that could also raise its gaze to the future when fulfilment of the promises would come. Of its nature faith is open to God's future intervention and thus issues in hope. The tragedy of Israel in Christ's day was that its faith was no longer open in this sense. Having made of its own human traditions the yardstick for the relevance of God's word Israel was not prepared to see God speaking again to his people through Christ. As St Paul could say of his own people in the passage immediately preceding that read today:
'To their zeal for God I can testify; but it is an ill-informed zeal. For they ignore God's way of righteousness, and try to set up their own, and therefore they have not submitted to God's righteousness. 'God's way of righteousness' was acceptance of redemption through faith in Christ This is what is implied in the Christian credo which in its simplest form, given in today's reading, ran: 'Jesus is Lord'; that is, Jesus is the risen Saviour, the one who died for our sins and was raised up for our justification. To profess Jesus as Lord is to profess faith in the Trinity, in the Father who raised him from the dead and in the Spirit which as Lord, seated at God's right hand, he has sent on his Church. That this faith be genuine it implies acceptance of what Christ's death and resurrection mean:
death to sin and a life in accord with Christ's law. It implies acceptance of the divine origin and nature of Christ's Church, of which he is head and Lord. It is a faith accessible to all who have heard the preaching of the gospel, without distinction of persons, of Jew or non-Jew, of rich or poor. When Paul here speaks of confessing with the lips and professing faith in the heart he does so to balance his phrases. No clear distinction can be drawn between the two.
