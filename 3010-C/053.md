# The temptations of Jesus

The Gospel (Luke 4:1-13). At his baptism, recounted immediately before the narrative of the temptations, Jesus had been proclaimed Son of God by a heavenly voice: 'You are my beloved Son; with you I am well pleased.' The account of the temptations will make clear what this honour meant for Jesus. Although 'his state was divine he did not cling to his equality with God but emptied himself to assume the condition of a slave, and became as men are ' (Phil. 2:6-7). Satan tempted him to profit from his divine state; Jesus' replies show that for him Son of God implied being Son of Man, truly human, accepting in obedience God's plan of salvation which was, and is, that of the cross. As man Jesus hungered; he was tempted to avail of his divine state to give himself to eat miraculously. Satan arrogantly claimed lordship of creation which he promised to Jesus in exchange for his allegiance. Jesus will become Lord and dethrone Satan from his usurped position, but only in the manner willed by the Father - through the cross. The Jews looked for miracles and many of them expected their Messiah to appear suddenly and miraculously. Jesus is tempted to avail of his divine status and have God's special providence manifested in a special manner towards him. He even cites scripture for his purpose. The reply is that God is not to be tempted, even under the guise of reverence for the words of scripture.
These temptations of Christ were internal, in his imagination. (We are not to presume that Satan lifted him bodily on high, or on to the pinnacle of the temple) They were, however, real temptations and all three had the same purpose: to turn Christ away from God's plan of redemption which was that his Son should save the world through trials, sufferings and finally the cross. The three he experienced in the desert were of the kind he would meet again during his ministry. His own relatives will ask him to work his miracles publicly and show himself to the world (John 7:4). Peter, prince of the apostles, would have him turn aside from the cross and is sternly rebuked by Christ for acting as Satan (Mt. 16:23). The crowds will attempt to make him king (John 6:15) and ask him to work miracles to satisfy their curiosity (Mark 8:11-12).
In each of his three replies to Satan Jesus cites texts from the book of Deuteronomy. They take us back to Israel's forty years ' sojourn in the desert at the beginnings of her history as a nation. Then she, metaphorically called God's son, was tempted and put to the test. She succumbed and in turn tempted the Lord. Now at this new beginning of sacred history Jesus, the true Son of God and the true Israel, is tempted and remains faithful. He resists Satan who, Luke tells us, left him until an opportune time. All Christ's life will be an onslaught on the kingdom of Satan who will return to the attack at Christ's passion which was the hour of the power of darkness (Luke 22:53). 'The prince of this world comes . . . With Christ's death, however, a new age had begun and the power of sin and of Satan was broken: 'Now is the prince of this world (i.e. Satan) cast down ...' (John 12:31).
Christ's temptations were real and were willed by God to show us the stark reality of the incarnation. He was 'tempted in every respect as we are, yet without sinning' (Heb. 4:15). He redeemed us as one of our race. God saw it fitting that the Son who leads men to salvation should be made perfect through suffering (cf. Heb. 2:10). Christ learned from his sufferings what obedience to God's will means for men 'and being made perfect he became a source of eternal life to all who obey him' (Heb. 5:8). The temptations of Christ are the temptations of Christians of all ages: the temptation to seek one's own glory even in religious matters, to seek the easy way and turn aside from suffering, the temptation to forget that the source of Christian life is to be found in the death and resurrection of Christ. Christ redeemed mankind as the Suffering Servant, as Son of Man, by taking human nature on himself and uniting himself fully with his fellow-men. Man in his turn is redeemed by uniting himself with Christ, in his death and resurrection, by resisting sin and the temptations to it that assail him on many sides. It is these truths that the Church wishes to keep before our minds during the lenten and paschal seasons.
