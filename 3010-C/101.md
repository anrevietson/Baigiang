# The offering of first fruits

First Reading (Deut 26:4-10). In this scene the worshipper presents first fruits of the harvest to God. Linked to the ceremony of tithing is an important profession of faith which recalls the experience of the people of Israel and the great deeds of God which were at the heart of their history of salvation. The story goes back to Abraham, the first representative of God's people, the exemplar of all believers, the first person to take God at his word and entrust his whole life to him.
Abraham as a wandering nomad experienced a deprived and precarious existence. His shepherd wanderings brought him to Egypt where his descendants became a great people. In accord with Deuteronomic theology, they were oppressed, called on Yahweh, and were rescued by a compassionate and powerful God. The final crowning experience of salvation was possession of the promised land. God was good; he was true to his promises. The people, in a response of thanksgiving, ritually offer the first fruits of the soil back to him.
