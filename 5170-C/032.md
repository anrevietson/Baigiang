# Abraham intercedes for Sodom

First reading (Gen. 18:20-32). The scene described in this reading took place on the heights near Hebron which overlooked the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah. God who is addressed by Abraham as 'the judge of the whole earth' is depicted as deciding to go down to the cities to make enquiries about complaints that he had heard concerning their sinfulness. Abraham has a feeling that the result of the divine investigation will be condemnatory, and he intervenes on behalf of the guilty cities with a boldness that surprises us. His questions show that he is grappling with the problem of divine retribution. Perplexed by the question of what standard God uses in judging the cities, he asks if the innocence of even a small number is so pleasing to the divine judge as to merit the pardon of the wicked. The patriarch continues to bargain about the number of just that would suffice to save the wicked until he reached a mission beyond which he did not dare to go. He had established that God's mercy exceeds his justice, but he knew that a moment comes when the supreme judge has to punish (cf. Jer. 16:2-13), even though he does so reluctantly (cf. Hos. 11:8-9).
This story must be understood in the light of primitive Israelite religion which was essentially a religion of the group. Although each individual was considered responsible for his own actions, these actions had a significance for the community. On the other hand the individual's existence was inseparable from that of the family, clan, or nation, and he could not escape from the destiny of the tribe or community to which he belonged. The principle of individual retribution found expression at a much more developed stage of Israel's religion (cf. Deut. 7:10; Ezek. 18).
