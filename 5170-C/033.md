# The Gospel of prayer

the Gospel (Luke 11:1-13). The Third Gospel might well be called the Gospel of Prayer for it is clear that for Luke prayer was an important factor in the life of Jesus (cf. e.g. 3:21; 5:16; 6:12; 9:29) and an essential part of Christian life. Today's Gospel reading which is a short treatise on prayer is composed of several distinct sections. The first two verses set the scene for our Lord's discourse on prayer and probably give the circumstances in which he spoke the 'Our Father'. It would he reasonable for the apostles to ask the master to teach them how to pray when they saw him in the act of praying. The second section of our reading gives Luke's recording of the model of prayer which Jesus gave to his followers. This is followed by two parables which teach the effectiveness of persevering prayer.
There are several differences between Luke's version of the 'Our Father' and that given in Matthew 6:9-13. It is generally agreed that Luke who only gives five petitions (omitting Matthew's third petition) remains closer to the number of petitions actually spoken by our Lord than Matthew who records six petitions. The difference between the two texts probably reflects different liturgical traditions. Still another liturgical tradition may be represented in early versions which conclude with the doxology 'For yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory for ever. Amen.' Many of the phrases in the 'Our Father' are borrowed from Jewish prayers but they now contain a note of familiarity with God which would not be known to a Jew. The first two petitions in Luke's versa ion of the prayer (the first three in Matthew's) form three different formulations of the one petition. They ask that God might fully manifest his own holiness and power by finally establishing his kingdom and by bringing to perfection his plan of salvation. In asking for our daily bread in Luke's third petition we pray that God may provide us with our daily sustenance. In his fourth petition we are reminded of our position as sinners and of our continued need for divine forgiveness. The final petition may be understood as a prayer for deliverance in the time of the final struggle between God and Satan that will herald the end of the world. The 'Our Father' is truly a model of Christian prayer for it take's into consideration both the glory of God and the needs of man. It is a prayer of the community that does not ignore the needs of the individual. It expresses man s confidence in a loving Father who can be trusted to provide for our genuine need.
