# The benefits of baptism

Second Reading (Col. 2:12-14). The benefits brought by Christ can be acquired by each individual in Christian baptism. The imagery used by Paul seems to imply baptism by immersion which symbolised the Christian's sharing in the death, burial and resurrection of Christ. Those who had been dead because they were sinners 'die' to their sins because they go through the experience of dying with Christ. They renounce sin and can have nothing more to do with it (cf. Rom. 6:2). Those who share in the death of Christ are in a privileged position, for that death annulled all the legal codes that forbade sin without giving the necessary help to avoid transgression. But baptism is not simply something negative. It does not merely wash away sins (cf. Eph. 5:26; Tit. 3:5). It identifies the baptised with Christ's resurrection so that he begins to live a new life. Resurrection with Christ is here described as having taken place for each Christian. In Romans 6:1-11 the Christian's share in Christ's resurrection is described as something that will take place in the future (verses five and eight) although the present effect of the new life is also brought out (verses four and eleven). Of course the external rite of baptism has no value unless it is an expression of belief in the power of God who can raise the baptised to a new life. We must also guard against a mechanical understanding of baptism which gives the idea that reception of the sacrament assures one of salvation. The new life received at Christian initiation must be developed through a lifelong struggle against all that threatens it.
