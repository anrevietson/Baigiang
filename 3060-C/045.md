# The Servant's ear opened

First Reading (1s 50:4-7). This passage comes from the second section of the book of Isaiah (chapters 40-55), written during the exile and known as the Book of Consolation. This part of Isiah contains four literary and theological masterpieces, the four songs of the suffering servant of the Lord (Is 42:1-7; 49:1-7; 50:4-9; 52:13-53:12). These poems are written in the first person and describe the sufferings of a special servant of God (Is 50:10). The servant could be the prophet or the people of Israel but refers to himself in the song as a disciple (1s 50:4).
The mysterious figure of the servant suffers on behalf of others and becomes an example of the kind of mission Jesus accepts. The early Church very soon identified Jesus as the servant prophetically referred to in these songs. The first describes his call, the second his mission, the third the kind of life that he lives, and the fourth the bitter suffering he endures. The third song picks up ideas from the second and anticipates the fourth's emphasis on suffering. Accepting his mission with confidence in God the servant overcomes the hardships of his mission through his daily trusting relationship with the Lord. The Church considers that the song portrays Jesus as the perfect disciple, always listening to, and proclaiming to others, the word of God (v.4). He also accepts the consequences of his commitment to discipleship by enduring the sufferings that accompany his ministry (vv. 5-6), always aware of God's supportive presence (v 7).
