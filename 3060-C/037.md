# Christ emptied himself

Second Reading (Phil 2:6-11). The letter to the Philippians constitutes the apostle's last will and testament. In confronting some of the problems in the Philippian Church Paul presents Christ as an example for therefore community life. Our passage is a hymn about Christ which Paul may not have written but found appropriate enough to include in his letter. Paul calls his community to a unity built on humility and urges them to discern the truth in the preaching of others who have come to Philippi in Paul's absence. He defends his ideas by presenting the hymn in which the selflessness and humility of Jesus become examples for the Philippians in their struggles to build community.
The hymn describes the original Status of Jesus as pre-existent Lord (v. 6), the humiliation he undergoes in his incarnation (v.7) and his life of humiliation that culminates in his death (v.8). Because the Lord accepted humiliation that accompanied his ministry for others (and that is what Paul is suggesting the Philippians should also do) God exalted him in his resurrection (v.9), and gave him homage in the cosmic glory (v.10) and universal worship (v.11).
Since this letter is written from prison, Paul himself is also an example of the selfless service of others to which he is encouraging the Philippians to commit themselves.
