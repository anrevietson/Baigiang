# The third Servant Song

The first reading for the Mass (Is. 50:4-7) is from the third Servant Song. In it the Servant tells us that before preaching to others, to downcast humanity, he must first learn God's message himself. This he does by becoming one with humanity and by undergoing sufferings, trials and humiliations. So, too, it was with Christ, as the author of the Epistle to the Hebrews reminds us more than once. Christ was fully man and in his own person suffered the trials of those he came to save. 'It was appropriate that God . . . should make perfect through suffering the leader (i.e. Christ) who would take them to salvation' (Heb. 2:10). The same thought is also found in other passages of the epistle (e.g. Heb. 4:15; 5:8-9) read on Good Friday, and on the fifth Sunday in Lent for year two. In the responsorial psalm Christ's sentiments are expressed in Psalm 21, the Messianic psalm par excellence.
