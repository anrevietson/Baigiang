# Through suffering to glory

In today's Mass we concentrate on our Lord's suffering, through which he entered into his glory. Through our taking part in this Mass and the liturgy of Holy Week, and putting its message to practice in our daily lives, we follow him more closely and are assured that if we suffer with him we shall certainly be eventually glorified with him.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
As we begin our celebration of our Lord's fulfilment of his mission as servant on our behalf, let us acknowledge that we have often failed to follow his example, asking him to forgive us and to give us his spirit of service of others.
Lord, you served us to the extent of dying for us. Lord, have mercy.
Lord, you prayed for the very people who killed you. Christ, have mercy.
Lord, you promised entry into paradise to the criminal who prayed to you. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 50:4-7). The servant, as the perfect disciple and teacher, patiently suffers out of his fidelity to God.
Second Reading (Phil 2:6-11). Because Jesus accepted the r6le of a servant, to the extent of being crucified for others, God raised him to glory
Gospel (Lk 22:14-23:56). Jesus knowingly and willingly accepts suffering as the fulfilment of his mission, praying for his enemies and confiding totally in his Father.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: We have just heard how our Lord, during his suffering, prayed to his Father. Let us now, imitating him, pray to this same Father in our needs.
Response: Lord, hear our prayer.
Reader(s): For the whole Church: that all those who profess to be Christians may learn from their participation in the liturgy of Holy Week how to suffer patiently in the service of God and their fellow human beings. Lord, we pray.
For all who suffer: that they may experience the presence of our Lord, taking part in their sufferings and giving them hope and courage. Lord, we pray.
For all those who work to alleviate suffering in the world: that they may be supported and encouraged through the suffering of our Lord. Lord, we pray.
For ourselves: that as individuals and as a community we may enter into the liturgy of Holy Week, learning the message of the Lord's
suffering and applying it to our own lives. Lord, we pray.
President: Father, may we, as we commend ourselves and the needs of our world to you, receive the same comfort which you gave to your Son in his time of need. This prayer we make to you in the name of that same Son, our Lord, Jesus Christ.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Following the example of Jesus, dying on the cross, let us now pray confidently in the words which Jesus gave us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Let us give each other a sign of that peace which Jesus our Lord showed even in his suffering.

COMMUNION REELECTION
By receiving our Lord in the form of bread and wine we symbolise that we want to be united with him, as the source of our life and joy, in his suffering and death. Let us allow this truth to penetrate our minds and hearts to enable us to be ready to suffer and die with and for our Lord in order to participate in his resurrection.
