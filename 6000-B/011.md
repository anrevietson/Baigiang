# The mystery revealed to the Gentiles

Second Reading (Eph. 3:2-3, 5-6). In this reading Paul shows how the prophetic words of today's first reading have been mysteriously fulfilled in Christ in whom the light of God has shone forth to the nations. Paul was convinced that God had entrusted himself with the task of making known ?o the Gentiles God's plan of salvation which extended even to them (cf. Gal. 1:16). When he says 'by a revelation I was given the knowledge of the mystery' he is referring to the dramatic vision on the road to Damascus (Acts 9 :1-9; 26 :9-18) in which he was given to understand that the Gospel was to overflow from Judaism into the pagan world. The truth that God was going to break down the barriers that separated Jew from non-Jew had not been made known even to the prophets. Now God has revealed to Paul and to the apostles his hidden purpose of bringing all peoples into one body under one head, Jesus Christ (cf. Eph. 1:9 f.; Col. 1:26 f.; Rom. 1:16; 10:12). All those who are united in the one body of Christ, whether they be Jew or non-Jew, are joint) heirs of the divine gifts, sharing equally in the blessings once promised to Israel alone. One can see how this text is a suitable reading for this feastday when we celebrate the epiphany or manifestation of God in Christ to all peoples. It also contains an important message for our day when the peoples who should be one in Christ are divided by so many barriers of race, nationality and politics and even religion
