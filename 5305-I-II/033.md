# Paul and Israel

First Reading (Rom. 9 :1-5). Chapters 9-11 correspond to chapter 4 as the scriptural proof of Paul's thesis. He has been at great pains to demonstrate that God has united men with himself through Christ independently of the Jewish law. This poses, however, the problem of the relationship between Israel and the Church and Paul is now intent on showing that his 'Gospel' does not run counter to the religion of the Old Testament, indeed that it fulfils it in its proper sense.
At the beginning of this section he recognizes painfully the historical situation of Israel, but is careful to point out that this status was 'according to the flesh'. The whole problem seems to be resolved in Paul's mind principally by the relationship between flesh and Spirit. The true Israel of God consists not of the mere carnal descendants of Abraham but of his spiritual sons, that is, those who imitate his faith and are imbued with the Spirit, namely the Christians. Throughout the whole of this section Paul acknowledges the existence of an historically privileged social entity but refuses to identify it as such with God's true people.
