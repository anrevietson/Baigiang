# Water from the rock

First Reading (Ex 17:3-7). Moses, fulfilling God's instructions, had led the Israelites to freedom from their slavery in Egypt. As they wandered through the desert on the way to the promised land, they experienced hardship, particularly thirst. They blamed their predicament on Moses and even God and became very angry. They doubted God's care for them: 'Is THE LORD with us, or not?' God, however, used the occasion to give evidence of his presence with them and his provision for them. Moses, again obedient to God's instruction (although see Num 20:1-13 for a different version of this story) struck a rock with his staff and water miraculously flowed forth. The identification of the rock here as Horeb (Sinai) is probably incorrect as the place where there was no water was Rephidim (Ex 17:1) or perhaps near Kadesh (Num 27:14; Deut 32:51). The place-names Massali, meaning test or trial, and Meribah, meaning contention or grumbling, are mentioned a few times in the Bible, recalling the lack of faith and good will of the Israelites. Some rabbis held that the rock followed the Israelites and St. Paul applies this to Christ (1 Cor 10:4).
