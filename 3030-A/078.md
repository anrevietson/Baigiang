# Jesus communicates divine life

Gospel (Jn 4:5-42). A central theme of St. John's gospel, that Jesus communicates divine life to believers, finds expression in this story of his meeting with the Samaritan woman. The scene is set in the first few verses. Jesus, tired from walking in the heat, since it was just midday, sits down to rest at the ancient well of Jacob near Sychar (probably the same as Shechem). The Samaritan woman is surprised when Jesus asks her for a drink, since Jews did not accept Samaritans as pure Jews and so did not share food or drink with them, in case of ritual defilement. But Jesus quickly moves to lead her to more important things. As is usual in John's gospel, there is at first misunderstanding. When he offers her living water, the woman thinks he refers to the fresh spring water at the bottom of the well. But he refers, first of all, to his own words which are spirit and life and secondly, to the gift of the Holy Spirit, whom he will bestow on believers after his resurrection (cf. Jn 7:38-39). Jesus leads the woman to acknowledge her own situation and his
knowledge of her leads her to call him, first, a prophet and later to suggest he may be the Messiah, a title which Jesus here affirms of himself. Finally he is called Saviour of the world by many other who believe in him. He is greater than Jacob. This gospel passage emphasises the importance of sincere and spiritual worship, rather than one concerned with place or ritual. It also speaks of the participation of the disciples in the mission of Jesus, confronting people with the revealing and saving word of Jesus, leading them to believe in him and so have eternal life where sower and reaper will rejoice together. Indeed, the Samaritan woman herself is shown as a missionary.
