# Water from the rock

First Reading (Exod. 17:3-7). In the Bible there are two accounts of the origin of a particular spring of drinking water, a small desert oasis. The Lectionary refers to Exodus 17:3-7; the parallel account is Numbers 20:2-13.
Attention should be concentrated on the theological lessons which the biblical authors intended to convey rather than on the actual historical details of the scene itself.
The first point is the reaction of the people to the situation in which they were desperately short of water, viz. they looked to Yahweh for help. This was only to be expected because he had already intervened on their behalf : by making the water of Marah drinkable (Exod. 15:22) and by sending them the quails and the manna (Exod. 16:1-36).
However, the faith of the people was tainted. They were more concerned with their immediate physical needs than with their destiny in God's salvation plan. They complained of the hardships involved in following Moses and aggressively challenged God to be as good as his word, if he could. These two attitudes were enshrined in the place's name in later times - Meribah, from the root 'to quarrel' and Massah, from the root 'to challenge'.
The second point is that in spite of the inadequacy of their faith God's power was again exercised for them in their hour of need, through the use of Moses' rod, which had already been the symbol of the power by which they had been rescued from Egypt.
In this episode two important messianic symbols are involved :
(1) The power of Yahweh is represented as associated with the rock from which the drinking water had come. This recalls the custom of addressing Yahweh as the 'Rock of Israel', an epithet which expressed his strength and permanence, his role as the source of stability of his chosen people and the object of their faith.
(2) The significance of water as the condition and cause of life.
This was much more expressive in a dry country like Palestine, where water is scarce, than in Ireland.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 94) recalls Israel's sin in the desert and calls on us not to repeat it.
