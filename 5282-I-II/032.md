# The power of the gospel

First Reading (Rom. 1:16-25). The gospel is- not simply preaching about Christ, it contains and communicates Christ. Whenever the gospel is proclaimed authentically and authoritatively the whole of Christ's unique saving Work is actualized. Hence all that can be said about the Christ-event can be said about the gospel Which represents this event.
Paul's gospel is the Good News that men now enter into right relationship With God through faith in God who saves them in Christ. All that is required for this right relationship is trust, confidence and dependence - all that is involved in biblical faith. This means that the old division between Jew and Gentile is abolished. All men can now have access to God on equal terms.
Paul's experience of salvation's gratuitousness has convinced him that God's righteousness is neither vindictive nor distributive but salvific that is fidelity to his own saving love. God's righteousness does not condemn, it saves. It does not merely recognize that a man is righteous because of his own virtues, rather it makes the sinner righteous. For, according to Paul, God's love manifested in Christ is so free and gratuitous that it cannot be deserved. Indeed it reveals that, without it, all men - Jews and Gentiles alike -are sinners and, therefore 'deserve' nothing more than condemnation. This is what Paul means by the revelation of God's 'wrath'. In order to emphasize the universal need of God's saving love,
Paul paints an exaggeratedly sombre picture of mankind's sinfulness, beginning with the pagan world (1:18-32), stressing that the basic, indeed the only sin is the culpable refusal to acknowledge God's revelation of himself. Vices and moral depravity are not so much sins in themselves as the consequences of this basic sin, which Paul, in typically Semitic fashion, sees as being caused directly by God.
