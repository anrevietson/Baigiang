# Paul in Jerusalem

First Reading (Acts 9 :26-31). Paul's reputation as a persecutor of the Christians had been widespread. According to his own account (Gal. 1:22) the members of the Church in Judea did not at the time of this visit know him by sight and we see here that they were somewhat doubtful of the sincerity of his conversion. But they had known Barnabas, a generous Levite of Cypriot origin (Acts 4 :36-47) and obviously trusted him.
Paul himself, in the Epistle to the Galatians, emphasises how he was called directly by Christ and was not dependent on the apostolic community at Jerusalem, yet he also notes that he is faithful to tradition (cf. 1 Cor. 11) and he exhorts Timothy to be likewise (2 Tim. 4). According to Acts, too, Paul is anxious to maintain close contact with the Jerusalem authorities.
The experience which Paul had on the way to Damascus governed his whole life. His personal conviction that Jesus was Lord and that his followers were most intimately united with him (an idea later to be developed in the image of the Church as the body of Christ) was unshakeable and henceforth he used his previously gained knowledge of the scriptures to win men to Christ. He became involved in arguments with the Hellenists, Greek speaking Jews. These seem to have been the most zealous proselytisers, just as Paul had been before his conversion.
'The brothers' is a common title for Christians in Acts and is used also by Paul in Romans It seems to have denoted often the laity of the Church as distinct from the apostles and elders. The new Christian community is a brotherhood, in which the commandment of fraternal service and charity is all4mportant. Concerned for Paul's safety, the Christian community in Jerusalem sent some of its members to take him through Caesarea - a seaport built by Herod the Great and named in honour of the emperor to his native city of Tarsus. .
Short summaries are a characteristic of Acts We have one of these in V. 31. Luke sums up the position of the Church before turning to another point. He notes in particular how the spread of the Church and the joy which filled its members is the work of the Holy Spirit.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 21 :26-28, 30 -32) expresses the gratitude of the psalmist for a favour and speaks of how God will be praised universally for his great deeds of love.
