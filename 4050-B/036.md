# Paul in Jerusalem

First Reading (Acts 9:26-31). Saul - now a committed follower and messenger of the risen Lord - returns to Jerusalem, from where he set out as a persecutor of the Church (Acts 8:3; 9:1-2). Although called directly by Christ (9:3-6) and, therefore, radically independent of the Jerusalem disciples, he contacts the mother Church and enters into communion with the other apostles. His independence is stressed by the reluctance of the Jerusalem community to accept him and by need of Barnabas to introduce him to the apostles. Once welcomed within the Church, however, he proclaims the Lord with the same confidence as he showed in Damascus. We first meet Saul in Acts as the one who witnesses and consents to Stephen's death (7:58-9:1). He now takes over where Stephen left off, arguing with, and being opposed by Greek-speaking Jews in Jerusalem (6:9-10). His conversion is complete, as is his reconciliation and solidarity with the Jerusalem brethren who now help him to go to Tarsus, from where he will be summoned by Barnabas (Acts 11:25), to begin his work among the Gentiles. With the persecutor turned disciple and apostle, the Church throughout Palestine enjoys peace, is consolidated and increases by being faithful to the Lord and by the power of the Holy Spirit.
