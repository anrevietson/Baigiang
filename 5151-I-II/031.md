# Israel oppressed in Egypt

First Reading (Ex 1:8-14, 22). The sad plight of the Israelites in Egypt, from which God in answer to their prayer (Ex. 3:7) was to deliver them, is briefly but vividly described. The decree to drown the new-born males reminds us of the extermination camps or abortions of our own time.
A change in the political situation in Egypt led to the oppression of the Israelites now seen as a menace to the country. The mention of the building of the store-cities of Pithom and Rameses (Ex. 1:11), in which the Israelites were employed as slave labourers, favours the commonly accepted date (about 1250) for the oppression and exodus. It is known from extra-biblical sources, that the Pharaohs Set I (about 1309-1290 BC) and his son and successor Rameses 11 (about 1290-1224 BC) started' extensive building operations in the Nile delta where the Israelites were situated, and where Rameses 11 gave his name to a city he built.
