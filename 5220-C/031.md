# Mount Sinai and Mount Zion

Second Reading (Heb. 12 :18-19, 22-24). The author sketches contrasting pictures of the assemblies which received the old and the new covenants or testaments. The first picture is full of the imagery of storm, which engendered some dread in the relations of the Sinai people and their Lord (cf. Ex. 19-20). The second depicts the heavenly Jerusalem where angels are gathered to meet us, and the saints who have already been made perfect. The mediator of our covenant is Jesus, whose priestly intercession on our behalf has been the key-theme of all this letter. He has bridged the gulf between God and man. There is no longer place for dread. We have the freedom of God's own sons (cf. Gal. 4 :26). We already belong to this company because we are united with Jesus, but we have not yet achieved perfection. We are still on the way to the fulfilment described in Rev. 21 : 22ff. In other words:
since we already possess the heavenly life within us (the grace of God), the writer can speak of our arrival in our homeland.
