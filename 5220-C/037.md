# Compassion made real in gentleness

In today's Mass we are invited to turn our thoughts to compassion made real in gentleness, to a way of living that represents a radical alternative to the self-centred aggressiveness and violence that so often characterises human affairs. The Gospel way demands that we know how to be last and least if we are to give leadership in a genuinely Christian fashion. Power needs and control needs must be transformed by love, insight, compassion and solidarity.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Aware of how easy it is to choose harsh, negative, destructive ways of dealing with each other, of how easy it is to quench smouldering wicks and break bruised reeds, let us stand before God together and allow divine gentleness to touch us and heal us.
Lord Jesus, in a world which is hard, harsh and proud, you melt us afresh. Lord, have mercy.
Lord, you give us new insight into the needs of our sisters and brothers. Christ, have mercy.
Lord, you open our ears and help us to understand the cry of the poor. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Sir 3:17-20.28-29). Ben Sira, in the short selection from chapter 3 of his book of instructions intended to help his Hebrew community understand that its own culture and wisdom was superior to that of the Greeks, teaches the central importance of meekness and gentleness cornerstones of a good life. Superiority requires only pride and aggression. Humility demands true strength of character, gentleness demands real concern and respect for another's dignity.
Second Reading (Heb 12:18-19.22-24). Hebrews reminds us that we stand in God's presence, stand in need of the true religious attitude, in need of awe and wonder and amazement.
Gospel (Lk 14:1.7-14). The Gospel faces us once again with the paradoxical nature of Christian life and spirituality. We are being challenged to face up to the polarities of existence, to our learnt capacity to split reality into acceptable and rejectable parts and to avoid the truth as we fall into one-sided living.

PRAYERS OF THE FAITHFUL
President: In the Liturgy of the Word we have been challenged to face the paradox of life. Let us now pray together for the courage and strength to accept this challenge, however falteringly, in our lives and relationships.
Reader(s): We pray for all who exercise authority over others in the human family. May true gentleness be valued and real humility grow.
We pray for all Christians everywhere. May self-righteous attitudes give way to honest self-awareness.
We pray for all those whose lives have been blighted by oppression, who have been manipulated, abused, and victimised. May they experience justice and care in healing communities.
We pray for our own parish and locality. May barriers fall, may God reign, may reconciliation be the fruit of gentle perseverance.
We pray for our departed sisters and brothers. May they enter God's reign of wholeness and peace.
President: Lord, Creator of the universe, teach us new ways of caring and healing, new ways of strong and gentle responsibility for each other and the world. We ask this in Jesus' name.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As we pray as Jesus taught us, we trust the Father to fill us with wonder at God's compassion and love.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
We have all been hurt on our journey of life. Let us lift our gaze towards healing as we wish each other the blessing of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Jesus, you show us how to be strong and meek, committed and humble. Through our sharing in this Eucharist help us bring these seeming opposites together in our own lives. As we overcome the false divisions we have generated we will become agents of healing and change. As we cross the false boundaries we have created we will enter your freedom and become bearers of peace.
