# The banquet parables

The Gospel (Lk. 14 :1, 7-14). All the parables of Lk. 14:1-24 centre around the idea of a banquet. In their own concrete way, the Israelites described heaven as an eternal feast. The first of our two parables to-day makes the point that the Lord's special invitation goes out to those who consider themselves most in need of his help. The people who realize their own nothingness before the Master will receive the higher places, when the kingdom comes. On the other hand, the leading Pharisees, full of their own importance and sufficiency, are warned that they may be fortunate even to receive the lowest place. The lapidary phrase 'For everyone who exalts himself . . ' concludes fittingly all the parable. It will be found again in the story of the Pharisee and tax-gatherer (18 :14).
The second parable illustrates what disinterested kindness is all about. If you invite a person, so that you can be invited back in turn, you have received your reward. We should keep 'open house' to the poor of this world. Our repayment will come at the Resurrection. This parable puts a passage from the Sermon on the Plain in story form, i.e. Luke 6 :32-36.
A suggested, unifying, theme: If we are humble in our living, and generous in our giving, we will receive a high place in heaven (or : at the heavenly feast)
