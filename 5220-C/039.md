# Choosing places at table

Gospel (Lk 14:1.7-14). The gospel, taken from the first half of chapter 14 selects one of two stories which Jesus tells while sharing a meal with some members of the Pharisees. What takes place is the final con-troversy concerning the Sabbath observance. Note the meal setting. Luke tends to offer radical solutions to controversial questions in this context of conviviality. The first story, not included in the reading, stresses the link between the Sabbath and compassion made concrete in healing. The second story, which is drawn to our attention, involves a parable concerning places of honour (at a marriage feast) in which Jesus offers a teaching which focuses our attention on the paradox of self-promotion. A person's projected self-image does not fool God. The Kingdom is not for those who seek advantage or status: it is about sharing life with the disadvantaged, the marginalized, those who have been ignored by society and pushed to the edges of human existence. This, for Luke, is what resurrection living is all about - being freed from social distinction and being radically opened to broken humanity.
