# In praise of the meek

First Reading (Sir. 3 :17-20, 28-29). A passage in praise of the man who is meek, gentle, kindly, affable, humble. These closely allied virtues could all be used to explain the Greek word prautes (cf. Mt. 11 :29; Col. 3:12; Eph. 4:2). The Beatitude (Mt. 5:5) repeats the words of Ps. 37 :11. . 'But the meek shall possess the land and delight themselves in abundant prosperity'. Meekness involves a consciousness of one's own weakness (even when established in power). If we are meek, we realize we are nothing before our creating God, that we have to rely on his forgiveness of our faults, so we are more ready to avoid judging the faults of others.
The disease of pride cannot be cured. It infects every action. The Lord rejects the proud man (cf. also Prov. 15 :25; 16 :18). Note the echo of all this theme in the Magnificat statement of
Lk. 1:51-52.
The humble man acts in an intelligent way. He does not pursue novelty, but listens attentively to the wisdom of those who have gone before him. In a modern idiom we might say: he relies on the experience of others to direct his activity, rather than seeking continually 'to do his own thing'.
The Responsorial Psalm comes to us from an ancient processional rite, probably held during the Autumn. It expresses the goodness of God towards those who are humble in heart, and lowly in station. He gave his chosen people a homeland, and he continues to pour fertile rain upon it.
