# Philip in Samaria

First Reading (Acts 8:5-8,14-17). Persecution of the Church in Jerusalem leads Philip, one of the "newly ordained" deacons of 6:1-7 to bring the Good News further afield. That the gospel should be well received by the Samaritans probably came as a surprise to the Jerusalem Church, yet Luke is very insistent on the reality of this "Samaritan Pentecost." The word of salvation accompanied by signs leads to a great joy among the people. This event brings the Samaritans who would have been enemies of the Jews to the attention of the apostles who confirm, by formal baptism, the events that have taken place. The text does not indicate two baptisms but rather two stages in their coming to the Church. The Acts of the Apostles emphasises the importance of the role of the Twelve in the spread of the gospel. The church is to be rooted in the apostolic tradition.
