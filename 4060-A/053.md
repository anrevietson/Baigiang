# Christian initiation in Samaria

First Reading (Acts 8:5-8.14-17). This passage is one of several in Acts that relate Christian initiation and the reception of the gift of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:14-36; 8:4-24; 8:26-40;1O:44-48). Each of these 'mini-Pentecosts' highlights the early Church's conviction on the necessity of the gift of the Holy Spirit as part of Christian initiation. Later in the case of the centurion, Cornelius, his household receives the out-pouring of the Spirit, and Peter concludes it is obvious he has to be baptized. In our present reading the opposite happens; the Samaritans receive the word and the apostles conclude that they must therefore have the gift of the Holy Spirit too ('not yet' and 'only' indicate the abnormality of one without the other). So the episode presupposes the union of baptism and the gift of the Spirit. However, this episode describes the Pentecost of the Samaritans and is the first crossing of the threshold into the non-Jewish world, indicating that this gift is not merited by race or prior religious commitment.
