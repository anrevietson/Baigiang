# Always be ready to give an account

Second Reading (1 Pet. 3:15-18). The first letter of Peter stresses the essential dimensions of Christian initiation and commitment, exhorting the faithful to the consequences of their baptismal vocation. Today's passage begins a section of the letter that explicitly deals with a Christian approach to persecution. It highlights the cost of living virtuously. The model offered is Jesus, and just as his suffering was life-producing so too the suffering of Christians leads to the quality of life that is part of the hope that comes with baptism. The last couple of lines seem to be a baptismal confession (see also 1 Tim. 3:16), and remind the recipients of this letter that baptism itself is a ritual of immersion in the sufferings of Jesus. As Jesus was raised to new life in the spirit because of his acceptance of suffering, so too Christians can rise to new life in the spirit through their faithful acceptance of sufferings that are a consequence of virtuous Christian commitment.
