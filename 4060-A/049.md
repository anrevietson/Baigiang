# The promise of the Spirit

The Gospel (John 14:15-21). As mentioned in the commentary for the Fifth Sunday of Easter, this chapter in John's Gospel is to encourage the disciples who were troubled at the news of the departure of Jesus. In today's reading we have the promise of the Paraclete, the Spirit of Truth. But first we have the statement of Jesus, which is repeated in verse twenty-one, that we prove our love for him by keeping his commandments. John has in mind especially, as we see from his first epistle, the commandment to love one another.
Next we have the first 'Paraclete saying'. (There are five together in the Gospel.) Paraclete was a Greek term mainly of legal usage, meaning defender or helper. Taken over into Jewish usage
it received the added meaning of intercessor or mediator. John speaks of Jesus glorified as our Paraclete or intercessor with the Father (1 John 2:1). The promised Spirit is another Paraclete, distinct from Jesus. He comes from both the Father and the Son. Like them he is a person - John uses the masculine form of the word and a masculine pronoun when referring to him. He is the Spirit of Truth because he is the Spirit of Jesus who is the Truth and part of his function is to reveal to the disciples ever more deeply the meaning of the revelation brought by Jesus. The age of the Church is the era of the Spirit and as long as the Church exists the Spirit will be with it. But the world, i.e. those who refuse to accept Christ and commit themselves to him, cannot receive the Spirit. Faith in Jesus is a necessary condition for receiving the gift of the Spirit. His activity is in us He enables us to assimilate the word of Jesus so that it may be a source of life for us.
After his death and burial those who have not faith in Jesus will no longer see him, but the disciples will see him in his glorious life. His glorified life will also be the principle of life for all Christians. 'On that day' was a phrase used by the prophets to indicate some special intervention of God in history. Here it probably refers to the whole time after the resurrection. The Christian, enlightened by the Spirit, will understand that the Father, the Son and Christians share the same life. The condition of sharing this life is to accept and keep the commandments of Christ, to love and obey. Obedience is the proof of love.
From this reading we learn that we cannot claim to be one with Jesus Christ, guided by and living according to his Spirit, unless we observe his demands in our everyday lives. If these in concrete circumstances are sometimes vague to us we should ask the Holy Spirit to enlighten us. That is part of his mission.
