# The parables of Mark

The Gospel (Mk4 :1-20). Chapter four of Mark is devoted to parables. It is well known that the' Semitic parable normally illustrated one point. The details of the story had no significance in its interpretation. When the details of the story correspond to different points in the speaker's mind (as in the interpretation today, e.g., different types of soil corresponding to types of people) we have an allegory.
It seems that the allegorical explanation of this parable as offered by Mark is a re-interpretation a of the original. The allegorical meaning is clear. What of the original point? a The emphasis is on the huge harvest. In Palestine, anything like a ten to one ratio would be considered a great harvest. Thence, a thirtyfold and upwards underlines a tremendous quality. Jesus is s teaching that despite setbacks and persecution, the kingdom of God will appear in a glorious manner. It is growing even now, despite appearances to the contrary. A Note on verses ten to twelve: Parables were meant to stimulate the hearer to arrive at the meaning intended. They were usually reasonably' clear. Here Mark is concentrating on the special relationship between Jesus and the twelve (cf. Thursday, Friday, week two) and his own idea of Jesus' withdrawing from the crowds. Consequently, the parables are presented as if concealing something from the crowd. The theme of the messianic secret is at least hinted at in this passage.
