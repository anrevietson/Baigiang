# John the Baptist

Gospel (Mt 3:1-12). This gospel reading falls into three principal parts: (i) A focus on the person and mission of John the Baptist, an unconventional figure who made a powerful impact on people. He was a prophet, the last of the great prophets; his dress would have reminded people of Elijah, and his mission was like that of Isaiah. His basic message was in two parts: an announcement of good news ("the kingdom of heaven is close at hand") and a call to repent, in the light of that good news.
(ii) The encounter between John and those who came to hear him. John warns the religious leaders against a complacency which presumes on being in God's favour in virtue of descent from Abraham. They too need to repent and to show concrete signs of such repentance, appropriate fruit. (iii) John's witness to Jesus. Jesus is more powerful than John, because, unlike John, he baptises with the Holy Spirit. Empowered by the Spirit of God, he can pour out that Spirit on others. John also believed that Jesus would come as a judge, separating wheat from chaff. John may have been surprised that Jesus turned out to be a Saviour more than a judge.
