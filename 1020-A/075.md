# A shoot from the stump of Jesse

First Reading (Is 11:1-10). After a terrifying passage describing the relentless advance of an invading enemy army on Jerusalem, the reader of the book of Isaiah finds this promise. The hope of the people in a time of darkness is focused on a particular individual. There is a reference to the promises made to David, son of Jesse, that his throne would last for ever. The Spirit of God will rest on this individual as once it rested on his ancestor, David. By contrast with the judges and rulers who depend on human wisdom alone for their decisions, the new descendent of Jesse will be able to see the truth of the situations presented to him for judgement. This truth lies in right relations with God. It is in its treatment of the poor and weak that the truth of a nation is tested. In showing justice to the disadvantaged, the ruler makes clear the attitude of God himself. Opposition to the wicked follows naturally, and wickedness dies away before the truth in his mouth, because it is the truth of God.
The new kingdom of justice brings with it a new age of peace in the relations between humankind and the world of nature. Nature will no longer be a threat to the human race and its domestic animals on which it depends. It is too easy to see here as a simple image for peace among the nations of the world. What is at issue is the relationship between humanity and the created world. If this has not been the case, to what extent do we bear responsibility as Christians?
The Psalm continues the reflection on the king and the benefits of his reign.
