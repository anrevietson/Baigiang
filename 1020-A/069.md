# John the Baptist in the wilderness

Gospel (Mt 3:1-12). The Gospel today falls into two parts. In the first, St Matthew introduces us to John the Baptist, and gives a general description of his message, his way of life and of his acts. This section resembles closely the corresponding section of the Gospel of Mark. The second part of today's Gospel passage is close in form to the Gospel of Luke and describes a particular meeting between John and the Pharisees and Saducees.
John the Baptist as a link between the time of promise and the coming of Jesus. He lives in the desert, a place of dry desolation, which will break into flower when the Lord comes. The desert is where the people of Israel journeyed before they arrived at the promised land. Perhaps it is necessary to return there before the final redemption. There are echoes of the actions of earlier prophets, notably Elijah who fled to the desert for safety, and in the end was taken up from there in a flaming chariot. Later in the Gospel (17:10-13), Matthew specifically links John to Elijah.
There are many parallels in the Gospel of Matthew between the life of John and that of Jesus. Some of the parallel elements are evident today. We see that both preached repentance, and the coming of the Kingdom. Both baptize. Both are opposed by the Pharisees and Saducees, important groups of their Jewish nation. Elsewhere in the Gospel we see that both John and Jesus are seen as prophets by the people, both are executed as criminals, and both are buried by their own disciples. Of course their lives did have parallels insofar as we can see, but it is also clear that Matthew wishes to draw attention to this similarity. Matthew draws a picture of John and uses it as a sign to point us to some understanding of the message of Jesus, which is so much greater. John himself tells us this at the end of today's passage.
