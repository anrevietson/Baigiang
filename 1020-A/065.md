# Paul prays for unity in the Roman Church

Second Reading (Rom 15:4-9). This reading is part of a larger passage (14:1-15:13) in which Paul deals with a serious dispute between two groups in the Roman Church. There, two factions in the Church had different opinions on what a Christian should eat and on how the Sabbath should be kept (14:2-3,5). There is nothing wrong about such differences of opinion, but the members of these two different groups had grown intolerant of each other and had judged each other wrongly. Paul is most unhappy about this state of affairs (14:10,13,19). In our reading, he calls on the Romans to accept one another as Christ has accepted them. As Christ died for us while we were still sinners, so we must remain faithful even to those who are at odds with us, who disagree with us. Paul knows that only God can bring about that communion of love between people which respects diversity. Hence Paul's prayer for the Church in this reading. Christ came for both Jew and Gentile, so that all men and women would be united in him towards God. Paul prays that this purpose of Christ's redeeming work would be realised in Rome.
