# Jesus troubled in spirit

The Gospel (John 13 :21-33, 36-38). Jesus is 'troubled in spirit' (cf. 11:33) at the onset of the final conflict, tragically marked by the betrayal of Judas. The action of the unhappy disciple is in reality the work of Satan, the adversary (v.27; cf. 13 :2). The phrase, 'and it was night' (13 :30) is dramatic in the fashion of John -Judas is one of those who love darkness rather than light (3 :19); he had turned his back on the light. Yet now, too, the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified (13 :31). The death of Jesus in shame and ignorance is, in truth, the supreme act in human history. God is glorified because, in his Son, he is the actor in this drama. A disciple had betrayed Jesus; another will deny him, while the rest will desert him (16 :32). Jesus challenges the self-confidence of Peter: he knows Peter so much better than Peter knows himself. In this divine and compassionate knowledge which the Lord has of us we can find real hope.
