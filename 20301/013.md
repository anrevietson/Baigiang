# Christ the Sacrament

Today we talk of Christ the Sacrament and speak of the Church as the universal sacrament of salvation. As the catechism once put it, a sacrament is an efficient sign of grace. It can make that of which it is a sign become a reality. Christ and the Church are signs of holiness, of the destruction of sin. Christ, in himself, and through the Church which is his Body, can make holiness and the destruction of sin become realities. Looking on the Church as Sacrament leads us to speak of her as being without crease or wrinkle, intended to be sinless and immaculate. John the Baptist pointed out Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world - as if sin had come to an end with his coming. The author of 1 John in today's reading is thinking along the same lines: Christians are incorporated into this Christ, the immaculate Lamb come to destroy sin. This same author can say that no one born of God can sin, since the seed of God is residing in him. In this present reading he does not go that far. He does insist, however, that the entire thrust of Christian existence is towards the conquest of sin; to live in Christ is by its nature to be committed to being holy, pure, like Christ himself. That simple designation 'the Lamb of God' is so full of meaning for Christians.
