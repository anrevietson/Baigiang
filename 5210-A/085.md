# From Caesarea Philippi to Rome

It is a long, long way from Caesarea Philippi to Rome. At Caesarea, Peter is given authority in the Christian community; he is hailed as the rock and is given the power of the keys. Years later in Rome, all this will have much greater implications for him and for the infant Church. A day to realise the connection between our local Church, the Church of Rome and the successor of Peter.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Peter recognised Jesus as the living Son of God. May he purify our hearts so that we too can recognise him.
Lord Jesus, you are the light and wisdom of God. Lord, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you are the Christ the Son of the living God. Christ, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you with your Church until the end of time. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 22:19-23). Dismissal of one man from high office and the appointment of another, Eliakim, as prime minister. He is given the key of the House of David on his shoulder, the sign of authority, the symbol which relates this passage to the Gospel one.
Second Reading (Rom 11:33-36). Paul tries to come to grips with the inability of his own Jewish people to hear the Good News of the Gospel though pagans accepted it. We try to make our own Paul's triumphant hymn of praise to the God whose ways are not ours.
Gospel (Mt 16:13-20). Peter confesses to the identity of Jesus. As a result he receives a new name which defines his vocation, and the power of the keys, to signify his authority in the Church.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Today we confess with Peter the apostle our faith in the Christ, the living Son of God. Through him and with him we pray for the needs especially of the Church, for our own needs and for the needs of the whole world.
Reader(s): For Pope John Paul, the successor of St Peter entrusted with the power of the keys. May his leadership continue to give the peoples of the earth heart and hope.
For those in public life: that the powers of evil may not prevail over them.
For all those who are engaged in ministry in the Church: that they may cheerfully accept the responsibility entrusted to them. May they grow in the service of God's holy people.
For those who find themselves adrift in God's Church: may they find enlightenment, hope and sure guidance.
For missionaries: that they may not feel forgotten. May they lead their people into a greater sense of Church.
For those who have died, especially N. May they find a place in the heavenly Jerusalem.
President: Lord our God, your love is eternal. We ask you to receive the prayers and pleas which we offer in the name of your Son and our Saviour, our Lord Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns for ever and ever.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Following the teaching of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the living God, we pray with confidence that his Kingdom may come among us.

INVITATION TO COMMUNION
This is the Christ, the Son of the living God. Happy are those who are called to his supper.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Caesarea Philippi, a strange place, full of unexploded bombs and mines, we can no longer traverse its hills. Gone is the glory of yesteryear, gone the temple to Caesar in gleaming white marble. Gone are the pagan shrines erected to Baal and Pan. The Jordan still flows out from the snow-capped hills. Was it here that the rugged fishermen hailed Jesus as the living Son of God? No memory of that unusual happening lingers here. One has to travel far, to Rome, to see where it all ends. Yes, in the person of that white-clad speck of a man who appears at a window high up above the crowds to bless the city and the world, urbi et orbi.
