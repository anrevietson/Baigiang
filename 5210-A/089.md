# Power and service

a person there are certain presuppositions. That the power will not be abused, that the law will be obeyed, that advantage will not be taken on others, that power will not corrupt. The exact details of the crime committed by the 'master of the palace' are not hinted at in the reading. It would seem that he had betrayed the policy of the King and sought to involve Egypt in the rebellion. The details don't really matter. When he is dismissed and his successor is named there is a phrase used which is important. The new man is 'to be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem'. The implication is that Shebna has not been a 'father' to the people. He has not treated them as part of his family. Authority is a service. When it becomes untruthful to this it demands dismissal.
