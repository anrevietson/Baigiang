# Repentance and redemption

First Reading (1 Thes. 1 :2-5, 8-10). Repentance and redemption are the keynotes of New Testament preaching. John the Baptist preached a baptism of repentance Jesus called on men to repent and believe in the gospel' . The apostles and all missionaries after them were commissioned by Christ to preach in his name to all nations repentance and the forgiveness of sin (Luke 24 :47). This the apostles did, ending their proclamation of the good news of redemption with a call to repentance. Repentance means conversion, a turning away from false teaching and immoral behaviour to a new way of life. This the Thessalonian Christians, most of whom were formerly pagans, did with a generous heart; and this despite persecutions from the Jews (cf. Acts 17 :1-9). Their faith and love, in fact, were an example for other Churches. (We may note that in this passage we have the first mention of the three theological virtues: faith, hope and love, although not in that order. Special prominence must have been given to these three virtues from the earliest days of the Church.)
