# An ending and an opening

First Reading (Acts 1:1-11). In this liturgy we have both the ending of one work and the opening of another. Luke has created in two separate books the story of salvation history. Yet with the final chapter of the Gospel and the first chapter of the Acts, Luke has joined together a continuing but different stage in that history. In this reading we hear Luke tell us that in the first account he related what Jesus did and taught. Now he intends to tell us the story of how Jesus' disciples continued that work. He emphasises how Jesus will still be with them but now in his Spirit. He will be actively aiding them in their mission to be witnesses to what he taught and did in that public ministry that Luke has described. The Ascension for Luke marks the new existence of Jesus that began with his resurrection and continues in the coming of his Spirit at Pentecost. The sacred number of forty symbolises the time in which the risen Jesus has shared with them anew existence to which they now will witness. For Luke, the Ascension marks the end of these appearances, and the work of disciples filled with the Spirit begins.
