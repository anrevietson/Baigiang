# The ascension

First Reading (Acts 1:1-11). The emphasis of the first verses of Acts is subtly different, pointed by the interpretation which the angels give. Jesus will no longer be present with them by a bodily presence, but his place as their guide and strength will be taken by the Spirit. The emphasis is all forward-looking and workmanlike: they are not to waste their time and energies in wondering idly when the end will come, but are to get on with their mission of being witness. The programme is given them: as the climax of Luke's Gospel was at Jerusalem, and Luke had built up to this carefully by his extended narrative of the journey up to Jerusalem and the Passion, during which he had placed a major part of Jesus' teaching, so now the message is to spread from Jerusalem. Jerusalem is the pivot and from there the Acts will show Christ's message spreading in ever widening circles, first to Judea and Samaria and finally to Rome, called in some contemporary Jewish documents 'the end of the earth'. It is difficult to imagine what exactly the phenomena of the ascension must have consisted in; exactly how Jesus' bodily presence on earth was terminated, for the elements of the description are drawn from stock biblical images, so that they show as much the meaning as the event of the ascension. Especially throughout the Acts, where Luke is moulding his material with some freedom, he is accustomed to teach theology by means of such little pictorial scenes.
