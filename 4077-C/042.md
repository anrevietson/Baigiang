# The ascension

First Reading (Acts 1:1 - 11). At the beginning of Acts, just before describing the ascension, Luke recalls a conversation between Jesus and his apostles sometime after the resurrection. The apostles enquire whether the political liberation of Israel is at hand. Jesus bluntly tells them to mind their own business.
He has gathered them together not to discuss politics but to remind them that the Spirit would soon empower them to do what he has commissioned them to do. Their job is to be his witnesses. From their base in Jerusalem they will spread out to the ends of the earth. For Luke, Jerusalem is the centre of sacred history. The ascension has left the apostles awe-stricken and immobilised. The two men who appear to them indicate that they will no longer experience Jesus' bodily presence but that he will return in glory. One can discern an element of urgency in these opening verses of Acts. The emphasis is on the need for action rather than speculation.
