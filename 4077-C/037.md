# The end of Luke's first book

The two readings from Luke, the end of his first book, the Gospel, and the beginning of his second, the Acts of the Apostles, must really be understood in the reverse order to that in which they are read at the Mass; together they form the watershed between the two volumes. The conclusion of the Gospel shows Jesus at Jerusalem, blessing the apostles before his departure from them; it is a great act of liturgical blessing, 'lifting up his hands', a sort of urbi et orbi, with which Jesus leaves them to carry on his work in the strength of the promise he has just given them.
The Gospel (Luke 24 :46-53). In this passage of summing up the Gospel, the main theological themes of Luke come thick and fast, gathering together the threads which are found scattered throughout the Gospel and Acts. One might group them into three areas: the object and the power of the apostolic preaching and the life of the Church. The object is always the resurrection of Christ; this is the central truth with which the earliest Christian preaching in Acts is almost exclusively concerned; It is to this primarily that the apostles are witnesses. But the ultimate object of all the preaching is conversion of all nations, and it is with an appeal to repentance that each sermon ends; they are intended to draw the hearers to change their lives. The power of the apostolic preaching is the Spirit, whose action is one of the most marked features of the story of the early Church in Acts; they are to wait for the coming of the Spirit before starting their mission, for the Spirit will guide their action at every stage of development. Finally Luke gives what will be three keynotes of the life of the early Christian community in Acts: joy, praise and prayer; they have been keynotes of his Gospel from the very first chapters (e.g. 2 :10-14) and will continue to be noted by him continually (e.g. Acts 3 :46-47; 11:18).
