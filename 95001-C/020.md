# The Servant

First Reading (Is. 42 :1-4, 6-7). Scholars have continually busied themselves with the problem of the identificati6n of the Servant who is mentioned in today's reading which is taken from the first of the famous Songs of the Suffering Servant. But the original identity of the servant baffles the learned who cannot decide whether he is to be considered as a personification of Israel or of the purified remnant of the chosen people, or whether he is to be identified with some individual king or prophet. However, the technical identification of the Servant is of no great importance for the Christian and liturgical use of the passage read today. It is very probable that Jesus identified himself with the Servant and thus gave to the apostolic witnesses and to the Church the right to apply to him and to his ministry the statements made in Isaiah about the Servant.
Most of the New Testament allusions to the Servant refer to the Passion. But the words spoken by the voice from heaven at the baptism of Jesus (see today's Gospel) are taken almost verbatim from the beginning of Isaiah 42. Jesus is thus identified at the beginning of his mission as the one who finally accomplishes the task of the Suffering Servant. Now, according to today's reading the Servant was endowed with the Spirit of God (cf. Is. 11:1 ff.) to bring salvation to the world. He was to fulfil the kingly role of David by bringing justice to all nations. He was to continue the work of Moses by teaching to the nations the justice, law and religious principles for which they had been waiting. He was to exercise a prophetic role by pointing out to the peoples that they were blinded and imprisoned by their sins and that he could open their eyes and set them free. The statement that the Servant was to be a covenant of the people' is rather obscure. It probably means that it was his task to make the God of the covenant and his promises known to the peoples and to remind them of their obligations to God.
God will extend his plan of salvation to the world through his Servant who will accomplish his task peacefully and modestly, without violence or pressure. What is said about the Servant here was fulfilled in a most remarkable way by Jesus who came not to observed but to serve and to offer salvation to all men.
The Responsorial Psalm which is taken from Psalm 28 sings the praises of Yahweh who 'sits as king for ever'. God's voice from heaven which begets awe and reverence and an attitude of prayer is poetically described as thunder (cf. Job 37 :2-5; 40 :9). The Lord who is said to be 'on the immensity of waters' or 'enthroned over the flood' is the God who dwells above the firmament -of heaven. Therefore when the psalmist says that God's voice resounds on the waters he is expressing the same idea as the evangelist who says (see today's Gospel) in less poetic language that 'a voice came from heaven'.
