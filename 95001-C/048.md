# An epiphany feast

Like the feast of the Solemnity of the Mother of God and the Epiphany, today's feast is also an 'epiphany', a manifestation of the divinity of Jesus, and a particularly solemn manifestation in that the divine voice from heaven declared: 'You are my Son, the Beloved; my favour rests on you' (Lk 3:22).
Each of the readings contributes to this manifestation in a different way. The first reading might be described as a prelude. It is a prophecy about the Servant of God, who is seen to be a figure of the Messiah. We hear a divine declaration about this mysterious character: 'Hear is my servant whom I uphold, my chosen one in whom my soul delights' (Is 42:1). Do we not hear more telling resonances in Matthew's account of the divine voice from heaven after Jesus' baptism: 'This is my Son, the Beloved; my favour rests on him' (Mt 3:17). The prophet in the first reading goes on: 'I have endowed him with my spirit' (Is 42:2). The gospel account expresses this in a graphic image: 'the Holy Spirit descended upon him in bodily shape, like a dove'. The dove, the symbol of peace, also sums up the description of the Servant in the prophecy. Whilst it is the kingly role of the Servant that is given emphasis in the prophecy: the 'chosen one' who will bring 'true justice', nevertheless, it is with gentleness that he carries out his mission: 'He does not break the crushed reed, nor quench the wavering flame' (Is 42:3). Jesus spoke of himself in like manner: 'learn of me, I am meek and humble of heart', and in his messianic entry into Jerusalem went 'humble, riding on an ass'.
The baptism of Jesus was, as it were, the official investiture of his mission as Saviour. Both the Father and the Holy Spirit verify his credentials. The events following his baptism serve to present him to the people so that the people will receive him and his message. We are told elsewhere that to those who did receive him 'he gave power to become children of God' (Jn 1:12).
Peter's account to Cornelius of the beginning of Jesus' mission is similar to the gospel account of the baptism of Jesus and brings the Isaian prophecy to fulfilment. The same elements are present: God anointed Jesus with the Holy Spirit and with power, and because God was with him he went about doing good. The good that he did was to cure those who had fallen under the power of the devil. 'Cure' is a significant word, since in Israel sickness was seen as an external manifestation of sin. To cure the sick and those under the power of evil was to make them whole again. Jesus bestowed upon them that shalom, the peace in the sense of wholesomeness, which is God's gift to those who are in right relationship with him. This was the peace that Christ promised: 'Peace I bequeath to you, my own peace I give you, a peace the world cannot give, this is my gift to you' (Jn 14:27).
Little wonder that the early Christians saw in Jesus the fulfilment of the Suffering Servant.
