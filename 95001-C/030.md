# The first Servant Song

First Reading (Is 42:1-4,6-7). This passage is the first of a series of oracles which scholars refer to as Songs of the Suffering Servant, who is a 'chosen one' of God, like Moses or David. As the Servant the subject of the song fulfils the role of the Davidic, Messianic king, and it is this kingly prerogative that is emphasizes in this passage. But the Servant stands in stark contrast to contemporary military attitudes, for it is with modesty that he fulfils his mission to bring 'true justice'. The description of salvation which the Servant will bring to humankind amounts to the removal of suffering in general. The opening of the eyes of the blind represents an end to suffering that is due to humankind's creaturely status, whereas the setting free of captives marks an end to people's suffering at the hands of other human beings. This Servant Song has been interpreted in Christian tradition as referring to the anointing and the mission of Christ the Messiah.
