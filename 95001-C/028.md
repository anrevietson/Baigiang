# The baptism of Jesus

Gospel (Luke 3:15-16,21-22). There is no explicit mention in the Bible of the precise date in history in which the proclamation of the good news spoken of in the first reading was first made by Second Isaiah to the exiles in Babylon. However, in a central section of the book (Isa 45:1) Cyrus is mentioned by name as God's chosen instrument for the salvation of Israel. This allows us to date Second Isaiah's gospel proclamation at about 539 B. C., shortly before the conqueror became master of Babylon.
Luke is at pains to situate precisely in history the beginning of the Baptist's preaching. This occurred in the fifteenth year of Tiberius Caesar's reign (Lk 3:1), i.e. A.D. 28 or 29, depending on the method of calculating one believes Luke was using. Thus, a little over half a millennium after the first proclamation by the Holy Spirit through Second Isaiah in Babylon, that same Holy Spirit inspired John the Baptist to live in expectation of its fulfilment - and this, too, in the desert, in keeping with what Isaiah was believed to have said. With the Baptist a significant moment in the course of the revelation of God's plan had been reached.
God would make this clearer still at the baptism of Jesus. It came in a moment of prayer and meditation and would be understood in later centuries in the same spirit of contemplation. Jesus, Luke tells us, joined the line of penitents and was baptised by John. Luke, true to his special interest in the prayer of Jesus (LK 3:21;6;6;6;12;9;18,28-29;11:1;22:41) notes that after his baptism Jesus was in prayer. In this prayer, at his baptism, anew age was perceived as having dawned. Heaven was seen as opened. God's loving care for humanity was now present in a very special way. The Father makes known to Jesus, and to all who read Luke's account, that he is the promised Saviour, God's beloved Son, in whom the Father takes delight.
Jesus will need this awareness of the Father's loving care in his trials and moments of agony. Jesus' baptism was a significant event in his entire ministry, as he preached the Gospel half a millenniun after the first proclamation of the good news by Second Isaiah.
Christian baptism should be looked on for what it is - in the line of the baptism of Christ. Like Jesus, we too receive the Holy Spirit at our baptism.
Like him we too have the ups and downs which are part and parcel of human existence. Our baptism bids us look back through history to the great vision of Second Isaiah and to the fulfilment-experience at baptism of Jesus. It invites us move forward with the conviction that God's grace is with us, and with the whole human race, for whose salvation Jesus came among us and
was baptised as one of our sinful race.
