# John's messianic preaching

Gospel (Lk 3:15-16,21-22). The gospel reading speaks about two things: the messianic preaching of John the Baptist, and the events following Jesus' baptism by John in the Jordan. John the Baptist had caused a great stir among the people by his preaching, so much so that the was a belief abroad that he might, indeed, be the long awaited Messiah. John puts the record straight by declaring categorically that he is not the Christ. He baptizes with water, but one greater than he will come who will baptize with the Holy Sprit and with fire. The differences between the baptisms is not in the elements but in the effects. John's baptism is a ritual sign that expresses outwardly a symbolic cleansing that must be achieved inwardly. Jesus' baptism, however, is definitive; it brings salvation (through the Holy Sprit) and judgment (symbolised by 'fire'). Though all four gospels mention Jesus' baptism by John, the descent of the spirit upon him and the voice from heaven, only Luke tells us that the Holy Sprit came upon Jesus while he was at prayer. The coming of the Sprit ushers in the new era spoken of by the prophet Malachi, while the voice from heaven declares Jesus to be Son of God, and messianic king. The new era ushered in by Jesus will be continued by his church, upon whom also will be poured out the Holy Spirit while the community is at prayer at Pentecost.
