# The end of the Christmas season

Today's feast marks the end of the Christmas season and the beginning of Ordinary Time. It commemorates the inauguration of Jesus' earthly ministry, when, anointed by the Spirit, Jesus brings good news to the poor and sets humankind free from the power of evil.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
As we commemorate Jesus' baptism for mission, let us reflect upon our own baptism, thank God for its privileges and ask his forgiveness for the times we have not lived up to its responsibilities.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 42:1-4,6-7). This reading speaks of God's Servant, specially endowed with his Spirit to bring justice to the nations. Christian tradition sees this prophecy as being fulfilled in the person of Christ.
Second Reading (Acts l0:34-38). This reading is part of Peter's sermon to Cornelius in which the baptism of Jesus is presented as an anointing with the Holy Spirit and power, so that, as God's agent, he goes about doing good and destroying the power of evil.
Gospel (Lk 3:15-16,21-22). Today's gospel speaks about the messianic preaching of John the Baptist, and the events that followed the baptism of Jesus: the Holy Spirit came upon him and he was declared by God to be his beloved Son.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: By our baptism we have been incorporated into Christ and made heirs to the Kingdom of Heaven. Let us, then, turn to our heavenly Father and ask him for our needs and the needs of others.
Reader(s): For all the baptised: that they may be faithful to their baptismal promises and carry out their baptismal responsibilities to bear witness to Christ in their lives.
For all Christian Churches: that, united in a common baptism they may unite in Christian commitment to be a common force for good in the world.
For the Pope and for all our spiritual leaders who have been entrusted with the care of those baptised into the body of Christ.
For all who are preparing for baptism: that they may bear this privilege with honour and carry out its responsibilities with ardour.
For those who have gone before us marked with the sign of faith: in baptism they died with Christ; may they also share his resurrection.
President: Lord God, hear the prayers of your faithful people, made with humble and sincere hearts, and offered through Christ our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE OUR FATHER
Through our baptism we are made children of God. Let us, therefore, pray to the Father in the words that Jesus, His Son and our brother, taught us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Through our baptism we have been incorporated into Christ who promised peace to his disciples. Let us offer one another a sign of that peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Jesus came into the world in order to reconcile humankind with God, and through his redeeming death to free us from the power of Satan. In the Eucharist he left us a memorial of that redeeming death his body broken and his blood poured out for the forgiveness of sins. Through our baptism we are baptised into his death so that, as he rose from the dead, we too, might rise to new life in Christ with all our sins forgiven. Blessed be Jesus in the most holy sacrament of the altar.
