# The great commission

The Gospel (Mt. 28:16-20). The passage is so well known that it scarcely needs comment. However, only by setting it in its larger New Testament context can we see its full meaning. The passage, let us recall, recounts an appearance of the Risen Saviour. The Christ the apostles now see is no longer the Jesus who a short time before walked with them through Galilee. He is now the risen and exalted Saviour. All things have already been placed under his feet. He is now Lord; all power in heaven and earth had been given to him. Christ, the epistle to the Ephesians tells us, ascended in glory to God's right hand 'to fill all things' (Eph. 4 :10), to bring everything together under himself as head (Eph. 1:10), head of a new humanity, of a community constituted through faith which is the acceptance of this new divine life, the life of the Trinity itself. Hence it is that Christ, in the present Gospel passage, sends forth his disciples to preach and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Trinity. But baptism cannot be all. The new community intended by Christ can only become a reality by a genuine Christian life, one conscious of its dignity as sons of God and of the obligations arising from this. When Christ tells his disciples, and through them the teaching authority of the Church, to teach men to observe all the commands he has given, he is not replacing Jewish law with a new Christian legalism. By all is meant the entirety of that rich Christian teaching (some of which we have already noted) on what constitutes genuine Christian life. And it is to this rich teaching we should inculcate today, in obedience to Christ's command. Baptism in the name of the Trinity means all this. The dignity of being the Church of the Triune God is no small thing.
