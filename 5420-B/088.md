# We are God's children

Second Reading (1 Jn. 3 :1-2). We find expressed here an important Johannine and Pauline teaching - the Christian's sonship of God. Because of the covenant by which they were bound closely to God, Israel had been called God's son in the Old Testament. But in the New Testament there is a realisation of just how intimate our relation with him is. Like Jesus himself we can address him as 'Abba'. We can love and trust him as a Father and he loves and cares for us as a Father. During his public life, Jesus spoke several times of this fatherly providence of God.
We are not only called God's children. We are in fact so. John teaches that faith is a prerequisite for becoming children of God (Jn. 1:12). St Paul tells us that it is the gift of the Spirit that effectively makes us sons of God (Rom. 8 :14-17; Gal. 4 :6). The Holy Spirit, the Spirit of God, the Spirit of Christ, unites us with Jesus and so makes us sons and heirs with him. However, like St Paul, John teaches that the full meaning of this sonship is not yet clear to us. Now we see God in faith; the eschatological vision will be much more intimate and clear. We already share in the life of God, in the glorious life of the risen Christ but this life will not be fully manifest until the parousia.
There is. a passing reference to the hostility shown to Christians by those who have rejected the truth of God revealed in Christ. The example of Jesus, who despite hostility conquered and was glorified is an encouragement to all who are persecuted.
