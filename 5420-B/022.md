# Children of God

Second Reading (Rom. 8:14-17). 'May they all be one. Father may they be one in us, as you are in me and I am in you. I have given them the glory that you gave to me, that they may be one as we are one.' The new life brought by Christ is a participation of the very life of the Trinity. Nor is this participation intended to be something merely ontological - the elevation of the soul through grace to a new level of existence - without any awareness of this on man's part. Christ's words should make us acutely aware that this is far from being the case. The source and model of Christian life is the very life of the Trinity itself. The life and glory which he has from the Father, Christ has communicated to believers. And his desire and prayer are that the life of those who believe in him be, and be seen to be, a real participation in the life of God himself - Christ living in Christians as the Father lives in Christ.
Today's second reading develops this point. True and living Christianity is grounded in an awareness of sonship. And this awareness is a gift of the Holy Spirit, the third person of the Blessed Trinity. 'Abba, Father' was first and foremost the prayer of Christ himself - in the Garden of Gethsemane (Mark 14:36). Only he, because he was really Son of God, could thus pray to the Father. We 'make bold' to call God 'Our Father' only because Christ has taught us and told us to do so. We are reminded of this truth in the liturgy of the Mass. Divine sonship for men means that they are made brothers and co-heirs of Christ. The privilege is as great as this. It is a dignity to which human nature could not aspire. Man's natural attitude before God is one of fear, a fear that makes man prey to his lower instincts, a fear which leads to superstitious rites in an effort to placate the deity. And into this fear even the Christian falls unless he keeps the new order instituted by Christ before his mind.
It is the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of Jesus, that comes to the aid of man's weakness. By keeping alive in him the spirit of sonship and divine friendship, he banishes this servile fear from the soul. It is the Holy Spirit, and he alone, who can make Christ's vision and prayer become a reality that the community of believers may be one, a united body, by living the life of the Trinity.
The Holy Spirit alone can bring about genuine renewal, by leading the Church to an awareness of its true nature and dignity, by inspiring it to so arrange its laws and institutions that the spirit of sonship, of belonging, and the peace and joy that flow from this may become a reality and show to man that in God alone will his soul find rest.
