# The one God

First Reading (Deut 4:32-34,39-40). Writing during the time of the exile the author of this passage focuses on Israel's covenantal relationship with Yahweh. While exile embodies the broken covenant, it also catches up the possibility of covenant renewal. To that end the author challenges his audience to review their history and act upon it.
The author presents Moses offering a vision all the way to creation and then back across history. The audience must indeed conclude that the Exodus and Sinai were very special occasions in which Yahweh entered into a most intimate relationship with Israel. God's involvement also betokens the involvement of Israel. Israel must renew its oath of allegiance by recognising Yahweh as Lord, no one else. It is this sense of family loyalty that must characterise Israel's everyday activity. By accepting covenant with this God, Israel must demonstrate its implications.
Second Reading Rom 8:14-17). In this passage Paul defines a Christian as one who is led by the Spirit. However, he does make some distinctions. Christians are not slaves - because of God's Spirit they cannot possess the minds of a slave; the Spirit is a liberating agent. At the same time Christians are not Gods natural sons and daughters --they are adopted. Indeed it is the Spirit who provides for Christians' special relationship with Christ and the Father. Because of the Spirit Christians are empowered to call God Abba or Father.
Christians also become God's heirs and heiresses. While Christ is the natural heir, Christians are heirs and heiresses through adoption. 'land in hand with this special status goes family living. Christians are to suffer with Christ in order to be glorified with him, For Paul Christians participate in those very acts through which salvation is achieved.
