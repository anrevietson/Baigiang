# Against the exploitation of the poor

First Reading (Amos 8:4-7). Amos preached in the northern kingdom of Israel in the eighth century B.C. during a time of great economic expansion and prosperity. However, there was an ever-widening chasm between the wealthy few and the destitute masses. In the section preceding today's pericope (8:1-3) Amos announced the fall of the northern kingdom. In today's passage he pronounces a judgement speech against (vv. 4-6) Israel: indictment and the announcement of judgement (v.7). Those charged are the oppressors of the poor.
The prophet makes the judgment all the more graphic by quoting the greedy conversation of the oppressors. They are so avid to resume their unjust trading practices that they look forward to the end of the liturgical celebrations to conduct 'business as usual.' They decrease the capacity of the dry measure (ephah) but increase the standard weight (shekel) used in working out the purchasing price. To make matters worse, their commercial ventures include people, i.e., they are eager to trade fellow Israelites. It is hardly surprising that the Lord takes an oath never to forget such abominable sins. That oath is as inflexible as Israel's pride.
