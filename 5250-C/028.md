# Using wealth, talents and gifts

Today's celebration emphasizes our manner of using wealth, talents, and gifts. As we reflect on the readings, we should be aware that our use of these realities and our worship with the community must go hand in hand. Our Eucharist must impact our procedures outside church.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must humbly admit that we have not always God's blessings for the benefit of others. Realizing our faults, we ask for our God's forgive-ness.
You reveal yourself to us in the needs and problems of the poor. Lord, have mercy.
You call us to promote the well-being of our sisters and brothers. Christ, have mercy.
You call us to consider the plight and concern of the community. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Amos 8:4-7). Amos decries the economic situation in Israel. The wealthy have oppressed the poor and must be indicted for violation of covenant.
Second Reading (1 Tim 2:1-8). The author states that God wills the salvation of all people. To support this, he points to the oneness of God and the redeeming death of Jesus, the one mediator between God and people.
Gospel (Lk 16:1-13). Luke has Jesus tell the parable of the dishonest manager. This manager becomes a model for Christians, not because of his dishonesty, but because of his prudence in handling a crisis.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Realizing through God's word that we are to use our gifts and treasures for all of God's people, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all civil and religious leaders. May they use their office and their talents wisely so that they advance the condition of the poor and hurting.
We pray for our local faith community. May we be the agents of both justice and charity to each other so that we promote the common good, not purely individual goals.
We pray for all those throughout the world who do not experience a truly human standard of living. May we make others aware of their plight and assist them with our time and talent.
We pray for the members of our own families. May we strengthen
our common bond by recognizing their problems and bringing our resources to assist them.
President: Father, you shower us with gifts so that we, in turn, may enrich others. Help us to live by the example of your Son so that in some measure we may be able to give ourselves for others. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we ask God to grant us our daily bread. We are challenged to see ourselves as distributors of this daily bread by our practice of both justice and charity. To that degree we are God's honest managers.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In our sign of peace we wish our sisters and brothers peace of mind, heart, and body. In offering that sign, we pledge ourselves as agents of that peace. If our wish is to be genuine, we must enflesh it with concrete demonstrations of concern and assistance.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
In sharing the Eucharist, we unite ourselves to the world of pain and joy of our sisters and brothers. To eat the bread and drink the cup is to express our willingness to use our time, talent, and treasure for the members of the community. To that extent genuine Eucharist must have repercussions that take place outside church or sanctuary, To honour our God is to meet the needs of our God's extended family in the entire gamut of human relationships.
