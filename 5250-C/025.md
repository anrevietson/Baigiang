# Against the exploitation of the poor

First Reading: (Amos 8 :4-7). It is true of most of the prophets that they are anti-monarchical, in the sense that they look upon the monarchy (north and south) as the source of Israel's ills and hanker for the days when Israel was a true federation ruled by a covenant, of which a primary demand was justice for all its people. The monarchy brought with it royal courts and attendant ills:
patronage, greed, bribery and corruption. Prophets were like our Saints, raised up to counter evils of the time. Amos is probably the earliest of all the written prophets; he had been a shepherd, but God 'called' him to go and speak against the evils of Jeroboam' s reign, especially the evils of semi-paganism and injustice to the poor.
The strong phrasing, 'trampling the needy', 'wiping out the poor ' is characteristic of Amos. The rich cannot wait for the end of the new moon feast (cf. Num. 28 :11-15) or the sabbath (both of them 'days of rest') before they are off to sell their stocks of grain. And they are not only greedy but fraudulent. Three kinds of fraud are mentioned; reducing the capacity of the ephah (a quantity about equivalent to a bushel), increasing the weight of the shekel and tricking with the scales. The victims are the poor (v.6, also in 2 :6 with a slightly different context). 'Buying the poor for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals' here means buying them into slavery. Selling the 'refuse of the wheat' - i.e. the trash instead of the real thing. Verse 7 threatens God's retribution
'Yahweh has sworn by the pride of Jacob (i.e. by himself), "Surely I will never forget any of their deeds".'
