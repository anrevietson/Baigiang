# Slaves of righteousness

First Reading (Rom 6:12-18). Once again Paul makes ample use of contrast, but this time between sin and obedience, between slavery to the law and slavery" to righteousness. It may sometimes seem strange to modern logic that Paul equates sin with observance of law. However, when it is remembered that he is trying to present the Good News in a milieu where there was an overemphasis on a blind, eternal observance of many rituals and rites that had lost their connection with any intrinsic moral value, it becomes clear that Paul is not advocating a Christian lifestyle without moral values or without some kind of "law". In trying to insist that no amount of eternal observance of the law will entitle us to God's mercy, he goes so far as to equate that kind of self-righteousness with sin. And sin it is, for how can we ever know our need of God, if we are preoccupied in trying to do it all ourselves, and effectively cancel him out of our lives?
