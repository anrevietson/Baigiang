# New heavens and a new earth

Second Reading (2 Pet 3:8-14). Peter, probably written around the turn of the first century, was written for a pluralistic Church where some embers were of Christian-Jewish background and others were Greek converts. Well written with good attention to technical intellectual terms its main thrust is the pastoral concern for those in an urban setting.
In this reading the author continues his defence of the parousia traditions. He accuses the scoffers of wilfully overlooking biblical truths; so, a' propos the delay of Christ's coming, he reminds them of the saying that a thousand years are like one day with the Lord (Ps 90:4). Since God's timetable is mysterious to us it is ridiculous that the scoffers should keep harping on the delay of the parousia. Balancing the remark about the delay being an opportunity for repentance is the sure but unknowable coming of Jesus.
What we believe affects our lives. We are to live lives of holiness and
reverence so as to stand on the final day.
