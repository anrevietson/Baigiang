# The beginning of the good news

Gospel (Mark 1:1-8). Has ever so solemn an event been so simply introduced? 'The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God' (RSV), Or as) interpreted in the Jerusalem Bible, 'The beginning of the good news about Jesus Christ the Son of God.' The good news, the gospel, in one sense is Jesus Christ himself, the fulfilment of Israel's hope. The beginning of this good news began with the preaching of John the Baptist who solemnly proclaimed that what was promised in the book of Isaiah (see first reading) had at last become a reality. John was thus the messenger who according to Malachi 3:1 would precede the advent of the great day of the Lord (messianic times). He was also the person who would fulfil the task of announcing the good tidings to Judah and Jerusalem that her God was here, that the kingdom of her God had come. And, Mark tells us, Judea and Jerusalem came to him to hear his message. The messenger spoken of by Malachi was in Christ's day considered to be none other than the prophet Elijah (cf. Mark 9:11) and John's peculiar clothing would, in fact, immediately bring the person of this great prophet to mind (cf. 2 Kings
1:8). John preached in the desert. There Israel would again meet her God and from there she would have a new beginning. His message was simple: 'Repent'; turn aside from sin to the true 8ervice of God. His baptism was for the forgiveness of Sin. Only the pure of heart can see God. Only they could recognise the presence of God in the work of Jesus. God's kingdom is a kingdom of grace and holiness, a kingdom of truth. Its advent must be prepared for by repentance which means turning aside from sin and all that goes with it. In this sense Christ's kingdom is always coming and John's call is thus always valid.
