# The sinner who turns away from sin

First reading (Ezek. 18:21-28). Ezekiel's words were written at a time when political disaster had brought about the ruin of his nation and shattered his people's religious institutions. Many who considered themselves just in God's eyes asked how they could continue to worship Yahweh who gave victory to the Babylonians in order to punish the Jews for sins they had not themselves committed. This way of thinking was based on the belief of many of Ezekiel's contemporaries that God punished the innocent for the guilt of their ancestors. This belief was expressed in the proverb quoted earlier (18:2) by Ezekiel and known also from Jeremiah (31:29): 'The fathers have eaten unripe grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge.' Ezekiel teaches that this belief is wrong. God does not punish one man for another man's sin. No one can say 'The way of the Lord is not just.' Each individual stands alone before God. His spiritual destiny depends on his own actions which will be judged by God who demands both the perseverance of the just and the repentance of the sinner.
Responsorial psalm (Ps. 129). This is one of the most familiar of the Church's penitential psalms. It is the plea of a sinner who knows the helplessness of his situation. He does not lose hope because he knows the mercy of God. He eagerly awaits the divine intervention that will save him and he finds joy in the thought that his sin will be blotted out.
