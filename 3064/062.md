# The Book of Glory

Gospel (Jn 13:1- 15).With chapter 13 of the gospel, John begins the Book of Glory where Jesus shares intimately with 'his own,' the disciples. This beautifully reflective and challenging section (Jn 13-20) begins with the washing of the feet, a custom that took place in every Jewish home as a sign of welcome and hospitality. This washing of the feet would take place as guests entered the home. They would place their sandalled feet in the water trough at the door. John places this action within the centre of the Last Supper and uses this prophetic act in place of the words of institution of the Eucharist.
The passage begins with a time flame: 'before the festival of Passover.' According to the account Jesus dies before the feast, on the day of preparation (Jn 19:14), at the time when the Passover lambs are slain and prepared for the feast. This sacrificial symbolism is important in the early Church's understanding of Jesus' death.
The text also refers to 'the hour,' a term used by John in the Book of Signs (Jn 1-12) to anticipate this culminating point in Jesus' life and ministry. Within the context of Passover and 'the hour' of suffering and glorification, Jesus demonstrates his love for his disciples. The new commandment of love fills this section of the gospel, drawing all believers into the very love Jesus and the Father share. However, when John describes Jesus' love, he uses the phrase 'to the end' or 'to the utmost,' an example of twofold meaning. Then he supplies an example of this faithful love in the foot washing, a sign of humility and service. Within the Jewish community not even a slave was required to wash the masters' feet Occasionally, a disciple would perform this service for a teacher or rabbi. However, the fact that Jesus presents himself as a servant by performing this action has great significance for the disciples. Rather than assume a position of honour and prestige, Jesus shows the style of leadership and love he expects among his disciples - one that witnesses to mutual humble service. 'I have given you an example' Jesus says, and then he challenges disciples to do what he has done.
Peter misunderstands the action of Jesus and asks for complete cleansing. Jesus' comment 'You are clean' refers to the atonement and new life he will bring through his suffering and death. In fact, the unfolding meaning of Jesus' prophetic action will continually challenge the disciples in their belief and action. If Jesus as teacher and Lord humbles himself, all those connected with Jesus must do likewise. If this action and the sacrifice that follows brings new life and salvation, then we can offer a new model of relationship to the world. For John, the Eucharistic words do not need to be restated. The early Church knows that tradition from other sources. What the community needs is this model of service and love in order to renew its commitment to Jesus' style of leadership. Only if disciples accept this dimension of service do they have a share in the fullness of life that Jesus brings.
