# The institution of the Eucharist

Second Reading (1 Cor 11:23-26). Paul received this tradition on the institution of the Eucharist at his conversion, which probably occurred AD 36, some six or three years after the institution itself. The chain of tradition, of course, goes back to the event itself. Paul's text is brief and touching, and indicative of what the Eucharist itself is the memorial. It is manifestation of Jesus' immense love for humanity, showing that he loved his own to the end. In proclaiming his death, celebration of the eucharist brings to mind all that this stood for and effected: God's love, the forgiveness of sin, the call to repentance and forgiveness of others. Written into the record of the Institution is the sad circumstances of its first celebration: It was 'on the same night that he (Jesus) was betrayed'. This, too, is part of the remembrance. All human life is part of the Eucharist, just as it is with the central figure, the One who through his suffering in Gethsemane, and indeed before and after it, had been made a compassionate and understanding high priest.
