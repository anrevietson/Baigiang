# The Passover rules

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8.11-14). This passage of the Book of Exodus prescribes the rules for the celebration of the festival of Passover which commemorates annually the escape of the Israelites from the slavery of Egypt. The climax of the festival is the family meal, at which all the elements are invested with a symbolic value For instance, the roasted lamb represents the sacrifice offered for the redemption of the Israelites; the unleavened bread symbolizes the haste with which they eventually left Egypt; the bitter herbs evoke the bitterness of the servitude in Egypt; the girded loins, sandled feet and staff in hand symbolize the preparedness of the Israelites to leave Egypt. Through the ritual enactment of the original Passover experience Jews of all generations do not simply remember with their minds what happened to their ancestors in the past, they actually participate in that experience, as if they were present at the original Exodus.
