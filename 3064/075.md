# The institution of the Eucharist

Second Reading (1 Cor. 11:23-26). Paul solemnly passes on a tradition which, he asserts, goes back to the words of Jesus himself; in fact, he is passing on an established liturgical formula. The command to repeat the action (v.24) does not merely bind the community to celebrate the Lord's Supper regularly and thus to keep alive the meaning of the death of Jesus, but places upon it the obligation to proclaim the redemptive meaning of this death. Paul spells it 6ut in his explanatory note in v.26: the Lord's Supper which Christians celebrate now is directly connected with the return of Christ, is a preparation for the parousia. The body of Christ, present on the Eucharist table, is in fact that body of the risen Christ, his glorious body, transformed by the power of the Spirit. The first covenant was ratified by blood (Exod. 24 :3-8; Lev. 1:5); Jesus now declares: 'The wine contained in this cup is my blood which establishes and ratifies the new covenant.'
