# Three significant days

This Holy Thursday Celebration marks the beginning of three significant days that allow the Christian community to remember the last events of Jesus' life. In these events, we as a community of faith recall the central mysteries that we profess.
On the night before he died, Jesus shared a meal with his disciples and friends. During that meal, reminiscent of the Passover celebration, Jesus gave us the promise of his continued presence when we bless, offer, and share the bread and wine in the assembly. He gave us further signs of his love in his humble service, symbolized in the washing of the feet. As we gather in faith, and re-enact the rituals of this feast, we will see and hear how the Lord shows his love for us and how we can express our love for one another.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
As we gather around the table of the Lord, we thank the Lord for his gift of new life, his continued presence and his special love.
Lord Jesus, you share a meal with your disciples emphasizing for us the value of hospitality and celebration. Lord, have mercy.
Christ Jesus, you express your love in acts of service giving us a model to follow. Christ, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you approach your hour in the company of your disciples showing us the importance of support and friendship in difficult times. Lord, have mercy.

HEADING FOR READINGS
First Reading (Ex 12:1-8.11-14). This reading from the Book of Exodus describes the Passover ritual, an ancient feast for the Jewish community. The symbolism of blood, sacrifice, unleavened bread, and bitter herbs, anticipates the Christian mysteries we celebrate today. The people remember God's action on their behalf.
Second Reading (1 Cor 11:23-26). Paul hands on the tradition he received regarding Jesus' action during the Last Supper. This reading contains the words of institution of the Eucharist spoken during a meal Jesus shared with his disciples.
Gospel (Jn 13:1-15). The gospel focuses on another dimension of Jesus' word and action during the Last Supper - the washing of the feet and its meaning. This act of service replaces the words of institution of Eucharist in John's gospel and believers are to remember this action and do likewise.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As we come to the table of the Lord, we remember our needs and offer our requests to the Lord.
Response: Lord, hear us.
Reader(s): For the Church: that we may be a sign of love to the world around us. We pray to the Lord.
For all Christian leaders: that they may take Jesus, the servant leader, as their model. We pray to the Lord.
For the people of God: that we may reflect the Lord's love in our mutual love and service. We pray to the Lord.
For families: that we may create an atmosphere of hospitality, generosity and care as we welcome others to our homes. We pray to the Lord.
For those who share at the table of the Lord: that we will be a sign to all of our union as the body of Christ. We pray to the Lord.
President: God of love, we pray that you hear our requests and strengthen us in our response to the gift of salvation, given to us in Jesus your Son. This we pray in Jesus' name.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As we remember the special meal Jesus celebrated with his friends, we offer our prayer in the words he gave us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Jesus washed the feet of his disciples using a gesture of welcome and hospitality, as a sign of his love. As we offer the Lord's peace to one another let it be a sign of our love and of our commitment to serve one another.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
As we remember the meal the Lord shared with his friends and the wonderful sign of his love in his role as a servant, we realize that we share communion with one another in him. Let us reflect on how the strength we receive at the Lord's table can be expressed in loving service during these holy days.
