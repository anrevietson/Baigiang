# The Christian assembly

Second Reading (1 Cor 11:23-26). In 1 Corinthians 11, Paul focuses on the Christian assembly, offering challenges and directions to a community that finds difficulty in living Out the gospel message of equality and mutual love. Within this context Paul hands on the tradition he received regarding the Lord's Supper and the words of institution. This tradition, known in the early Church, forms the second reading.
The early community assembled for a meal or agape' and in this context remembered the meal shared by Jesus and his disciples, the night before he died. This meal became ritualised as believers gathered together and used psalms, hymns, actions, and gestures to express their belief. The Lord's Supper, like baptism, became a boundary-sitting ritual that separated believers from unbelievers in this early period of Church life. Passed on by Paul, the apostles, and believers, the heart of the tradition conveys the word and actions of Jesus with striking simplicity.
Paul's account of Eucharist is the closest to that of Luke (22:15-20), while Mark (14:22-25) and Matthew (26:26-29) share similarities. These four accounts are the only ones we have in the New Testament writings because John's gospel docs not contain the words of institution. The traditions reflect a sacrificial element, the celebration as covenant meal, the memorial and eschatological dimensions, and the Passover symbolism taken to new heights.
What Jesus docs is 'for your sake' or 'for you' reflecting the functional dimension of Paul's christology. What Jesus docs through his sacrifice, the shedding of his blood, is to bring salvation and new life to his disciples: 'This cup is the new covenant in my blood.' By using these words, the Church reflects its conviction that the new covenant is established in Christ. It also recalls the prophetic words of Jeremiah (31:31), thereby connecting the traditions of Judaism and Christianity. The bread, taken, blessed, broken, and received, and the wine, shared around the table, proclaim the new life in Christ that comes through his death on the cross. By using symbols of Israel's deliverance from bondage, the Church confirms the full meaning of Christ's deliverance. Word and actions form the ritual - a prophetic word and act that anticipate the fullness of Christ's coming in the end times.
This celebration around the table of the Lord focuses on the body of Christ. When Jesus utters the word, 'This is my body,' he speaks in the presence of the disciples whom Paul understands to also be his body. In this passage, we see two levels of meaning - the presence of Christ, in the elements of the celebration and in the community of believers, both the body of Christ.
