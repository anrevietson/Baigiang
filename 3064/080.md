# The Passover

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8,11-14). This section of the Book of Exodus gives detailed directions on how the chosen people were to celebrate the Passover. The feast was so called because it recalled the time in Egypt when God's angel had passed over those houses of the Israelites who had applied the blood of the paschal lamb to their door posts. There are five components of the Jew's celebration which retain major significance for Christians in their celebration of the Lord's Supper. First, it must be a community celebration, at least a large family; smaller groups should join together as a community, a representative group of the grateful people of the world. Second, the sacrifice is of a lamb, in fact the leading ram of the flock, offered in sacrifice for the fruitfulness of the people. Thus, Jesus the new Adam is sacrificed in death for the redemption of us all. Thirdly, the blood of the lamb protects the people from death, and Jesus protects us from the death of sin. Fourthly, the lamb is sacrificed on the evening before the feast. John's gospel, in which Jesus is the Lamb of God rearranges the time of Jesus' death so that it coincides with the sacrifice of the paschal lamb. Fifth, the people celebrate dressed and ready as if for a journey. We come to the table of the Lord to be nourished and strengthened for life's journey.
