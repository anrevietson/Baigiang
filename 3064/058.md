# The Passover festival

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8,11-14). In the Passover festival three elements are combined. There is the memory of the last of the plagues of Egypt, a plague which this time takes human lives. The account emphasises its miraculous selectivity in the death of the first-born. There is more than a hint of two ancient spring festivals. The slaughter of the one year old lamb may recall the break-up of the nomadic shepherd camp for the move to spring pastures. The blood sprinkled on the tent-poles drives away evil powers. The lamb is roasted over the fire as the full moon lights the desert and eaten in readiness for the journey, with wild, not cultivated herbs. The feast of unleavened bread was an agricultural festival where the first fruits of the barley harvest were enjoyed. The feast was combined with Passover at a certain stage and the details of the account reflect the way the feast was celebrated afterwards in Israel.
