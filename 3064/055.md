# The beginning of the Easter Triduum

This evening's celebration begins the Easter Triduum - three days especially dedicated to remembering and reliving the final events of our Lord's life and the culminating expressions of his love for humankind.
On the evening before his passion, Jesus celebrated one last meal with his friends. It was on, or close to, the day when faithful Jews celebrated the Passover, their annual remembrance of how God loved them so much that he freed them from the slavery of Egypt. Jesus uses this occasion to let his disciples know that he loves them so much he will die to free them from the slavery of sin.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We approach this meal of the Lord's love aware of our unworthiness; and to prepare ourselves to accompany the Lord in these difficult days of his suffering we ask for his healing forgiveness.

HEADINGS FOR THE READINGS
First Reading (Ex 12:1-8,11-14). When Moses told the Egyptian Pharaoh to free the enslaved Jews and allow them to return to their own lands, he reinforced his demand with the ten great plagues, used by God to manifest his power to the hardened Pharaoh. The last and greatest of these plagues was the death of every firstborn in Egypt.
Our reading from the Book of Exodus recounts how God, through Moses, told the people to accompany the last plague with a special celebration in which each item would have a symbolic meaning recalling their enslavement and God's gift of freedom. Once freed, the Jews would repeat this Passover meal every year in order to relive and re-celebrate the Lord's loving care of the people.
Second Reading (1 Cor 11:22-26). Jesus used the Jewish Passover feast to emphasize that freeing the chosen people from the slavery of Egypt was an anticipation of the freedom from sin which he would attain for the world.
After Jesus' death for the life of the world, his early disciples would remember that special Last Supper they shared with him and would frequently repeat it when they gathered for prayer.
In our second reading St Paul instructs the Corinthians on how they ought to celebrate the meal of communion with the Lord.
Gospel (Jn 13:1-15). John's account of the Last Supper is different than Matthew's, Mark's, Luke's or Paul's. Instead of the words of institution of the Eucharist he substitutes the simple gesture of the washing of the disciples feet. Jesus' act of humility, service, and love for his friends symbolically portrays his humility, selfless service, and eternal love for all men and women shown so powerfully in his willingness to die for the redemption of all.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: 0 God our Father, on this day when we celebrate your Son's everlasting love for us all, we turn to you in his name to pray for the world he loved so much.
Response: Lord, hear us.
Reader(s): For the universal Church, that we may be known for the love we share, loving each other as Jesus loved us, we pray to the Lord.
For all Church leaders, that they may live the servant leadership of which Jesus gave the example, we pray to the Lord.
For leaders of all the nations that they may understand Jesus' lesson of humility and selflessness, we pray to the Lord.
For all men and women throughout the world, that they may welcome the redemptive love that Jesus offers, we pray to the Lord.
For our own parish community, gathered around the Lord's table, that we may proclaim his love by our lives, we pray to the Lord.
President: God of love, we pray in the name of your Son who gave himself up to death for us all. Hear these prayers through his name.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As we celebrate the Lord's Last Supper, let us confidently address to the Father the prayer which Jesus taught us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
During his Last Supper Jesus prayed that his disciples would be known by the quality of their love for each other. As a pledge of that love, let us share with each other a sign of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
As the Lord Jesus shared this Last Supper with his friends he also offered them a role in his own ministry for the redemption of the world. In our communion with the Lord let us deepen both our awareness of our ministry with the Lord, and our willingness to accept it
