# The Passover

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8, 11-14). In this reading we have what is regarded as the most recent of the biblical calendars. It is the one that (with the exception of some minor points) regulated the celebration of the Passover in New Testament times. The sacrificial animal was to be chosen on the tenth day of the first month (called Nisan in this calendar; it fell in March/April). It was to be sacrificed on the evening of the fourteenth of Nisan, the great feastday itself being from that evening to the evening of the fifteenth. There would be a full moon that day (as the months began with the new moon). From its earliest celebration the yearly Passover was intended as a commemorantion, a remembrance, of the exodus from Egypt and Israel's beginning as a free nation. Long before New Testament times such remembrance yearly heightened the hope of liberation from Israel's current political masters. In Jesus' day the master was mighty Rome. Jews of New Testament times would have celebrated the Passover with sentiments as varying as their concept of what redemption, liberation, freedom, was.
