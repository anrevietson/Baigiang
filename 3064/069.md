# The Passover meal

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8, 11-14). The Passover meal is the annual celebration and memorial of the most significant event in the history of the Jews: the liberation of the Israelites their ancestors from the slavery of Egypt. Every element of this meal is intended to symbolise and confirm in the memory an aspect of the great event: the young male lamb recalls the first-born of the Israelites who were spared by the sign of the blood of the lamb smeared on the Israelites' houses; the unleavened bread depicts the haste with which the Israelites left Egypt; the bitter herbs evoke the bitterness of the slavery in Egypt; the girded loins, sandled feet and staff in hand dramatise the Israelites' readiness to leave Egypt.
