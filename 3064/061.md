# The Passover

First Reading (Ex 12:1-8.11-14). This section of the Book of Exodus follows the ten plagues (Ex 7-11) and contains the rubrics for the feast of Passover (Ex 12:1-51). The celebration of this feast is a poignant reminder of God's choice of Israel as a people (Ex 3:7), God's guidance in Israel's deliverance from Egypt and during the entire period of wanderings in the desert. It constitutes the heart of Israel's faith experience.
As an ancient celebration, Passover reflects the semi-nomadic pastoral life of the people, with no mention of priest, sanctuaries or altars. Rather, households provide the setting and a roasted animal of either sheep or goat and unleavened bread constitute the elements for this yearly celebration. Israel honours its Lord and remember Yahweh's actions in this special festival that spans generations. Specifically mentioned is the blood rite which establishes the connection between the tenth plague and the Passover. Blood symbolises life and the sacrifice of the lamb reminds the community of its new life as God's chosen people. Another rite is the eating of unleavened bread with bitter herbs. These foods, eaten in haste, are the sustenance of desert life. The Passover symbols will be those that Jesus uses to represent his gift of life to the new community of faith.
Interestingly, the Jewish community celebrates Passover in the home or with another household if the family is small. The early Christians will celebrate the Eucharist in their households as well, using all the symbols and the theological overtones of Passover in their remembrance of the Lord's Supper.
