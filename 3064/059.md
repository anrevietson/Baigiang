# The washing of the feet

Gospel (Jn 13:1-15). This Gospel does not give an account of the institution of the Eucharist. in fact it seems to refer to an event that took place before the Passover (v.1). But then, for John, the Passover, the sacrifice of the Lamb, is the event that takes place on the Cross. Instead there is the foot-washing. The presentation of the foot-washing combines two elements, the sacramental and the moral or ethical. Indeed for John as for Paul (see commentary on the second reading) the sacramental and the moral or ethical dimensions of the event are not separated as they have tended to be in the theological tradition but belong inseparably together. Thus the foot-washing is a demonstration of the practical and unassuming love the disciples must practise towards one another. Peter's refusal to be washed may be seen in this context, in relation to his desire to establish a clear master-servant relationship. But this is to say no to Jesus' life-work, his message of the divine, unassuming love that shows itself in service. Not more profoundly but just as validly there is clearly a sacramental aspect to the foot-washing. The water of purification is a symbol of baptismal incorporation into Christ and of the baptism which Jesus must undergo on the Cross. Peter will also show himself reluctant to accept the Cross. But the Cross is the supreme example of self-emptying love. The ethical and the sacramental elements, the love shown in service and baptism into Christ's death on the Cross, thus belong entirely together.
