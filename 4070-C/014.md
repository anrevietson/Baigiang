# The gift of Christian unity

Today's Celebration places great emphasis on the gift of Christian unity. As we reflect on the readings, we should also be mindful that our very presence at Eucharist as a community should encourage us to foster that sense of unity.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must acknowledge that we have not always promoted unity. Aware of these shortcomings, we ask our God's forgiveness.
You call us to the challenge of unity in our daily lives as members of a family and you enable us to develop the sense of your presence in 0urh0mes Lord, have mercy.
You make us members of the Christian family through the grace of baptism. Christ, have mercy.
You empower us to create unity in a world that often does not recognize your presence. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Acts 7:55-60). The author of Acts describes the death of Stephen, the first Christian martyr. In so doing, he intends to make Stephen a model for Christian missionaries.
Second Reading (Rev 22:12-14.16-17,20). The author of Revelation concludes his work with several warnings and exhortations. He assures his audience that Jesus will come soon. He adds that his community longs for that return.
Gospel (Jn 17:20-26). The author of John has Jesus conclude his high-priestly prayer. He stresses the unity that should exist among believers and Jesus' final wish that such believers should share his company.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Realizing through God's Word that we are to be agents of unity, we confidently address these petitions to the Father through the Son.
Reader(s): We pray for all civil and religious leaders. May they effect genuine unity in their communities by their dedication to duty and self-sacrificing efforts.
We pray for our local faith community. May we strive to make unity a reality by acknowledging God's presence in all our sisters and brothers.
We pray for our work community. May we view our jobs not only as the chance to make a decent wage but also as the faith opportunity to foster our God's presence through compassion and concern.
We pray for the members of our own families. May we see our daily lives as the challenge to foster greater family unity and thus greater awareness of God in our homes.
President: Father, unity is both a gift and a challenge. We duly acknowledge the gift of unity within the Blessed Trinity. We ask that you heed our
prayers by challenging us to incorporate that gift in our daily lives. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we state our willingness to forgive all those who have wronged us. We are powerfully reminded that unity and forgiveness go hand in hand. To forgive is to reopen lines of communication and thus foster genuine unity.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In our sign of peace we express our belief that the members of the worshipping community are indeed our sisters and brothers in Christ. Our sign of peace, therefore, must be a genuine demonstration of that belief and of our charge to forge ever greater unity with those sisters and brothers.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
In sharing the Eucharist, we share the concerns and problems of the community. Paul reminds us: 'If one member suffers, all suffer together; if one member is honoured, all rejoice together' (1 Cor 12:26). To participate in Eucharist is to commit ourselves anew to the needs of the community. By its very nature Eucharist moves us in the direction of unity. To alleviate the pains and aches of a community member is to acknowledge and promote the bond of Christian unity.
