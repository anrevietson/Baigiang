# Come, Lord Jesus

Second Reading (Rev 22:12-14,16-17,20). This is part of the epilogue to the book of Revelation. We hear the last words of the Lord addressed to John. Jesus asserts himself as Lord. He will come soon. In God's sight a thousand years is 'no more than a watch in the night.' (Ps 89:4). The exact moment cannot be ascertained. The event is certain. It will be a time of judgment. Each will receive what their conduct deserves. Access to the holy city will be confined to those who have persevered in fidelity to the Lamb. Our first parents were forbidden to eat of the tree of life. When the Lord returns all who enter the city will be entitled to share in the heavenly blessings symbolised by the tree. Through the cross of Christ, the life-giving waters of Baptism are offered to all as sheer gift. The whole Church is encouraged to express its intense yearning for the fulfilment of Christ's reign by joining with the Spirit in praying 'Come, Lord Jesus.' The Church fulfils this injunction pre-eminently in the Eucharist.
