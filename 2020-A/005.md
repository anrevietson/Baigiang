# A hymn of praise

Second Reading (Ephesians 1:3-6, 15-18). This is taken from the opening of St Paul's letter to the Ephesians in which he speaks about the maturing of God's plan of salvation in Christ. It is a remarkable passage, hymn-like in structure, closely knit in texture, packed with meaning. What we get in this reading is the opening statement (vs.3-6) giving the theme, followed by the later verses (15-18) which are a prayer for the Ephesians - a prayer for 'wisdom', meaning here the understanding of how God's plan of salvation is fulfilled in Christ.
Christ is the centre of those opening verses, as he is of the whole letter. The key-phrase, 'In Christ', expressive of the main themes of Ephesians (salvific union with Christ), occurs twice already. It is through this union with Christ that we become God's adopted sons, which is another way of describing salvation, or, in theological language, the state of grace. This same theme of union with Christ is further developed in Ephesians as the doctrine of the Mystical Body of Christ - salvation through union with Christ in his Church.
Corresponding to this privileged state of grace is the Christian way of 'living through love', which is seen as the summing up of the whole Law of God (a characteristic Pauline theme, most fully worked out in 1 Cor. 13). This passage, incidentally, is a good example of how St Paul always links Christian dogma with Christian living.
