# God's chosen people

Second Reading (Acts 10:34-38). We Christians of non-Jewish origin take for granted that we are God's chosen people. We may not always be aware that the early Church (composed almost totally of Jews) only painfully came to realise that salvation had come to all -to Jews and pagans alike - through Christ; that in God's mind there was no difference between one group and the other. The Holy Spirit led the early Church to recognise this truth, and first of all through Peter, chief of the apostles, when God sent him to announce this good news to Cornelius, a pagan Roman official in Palestine. As we see from this reading, Peter recognised that this new age began with Jesus' baptism in the Jordan. The power of God was at work in Christ, and Peter significantly notes that Jesus Christ is lord of all people - Jew and non-Jew that is. This reading is not without a message for us today. Since Christ is the saviour of all people, of this and coming centuries as much as of the past, then the Church and each one of us must do all we can to ensure that the saving work of Christ and of his Church is not impeded by undue attachment to ways of thinking or to attitudes that are not required by the Gospel.
