# Peter's address to the Gentiles

Second Reading (Acts 10 :34-38). This is part of St Peter's address to his first Gentile converts (a breakthrough in the spread of the Church). It follows Peter's vision, which was what made him realise that Christianity was a religion for all men, not a preserve of the Jews, and it is followed itself by the baptism of Cornelius and his household. Here, then, the theme of the universality of salvation that we traced in Isaiah finds its first practical resolution : God wishes to save all men, even though his plan of salvation gave Israel preference or priority 'Jesus Christ is Lord of all men.'
There follows a compressed Gospel or summary of what Jesus did and said as Messiah during his public life. This represents the basic Kerygma (or proclamation of salvation) that underlies our written Gospels, the first preaching of the 'good news' out of which they were elaborated.
Note the reference to God sending his Word (not only the message of salvation, but the Word in person); the emphasis on peace (a Messianic theme from the Old Testament), meaning the true, inner peace of reconciliation with God; arid the phrase 'because God was with him' (which is the meaning of the name Immanuel, as in Is. 7 :14 and Mat. 1 :13).
This passage gives a basic Christology: Jesus is Messiah, Jesus is God. It is also Trinitarian in its reference to God the Father (author of the whole design of salvation), his divine Son as Saviour, and the holy Spirit consecrating him (lit. 'Christing' or anointing him) at his Baptism.
Thus the Baptism of Christ, subject of the Gospel, is put in perspective in the history of salvation.
