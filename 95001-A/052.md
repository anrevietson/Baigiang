# The first Servant Song

First Reading (Isaiah 42 :1-4, 6-7). This is part of the so-called first Servant Song in the second part of the Book of Isaiah. These poems portray an ideal figure, suffering, dedicated to God, holy, a means of salvation from sin, to which some have given a collective interpretation (the Servant is Israel), and others as individual one (the Servant is Jesus Christ). Both the New Testament (Mat. 12 :17-21 which quotes this text as a fulfilment-prophecy) and tradition support the latter view. The chief themes - redemption through suffering; the spirituality and universality of the Saviour' s mission would seem to fit it perfectly. Point-by-point the passage speaks of
(1) the call or consecration of the Servant to the task of bringing justice (-true religion, or knowledge of the true God).
{2) the qualities of the Servant: meekness, patience, mercy.
(3) the mission of the Servant : true justice, or equivalently, salvation.
(4) the idea of 'Covenant', suggesting the mediation of a new covenant between God and his people.
Note the conventional vocabulary of salvation: 'Darkness', 'Light', 'Blindness', 'Captivity'. (See Bible Dictionary or Encyclopaedia.)
Whichever interpretation (collective or individual) we give to the Servant here, the passage as a whole is about God's design to reveal himself to all nations.
The Responsorial Psalm (28) is a hymn in which God is praised as Lord of all nature (a kind of Storm-God), giving the theme of adoration which is appropriate to the liturgical season.
