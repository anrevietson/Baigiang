# The baptism of Jesus

Gospel (Mt 3:13-17). As we have seen in Acts 10, the early Church preserved the memory that Jesus' ministry had its beginnings in relation to that of John the Baptist Not only did Jesus begin his ministry after John but Jesus had been baptised by John. Gospel traditions made use of this episode to reveal key aspects of the identity of Jesus and his mission.
Through allusions to Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1 Jesus is presented as the Son and Servant of God, empowered by the Spirit. Through a possible allusion to Isaiah 63:1 1-12;64: 1 the moment of Jesus' baptism is presented as the coming of the longed-for moment of God's tearing open the heavens and coming for the deliverance of his people.
In Matthew's retelling of this story there are some changes of emphasis. Matthew adds a dialogue between John and Jesus in order to explain why Jesus submits to baptism by John even though he is greater than him. This identification with sinners is the fulfilment of God's righteousness.
After the baptism Jesus' identity is revealed publicly rather than just to Jesus alone. In Mark only Jesus is addressed: "you are my son..." In Matthew this declaration is made to all: "This is my son..." For Matthew there is no secret about Jesus' identity as Son of God something already made clear in his Infancy Narrative.
Given that Matthew 28:19 calls for the use of the trinitarian formula in the administration of baptism, one might argue that for Matthew this episode of the baptism of Jesus may have functioned as a model for the nature of Christian baptism. The Father grants sonship by the gift of the Spirit.
