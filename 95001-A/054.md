# The baptism of Jesus

The Gospel (Matthew 3 :13-17). This is St Matthew's account of the Baptism of Jesus at the beginning of his public ministry. It fits into the opening scene of the Gospel proper: John the Baptist preaching the message of repentance (conversion) in preparation for the Kingdom of heaven (the Messianic reign) which is now about to he inaugurated by the Saviour King or Messiah himself, and baptising the people in sign of repentance.
Now Jesus appears on the scene (by the banks of the Jordan), seeking baptism too. John, in his humility, wants to dissuade him, but Jesus makes the cryptic answer, 'Let it be so for this occasion, for we are accomplishing the plan of God ('all that righteousness demands').
Why is Jesus baptised? To inaugurate his mission (and, in effect the Messianic era); to raise the baptism of John to a new level, to show his solidarity with sinful humanity; to give an example of humility.
There follows the vision (seen by John the Baptist) which seals this mission of Jesus with the divine approval in the theophany of the three divine persons which also points to the divinity of Jesus himself. The appearance of the holy Spirit in the form of a dove recalls the text of Genesis 1:2 which speaks of the Spirit of God hovering over the abyss. There are echoes here of Psalms 2 and 109 (the divine approval of the Messiah), as well as of Isaiah 42 (first reading above). The descent of the holy Spirit on Jesus in this Messianic anointing is what is referred to in the second reading above (Acts 10 :38).
Once again, we are presented both with a Christology and with a Trinitarian theme, in a context which establishes Jesus as Saviour and inaugurates his mission of salvation.
The character of the feast, the emphasis on baptism, the role of the holy Spirit in Christ's baptism, all suggest as a theme for preaching the significance of the Sacrament of Baptism ('I baptise you in water for repentance, but the one who follows me . . . will baptise you with the holy Spirit and fire,' Mat. 3 :10) as our initiation into the Christian life, something which has constantly to be renewed within us by the repentance (metanoja, 'conversion') of which the Gospel also speaks.
