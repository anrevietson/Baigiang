# The call of Abraham

First Reading (Gen 12:1-4). At this point in the Bible there is a new development. The first eleven chapters are referred to, sometimes, as primeval history and are accepted as presenting religious truth in an imaginative way. With the story of Abram (or Abraham as he will later be called in Chapter 17), we enter the realm of history in a stricter sense. In the earlier material also, from the time of the fall of Adam and Eve, humanity had more and more turned from God (with a few notable exceptions), but in the stories of Abraham and the patriarchs there is a new turning to God. All humanity had been involved earlier but now God's plan of salvation is to be worked out through a particular people. God takes the initiative, calling Abram to leave his country, kindred and family and go to an unknown land where, God promises, Abram will become the father of a great people and his name will be used in blessing by people all over the world. (The concept of blessing occurs five times.) Abram obeyed and became 'our father in faith'.
