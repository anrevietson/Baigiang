# The call of Abraham

First Reading (Gen. 12:1-4). The readings at Mass last Sunday introduced us to the central Lenten themes of sin, man's weakness and the remedy provided by God. In today's Mass we are told more about the remedy chosen by God to restore us to the paradise of his love and favour.
The coming of God's Son into the world was such an important event in human history that preparations had to begin many centuries before the event. In the first reading today we are told how nearly two thousand years before our Lord's birth God carefully selected his Son's great human ancestor. This man, Abraham by name, would father the people into which Christ would be born. His descendants would therefore be a picked group, a chosen people. God would instruct them in his secrets, load them with his favours and gradually prepare them to receive his Son into their midst. But Abraham and his sons were never meant to forget that their privileged position was in itself a free gift and choice of God. And it was something they must eventually share with the lest of the world. 'In you shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.'
In choosing Abraham as his friend and helper in preparing for the work of redemption, God reveals a constant pattern in his 4ealings with men. Divine favours demand sacrifices. To fulfil the privileged task assigned to him by God, Abraham must cut with his roots set out on a journey - destination unknown, with only God for his guide. And what makes Abraham such a memorable figure in the record of sacred history, is that he believed and trusted God with all his heart. Without wavering, with never a glance behind him, Abraham took God at his word and set out for the land of promise.
We live about as many centuries after the birth of Christ as Abraham lived before it. But God's ways have not changed. He asks of us, as he did of Abraham, a wholehearted dedication to the call he has given us, and complete trusting faith in his love and care. It is easy to see this lesson in the Church's intrepid missionaries today - devoted men and women who cut their ties with country, home and family exactly as Abraham did. Let us remember their labours in Mass today. But in the more humdrum routine of living at home, there will he times when the way seems dark and uncertain; when because of God's claims we may feel obliged to pain or disappoint those who are dear to us. In such times of crisis, ]t is only complete, trusting faith that can lead us safely to Christ, as it led Abraham and his people.
In the Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 33) we profess our faith in God's fidelity to his word. He has fulfilled the promises made to Abraham.
