# Do we practise what we preach?

The readings in today's liturgy may make us feel uncomfortable. They bid us to ask ourselves some questions: Do we practice what we preach? Is ours a faith only of word or is it a faith that shows itself in deeds? What example do we give one another?

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We ask our Lord to forgive our lack of sincerity, to forgive our pride and to bless us with the humility truly to live our faith in loving service.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Mal 1:14-2:2,8-10). The Lord sternly rebukes those who are not sincere in their faith. Yet it is not so much a condemnation as a serious plea to take our faith to heart. The Lord urges us to realize how
our example affects one another and so calls us to be examples of love and unity.
Second Reading (1 Thess 2:7-9,13). Paul gives us an example of a faith that is truly lived. He practised exactly what he preached. Perhaps more than his words, his actions testified to the message of the gospel. The same should be true for us today.
Gospel (Mt 23:1 - 12). Jesus warns the disciples not the follow the ways of the Pharisees. They failed to practice what they preached. But does our pride make us partners in the company of the Pharisees? Are Jesus' words addressed to us?

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As we pray now for Christians throughout the world, let us also ask God to empower us to serve those for whom we pray:
Reader(s): For those who struggle for peace and justice throughout the world, for their continued strength and perseverance.
For those who seek to know the ways of the Lord, that we may be true witnesses to them of gospel values.
For the sick, those who are depressed, for those who live without hope or consolation.
For those without homes and jobs, that we may advocate on their behalf.
For those who are separated from us by death.
President: Lord, receive our prayer, empower us with your grace to pray not only with our words but with our actions. May we become the instruments through which our prayers are answered. As always, we pray in the name of Jesus the Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As Christians we are called to say what we mean and to do what we say.
Let us pray the prayer Jesus gave not only with our lips but with our lives'.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Christ calls us to serve and to love. As true followers of the Lord, let us offer each other a sign of peace than flows from our hears.

COMMUNION MEDITATION
The goal and glory of Christian life is service. Lord, may we always seek that goal with sincerity and strive to lift the burdens of life from the shoulders of our brothers and sisters. Then our lives, our actions and not only our words, will bear witness to the truth of your way, to the presence of your love in our daily lives.
