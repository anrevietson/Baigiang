# Paul's missionary preaching

Second Reading (Acts 13:22-26). Acts 13:14-43, a sample of Paul's missionary preaching directed at a mixed audience of Jews and God-fearing gentiles, is a resume' of the history of salvation from God's choice of "the fathers" to the resurrection of Jesus. Paul insists on what God has done for Israel: the call, deliverance from Egypt, promised land. God established the Davidic line. As a man after God's own heart who did his will, David appears as a type of the Saviour, Jesus the Saviour God brought forth from the seed of David according to his promise. This part of the discourse closes with the witness of John the Baptist who, as the last prophet, was Jesus' immediate forerunner; preparing the way for him by calling all the people of Israel to repentance (Lk 3:1-20) and sealing their repentance with the sign of baptism. Not surprisingly, the discourse presents the role of the Baptist as Luke's gospel has done. He recedes in importance before the one who replaces him (cf. Lk 3:16). John stands on the dividing line between the time marked by the promise and the time marked by the Saviour and his message on salvation.
