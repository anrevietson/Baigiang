# God's love gives John being and calls him to co-operate in carrying out his plan of salvation.

Gospel (Lk 1:57-66.80). This passage is taken from the rich weave of the Lucan infancy narrative. It deals with the birth, circumcision and naming of 'the Baptist' and it should be read in conjunction with the passage that parallels it with regard to Jesus (2:1-40). The Infancy Narrative conveys a strong sense of God being powerfully at work in history. He shows his great kindness (mercy) to Elizabeth by giving her a son just as he has to Mary (1:50). Joy, fear (awe) and wonder are there-action of the neighbours to the events surrounding John's birth and naming just as it is with the Shepherds and those who hear their message (2:8-20). The main emphasis is on the naming of the boy: the parents, with divine support, insist on the name John which means 'Yahweh has shown favour'. The people of the 'hill country' wonder at the whole episode and as to what will be the destiny of the child. This is revealed in the song Zechariah (1:76-79) and the final verse shows us the child being prepared for his task.
