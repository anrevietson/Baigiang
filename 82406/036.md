# The second Servant Song

First Reading (1s 49:1-6). This is the second of the 'Servant songs' (1s 42:14; 50:4-11; 52:13-53:12). They are usually applied to Christ in the liturgy (see the first readings for Holy Week). Here the song is applied to 'the Baptist', foreshadowing the various stages of his life. The servant is called and named (49:1 cf. Lk 1:13) before his birth. A sharp sword (v.2) in the scriptures is an image for the word of God (ef. Heb 4:12-13; Eph 6:17) and here refers to the preaching ministry of the servant. God's protection and preparing of him (cf. Lk 1:66) and the apparent failure of his mission (cf. Lk 9:9) also foreshadow the life of the Baptist. John however is referred to in the gospels only as a lamp (Jn 7:35): Jesus is the light and John is the witness to the light (Jn 1:6-9). The sense of God being at work to bring about his plan for the world which we see in the Gospel is also strong here. God is the principle protagonist.
Responsorial Psalm. The psalm which speaks about the provident care of God for the psalmist reflects the themes of the servant song and yet gives a new perspective. God is not concerned with the psalmist because of what he does but is concerned with him because he created him and he is.
