# Victory over death

Second Reading (1 Cor. 15:54-58). All through this long chapter, from which passages have been read during the past two Sundays, Paul has been speaking of the resurrection of Christ and the resurrection of believers which results from it. This general resurrection at the end of time will be the completion of Christ's victory over sin and death, and thus the undoing of the effects of Adam's sin. 'Sin entered the world through one man (Adam), and through sin death, and thus death has spread through the whole human race because everyone has sinned' (Rom. 5:12). Christ overcame sin and death (death of soul and body) through his own death and resurrection. Yet this victory will only be complete at the end of time when sin and death are no more (cf. 1 Cor. 15:22, 26) and when the redeemed have glorious and immortal bodies. Contemplating these truths Paul cannot help seeing in the resurrection the fulfilment of Old Testament prophecy : 'death shall be no more ' (Is. 25:8; cf. Apoc. 21:4), or as Paul read this prophecy in the Greek text he used : 'death is swallowed up in victory', i.e. in the victory won by Christ and of those who believe in him. Death, which in Paul's words once reigned as a monarch, has in the words of the prophet Hosea (13:14) lost its sting. Paul goes on to explain that 'the sting of death is sin and sin gets its power from the law' . In saying that sin is the sting of death Paul does not mean that sin makes death a bitter pang. Rather are the apostle's words here a summary of what he explains in greater detail elsewhere (e.g. Rom. 5 :12-21). What he means is that sin, i.e. the power of sin that entered the world through Adam, leads inevitably to death, to death of the soul and finally to the eternal death of the body in hell. Sin for Paul is a latent power within man which is brought to life by the law, or external commands. This 'law' tells us the good actions to be done and the evil to be avoided but of itself it does not give the moral power to perform what is commanded or to avoid the evil actions which are forbidden. This power to live as one should comes from Christ's grace (cf. Rom. 8:1-4 etc.) which gives us the victory over sin on earth and thus leads to the glorious resurrection of the body. This grace of Christ is ever present and ever active, a belief that should beget confidence in believers in their constant struggle to live a Christian life.
