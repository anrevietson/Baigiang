# Good works from a good heart

The Gospel (Luke 6 :39-45). Christians should exercise self-criticism; good works must flow from a heart in harmony with God's law. Without this personal relationship with God Christian behaviour will not be genuine. This is more or less what the short parable and the different sayings of Christ read today mean. Christians are to be the light of the world; they should lead others to Christ. But this they cannot be unless they themselves are enlightened in mind and morals by their faith. They must teach both by precept and example. The one true teacher in Christianity is Christ himself. The more his followers come to know, the more should they be like their master. And to be like Christ means being understanding and tolerant. How easy it is to criticise others and to forget one's own failings, to notice the minor faults of our neighbour while neglecting more serious shortcomings in ourselves! This is pseudo-religion, hypocrisy, against which Christ puts us on our guard. (The 'splinter' or 'speck', and 'plank' or 'log' are examples of oriental hyperbolic language.) What God wants is that first and foremost each individual be in the proper personal relationship with his creator. Only in this way can he bear true fruits in good works.
Today's reading has a message for all ages, but particularly for our own. Everything and anything is criticised. Now, while criticism is called for where abuses exist, we can easily be so caught up in what is wrong or believed to be wrong in others and in society that we turn away our gaze from ourselves, and forget our obligations to ourselves. Criticism can be a corroding force within the soul. In today's reading Christ reminds us of this danger. He came to transform the world; but this he does by transforming man himself.
