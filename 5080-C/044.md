# Victory over death

Second Reading (1 Cor. 15 :54-58). Through this long chapter, Paul has been speaking of the resurrection of Christ and the resurrection of believers which results from it. This general resurrection at the end of time will be the completion of Christs victory over sin and death. Then the redeemed will have glorious and immortal bodies. Contemplating these truths Paul cannot help seeing in the resurrection the fulfilment of Old Testament prophecy: 'Death shall be no more' (Is. 25 :8; cf. Apoc. 21:4), or as Paul read this prophecy in the Greek text he used: 'Death is swallowed up in victory'. Death, which once reigned as a monarch, has in the words of the prophet Hosea (13 :14) lost its sting. This does not mean that sin makes death a bitter pang. Rather are the apostle's words here a summary of what he explains in greater detail elsewhere (e.g. Rom. 5 :12-21). Sin, i.e., the power of sin that entered the world through Adam, leads inevitably to death, to death of the soul and finally to the eternal death of the body in hell. Sin for Paul is a latent power within man which is brought to life by the law, or external commands. This 'law' tells us the good actions to be done and the evil to be avoided but of itself it does not give the moral power to perform what is commanded or to avoid the evil actions which are forbidden. This power to live as one should comes from Christ's grace (cf. Rom. 8 :1-4, etc.) which gives us the victory over sin on earth and thus leads to the glorious resurrection of the body. This belief should beget confidence in the struggle to live a Christian life.
