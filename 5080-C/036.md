# The end of the sermon on the plain

Gospel (Lk 6:39-45). Like Jesus ben Sirach, the Lucan Jesus rounds off the sermon on the plain with a series of parables; images drawn from everyday life to provide imaginative reflection on his teaching. There are three such parables in this reading: the blind leading the blind, the splinter in a companion's eye, and the good tree and its fruits. It is fairly easy to construct a general moral for these parables. But more than an abstract message is entailed. We should attempt a meaning which takes account of the context of the sermon's call on would-be disciples to listen, love their enemies and share their possessions. We should apply that message to our own concrete circumstances.
The disciple are blind until their eyes are opened by the teaching of Jesus. Christians play both roles of disciples and teachers; learning for themselves and teaching others. Their willingness to learn will determine their ability to teach by example. The preacher who draws no more than an abstract moral is not going to lead others very far in following the example of Christ. When it comes to sharing possessions we have much to learn from Jesus. A recognition of this fact will caution us in the all-too-human eagerness to find fault with others. Where money is involved we are all too easily critical of the lack of generosity shown by others. The third parable is a reminder of the essential element in following Jesus; the conversion of the heart. Adequate discipleship can only come from a deep conversion to the generous God revealed in the proclamation of Jesus.
