# Life, death and afterlife

Today's celebration urges us to focus on the great themes of life, death, and afterlife. They invite us to see these three realities as related, not separated. Living now will prepare us for living forever.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Today's liturgy speaks of a full life. Our living today is linked to our death and resurrection. Aware that we have sometimes chosen to forget that inter-connection, we make these petitions to our generous God.
Lord, you give us the insight to value that precious reality of life. Lord, have mercy
Lord, you give us the courage to see our death as the springboard for a fuller life. Christ, have mercy.
Lord, you give us the grace to see you as vibrant, living person and hence to see the afterlife as a dynamic, loving relationship with you and your people. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR THE READINGS
First Reading (2 Macc 7:1-2,9-14). We see the undying loyalty of a Jewish mother and her sons. In the midst of persecution their faith is tenacious and their courage unflagging. God will vindicate their deaths in the resurrection.
Second Reading (2 Thess 2:16-3:5). The author asks prayers for himself and that God's Word may make progress. He assures us that the Lord will be faithful by strengthening and guarding them.
Gospel (Lk 20:27-38). Jesus responds to the question of the afterlife. He shows that Moses speaks about the resurrection with regard to the patriarchs. Hence God is the God of the living, not the dead.

PRAYERS OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware of our call to live our lives fully in hope of the resurrection, we pray to the Father through the Son.
Reader(s): That we may see each moment as the Opportunity to live for God as well as our sisters and brothers.
That we may have the courage to see our death as a new beginning.
That we may see eternal life with God is eternal life with our sisters and brothers.
That we may focus on the resurrection of God's Son and thus see life, death, and resurrection interconnected in the service of others.
President: Father, we lovingly place these petitions before you. Because of your Son's embrace of passion and death, we believe that he continues to live with you and in us. We pray that his life-style and death-style may be ours as well. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As we ask our God not to lead us into temptation but to deliver us from evil, we ponder our call to lead trusting lives that will one day culminate in the unending life of the resurrection.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
We have shared the bread and the cup. In the midst of our community we boldly announce that we need food and drink to sustain our daily living. Eucharist is the challenging symbol that urges us to become food and drink for each other today and every day. Eucharist also powerfully reminds us that we are called to the heavenly banquet in the company of sisters and brothers. Ultimately Eucharist is the dramatic symbol of the union of both present and future in community.
