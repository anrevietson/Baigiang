# The resurrection of the dead

The Gospel (Luke 20 :27-38). In the first reading we noted that belief in the bodily resurrection came to be explicitly formulated towards the beginning of the second century BC (170-160 BC). We can also trace the origins of the three Jewish religious groups, the Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, back to about the same time. The Pentateuch (i.e. the Law or the Five Books of Moses) makes no mention of a bodily resurrection, and for centuries later Jewish religion knew only of a rather dull existence after death for good and bad alike in Sheol, a place which more or less corresponded to the Hades of the Greeks. The Sadducees refused to accept the new doctrine of the resurrection of the body which became a point of dogma for the Pharisees and belief in which was taught to the mass of the Israelites by them. The division between the Pharisees and Sadducees on this point, and their discussions on it, were a feature of Palestinian life in our Lord's day. The Sadducees must have often embarrassed the Pharisees and their learned scribes with the case they now put to Jesus. It rests on a law found in the Pentateuch ('Moses' - Deut. 25 :5-6) concerning a form of marriage known as Levirate marriage (from the Latin levir - 'brother-in-law'). Deut. 25 :5-6 intended to guarantee a legal heir for a man who died childless. Jesus replied that their error lay in a misconception of the resurrection and the after-life. Then marriage will be no more. It belongs to this order of things and is designed to beget children to replace those who die, and thus to perpetuate the human race in an age of mortality. The Sadducees misconstrue the after-life. They also fail to understand the deeper meaning of the revelation made to Moses. In Ex. 3 :6 (a passage apparently known to the Jews of Christ's day as 'the passage about the bush'), God from the burning bush revealed himself to Moses as the God of the Patriarchs. Abraham, Isaac and Jacob still lived before him. He is the God of the living. He is the living and life-giving God in the fullest sense of the word, the God who not only has life in himself but also conveys that life to others, by calling the dead back to life. It was very probably a deeper understanding of the nature of the living God that brought Israel (the Sadducees excepted) to belief in the bodily resurrection of the just. But that divine power which will bring the dead to life again and transform them on the last day has been communicated by the Father to the Son and by him to those united to him through faith, hope and love. The power of the Risen Saviour, the power of his resurrection, is already at work in the Church through grace. Eternal life has come and Christian life leads to the resurrection.
