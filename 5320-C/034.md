# Questions about the after-life

The Gospel (Luke 20 :27-38). Questions about the after-life were no less popular among the Jews in Jesus' time than they are today in different cultures. It was really only about 200 B.C. that the widespread speculation about the possibility of life after death took on a precise form in an affirmation of bodily resurrection (first reading). This was also the period which saw the birth of three religious groups within Judaism which were to become famous by Jesus' time, the Pharisees, Sadducees and the Essenes. Their views about the finality of death differed radically. The Essenes (according to Josephus, The Jewish War) were strongly influenced by Greek thought that considered the body corruptible, but the soul immortal. This immortal soul, trapped in the prison of a corruptible body, rejoices and soars aloft when the man dies: the souls of the just are wafted into gentle surroundings beyond the sea (The Isles of the Blest), while the souls of the wicked are consigned to darkness and pain (Hades). The Pharisees believed that, while all souls are incorruptible, only the souls of the just pass into other bodies - the souls of the impious being consigned to punishment. The Sadducees denied the immortality of the soul, and the doctrines of reward or punishment after death. In such a climate, trick questions, like the one asked Jesus in today's Gospel, were a commonplace. Jesus' reply shows the Sadducees that their notion of a next life is determined solely by this-worldly considerations. They err in a twofold fashion: they are ignorant of the scriptures (and the reference to Moses and the burning bush, Exod. 3 :6, was particularly telling against the Sadducees since they accepted only the books of the law) and of the power of God (Mark 12 :24).
