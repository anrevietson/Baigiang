# The Holy Family

The Gospel (Luke 2 :41-52). When speaking of the Holy Family, Jesus, Mary and Joseph, we must never forget that it was a family with a difference - the difference being Jesus who was Son of God as well as son of Mary. Jesus was indeed 'subject', obedient, to Mary and Joseph, but he was also, on occasion, 'about his Father's business'. These two truths are brought out in today's reading from Lukes Gospel.
In the opening chapters of his work Luke lays stress on the obedience of Mary and Joseph to the law of Moses. Jesus was presented in the Temple 'according to the law of Moses' (Luke 2 :22-24); they presented the offerings and made the sac4fices required by the law. In short, they performed everything according to the law of the Lord (Luke 2:39). And it was in the context of this obedience to the law of Moses that the Spirit revealed the nature of Christ s mission. In today's reading we see Mary and Joseph going to Jerusalem for the Passover feast, in accord with the law of Moses (cf. Deut. 16 :1-8; Ex. 23 :15), taking Jesus with them. We also read of Jesus coming from Jerusalem to Nazareth with Mary and Joseph and being subject to them. The entire family professes reverence for God's law and Jesus is subject to his mother and his foster father. The Holy Family is the model of the teaching given in the first two readings.
Then we have the other aspect of Christ's person and mission. He caused sorrow to Mary and Joseph. His reply to his mother's sorrowful remark, 'Son why have you done this to us?' was: 'Why were you looking for me? Did you not know that I must be busy with my Father's affairs?' (or, 'that I must be in my Father's house', i.e. the Temple). Luke's remark is that Mary and Joseph did not understand what he meant. The person and mission of Jesus were a mystery, even for his own mother. She would come to know them more deeply through faith. She kept in her heart what her Son Jesus said and what others said about him, thus coming to an ever-deeper understanding of his person.
