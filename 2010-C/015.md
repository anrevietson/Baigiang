# The boy Jesus in the temple

Gospel (Lk 2:41-52). This story of a journey to Jerusalem anticipates the great central section of the Gospel which describes the journey of Jesus to Jerusalem, the place of his suffering and glory. Just as Luke's Gospel begins with an incident in the Temple (the annunciation to Zechariah), and ends with the apostles 'continually in the Temple blessing God, so Jesus, on reaching his religious coming-of-age, also looks to the Temple as the place where he would discover his vocation. No longer is it an angel or the shepherds or any third party who declares who Jesus is and what destiny he faces, but Jesus himself declares his own identity as the one who must he 'about his Father's business'. The expression here is rich in meaning: the phrase could also mean 'among those belonging to my Father'. Jesus does not underline the meaning of this pronouncement about his identity by working any miracle. Instead, the Gospel stresses his identity as the Servant of God.
In showing God's Son as submitting to obedience, Luke is presenting Jesus under the image of a servant, and thus we find the shadow of the cross reaching back into early life of Jesus. The Letter to the Hebrews insists that he 'learned to obey'. We see here that this occurred not by dramatic incidents but by sharing in the humdrum pattern of family life in what Albert Schweitzer has described as 'the almost unimaginably narrow and primitive environment of a small near Baster town'.
