# A blessing and a curse

First Reading Deut. 11:18, 26-28). This passage concludes Moses' introductory discourse (chapters one to eleven) to the Deuteronomic Code. 'These words of mine' (v.18) refer both to this Code (chapters twelve to twenty-six) elaborated to legislate for the people settled In the land of Canaan, and to Moses' exhortations to observe it The law or 'torah' is presented to the people by Moses in the setting of the covenant which Yahweh has entered with his chosen people. By observing it, the people keep their side of this agreement. They are thus presented with an option: to accept or to refuse' commitment to their God. If they do the former they will be blessed, if the latter they will be cursed, in terms of material possessions and prosperity. In the cultural, religious context of the time, refusal to accept Yahweh as the unique Lord entailed necessarily recourse to the baals or 'lords' of Canaan.
The Deuteronomic view of man's relationship with God through the law: observance of the law means prosperity, disobedience means adversity, is, if seen superficially, inadequate and appears contradicted by human experience. Only the later revelation of an after-life, with its implications of retribution beyond the grave, rendered it valid.
