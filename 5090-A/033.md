# The house built on rock

The Gospel (Mat. 7 :21-27). This passage culminates Jesus' Sermon on the Mount (Mat. 5-7). In the first verses (vv. 21-23) Jesus draws a clear contract between those whose faith is only superficial, stopping at mere notional assent, among whom can be numbered even the Apostles, and those who do the will of his Father.
Jesus proceeds subsequently to define in what this 'will of the Father' actually consists. It is a matter of carrying out Jesus' teaching presented essentially in the Sermon on the Mount. throughout this latter Jesus has been portrayed as the New Moses communicating the new law or teaching of the new covenant. The tone of Jesus' voice here clearly evokes the passage from Deuteronomy heard in the first reading. The Ten Commandments and the later legislation of Israel, however, have been resumed in and surpassed by Jesus' own words. He not only communicates God's torah and exhorts men to observe it, he also incarnates it.
The basic option between acceptance and refusal is now to be made in relation to Jesus' teaching, in terms of wisdom and folly.
The image of the house built upon rock provides a fine example of the meaning of biblical faith (as it was suggested in the second reading). A person who believes in Christ bases his whole life and existence upon his teaching in the profound conviction that nothing will ever be able to shake his confidence.
