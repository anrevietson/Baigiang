# The just man persecuted

First Reading (Wisdom 2 :12, 17-20). Wisdom 1-5 represents the climax of the Old Testament thought on the suffering of the righteous man at the hands of the wicked. These chapters describe the reversal of the respective lots of the righteous and the ungodly at the judgement, and thus provide encouragement for the oppressed and a warning for the oppressors. In the present context, the latter are those Jews who (like Ecclesiastes) have become disenchanted with traditional wisdom and who (unlike Ecclesiastics) have resorted to hedonism: 'Come, therefore, let us enjoy the good things that exist, and make use of the creation to the full as in youth' (2 :6). The righteous, on the other hand, are those who, no more sold on the tradition than the ungodly, nevertheless hold on to their faith in God and obey his commandments. Their faithfulness cuts the ungodly to the quick and threatens their existence : 'Let us lie in wait for the righteous man, because he is inconvenient to us and opposes our actions' (2 :12).2 :17-20 present suffering as a test or trial, primarily for the benefit of the ungodly who want to prove their own thesis, but also for the righteous whose true mettle is revealed in their affliction : 'Having been disciplined a little, they will receive great good, because God tested them and found them worthy of himself' (3 :5).
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 53 :1-7). Like Ps 115, this is a confident prayer of a suffering just man, appearing for vindication. The final verses express the certainty of being heard: 'For thou hast delivered me from every trouble, and my eye has looked in triumph on my enemies' (v.7).
