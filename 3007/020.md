# The call of Levi

The Gospel (Luke 5:27-32). This passage really contains two narratives. In the first the stress is on the power of Jesus to call anyone he chooses and on the efficacy of his call. But since Levi was a tax collector and therefore regarded as a religious outcast the account of his call naturally leads on to the second story which explains Jesus' association with sinners.
In the ancient world table-fellowship was regarded as a sign and guarantee of intimate friendship. The scribes and Pharisees, the defenders of God's law and religion, could scarcely fail to be surprised at seeing Jesus show this sign of friendship to sinners. In reply to their question Jesus quotes a proverb about a doctor and his patient that would have been familiar to his hearers and that could easily be applied to himself and to his mission. This passage reflects the spirit of Jesus and the mentality of Luke. For Jesus continually sought out sinners and Luke likes to give striking examples of his efforts to reach their hearts, cf. Luke 7:36-50; 19:1-10; 7:34.
