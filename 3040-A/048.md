# The man born blind

The Gospel (John 9:1-41). The miracle described in this reading illustrates our Lord's compassion and power, but it also suggests that his mission is to impart religious insight to those who are spiritually blind. This is a point which is frequently made in the Fourth Gospel, e.g. 12 :25. It is explicitly stated here in verse six :
'I am the light of the world.'
The narrative is easy to follow and the dialogue which takes place after the miracle is dramatic and skilfully constructed. The miracle itself made a deep impression on those who witnessed it and raised the question whether the person who performed it might not be a prophet sent by God (vv. 16-18). In St John's eyes the very miracle itself was an invitation to faith. Towards the end of the chapter the man born blind himself makes his personal profession of faith (v.35).
The Pharisees (or some of them) withheld this faith because, although they could see in the physical sense, they are shown to have been spiritually blind (v.39). We are not told why this is so, but St John in the prologue to his Gospel had already warned us that this kind of thing would happen : 'In him was life and this life was the light of men; the light shone in the darkness and the darkness did not receive it' (John 1:4). This combination of mystery and tragedy casts a shadow over St John's presentation of the good news' of salvation. The spiritual blindness of those Pharisees who witnessed this miracle and still would not believe in Christ was to some extent self-induced; it is described as sin in verse forty-one.
The fact that a miracle had taken place could not be denied. It was called in question by the Pharisees but placed beyond doubt by the blind man's parents, who were, however, reluctant to be involved in an argument with the Pharisees. The man himself was convinced that Jesus must have been sent by God since he had the power to work such a miracle and this was the basis of his own personal faith.
The Pharisees tried to avoid the same conclusion by a theological argument which was not without some validity, viz. God will not choose a sinner to be his spokesman and representative.
Now this man is clearly a sinner because he has violated the law of rest of the Sabbath. Therefore he is not God's spokesman and representative.
This seems to have been a regular argument used by those who could not see their way to accept Christ's claims. It is not developed further in this text but there is material in the other Gospels which shows the lines which this development could take, and the way in which Christ answered it.
He maintained that it was not necessarily a sin to use his power to relieve suffering on a Sabbath. The precept of love could take precedence over the cultic law of Sabbath rest. The moral and ethical principles which held sway during the week were not to be thought of as suspended on the Sabbath (cf. Mark 3:2 ff.). Hence his claim to be God's messenger could not be dismissed a priori.
Those who assumed that Jesus was not sent by God were left with the problem of explaining a particular miracle whose reality they were not able to deny. In St Mark some Pharisees were said to have regarded his miracles as diabolical prodigies, accusing him of being in league with the devil. This argument is highly offensive and defamatory and impossible to prove. In Mark 3:22 Jesus shows how it is also inherently contradictory. Some such line of argument is implied here because in spite of the miracle which they could not deny, they still regarded Jesus as a sinner. However, the argument is not developed; the Pharisees simply use their authority ('We are the ones who must judge whether a man is sent by God') and the threat of a kind of excommunication in order to have their opinion accepted.
Finally, St John is not really interested in using this miracle in order to prove that Jesus was sent by God. Rather he is using it to suggest certain aspects of this mission, in the hope that his readers would understand better Christs role as the one who brought light into the world; that light which gave those who received it the power to become the sons of God.
