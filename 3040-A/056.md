# The man born blind

Gospel (Jn 9:1-41). The contrast found in the second reading is found here also. The blind man who is healed receives spiritual sight and confesses his faith in Jesus; the hard-hearted Jewish leaders refuse to believe and are spiritually blind. The purpose of the miracle is to show the power of God at work in Jesus, to show that Jesus is 'from God', and so bring people to believe in him. Here, as in the story of the Samaritan woman (Third Sunday) there is a growing understanding as the account proceeds and a threefold reference to the identity of Jesus:
as prophet (v.17), as Messiah (v.22), as Son of Man (v.35). Jesus affirms here also what was implied in the second reading today that he is the light of the world and the giving of sight to the man is a sign of this. There is both revelation and judgment. Jesus comes to bring salvation to those who believe, but this results in judgment and condemnation for those who are obstinate in unbelief
Also to be noted is the fear of the parents and the courage of their son. The parents feared banishment from the synagogue. Quite likely some of the Johannine community were threatened with this and this passage is an encouragement to those who suffer because of their profession of faith in Jesus, by word or deed.
