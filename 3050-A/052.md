# I will open your graves

First Reading (Ezek. 37:12-14). This short reading is the conclusion of a famous vision which was experienced by the prophet Ezekiel (Ch. 37). This whole narrative was formerly read in the liturgy of Holy Saturday and today's reading loses much of its meaning unless Ezekiel 37:1-11 is read with it.
In the historical context Ezekiel was speaking to the people of Israel in exile, when they were discouraged and as good as dead. They felt that there was no significant destiny in store for them in spite of Yahweh's promises to them. Ezekiel now receives a promise that Yahweh will bring them back to life and re-establish them in Jerusalem as his chosen people. This was in fact accomplished in the year 539 BC and in the course of the following years.
The later prophets regarded this almost miraculous event as a proof that Yahweh had not rejected his people and that he would exercise an equal or even greater power to enable them to bring their destiny to its fulfilment.
When John the Baptist was asked what he meant by proclaiming that the kingdom of God was at hand, he quoted Isaiah, chapter forty (which contains the accomplishment of the promise made to Ezekiel) and explained to them that the events through which they were about to live were the continuation and completion of the movement which included and was symbolised by the return from exile. Jesus of Nazareth was the great figure who would bring God's plan of salvation to fulfilment.
It should be noted that the power which would bring God's (almost) dead people back to life is described as God's spirit. This must not be understood as indicating that the Jews were aware that there are three Persons in God of whom the third is the Spirit. But by a literary device the Jews personified God's saving and life-giving power and spoke of it as though it were a reality distinct from the Person of Yahweh which he could send or give to those who had a role to play in the salvation of the world. The Baptist is speaking in this tradition when he announced that Jesus of Nazareth would be able to baptise with the spirit and with power.
In the Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 129) the De Profundis, we ask God to forgive us our sins and give us true life.
