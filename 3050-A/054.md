# The raising of Lazarus

The Gospel (John 11:1-45). By his account of the raising of Lazarus St John intended to emphasise and illustrate Christ's statement 'I am the resurrection and the life', and to indicate that faith in him is the necessary condition of achieving a share in this life. The necessity of faith is suggested early on in the narrative when Thomas shows himself willing, as a consequence of his friendship with Jesus, to be brought into a situation in which he would be in real danger of death (v.16).
What is implicit in this attitude is explicitly formulated by Jesus himself when faced with a situation in which a man, Lazarus, was already dead. He states that Lazarus was allowed to die precisely in order that Christ might be glorified; and in St John's terminology this can have two meanings :
(1) Jesus is glorified by every situation which reveals his divine nature and power and leads men to believe in him, e.g. the marriage feast at Cana (John 2).
(2) He is glorified especially by his resurrection or rather by the total paschal mystery which consists of his suffering, death and resurrection . and this total event becomes more imminent as a result of the raising of Lazarus (v.40).
In the actual scene at Bethany the first theme to be treated is Mary's faith, which is to be confirmed and have its scope enlarged.
Mary is shown as believing :
(1) That the just will arise on the last day. (This was the accepted belief of most Jews at that time.)
(2) That he had the power of working miracles. (This was the conviction of his followers, and could not be denied even by his enemies.)
(3) That he was the Christ, the Son of God, who had come into the world.
As a result of the raising of Lazarus and the conversations associated with it these elements will be integrated into a fuller understanding of Christ's role in his Father's salvation plan. Jesus will convince her :
(1) That it is he himself who is the source of the life which is in store for the just on the last day.
(2) That the imparting of this life is the purpose for which he was sent into the world.
(3) That his claim to be the source of eternal life is neither farfetched nor fantastic since even now he is in a position to bring a dead man back to life by power given to him by his Father.
Again it must be noted that St John was writing for a believing Christian community and that his purpose is to present various aspects of Christ's role as the way which leads to his Father.
