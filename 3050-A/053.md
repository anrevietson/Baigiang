# Life in the Spirit

Second Reading (Rom. 8:8-11). This reading describes some aspects of the situation in which Christians find themselves in this world. They have already been sanctified by the gift of the Spirit of Christ but are still possessed of the same human nature which formerly was sinful. It is necessary therefore that their whole outlook an4 way of life be determined by the Spirit in which they dwell and which dwells in them. Obviously the Spirit is a spiritual reality and the preposition 'in' which refers primarily to material objects cannot, strictly speaking, be applied to the Spirit. St Paul, however, makes use of this material metaphor in order to express an aspect of the intimate union of the Christian with God through Christ.
St Paul describes this 'Spirit' as both the 'Spirit of God' and 'Spirit of Christ', and eventually changes his ground to assert that Christ himself 'is in you'. He is straining language to the limit in order to express the almost complete absorption of the human soul into God through Christ. But this spiritual experience takes place in a human nature which was formerly sinful and still has a tendency to relapse into sin. This sinful tendency has not been destroyed but merely brought under control so that it is now ineffective - no longer able to lead men inevitably into sin.
It is quite typical of St Paul's theology that he does not hope that men will escape from their sinful nature but that that sinful nature will be radically sanctified and that the whole human personality be integrated and unified by the rebellious element being brought totally under the influence of the Spirit. If this is not done by the grace of Christ the personality is split and the whole man is separated from Christ.
In verse eleven we have one of the strongest of St Paul's expressions of Christian hope. This complex sentence can be broken up into three simple propositions even if this process deprives his thought of some of its vigour and energy :
(1) God has given you the same spirit which he gave to Christ's human nature.
(2) God the Father raised up Christ from the dead by his glorious resurrection.
(3) You can be absolutely certain that the Father has assigned to you all the same destiny of glorious resurrection precisely because the same spirit dwells in you.
In this text St Paul regards salvation not so much as a thing which has been accomplished by Christ, but as a thing which has been planned and executed by the Father.
The last day is represented here not as the day of general judgement but as the day of general glorification.
Finally this glorification is not something which we can achieve but something which is given to us by God, whether we look at it from the point of view of the Spirit which has already been given to us or from the point of view of our ultimate glorification of which the present Spirit is the guarantee. All man can do is to ruin God's plan in his own case by rejecting the Spirit and walking according to the flesh; and St Paul warns us not to do that.
