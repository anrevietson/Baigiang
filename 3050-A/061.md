# The raising of Lazarus

Gospel (Jn 11:1-45). The raising of Lazarus is the final sign in the Book of Signs (Jn 2-12) and anticipates the last events of Jesus' life retold in the book of Glory (Jn 13-20). The episode is typical of this gospel since it consists of narrative and discourse, uses contrasting imagery, unfolds the implications of belief, and reveals the identity of Jesus.
This narrative includes important and colourful figures in John's gospel: Jesus, Martha, Mary, Lazarus, the disciples, Thomas, the Jews. The situation involves the death of a close friend of Jesus. Family and friends mourn Lazarus whose funeral was held on the day of death. The rabbis believed that the soul hovered near the body for three days and so with Lazarus four days in the tomb, no one held any hope of resuscitation. Jesus' love for Martha, Mary, and Lazarus prompts him to go to Bethany.
While the disciples with Jesus misunderstand Jesus' purpose, Martha believes that his presence would have saved her brother. The narrative and discourse continue with the final outcome being the raising of Lazarus. In the episode Martha is portrayed as the believer, Mary is in distress, the Jews closely follow the drama, and Jesus himself becomes the central figure who challenges each participant in the story by clarifying the central issue: the real meaning of death and life.
The imagery is powerful because of John's use of contrast: light and darkness; presence and absence; belief and disbelief; death and resurrection. The use of glory, the hour, and signs demonstrate that this episode develops the interests of the writer, for these references are used through-out the first half of the gospel.
The central theme in this episode is belief. Martha's statement "you are the Christ, the Son of God" (Jn 11:27) reminds us of Peter's confession in the synoptic gospels (Mk 8:29). It is the strongest statement of belief in the discourse for the disciples and Thomas register doubt, while Mary and the Jews seem caught in the ambiguity of the human experience. The sign of raising Lazarus does lead to belief on the part of many of the Jews (Jn 11:45), but belief is on different levels for each of the observers. Martha states her faith conviction prior to the sign, while the Jews believe only after the sign, an inadequate faith in John because it relies on the wonder-working ability of Jesus, rather than an understanding of his true identity.
The identity of Jesus in this episode contrasts the human and the divine. In this most human moment in the gospel when Jesus weeps for his friend, he also reveals his true identity "I am the resurrection
whoever believes in me will never die" (Jn 11:26). The "lam" statements used in John allude to the meaning of the name of Yahweh. The focus of belief in Jesus himself, as opposed to believing in God's action in Jesus, also indicates the high Christology of John. Reference to Jesus' being sent by the Father echoes the prologue, creating little doubt of how John's community understands Jesus as the Son of God.
Not only does the gospel reveal the true identity of Jesus, but it also points to the irony developed in the rest of the gospel, that the death of Jesus will give true life to the world.
