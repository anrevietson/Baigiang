# The raising of Lazarus

Gospel (Jn 11:1-45). This gospel passage is a climax to the public life of Jesus and with chapter 20 (the account of the Resurrection) encloses the Passion narrative. There has been a build-up of opposition to Jesus among the Jewish leaders and as a result of the incident reported here, they determine to kill him (11:53). The raising of the dead to life (of which only three occurrences are reported in the gospels) was one of the surest signs that Jesus worked by the power of God, since God is the author of life. The fact that Lazarus had been dead a few days and buried made the miracle all the more marvellous. The purpose of the miracle is that God may be glorified and that Jesus, the Son, may be glorified. He is glorified both in the miracle itself and in that it leads to his death which, for John's gospel, is the 'hour' of Jesus' glorification, the death and resurrection both being considered a 'lifting up' of Jesus (cf. Jn 3:14; 12:32). The miracle leads to faith in Jesus, both for the disciples and for many others. It is through believing in Jesus that they come to life and are assured of resurrection. Disciples committed in faith to Christ, the crucified and risen Christ, who is the resurrection and the life, will live eternally. Martha expresses faith in Jesus as the Christ, the Son of God, the one who was to come into this world, that is, the promised Messiah. All these titles have been introduced in John
1:41,49,27,30.
