# The trap about taxes

Gospel (Mt 22:15-21). Representatives of the political and the religious authority combine to trap Jesus. The question they ask is not a genuine attempt to find the truth, and Jesus highlights their insincerity by addressing them as hypocrites. Theirs is a trick question designed to ensnare. If Jesus answered in the affirmative he would lose the esteem of the people who resented the Roman occupation. If he answered negatively, he could have been interpreted as siding with the zealots who wanted to overthrow Roman authority by force. Jesus does not possess a denarius, the Roman coin used to pay the poll tax, but his questioners do, showing their willingness to use Caesar's money when it suits them. They possess Caesar's money; they should give back to Caesar what belongs to him. However, they must do this in a way which acknowledges the ultimate sovereignty of God over all human authority, including that of Caesar. There can be only one king in Israel and the king of Israel is Lord of all.
