# The Gospel (Mat. 22:15-21). Contradiction was part of life for

Jesus. Mutual enemies patch up their differences in the hope of
getting rid of a teacher whose integrity is a reproach to them.
Jesus is without guile.
When he is handed a coin he points out what is implied in the inscription. Minting money is an exercise of authority. Caesar claims absolute power. Will Jesus compromise himself with the Romans?, his pursuers wonder. Jesus serenely exposes their shallowness.
Christ's purpose is not to prescribe a solution to the political problems of Palestine. He insists on priorities that are absolute -God must be served. Men are to resolve the claims of the state in the light of God's sovereignty. Jesus cuts through the tangled brushwood of quibbling to force us into the clearing of truth. No man can serve two masters (Mat. 6:24). Cardinal Wolsey tried in vain.
Paul tells proud Roman citizens that the peace of Christ far surpasses Caesar's much vaunted peace, pax Romana. Jesus applies to the man in the street what God said to Cyrus and continues to say to each one of us today about what the new Israel implies and demands of us. The ideal living conditions created for us by technology today are no substitute for the recognition of God's transcendence and a realistic acknowledgement of man's servant status vis-à-vis his maker.
