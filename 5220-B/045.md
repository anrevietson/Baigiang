# Doers of the word

Second Reading (James 1:17-18, 21-22, 27). Coincidentally, this reading evokes the motifs discovered in both the first reading and the Responsorial psalm. The 'good endowment' which James has in mind (1:17) is the 'wisdom' of 1:5 which he subsequently identifies with 'the word of truth' (1:18). This 'word of truth' is Christ him self communicated in the gospel and received by faith at baptism. For James (as for Paul, 1 Cor. 1:24) Christ is the 'wisdom of God'. It is in him that we have the final recipe for the good life and human success.
'Implanted' in men's hearts (1 :21), this wisdom can succeed only if, like a seed, it germinates, grows, flowers and bears fruit. This 'fruit' is the new moral life of the Christian, regenerated by baptism (1:18). Without it, the Christian life is barren. According to James, the Christian must either bear fruit or die. He must practise what he preaches (1:22). The true worship of God consists not in mere ritual purity (cf. the Responsorial psalm) but in obedience to the commandments, all of which are resumed in the service of one's fellows, especially the
afflicted (1:27). This is not the 'wisdom' of the 'world' (1:27) from which the Christian is to keep himself 'unstained'. True purity for the Christian is the new way of life revealed by Christ.
As Israel of old (cf. the first reading), the Christian Church is 'the first fruits' of all God's creatures (1:18). The Christian vocation is not to leave the world but to go into the world and there manifest God's plan for the world. All men should be able to see in the Christian's behaviour the fulfilment of their hopes and their aspirations .
