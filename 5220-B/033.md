# The law of Moses and human tradition

The Gospel (Mark 7 :1-8, 14-15, 21-23). At the time of Jesus, the law of Moses (which, as we have already seen, comprised many 'statutes and ordinances') was virtually smothered by a plethora of minute interpretations and applications of the law by the rabbis which constituted the 'tradition of the elders'. The ritual washing before meals was one such man-made 'tradition'. One effect of this exaggerated juridicism was that the religious leaders of Jesus' day very often appear to give more importance to the man-made traditions than to the law itself. Jesus saw this as the re-appearance of the hypocrisy and formalism condemned by Isaiah (Mark 7 :6-7). The case in point (the washing of the hands) is particularly apt to receive Isaiah's stricture. The Pharisees and the scribes were worried about anything unclean passing through the lips (this people honours me with their lips'), but they were little concerned with what happened in the heart ('but their heart is far from me'). Their worship is empty because (as we have seen in the second reading) it is not inspired by obedience to the commandments. In vv.9-1 3 Jesus gives an example (the case of the 'Corban') of this preference of man for God.
Jesus takes advantage of this dispute with the Pharisees and the scribes to teach the true meaning of purity. His appeal to the people to 'understand' a point which is so obvious to us today (v.14) suggests the revolutionary character of this teaching, and the fact that he should have to explain this 'parable' (v.17) to his uncomprehending disciples (v.18 f.) shows how human traditions had managed to stifle even common sense.
Jesus goes, quite literally, to the heart of purity. This is a matter not of exterior ritual but of interior rectitude. According to the Bible the heart is not just the seat of the symbol of man's affermitivity. It is the centre of his distinctive life as a man. If his heart is evil, he will be evil. If his heart is good, he will be good. Everything
proceeds from the heart. Jesus shows that true purification is the conversion of one's heart.
