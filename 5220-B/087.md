# The wisdom of the Law

First Reading (Deut. 4 :1-2, 6-8). The major part of the book of Deuteronomy consists of a code of 'statutes and ordinances' (12-26) and a series of sermons, modelled on the sermons of the levitical priests in the Israelite sanctuaries but placed in the mouth of Moses to produce greater effect. The main topic of these sermons is faithfulness to the covenant by obedience to God revealing his will through the law (that is, the whole complex of Israel's social and religious 'statutes and ordinances', seen as coming from God through Moses and as Israel's part in the covenant agreement). According to the Deuteronomic theology, Israel's existence and prosperity depend upon the execution of God's law: .... that you may live, and go in and take possession of the land which the Lord gives you' (4 :1). Although the land is God's gift to his people, it has to be received freely and recognised as a gift. Unlike the Baals of Canaan, Israel's God is to be worshipped not by fertility rites but by obedience to his commandments. True life and prosperity for the Israelite consists not in attachment to the soil but in relationship with the transcendent God which is achieved by obedience. The clarion call of Deuteronomy is 'Hear, 0 Israel' (6 :4). Far from being an infringement of man's freedom, Deuteronomy's notion of commandment is truly liberating. It is by the commandment that Israel really begins to live, because 'man does not live by bread alone, but . . . by everything that proceeds out of the mouth of the Lord' (8 :3)
In the ancient Middle-East 'wisdom and understanding' were essentially practical attributes, oriented to the happy life and human success. For Deuteronomy, Israel's 'wisdom' (that is, its recipe for success) is the law of God. Although it is a 'revealed' wisdom, it is directed towards the happy life (Deut. 30 :15-20). Deuteronomy knows no life other than the present one. If it is to be happy, it must be inspired by obedience to the commandments.
Israel's wisdom is not, however, a treasure to be locked up and hidden from the world. No book of the Old Testament stresses more than Deuteronomy the fact that Israel is the Lord's 'chosen' people (7 :6), but it also emphasises that this choice is a pure gift (7 :7) and that it is not an exclusive choice. In 'choosing' Israel, God has not excluded other nations. These latter are just as 'chosen' as Israel, since God's choice is inclusive. His choice of Israel is for the other nations. Israel was intended by God as a pilot nation (Exod. 19 :6). It is in contemplating Israel's 'wisdom' that other nations are to arrive at the acknowledgement of God's presence among men (4:6-8).
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 14 :2-5). The Lord's 'tent' (probably a reference to the Jerusalem Temple) is a symbol of God's presence with his people. To 'dwell' with him (that is, to be related with him) it is necessary to keep the commandments. The liturgy is useless (positively harmful even) if it is not supported and inspired by righteous behaviour.
