# The call of Jeremiah

First Reading (Jer. 1:4-5, 17-18). The great prophets of Israel were called directly by God and sent by him to bear his message to his people. (We shall read of the call and mission of Isaiah' next Sunday.) This divine mission of theirs, the fact that they were sent by God, is what constituted true prophets. In today's reading we hear of Jeremiah's vocation to be a prophet. The Lord tells him that even before his birth he was consecrated for this task. This does not mean that he was sanctified in his mother's womb, but that in the divine plan he was set 'aside to do a particular work. In general the mission of the true prophets 'of Israel was a difficult one. More often than not they were raised up to preach repentance to a sinful people Who rejected their message. This was particularly true of Jeremiah who had much to suffer. God forewarns him that this will be so. Yet by reason of divine help the prophet will prove unassailable against the taunts and attacks of the country's kings, princes, priests, and landed gentry with the full rights of citizens (literally : 'the people of the land', rendered 'the country people' in the Jerusalem Bible!). Jeremiah is 'appointed prophet to the nations'. This does not mean that, like the servant of the Lord, he was sent to convert the pagans. Jeremiah was sent to proclaim God's will concerning the pagan nations, to declare that the God of Israel was master of their destinies also and that he would punish them for their sins. This Jeremiah did (cf. chapters forty-six to fifty-one). The passage is chosen to go with the Gospel reading which speaks of the true prophet to the nations, one rejected by his own people.
