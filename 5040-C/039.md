# From admiration to rejection

Gospel (Lk 4:21-30). People were deeply moved by Jesus' words last Sunday when he said that he came in the spirit of God to bring Good News to the poor, the imprisoned and the blind. But now they begin to wonder how such a local boy can really be a prophet in whom they should put their trust. They are enraged when he reminds them of earlier biblical examples where people would not honour such prophets as Elijah and Elisha, and they had to be recognized by foreigners instead. Did his audience really consider Jesus to be blasphemous when he identifies himself with prophets like Elijah or Elisha? Or were they disturbed by what they perceived as his arrogance in thinking himself better than they were? Or were they just insulted that foreigners would heed a prophet better than Israelites would? Whatever their motives, clearly they did not put their faith in Jesus as a result of what he said. The reader, however, knows something more than they did: Jesus' message was destined to reach out far beyond Israel itself to win over gentiles and foreigners to the Kingdom of God which he preached.
