# The rejection at Nazareth

The Gospel (Luke 4:21-20). This reading continues that of last Sunday and opens by noting the admiration of the men of Nazareth for Jesus because of his homily in their synagogue. Then, in what must look like an incredibly abrupt change of mind, these same people of Nazareth take exception to Jesus, throw him out of
their town and attempt to kill him. What has really happened is that Luke has combined accounts of two distinct visits of Jesus to Nazareth, the second one (also recounted by Matthew, 13:53-58, and Mark, 6:1-6) a visit that took place towards the end of his Galilean ministry. Mark and Matthew tell us that the Nazarenes failed to see in Jesus any other than the son of the local carpenter. Through lack of faith they lost the promises. In this they were typical and symbolical of Israel in general and in his references to the earlier prophets Elijah (Elias) and Elisha (Eliseus) Jesus pointed out to them that as God's mercy had formerly reached beyond Israel so would it now. (For the episodes narrated of these prophets see 1 Kings 17:1, 8-16; 2 Kings 5:1-14.) The Nazarenes got the message and tried to kill Jesus. But his hour had not yet come and in one way or another he went unscathed. (There is no need to postulate a special miracle.) No cliff such as that mentioned by Luke is now visible at Nazareth itself, but recent excavations have shown that in Christ's day there probably was.
