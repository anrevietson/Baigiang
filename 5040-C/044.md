# The greatest gift is love

Second Reading (1 Cor. 12:31-13:13). The Christians at Corinth had received very special gifts from God ; the gift of knowledge (guises, or special insight into revealed truths) ; that of speaking in languages not understood by other members of the assembly; the gift of interpreting these strange languages ; the gift of prophecy, and others besides. There was a danger that these special gifts would lead to pride, to ostentation and to divisions within the community. There was also the likelihood that the newly-converted Christians would esteem as highest those gifts that 'attracted most attention. For Paul the service they rendered to the Church was the criterion by which they were to be evaluated. The higher gifts, he tells them, are those they should aim at (12:31). What these 'higher' gifts are he explains in chapter fourteen, which is the natural continuation of chapter twelve. He recalls, however, that above all these gifts there is a higher one, 'the best way of all'. This leads him to set forth this way, the way of love.
Love is the one gift that is necessary ; the divine love poured out
on men's hearts at baptism. Without it possession of all other gifts, knowledge of all conceivable languages ('of angels and of men'), charismatic faith ('that moves mountains'), humanitarian interest in others and all the other gifts, are useless. The love of which Paul speaks is that Christian virtue which has men love both God and men and look on God and men as Christ did. It loves God for his own sake and man for the sake of God, that is for man's own sake, because it loves man and looks on him for what he is in himself :
the image of God. It goes out to others to help them, not to use them. It knows that God cannot be honoured by men except through his image which is man. It seeks the good of others, what is good for them. Christian love is consequently respectful and tactful. Alien to it are all thoughts and actions that might give offence or lead to divisions. Love will never come to an end, whereas the other gifts will. Even the most prized of these such as 'knowledge' and prophecy are as toys for children when compared with the perfect possession of God and the knowledge of him that await men in heaven.
