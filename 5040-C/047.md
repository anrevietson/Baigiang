# The greatest gift is love

Second Reading (1 Cor. 12 :31-13 :13). The Christians at Corinth had received very special gifts from God. There was a danger that these special gifts would lead to pride, to ostentation and to divisions within the community and that the newly-converted Christians would esteem as highest those gifts that attracted most attention. For Paul the service they rendered to the Church was
the criterion by which they were to be evaluated. He recalls, however, that above all these gifts there is 'the best way of all', the way of love.
Love is the one gift that is necessary; the divine love poured out on men's hearts at baptism. Without it possession of all other gifts, knowledge of all conceivable languages ('of angels and of men'), charismatic faith ('that moves mountains'), humanitarian interest in others and all the other gifts, are useless. The love of which Paul speaks is that Christian virtue which has men love both God and men and look on God and men as Christ did. It goes out to others to help them, not to use them. It knows that God cannot be honoured by men except through his image which is man. It seeks the good of others, what is good for them. Christian love is consequently respectful and tactful. Alien to it are all thoughts and actions that might give offence or lead to divisions. Love will never come to an end, whereas the other gifts will. Even the most prized of these such as 'knowledge' and prophecy are as toys for children when compared with the perfect possession of God and the knowledge of him that await men in heaven.
