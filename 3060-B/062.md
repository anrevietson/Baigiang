# The Passion according to Mark

Passion (Mk 14:1-15:47). The high point of Mark's Gospel is the centurion's confession: 'Truly this man was the son of God'. It is the first confession by a human creature of the divine sonship of Jesus. The Father's voice had called Jesus his Son at his Baptism (1:9-11) and again at the Transfiguration (9:7). The demons had known who he was (1:24) and called him the Son of God (3:11; 5:7). The High Priest had challenged him on the point and took Jesus reply for blasphemy (14:61-64). But the people merely questioned who Jesus was and if Peter eventually affirmed that Jesus was the Christ, it was at the cost of much misunderstanding (8:29.32-33). Mark lays much emphasis on the disciples' failure to understand. But it would be wrong to seek the cause of their failure in stupidity or lack of generosity. There is an inevitability about Judas' betrayal, Peter's denial and the disciples flight from Jesus, shown in its correspondence to prophecy (14:21.27-31). Jesus had not died and they had not experienced the passion. They did not know that Jesus' messiahship included suffering and death. The centurion can begin to under-stand because he stands on the other side of Jesus' death. If there is a hidden impatience in Mark's account of their failure, it lies in his dissatisfaction with the acceptance of the Cross in the community to which his Gospel is addressed. What the centurion dimly grasps, the disciples are to realise in full once they have experienced the Resurrection: it is the suffering Jesus who is the Messianic Son of God. Then the Temple made with hands will be replaced by the Temple not made with hands (15:58), the Christian community of prayer, worship and reconciliation, the Body of Christ.
