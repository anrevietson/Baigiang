# The Passion according to Mark

Passion (Mk 14:1-15:47). To attempt any meaningful commentary on two full chapters of Mark's Gospel would be foolhardy. And indeed any such explanation would be uncalled for on a day such as this. The liturgy gives us the full passion story of the second evangelist. This presents us with the opportunity of considering the entire passion of Christ as one whole, from the completed plot of the chief priests and scribes two days before the Passover to the execution of this by the crucifixion and burial of Jesus. Mark presents these events in a Passover context. The first event is given as taking place at Bethany where Jesus is anointed by a woman who for Mark remains anonymous. She recognises him as the anointed one and Jesus himself acknowledges the significance of the event: he is aware that the end is near. The woman's recognition of Jesus and his mission (while his apostles do not) will be proclaimed with every reading of this Passion narrative. Named women would attend to Jesus' burial and named women would be the first to get the message that he had risen from the dead (Mk 16:1-8). The Bethany episode is neatly rounded off my Mark with the account of the betrayal by Judas. Next comes the account of the Last Supper and the institution of the Eucharist. The unnamed woman will have her memorial. Jesus leaves a memorial of himself in the Eucharist, in his Body, the bread that is broken and in his blood of the covenant. Judas' betrayal has already taken place. Next comes the revelation of Peter's denial and desertion by the other apostles. Jesus is alone and he faces the morrow in the agony of Gethsemane. Here we see the extent of his hutnanity, but also the mystery of salvation that is at work
- the tragic mixture of human treachery, human weakness, the dedication and dignity of Jesus right through the trial and passion. At his death the end of an era is signalled by the rending of the veil of the temple. The ending of the narrative is very touching. There is no mention of his apostles; these have fled. At Calvary there were some women watching from a distance. He is laid to rest through the good offices of the Sanhedrin member Joseph of Aramathea and the two named women take note of the place of burial. The scene is set for the good news of Easter Sunday.
