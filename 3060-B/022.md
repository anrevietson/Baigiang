# The third Servant Song

First Reading (Is 50:4-7). There is little that need be said about this reading, the third of the four servant songs. (The others will be read on Monday, Tuesday and Friday of Holy Week.) We are not quite sure who precisely the original author of these poems had in mind when he wrote, if indeed he was thinking of any particular individual. The servant would be the one raised up or sent by God to fill this role. Part of the unfolding role of this servant will be to know rejection and insult. The poem aptly describes Jesus and his sufferings.
Second Reading Phil 2:6-11). This is a very early Christian hymn on Jesus and his sufferings, one taken up and incorporated by St Paul into his letter to the Philippians. In today's liturgy it calls for reflection rather than detailed exegesis. It speaks of Jesus and his patient acceptance of the humiliation of human existence and the shame of the tragic events of the passion and death - all of which ended in glory. Although not included in this lectionary reading, it is worth recalling that Paul's reason for including this hymn in his letter is to present his readers with an example to follow in the daily trials of Christian living. 'Do nothing from selfishness or conceit, but in humility count others better than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others. Have this mind among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus...' (Phil2:3-5). Our liturgical celebrations centre on Christ, but on Christ who is life and our model.
