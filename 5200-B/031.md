# Living wisely

Second Reading (Eph 5:15-20). Christians manifest the virtue of wisdom in prudent actions. When one is not sure how to act, one ought to take measures to discern God's will ,and then carry out one's decision. At Baptism the Christian promised to do God's will.
The author admonishes members of the community not to drink excessively at Christian gatherings. Christians are asked to distinguish themselves from members of mystery cults who drank abusively at religious gatherings. Christians are asked to converse with one another and to draw their fellowship from being filled with the Spirit. The Holy Spirit prompts them to encourage one another and to extend their service beyond the community. Their faith may draw them into a life of suffering and persecution, but the foolishness of the Cross tells them how to face difficulties with joy. Wisdom has come full circle. It entails a decision for good and against evil which will bring a godly peace. This peace, in turn, may upset cultural balances which will then cause one to suffer. In such cases, the mutual support and love of Christians for each other draws their attention to a level of life that transcends the everyday comforts and needs they used to have. The presence of God can then offer them peace even in the midst of conflict, a peace no one can take away.
