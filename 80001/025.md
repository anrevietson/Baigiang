# God's faithfulness

Second Reading (1 Cor 1:3-9). (Note the link between Is 61:1 and 1 Cor 1:9) Too often we simply think of Advent as a time for waiting for Christmas. Our reading makes it clear that what we await is the final working out of God's plan for the world. It could be summarised assaying that God loves each one of us as if there were only one of us to love. Grace and peace is a standard blessing at the beginning of Paul's letters
possibly a mixture of traditional greetings in Greek (karis - grace) and in Hebrew (shalom - peace). In all Paul's letters the same basic formula is followed: he (1) gives thanks, (2) always, (3) to God, (4) on behalf of the Christians to whom he writes (5) because of the things God has done for them and in them, and which he then proceeds to list. Clearly Paul approves of the gifts the Corinthians had received, though, as the letter continues, he makes it clear that he disapproves of the way in which they have used their gifts. Faith must involve witness. 'The day of our Lord Jesus Christ' was the time when the Jews expected God to intervene directly into history, the day when the old world would be wiped out and the new world born, when humankind would be judged. Sometimes it is difficult to know whether to be more astounded at the dauntless faith and courage of Paul, who might have given up in despair, or whether to be more heartened that it was upon such stony ground and such unpromising foundations that God was able to build his Church.
