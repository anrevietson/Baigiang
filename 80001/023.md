# Awaiting the coming of Christ

Second Reading (1 Cor. 1:3-9). The porously, or the second coming of Christ, was ardently awaited in the early Church. It occupied a prominent place in Paul's mind, at an early period. This is evident from the two epistles to the Thessalonians, which are the earliest of Paul's letters we possess. In his later ministry and letters the expectation of an immediate return faded from view. Yet the parousia itself and the completion of the redemptive work of Christ implied in it remained very much part of his faith and continued to play a formative role in his presentation of the Christian message. In today's reading the apostle reminds the Christians of Corinth of the many gifts and graces with which God has enriched them, all for one purpose. the building up of the Church through a genuine Christian life. In this way the kingdom of God is now coming as his reign is being established in men's hearts. This work will be perfected at Christ's second coming, the last day, the end of time, the 'Day of Our Lord Jesus Christ'. All Christian existence is but as a vigil in which believers await and look forward to this great event. This attitude of joyous expectancy characterised the early Church, and this because the early Christians looked on Christ at his coming as friend and redeemer rather than as judge. The parousia meant the return of Christ to take his own to himself for all eternity. In a later presentation of the mystery the end was looked on as a dreaded event, the dies irae. 'the day of wrath, that dreadful day ...' All joy was put out of it. Renewed acquaintance with the message of the New Testament is now redressing the balance.
