# They will look on the one they have pierced

The obscurity of the first reading (Zech. 12:10-11) in its original context in the book of Zechariah is notorious ; even the textual reading is uncertain (but that of the Jerusalem Bible is here assumed to be correct), and even more, who is 'the one whom they have pierced'. The passage comes from a prophecy of the final restoration of Jerusalem written about 300 BC, and the events here mentioned are to be moments in this restoration. But whatever their original meaning we can perhaps best interpret them in the sense given them in the New Testament. The 'spirit of kindness and prayer' is that spirit which is poured out upon the apostles at Pentecost; as Peter interprets it in his speech on that day (Acts 2:17). This new spirit promised for the last times which was to bring a new and enhanced life of greater personal intimacy with God, was one of the central elements in the prophecies (e.g. Ezek. 37:1-14; 36:25; Joel 3:1); it was fulfilled in the presence and activity of the holy Spirit in the Christian community, of which the early Christians were so acutely aware (see SCRIPTURE IN CHURCH, vol.1; no.1, 'Appendix III'). The 'one whom they have pierced' is seen by John 19:37 as Jesus; in his role as the suffering servant of the Lord prophesied by the book of Isaiah (52:13-53:12), the sinless one whose sufferings atone for the sins of others, and who reaches his final vindication only through pain and humiliation.
