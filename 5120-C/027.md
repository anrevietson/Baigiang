# Peter's confession

Gospel (Lk 9:18-24). Peter's confession of Jesus in Luke is not as central as in Mark and Matthew. Here Luke makes a connection with Herod Antipas' questioning of Jesus' identity in 9:9. Given that context, Peter dismisses the common opinions and confesses Jesus as the Messiah of God. It is significant that Luke sets this scene with Jesus at prayer. It is even more significant that Luke omits any rebuke of Peter for refusing to accept a suffering Messiah (see Mk 8:33; Mt 16:23).
While Jesus acknowledges Peter's perception, he goes beyond it. 'Messiah' was the anointed Davidic King who was pledged to provide for the lowest members of society. In this stage of the gospel tradition 'Messiah' is linked with 'Son of Man' and Suffering Servant. In Daniel 7 and the pre-Christian Book of Enoch the Son of Man is a divine being hidden in God's presence who would be revealed at the end time and preside in glory over God's kingdom.
The Suffering Servant (see Is 52:13-53:12) is a prophetic figure who is rejected by Israel. He is a guilt offering who is finally exonerated for bring about the survival of the nation. Jesus, therefore, will be Messiah but only as Suffering Son of Man. Hence it is a messiahship that includes suffering, rejection, death, but finally exoneration on the third day.
Jesus in the end addresses not just the disciples mentioned earlier but all followers. The essence of following Jesus consists of three elements:
denying oneself, carrying one's cross, and following. Luke has Jesus insist on a daily carrying of one's cross. Finally there is the paradox: if you lose your life for Jesus' sake, you save it; but if you save your life, you lose it.
