# The suffering servant

The Gospel (Luke 9:18-24). The same figure of the suffering servant undoubtedly played an important part in Jesus' own thought about his mission . in many of his sayings about his coming passion he uses the actual phrases of this passage of Isaiah. Today's Gospel passage is the earliest occasion on which Jesus is explicit about his destined suffering. It forms a sort of watershed in the central section of the gospel constituted by the instruction of the disciples. After he has been rejected by the Jewish leaders and by the masses, Jesus turns to his small band of followers and begins to ensure that they, at least; understand. First he brings them to the recognition that he is the Messiah or the Christ (equivalent Hebrew and Greek terms ; each meaning 'the anointed one') of God. The crowds had come some of the way to understanding by saying that he was the reincarnation of one of the ancient prophets, for the Messiah was to be - among other things - a prophet. The title of Elijah is even greater, for he was expected as the final prophet, the herald of God's final coming at the consummation of the world (see on the tenth Sunday of the year), and Jesus did indeed play this part of Elijah. John the Baptist was thought by many to be Elijah because he preached that the kingdom of the last times was at hand. But it is Peter, the spokesman of the apostles, who is brought to recognise the fullness of Jesus' personality ; Matthew at this point inserts a saying of Jesus praising his faith for this, and showing that such faith can only be supernatural (Mt. 16:17); Luke has a similar mention of Peter's faith, and a promise similar to that which follows in Matthew, in his account of the last supper (Luke 22:32).
But secrecy is still enjoined on the apostles because they do not as yet understand the 'nature of Jesus' messianic function ; only now do they begin to learn that suffering is the lot 'of the Messiah; they could not spread the message of the Messiah until they understood him. It was only with difficulty that they learnt it, so he forbade them to spread the message 'until after the Son of Man had risen from the dead' (Mark 9:9), and indeed Luke shows them still hoping for the establishment of an earthly kingdom right up to the time they received the enlightenment of the holy Spirit (Acts 1:6).
At the same time Jesus begins to teach the apostles that to follow Christ means to suffer with him, a lesson which will follow each of the great prophecies of the passion, for their role is to be other Christs in the world.
