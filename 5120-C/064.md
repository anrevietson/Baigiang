# The one they have pierced

First Reading (Zech 12:10-11). Zechariah 12 is part of a second anonymous collection of oracles often called Third Zechariah (chs 12-14). This third-century B.C. collection speaks of a time of conflict and pain prior to God's definitive victory. After a time of victory over the pagan nations (Zech 12:1-9) there will be a period of renewal and repentance for God's people. God will pour out a spirit of grace and supplication. Hence God will take the initiative in creating a new attitude on behalf of the people whereby change becomes possible. As a result the people will be able to turn toward God and seek his favour.
The more difficult and probably correct reading of 12: lOlb is: 'They shall look to me.' Hence the people will turn to the Lord. What follows, however, is a contrast. The people shall mourn for the one they have pierced. Who this pierced person actually is not at all clear. Because of this sin the people will break out in mourning. It will be a mourning as intense as that for an only child or firstborn. Hadad was the Syrian name of Baal, the Canaanite god of fertility, and Rimmon was an epithet probably meaning 'thunderer.' This may suggest the pagan rite of mourning for the dead god. Thus the renewed people of God will indeed recognize the folly of their former ways.
