# The pain of discipleship

Today's celebration focuses on the unique status of the disciple but also on the pain linked to that status. We are reminded that through baptism we have become the sons and daughters of God. At the same time we are told that the daily carrying of the cross must be part of our identity. Through this celebration of the Eucharist we are to receive the strength to cope with the cross and thereby give evidence of our calling.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must acknowledge that we have often preferred the honour of the term "Christian" without the daily effort to show its meaning. Aware of these failures, we humbly approach both our God and our community and seek forgiveness.
You call us to act in ways befitting sons and daughters of God. Lord, have mercy.
You callus to take up our cross and follow in the manner shown by our God. Christ, have mercy.
You call us to lose ourselves for our God and our community. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR THE READINGS
First Reading (Zech 12:10-11). The author reminds his audience that God will show his graciousness to them. However, they will also mourn because of a violent action.
Second Reading (Gal 3:26-29). Paul assures his audience that they have a unique dignity as sons and daughters of God. Moreover, all the old barriers that kept them apart are now abolished.
Gospel (Lk 9:18-24). Luke presents Jesus as one who accepts the title of Messiah but links it to the experience of suffering and death. All who would follow Jesus must be willing to bear their cross daily.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Realizing our special status as sons and daughters of God, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all religious and civil leaders. May they recognize the dignity of each person and thus act in justice.
We pray for the members of our local faith community. May we recognize our mutual relationship as members of God's family by helping carry each other's cross.
We pray for all those who have despaired of their dignity as sons and daughters of God. May our efforts to assist them reveal that they are truly worthy of such special status.
President: Gracious and loving God, we take pride in being called your sons and daughters. Continue to give us a sense of our family heritage so that our lives may reflect your great love. Enable us also to imitate Jesus our brother by making the cross a daily reality. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we acknowledge not only that God is indeed our Father but also that we are members of God's family. In praying to our Father, we must also pray with and for our brothers and sisters. One particular way we show our bond with each other is our readiness to forgive all those who hurt us. The willingness to offer reconciliation is the clear sign that we are indeed one family.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Our sign of peace is a telling symbol of our relationship to each other both during liturgy and after it. To offer that sign at Mass is to express the willingness to be agents of peace and concern outside the Eucharistic celebration. Family members must uphold the bond of unity for all the other moments of the week.

COMMUNION REELECTION
In Eucharist we experience once again both the self-giving of Jesus and our readiness to be food and drink for others. To share the bread and the wine is to participate in the destiny of our brothers and sisters. Jesus' style of losing himself for others is to become our style as well. Eucharist must become that catalyst that enables us to recognise the dignity of others and then to help them cope with their daily effort to bear the cross. In Eucharist we pledge ourselves to carry our cross by helping them carry theirs.
