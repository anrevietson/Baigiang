# The destruction of the temple

The Gospel (Luke 21 :5-19). Today's reading brings together two items which were probably treated separately by Jesus in his preaching: the destruction of Jerusalem and the end of the world. Indeed the association of the two is so close that it must have been the intention of the Gospel writers to show that the two topics are, from the theological if not the chronological standpoint, closely related to each other. The destruction of Jerusalem was seen as marking the end of the old order of things: a decisive judgement by God on Judaism. It was, at the same time, climaxing the inauguration of the new, when the good news would be brought well beyond the narrow confines of Jewish tradition. The end of time is foretold with the apocalyptic language that was used to describe the 'Day of Yahweh' in the Old Testament (cf. e.g. Amos 5 :18-20). The destruction of Jerusalem, interpreted theologically as a judgement of God on his people, is presented as a forerunner of his judgement on all peoples at the end of time. Presumably the signs that the end-time is coming are to be interpreted symbolically, as they were also in the Old Testament, although each generation in the history of the Church has already witnessed its own share of wars, earthquakes, famines and plagues. In the meantime, however, Jesus calls for the virtue of endurance, endurance in a background of suspicion, hate, misunderstanding and even persecution. If our own understanding of the Gospel and our preaching of it, both as communities and individuals, does not lead to our being misunderstood and even persecuted, we have reason to fear that we have not interpreted Christ authentically.
