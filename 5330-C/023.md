# The apocalyptic discourse

The Gospel (Luke 21 :5-19). Christ's discourse on the End, or the Apocalyptic Discourse' as it is generally called, is found with certain significant differences in each of the first three gospels. It is a difficult discourse to understand and interpret, in good part by reason of the fact that it contains references both to the destruction of Jerusalem and to the end of the world. Fortunately, this year s reading from Luke is less difficult than other sections, as it refers principally to the destruction of the Holy City and the Temple by the Roman armies AD 70, some forty years after Jesus had prophesied their destruction. The disciples were legitimately proud of the Temple. In Christ's day it must have been a magnificent structure indeed. Herod the Great had commenced its reconstruction in 19 BC and the work was still in progress during Christ's lifetime. All that now remains is the platform on which the Temple once stood. But even the huge and splendidly cut stones of this give an idea of what must have been the splendour of the Temple itself. But its beauty was short-lived. A chain of events led the Jewish nation to revolt against Rome AD 66. The uprising ended in national disaster four years later. The period preceding the final catastrophe was fraught with dangers for the Christians of Palestine. The troubled situation would pave the way for false Jewish messiahs, proclaiming themselves the redeemers of Israel. These would prove a temptation to Christ's followers. Those who believed in Christ must also be prepared for social ostracism, for suffering and persecution. In their trials they will have need of patience and fortitude.
Christ's words hold true for all ages. Trials of one kind or another are part and parcel of Christian living. Christians must ever be prepared to counter false philosophies of life which try to win them from their only Teacher and Leader. And in these trials the secret of victory lies in the steadfastness Or endurance of Christ (cf. 2 Thes. 3 :5, read in the second lesson last Sunday), that steadfastness of which Christ is the exempler and the source.
