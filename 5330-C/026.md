# A dark picture of suffering

The readings for the last Sundays before Advent always present a dark picture of suffering and destruction. If we look at them closely, how-ever, all these dark scenes are just the opening act in the coming of the Son of Man and are preliminaries to happiness.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We ask God's forgiveness for the times when we have given way to hopelessness and have doubted the promise that all will be well.
You are the hope of every man and woman. Lord, have mercy.
You will shine out and heal us in your warmth. Christ, have mercy.
With your help, nothing will crush us. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Mal 3:19-20). Life as it is will not go on forever. The future will bring destruction for the evil and healing for the good.
Second Reading (2 Thess 3:7-12). Paul is writing about those who had lost the run of themselves with their ideas about Christ's second coming. He tells us all to go about our lives in an orderly and responsible way.
Gospel (Lk 21:5-19). Even though the full resources of the state were mobilized to crush the followers of Jesus, he imagines a peace beyond suffering where all will be transformed by the power of Christ's love.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: We turn to our Father in heaven who invites us to walk in our pilgrim way through life ready to shed what blocks us from true happiness.
Reader(s): We pray for the whole Christian community that it may be alert and responsive to the paths along which God is calling it in
these days.
We pray that no custom, way of doing things or attitude of mind will inhibit the full flowering of our Christian vocation.
We pray for strength of purpose and endurance as we struggle to cope will all the problems of daily life.
We pray that we may be prudent and diligent in our daily lives and be respected for our honesty and justice.
President: Help us, Lord, to so live with confidence and trust, that our lives may be happy and our hope assured.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
We pray that the coming of God's Kingdom into our lives may root out all that is evil and unworthy.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
May God's peace heal our bitter memories and soothe our wounds and hurts as we offer one another a sign of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Jesus never promised an easy life. He offered rather a strenuous and demanding one. However, he did contract to be with us always and give us the strength to win our lives. In the confidence which he has given us, we leave this church in this power.
