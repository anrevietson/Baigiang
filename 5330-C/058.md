# Against idleness

Second Reading (2 Thes. 3 :7-12). Expectation of an immediate return of Christ had some of the Christians at Thessalonica abandon work. The free-Greeks, unlike the Jews, did not look kindly on labour. This they left to slaves. The imminence of the second coming (parousia was but a pretext for idleness among the section of the community Paul now mentions. He had noted their tendency to this vice and had warned them against it when he first preached to them. He again brought it to their attention in his first letter to them (1 Thes. 4 :11; 5 :14). Now he speaks out quite strongly. Idleness among Christians, which has them live on the shoulders of others, simply cannot be condoned. Christians should set a good example of quiet living to outsiders, and in any event man is intended to earn his bread by the sweat of his brow. Charity and almsgiving is one thing and a Christian obligation. But condoning idleness which leads to meddling in other peoples' affairs is quite another. Paul worked, as a tent-maker, for his living while among the Thessalonians. This he did, so as not to be a burden for them. But his life also set them an example which they should follow. The application of Paul's words to our own day should be obvious.
