# The third Servant Song

First Reading (Is. 50 :5-9). This is taken from the third Servant Song (Is. 50 :4-li). In the first song (Is. 42 :1-7) the Servant appears as a prophet. In the second (Is. 49 :1-6) he assumes the role of a teacher. In the fourth (Is. 52 :13 - 53 :12) he takes on the character of a self-sacrificing priest. In the present one we find the note of opposition to his teaching and his suffering and persecution being introduced. It is not difficult to understand why some have referred to this set of songs as 'the fifth gospel', since, read together, they appear to plot out the future career of Jesus. This does not mean that the anonymous prophet of the exile actually had a preview of Jesus' career some six hundred years before Jesus, but rather that Jesus himself understood his own mission in the light of these songs. They must have formed one of the most important bases of his own meditation. Certainly the New Testament authors found in them an admirable expression of his mission and destiny.
Despite the opposition and the persecution of his fellows, the Servant knows (because he is the Servant of the Lord) that the Lord will vindicate and deliver him. Although it is impossible to identify the Servant with any historical figure (perhaps because of this), he is clearly a type of the suffering just man, the person who is oppressed by others. We saw last week that God is the vindicator of such a person. The Servant suffers, however, not merely in spite of his innocence but precisely because of it and, further, because of his preaching and teaching mission. If ever a case cried to heaven for vindication it was his. The author of these songs is so convinced of the righteousness of God that the eventual rescue of the Servant (despite even death, 52 :13 - 53 :12) is guaranteed.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 115 :1-6, 8-9). This psalm is the thanksgiving prayer of a suffering just man (cf. the first reading), whose prayer for help has been heard and who has been delivered from death.
