# The Suffering Servant

First Reading (Is 50:5-9). This is one of four songs/poems scattered through chapters 40 to 55 of Isaiah which depict a perfect servant of Yahweh God. This person is often called the suffering servant', but who exactly it is remains an enigma. Suggestions include the prophet himself, Moses, Jeremiah or even the people as a whole. In truth, perhaps even Isaiah didn't know who it was. Through his poetry he was putting words on an intuition; an intuition inspired by faith in a desperate situation. These poems come from the time the Israelites were in exile in Babylon. When innocent people were in misery no one could provide an avenue of hope - not even politicians, diplomats or experienced pragmatists. The prophet/poet imagines some innocent sufferer being able to stand up to all oppression - non-violently - and thereby redeem all. It remained a dream until Jesus came to put flesh and blood on this inspired insight of Isaiah. The early Church, and probably Jesus himself, turned to this elusive person for so long existing only in faith to make sense of Jesus' paradoxical way of setting God's reign in place.
