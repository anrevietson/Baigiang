# The turning point of Mark's gospel

Gospel (Mk 8:27-35). This extract is found half way through Mark's gospel and is regarded by many as a key episode. Already in the gospel different interest groups - demoniacs, evil spirits, Jewish authorities -have given their opinion as to who Jesus is. Now it is the disciples' turn to come public with their understanding. Jesus' question shouldn't be seen to imply any identity crisis on his part, the focus is on the state of the disciples' faith. Both he and Peter, who responds on behalf of the disciples, agree implicitly on who Jesus is. The point at issue is what both parties might mean when they call him 'Christ'. Jesus sees suffering as an intrinsic component of his being the Christ, while, at this moment at least, Peter's view is quite the opposite. Mark, writing perhaps thirty-five years after the incident, would have known many believers who found it difficult to move from Peter's view to that of Jesus. It wasn't easy for them to accept suffering in their lives as they felt Jesus had conquered it forever - and for everyone.
