# Faith without works is dead

Second Reading (Jas 2:14-18). With James more than any other New Testament writer you feel the contemporary world burning the reader's tongue. You sense in James a sensibility made sharp and resolute by everyday hunger and injustice. His words take on the urgencies of our own time. He becomes, and is, the voice of poor. In their name he indicts the times for their oppressions. His accusation is heard across the sanctuary of the church. He cannot accept a faith which is without love for the hungry. James' faith insists on deeds of mercy and justice. Faith without deeds is a dead seed. In acts it seeds, and we know it by its fruits.
