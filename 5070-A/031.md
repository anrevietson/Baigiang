# The body of Christ

The sacrament of the Eucharist, the Mass, reminds us of the Church, the body of Christ. One of the four notes, distinguishing characteristics, of the Church is that it is holy: One, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic. We are God's holy people, called to be holy as God himself is holy. In today's liturgy we are called on to reflect on this truth.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We are called to be holy, without sin, as God himself is holy. We are only too aware that this is far from being the case. Let us now for a moment call our sins to mind and ask for God's pardon.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Lev 19:1-2, 17-18). This reading is from a section of the book of Leviticus actually known as "The Holiness Code". Holiness is not just otherworldly. To be holy like God, we must show concern and care, as God does, for those in trouble.
Second Reading (1 Cor 3:16-23). The Christian community, and each individual Christian, is a temple of God, made holy by God's holy Spirit. This implies that we should respect and honour Christ's Church, and see that there are no harmful divisions within it.
Gospel (Mt 5:38-48). We are to be perfect, Jesus, says as God, our heavenly Father is perfect. This Christian perfection is to be shown particularly in kindness and forgiveness to those who injure us.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As God's holy people, called together by God to pray for the Church and for all races of the world, let us raise our prayers of intercession to our heavenly Father through Jesus Christ our mediator.
Reader(s). Let us pray for all peoples, of all races and religions: that they may come to know of the great love and care which God, their heavenly Father, has for them.
Let us pray for the Church, the Body of Christ: that it may be aware of the holiness and unity to which it is called.
Let us pray for all Christians divided among themselves in faith while still believing in Christ as the Son of God. May God bring such divisions to a end and restore among believers that unity for which his Son Jesus prayed.
Let us pray for our Holy Father Pope John Paul: that God may continue to inspire him to work for unity among Christians and nations.
Let us pray for those countries and peoples divided by hatred, war and strife. May God pour out on them a spirit of forgiveness and compassion. May they learn to practise that forgiveness which Jesus has taught us.
Let us pray for the sick and lonely everywhere, particularly those in our own community.
Let us pray for our faithful departed.
Let us pray in silence for a moment for our own private intentions.
President: Heavenly Father, your Son and your Holy Spirit have spoken to us through your sacred scriptures, read in this Mass. We make our humble prayers to you in response to your own word to us. We ask you to hear these our prayers which me make in the name of Jesus your Son.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As we come to the Lord's Prayer let us first ask God to help us to forgive those who have sinned against us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
We are all one in the Lord, called to live in unity with one another and to wish one another well. As a sign of this Christian belief let us offer one another the sign of peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
The Eucharist is a sacrament, a indication that what it stands for, that of which it is a sign, can and must become a reality in our lives through the all-powerful grace of God. We are called to be holy. Communion with Christ the bread of life, the source of life, brings us this holiness. This holiness is not something merely internal, within our souls. Communion with Christ, with the Body of Christ, in the Eucharist must mean concern for and communion with Christ's brothers and sisters around us, in particular with the weak, the neglected, the hungry. May our communion with the living Christ in this Eucharist bring us into that wider communion with all that Christ and the Eucharist stand for.
