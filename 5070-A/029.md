# Love your enemies

The Gospel (Mat. 5 :38-48). This passage is a continuation of that read last Sunday and in it Christ continues the contrast between his own teaching and that of the Old Testament period. He has come to reveal to man what the goodness and holiness of God, the heavenly Father, really means, and to call on men to live according to this teaching. We look on the law of talion: 'eye for eye and tooth for tooth' as an example of primitive savagery. Actually, the law, found in the Bible and outside of it in Babylonian law codes, was originally intended to mitigate revenge. The punishment was to correspond to the injury, not to exceed it - one eye, not two eyes or a life, was to repay damage to an eye, etc. Christ's remedy for evil goes deeper. Revenge, as all other sins, is born in the heart. The remedy indicated by Christ is to purify one's heart, and to win others not through fear but through love. Kindness is to repay evil.
We have already treated of what Christ says of the love of enemies in the commentary on the first reading. Love must be universal simply because God's Fatherhood is universal and the model for our imitation is the perfection of God himself. You must therefore he perfect as your heavenly Father is perfect.' Too often this maxim is shorn clear of its biblical context, with the result that Christian perfection has tended to he looked on as something of an abstraction, hidden within men's souls and seen only by God. The perfection of which Matthew speaks is not such. God's perfection (Luke says 'mercy') is shown in his consideration for all men. Christian perfection is at once interior and external,, possessing man's inner being and shown in that behaviour towards others of which the' Sermon on the Mount speaks.
