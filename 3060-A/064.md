# The hymn of Philippians

Second Reading (Phil 2:6-11). The letter to the Philippians, filled with joy and thanksgiving, is actually written while Paul is in prison (Phil 1:13). In chapter 2, the apostle speaks about Christian life in community, appropriately using the example of Christ to challenge the community to attitudes and actions consistent with the gospel message. Quoting from an early Christian hymn, Paul's emphasis is on the selflessness and humility of Christ that ultimately resulted in his death. He 'emptied himself,"" did not cling to his equality with God," and accepted "death on a cross." These attitudes are poignant reminders to the community to imitate Christ in their humility, concern for others, and self-gift. The servant qualities become the model for the followers of Christ who became a slave-servant for our sake. The second part of the hymn stresses the glory given Jesus, completing the paradigm of death/resurrection. This twofold perspective of the hymn reinforces the paradox of Christian life, that of suffering and glory.
