# The beginning of Holy Week

Today is the beginning of Holy Week, during which we remember in a special way our Lord's suffering and death for the life of the world. In this Mass, we recall his entry into Jerusalem as a humble Son of David and we remind ourselves that it was only through his resurrection from the dead that he became our Lord and Saviour.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Our Lord suffered, died and rose again, out of obedience to his Father and his love for us. As we begin our celebration, let us recognize our own disobedience to God and our lack of love, asking our Lord to create his own attitude within us.
Lord Jesus, you loved us and gave yourself in suffering and death for us. Lord, have mercy.
You have shown us the path to glory with the Father. Christ, have mercy.
You teach us how to do our Father's will. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
Procession Reading (Mt 21:1-11). Jesus enters Jerusalem as a humble Messiah and is acclaimed by the crowds.
First Reading (1s 50:4-7). The Servant of Lord, the ideal disciple and teacher, suffers for his faithfulness to the Lord.
Second Reading (Phil 2:6-11). Jesus humbled himself to the point of death and was elevated by God to share in his glory.
Gospel (Mt 26:14-27:66). Jesus knowingly and willingly suffers in-justice and violence, in the calm conviction that he is doing his Father's will.

PRAYER OR THE FAHTHFUL

President: Following the example of our Lord during his suffering, let us pray to God to help us in our needs.
Response: Lord, hear our prayer.
Reader(s): For the Church throughout the world: that all the members of the Church may learn from our Lord's suffering how to serve God and our neighbour in a spirit of humility. Lord, we pray.
For all those who are suffering: the sick, the poor, the lonely, the depressed, the homeless, especially those who are the victims of injustice:
that they may experience the saving power of our Lord's suffering in their distress. Lord, we pray.
For all those who care for the suffering people of our world: doctors, nurses, social workers, and for all those who support them: that they may be encouraged through their understanding of our Lord's suffering. Lord, we pray.
For this community: that during the next week we may enter into the spirit of the liturgy, watching and praying with our Lord and so coming closer to him. Lord, we pray.
President: Father, Just as your Son was strengthened by his prayer during his suffering, so may we be encouraged by these prayers which we make to you through this same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In his prayer in the Garden of Gethsemane, our Lord addressed God as his Father. Let us now do the same.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Our Lord showed peace even in the midst of his suffering. Let us now greet one another with a sign of this peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Our communion with our Lord reminds us that the one whose suffering and death we are remembering at this time was raised from the dead, is alive and with us at this moment. May our realization of this enable us to bear humbly and patiently all our suffering, especially that which may come from our being faithful to our Lord.
