# The Servant's ear opened

First Reading (Is 50:4-7). This reading comes from Second Isaiah, the prophet associated with Chapters 5 to 56 of the book of Isaiah. The major theme of the prophetic utterance is the holiness of Yahweh, a holiness that transforms human history (Is 43:15-21). However, the prophecy also contains the four servant songs (Is 42:1-4; 49:1-6; 50:4-9; 52:13-53:12) that poetically reflect on the mission of the suffering servant figure, with this reading being a selection from the third song. The servant speaks "the Lord has given me a disciple's tongue", and then acknowledges that he listens "like a disciple" to the Lord who has opened his ears.(Is 50:4). In Isaiah 8:16 a similar notion of discipleship occurs: "Bind up the testimony, seal the teaching among my disciples." The prophets' word is the word of the Lord and it must be conveyed to the people. Referring to the difficult mission of the prophet who meets resistance, the passage shows perseverance in mission with little concern for self-preservation. The servant/disciple becomes the symbol for Jesus during his passion. As Christians reflect on the meaning of the passage, the descriptions aptly apply to the suffering servant Jesus, who, struck, insulted and scorned, continues confidently in his mission.
