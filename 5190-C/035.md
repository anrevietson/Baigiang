# The faith of Abraham

Second Reading (Heb. 11:1-2, 8-19). The author encourages those Christians to remain steadfast in faith, who are in danger of rejecting their Lord because of persecution and hostile statement about them. Among the exemplary heroes of the Old Testament, who continued to hope in the fulfilment of promises made to them at their call despite set-backs, special and prolonged mention is made of Abraham.
We, Christians, can also win the divine approval by working out our call in Abraham's way. He left his own people, not knowing where he was to go. He, and the other Patriarchs, were to live in the Land of Promise as travelling people. They did not possess it. Their descendants continued to hope, setting us an example to regard our sojourn here on this earth as a pilgrimage and not a real home.
The climax of Abraham's faith lies in his readiness to sacrifice the son of his hope, Isaac. This was a death to himself, which merited the resurrection involved in Isaac being handed hack to him.
We might point, lastly, to the charity of Abraham. The Eastern Churches see him as a model of this virtue. He was called, and went out, for his future descendants. This man for others was buried in the Only plot of ground he himself possessed of the Promised Land.
