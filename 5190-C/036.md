# Do not be afraid, little flock

The Gospel (Luke 12 :32-48). The passage begins with a note of consolation to followers of Jesus who find that their lives may become a daily crucifixion in an unappreciative world. cf. 9 :23. Future happiness is assured if an effective poverty exists in their lives. Generous giving lays up treasure in heaven.
It is through the practice of almsgiving that Luke sees the Christian disciple as bringing the motto 'Be Ready' into effect. The parables of the servants awaiting the return of the Master illustrate clearly that we should remain constantly in a state of readiness for the second coming of Jesus.
Note particularly the last sentence. Those of us who have many gifts, intellectual, physical, material, . . ., have, in the very giving, received a call to greater service. If this is not forthcoming, we shall be judged far more severely than the under-privileged when the Master comes again.
It would seem that a unifying theme can be presented at the beginning of Mass from our discussion of the three readings. I would suggest 'Our call to follow Christ involves the worship of God, our loving Father, and a greater service of our needy brethren.'
