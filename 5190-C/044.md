# Faith in what is hoped for

Second Reading (Heb 11:1-2.8-19). Hebrews, like Wisdom, was written for people who were tempted to abandon their faith. Here the source of the temptation is the hostility and persecution which the Christians of the day had to face. The author urges trust in God.
The present passage has been called the roll-call of the heroes of the faith, heroes because they trusted. Abraham obeyed God's call to set out for an unknown land, because he trusted. He obeyed God's call to sacrifice his only son Isaac, because he trusted. Sarah too trusted, even though she knew that what God had in mind for her was humanly impossible. In each case, these heroes of the faith trusted in God because God had made a promise. And God was faithful. Faith, trust in God, is our guarantee of the blessings for which we hope.
Abraham is a favourite choice for those who sculpted the ancient kish high crosses. The thousand-year-old Ballyogan cross - at Graignamanagh, Co. Kilkenny - shows Abraham with knife poised to sacrifice Isaac. If trust in God is the basic attitude of the believer, then the fostering of such trust among our forebears, by recording Abraham's story in stone, is understandable.
