# The night of the Passover

First Reading (Wis 18:6-9). The Book of Wisdom dates from fifty years or so before Christ. At the time, Jews living in Egypt were going through a crisis of faith, a crisis occasioned by the sceptical and secular attitudes of the pagans among whom they lived. The author, himself a devout Jew whose name we do not know, is trying to strengthen the hope of his country men and women in Egypt, and to affirm them in their faith.
The present passage hints at the courage and joy (v.6) which ought to characterize the outlook of the pious Israelite. The basis of that courage and joy is the Israelite's trust in the utter fidelity of God, the God who had promised ('oaths', v.6) to be the deliverer of his people. God's fidelity is marvellously attested to in the exodus event ('that night', v. 6) when he liberated his people from the bondage of Pharaoh. The exodus was powerful proof that the Israelites were special in God's eyes, a people called for a mission and made glorious in that calling. In the final verse, the author in attributing to his forebears the practice of chanting the Hallel psalms at the first Passover, is doubtless thinking of the Passover liturgy of his own day.
The passover, in sum, provides a historical basis for trusting utterly in the fidelity of God.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps 32:1.12;18-20.22). This psalm vibrates with joy. Carroll Stuhlmueller terms it a symphony of praise. The basis for this joy and praise is God's loving care for his chosen ones, those who hope in him. For them, God is their help and their shield.
