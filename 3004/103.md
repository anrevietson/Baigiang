# Return to me with all your heart

First reading (Joel 2:12-18). The book of Joel was written after the return from the Babylonian exile, sometime about 400 BC. The book was occasioned by a disastrous locust plague which Joel describes in 1:1-12. Like the prophets who preceded him Joel saw in this calamity a punishment for Israel's sin. Knowing that punishment could not be avoided unless sinners repented he summoned the people to the traditional expressions of repentance - prayer, fasting, and the wearing of sackcloth (1:13-15).
But Joel saw in the locust plague a foreshadowing of the ,day of the Lord', that day of terror when Yahweh would finally crush his enemies. So in 2:1-11 the prophet gives a new description of the plague in terms of invading armies that represent the final attack of the nations on Israel before their destruction by Yahweh. It is this description that motivates the summons to penance which we read today. The penance to which the Jews are called is a matter of internal dispositions rather than of external signs of sorrow. ,Rend your hearts and not your garments.' Joel is aware that the God who now punishes Israel is still merciful and ready to forgive. This awareness is the basis for his hope even when disaster threatens his people.
Responsorial psalm (Ps. 50). The choice of this, the Church's most characteristic prayer of penance, as today's responsorial psalm is most apt. The first verse we read is the appeal for mercy made by a guilty sinner to a God who is declared merciful and kind. The second is a humble admission of guilt. The third is a prayer for interior purification that leads to true conversion. The fourth is an earnest plea for grace which alone can make a true change of heart possible.
