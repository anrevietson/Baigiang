# Human involvement

Today's celebration underlines the necessity of human involvement/action to sustain life with God and the community. We are reminded that good intentions by themselves are simply not adequate. As we gather for worship this Sunday, we are challenged to renew our Christian commitment to God and the community by doing, not merely saying.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must acknowledge that we have not always responded to God's call and the community's call through involvement. Aware of these failures, we approach our God and our community, asking for forgiveness.
You call us to assist our community. Lord, have mercy.
You call us to recognize the image of our God in the needs of the community. Christ, have mercy.
You enable us to become not only sayers but also doers. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Ezek 18:25-28). Ezekiel points out to his audience that their plight stems from their own sinfulness. Nevertheless God is willing to begin anew if they change their lives by doing what is right.
Second Reading (Phil 2:1 - 11). Paul appeals for harmony, humility, and selflessness in the Philippian community. As the supreme example of such conduct, he presents the self-emptying Jesus.
Gospel (Mt 21:28-32). Matthew offers a parable by Jesus on doing, not merely saying. He contrasts the religious outcasts who repent with the Jewish Leadership that refuses to repent.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware that we must respond to the call of our God and our community through action, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all civil and religious leaders. May they fulfil their obligations to the community by action and not mere words.
We pray for the members of our local faith community. May the example of the self-emptying Jesus lead us to sustain and nourish one another by involvement in our common needs.
We pray for all those, living and dead, who adopted a plan of action to reach out to others. May their memory provide us with the energy to take concrete steps to assist our sisters and brothers.
We pray for all those who have never put their good intentions into action. May our manner of living become an incentive to move from word to deed.
President: Generous and loving God, we know that you insist on action, not mere words through the lifestyle and deathstyle of your Son. Help us to realize that our self-emptying will necessarily lead to our self-fulfilment. Enable us to translate our pledged words into deeds.
We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we ask that his name be hallowed and his Kingdom come. By saying these words, we implicitly dedicate ourselves to precise ways in which we will help to make God's name hallowed and his kingdom a greater reality. By its very nature this prayer is action-oriented.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Our sign of peace is essentially a pledge that we take our community seriously. To offer such a sign is to involve ourselves in the needs and concerns of the community. To wish peace is to promote peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
In Eucharist we share in the death-style and life-style of Jesus. In both styles he was a person for others. By participating in the bread and the cup, we assume a course of action on behalf of the members of the community. Our worship is only real to the extent that it involves the sisters and brothers of Jesus. By its very nature Eucharist is an unambiguous call to involvement on their behalf.
