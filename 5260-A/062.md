# Personal responsibility

First Reading (Ezek 18:25-28). Following the destruction of Jerusalem Ezekiel's exilic audience lived in an atmosphere of despondency and cynicism. They maintained that God was unjustly punishing them for the sins of their forefathers. In response, the prophet points out that they themselves had sinned and justly deserved this punishment. Here the prophet elaborates his teaching on personal responsibility. If the wicked person turns from wickedness and pursues what is right and just, that person will live. However, if the righteous person turns away from righteousness and commits iniquity, that person will surely die. To be sure, it is a question of actions, not just words.
The language of the prophet (live/die) reminded the exiles of the liturgy in the temple. Here, for example, the priest would readmit or refuse remittance to a leper (see Lev 13:3,13). Here too the priest would begin an examination of conscience whereby only the righteous were permitted to take part in worship (see P55 15; 24). It is meaningful that the community (here represented by the priest) would welcome back wayward members. Acceptance by the community is acceptance by the Lord.
