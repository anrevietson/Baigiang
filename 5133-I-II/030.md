# Let justice roll down like waters

First Reading (Amos 5 :14-15, 21-24). Amos saw that nothing short of a radical change of life could save Israel, and he feared that it would not come. He had warned those who looked to the 'Day of the Lord' as the time of triumph of God's people over all her enemies that the day would be darkness and not light (2 :18). He had warned that the sleeping Assyrian giant would soon waken and destroy Israel (3 :9-11). There was only one way to avert the wrath to come: 'Hate evil, and love good, and establish justice in the gate [i.e. in the law courts]; it may be that Yahweh, the God of hosts, will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph' (5 :15). Though social injustice, dishonesty in business and corruption in law-proceedings were rife, great zeal was shown in religious matters. These same people went on pilgrimages (4 :4 f; 5 :4) and took part in ostentatious and formalistic festivals. But these were provocation of Yahweh whose moral demands were being blatantly contravened. Yahweh has no use for the hypocritical ritual which was an insult to him and not worship of him; he looked for justice and goodness.
