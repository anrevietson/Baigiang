# The song of the vineyard

First Reading (Is. 5:1-5). Composed during the early years of his ministry, this prophecy of Isaiah takes the form of a parable, one of the most perfect of its kind in the Bible. It is in the form of a popular ballad that one might sing at a vintage festival. The prophet assumes the role of a singer and takes for his theme the relations between his 'friend' and his vineyard. He begins on a pleasant and happy note and reveals the hard truth only at the end. His sympathy is clearly for God whose tender care for his vineyard has been of no avail. God's grief and disappointment rather than the people's tragedy is the theme of the song.
Nothing was left undone to ensure an abundant yield of grapes. The prophet's 'friend' walled in the vineyard and built a watch-tower for the protection of his property. Only the best was good enough: he planted the choicest vines, a special variety producing fine-quality red grapes. Even the wine-press was made ready. This was usually hollowed out of solid rock, with two compartments:
the upper in which the grapes were crushed, and the lower into which the grape-juice was allowed to run.
But in spite of all this care, the vineyard yielded only sour or worthless grapes. In verses 3-6 it is clearly God himself who speaks. He has done everything possible; the fault must be in his people. Let them consider the paltry results which the vineyard has yielded in return for all the care lavished upon it; they must surely acknowledge that the verdict about to be pronounced is just. The idea of Israel as a vine, chosen by God and afterwards rejected, is touched on by other prophets, Hos. 10:1 f.; Jer. 2:21; 5:10; 6:9; 12:10; Ezek. 15:1-8; 17:3-10; 9:10-14. Jesus gives it a new emphasis in the parable of the wicked husbandmen, Mat. 21:33 if., and John 15:1-2 bring out the mystery of the 'true' vine.
The Responsorial Psalm continues the theme of the vine. The northern kingdom has fallen (it is after 721 BC) and has been colonised with a mixed population of idolatrous immigrants, like so many weeds in the garden of God. And the psalmist asks 'Why?' What has God done to his vineyard? 'Visit this vine and protect it
and we shall never forsake you again.'
