# The song of the vineyard

First Reading (Is 5:1-7). Can God be angry or hurt or disappointed with what men and women make of their lives? Does the Maker of all things really even care about what we do, or what we have failed to do? This kind of question, which occurs so readily to us today, never seems to have troubled Israel's prophets, who delighted in attributing to the Creator a whole range of human emotions. In this moving 'Song of the Vineyard', Isaiah emphatically affirms God's hopes of us, and the corresponding divine anger if we fail to 'yield a vintage harvest'. Was he projecting a painful fact his own romantic experience, when singing of God's unrequited love for Israel? A colourful anthropomorphism, of course! Yet this 'divine love-song' is an important parable, capturing with lyrical intensity the drama of salvation history. God's People have never responded (nor respond!) adequately to all the covenant-love lavished upon them. The prophet's sharpest criticism is directed at lack of justice and integrity among the faithful. These are the vital fruits required of a covenanted people.
