# The God of peace

Second Reading (Phil 4:6-9. These verses form part of Paul's final exhortation to his beloved Philippians. In it he admonishes them against fear. The Philippian community was inclined to be factious and divisive. There had been bickering and in-fighting and they appear to be downcast. Their anxiety, Paul says, betrays a lack of trust in God and so he urges them to pray to God with confidence. The peace of God which surpasses all understanding and which is a power at work in us, able to do far more abundantly than all we ask or imagine (Eph 3:20) will protect them. This peace which is rooted in God should characterise all their relationships with one another and is built upon truth, honesty and justice. All the virtues mentioned by Paul here are taken from popular moral teaching of his day, particularly that of the Stoic philosophers. He encourages the Philippians to discover these qualities in their own Christian tradition. Here as on other occasions, Paul sets himself up as an example for his hearers to imitate: 'be imitators of me, as I am of Christ' (1 Cor 11:1). The final outcome of such imitation is not only the peace of God but the God of Peace.
