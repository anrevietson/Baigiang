# The song of the vineyard

First Reading (Is 5:1-7). The parable or song of the vineyard is clearly sub-divided: it consists of a brief introduction (v.1 a), the song proper (vv. lb-6) and its interpretation in verse 7. The material content and the presence of the inhabitants of Jerusalem (v.3) would suggest that the occasion on which the prophecy was proclaimed was the annual harvest and vintage festival. It probably dates to the period of Isaiah's ministry prior to the Syro-Ephraimite War. The parable begins with words popularly associated with love songs. This would have been particularly appropriate at vintage time when the hearers would have easily associated the love song with the fertility myths of the ancient world which celebrated the union of the male and female deities. Quickly, however, it turns into a trial in which the audience is invited to pass judgement (v.3). In verses 5-6 the inevitable penalty is pronounced and its justice acknowledged. Then in verse 7 comes the whole point of the parable, the knockout punch: 'you are the worthless vineyard'. It is very similar to Nathan's parable of the ewe-lamb addressed to David on the occasion of his adultery with Bathsheba. Like the inhabitants of Jerusalem and Judab, David condemns himself out of his own mouth and Nathan declares:
'You are the man'. In the case of the present parable, Israel, God's elect community, is the vineyard of Yahweh. But they have proved faithless. They have violated the covenant of love which binds them to the Lord. In choosing and faithfully guiding his people God expected a response of faithfulness and righteousness. He looked for justice and found bloodshed, for compassion and found oppression.
