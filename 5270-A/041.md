# The parable of the tenants

The Gospel (Mat. 21:33-43). Conflict with the Jewish authorities has become characteristic of Jesus' mission. More and more urgently the Jews are being faced with the choice: Jesus and the Kingdom or rejection by God.
The parable in today's reading, which contains many allegorical details, is adapted from Isaiah. It calls up the whole drama of Israel, chosen and rebellious, and now faced with a final decision. Its meaning is clear to every Jew well versed in the scriptures, for the image of the vine is used in the Old Testament to designate Israel, and everyone knew from memory the 'song of the vineyard' of Isaiah 5:1-7.
The details - the protecting wall or fence, the winepress hollowed out of rock, the watchtower - have no allegorical significance but are meant to establish the identity of the vineyard by literary reminiscence of Isaiah. But the parable as told by Jesus contains a further point, not mentioned by Isaiah: the sending of the son introduced by the poignant 'They will respect my son.'
The landowner is God. The vineyard is Israel. The wicked tenants are the religious leaders in Israel. The servants are the prophets. The son is Jesus. The son's death is the crucifixion outside Jerusalem (cf. Heb. 13:13-14).
Only Matthew reports the saying about the transfer of the Kingdom to others who will cultivate it properly. In this he is envisaging the situation of the Church; Mark simply stops at the failure of the Jewish nation.
The parable emphasizes the responsibility of the leaders of the Jews. In Matthew's version, which differs from Mark and Luke here, the judgment of the owner on his tenants is posed under the form of a question (w. 40-41). In this way the hearers themselves are made to pronounce the verdict: the master of the vineyard will put to death the guilty vinedresser and lease out his vineyard to other tenants. How are we cultivating our little patch of God's vineyard? Has the sending of his Son made such a great difference to our way of life?
