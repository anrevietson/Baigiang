# Ezekiel as a sign

First Reading (Ezek. 24:15-24). Though Ezekiel's visions tend towards bizarre imagery, yet, simple everyday things could inspire him, like a sentry mounting guard (3:17-21), or a wall being built (13:10-16). His own personal experiences could move him: the death of his wife, as in today's reading. The prophet would have been expected to mourn his bereavement with manifest signs and ritual, in accordance with custom; that he did not do so would have been immediately open to question. Thus his symbolic action would not have gone unnoticed and he would have occasion to point to its lesson. What is involved is a grief too deep for mourning - so, too, the exiles Will be stricken, made speechless and immobile, by the destruction of Jerusalem. We cannot fail to be stirred by the depth of human feeling displayed in this passage. The grief of the prophet is not an act; it is the very reality of his sorrow for a beloved wife that makes him truly a sign to his people.
