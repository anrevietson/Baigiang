# Blessed are those who have not seen

Gospel (Jn 20:19-31). In this last chapter of the gospel, John tells of the appearances of the Risen Jesus to his disciples. Today's reading is easily divided into two parts: Jesus' appearance to the disciples (vv 19-23) and his appearance to Thomas (vv 24-29), followed by a summary of the purpose of John's gospel account. Each section includes a colorful description of the event and teaching for the disciples and ourselves.
When Jesus appears to his followers on the evening of the first day of the week, he finds them gathered in fear and offers them the customary greeting of peace. Recognition of the Lord occurs when he shows them his wounds. Jesus then commissions these believers to continue his mission. 'Just as the Father sent me, so I send you' (Jn 20:21). In language reminiscent of Genesis (2:7), the disciples receive the gift of the Spirit and the power to forgive sins. John's timing of the sending of the Spirit differs from Luke's account of this event on Pentecost (Acts 2). However, both writers associate Jesus' gift of the Spirit with his exaltation.
The Thomas episode occurs eight days later, following the apostle's refusal to believe the testimony of the other disciples. Thomas' hesitancy at this time is consistent with his previous questioning of Jesus during the Last Discourse (Jn 14:5) and his pessimistic view of Jesus' power at the death of Lazarus (Jn 11:16). However, following the sudden appearance of Jesus is the Lord's invitation to Thomas to believe. The apostle's lack of faith gives way to his confession: 'My Lord and my God!' (Jn 20:28). Jesus' response, John's only beatitude, refers to all believers since that time. We are blessed because we, unlike Thomas, believe the testimony of others.
The reading's conclusion refers to the purpose of Johns gospel and the point of all the signs, namely, that we believe in Jesus. The consequence of our belief is life in his name.
