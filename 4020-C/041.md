# Jesus appears to the disciples

The Gospel (John 20 :19-31) (K.C.). Today's reading is the conclusion of the Gospel proper (chapter twenty-one is an appendix). The high point is the confession by Thomas, and it is also the climax of the Gospel, the consummation of the thesis proclaimed in the prologue: 'The Word became flesh and dwelt among us; and we saw his glory.' The whole Gospel leads therefore to this, and the conclusion in verse thirty and following follows from it. But it is a conclusion of faith, not a demonstration of reason.
The real point of the 'closed doors' is to underline the new existence-form of Jesus after resurrection - indeed, his divinity. He is no longer subject to the laws of space. To a certain extent, this conflicts with the realism of the command to Thomas 'Put your finger here ...' but this should not be pressed. The point is -whatever about the form of existence - it is the same Jesus. He comes with 'peace'. In the Bible it never merely means absence of turmoil. More positively, it is messianic salvation, and all that this means. Jesus comes to impart a mezsson: 'as the Father has sent me . . . ' He comes with the Spirit - John's parallel to the Pentecost event. The fullness of the power of the Spirit is here linked with the forgiving - or 'retaining' - of sins. It is John's parallel to Matthew 16 :19; 18 :20. To say, however, that the sacrament of Penance derives directly from this would possibly be saying too much. In the context of the Gospel Jesus is the 'Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world'; men are saved in so far as they have faith, are judged if they refuse faith (3 :18). By faith in him men stand or fall. So too with the disciples; they are God's sign in the world (21b). And for John all sin (or 'sins') may be reduced to refusal of faith. The scene with Thomas is a lesson in faith. Thomas' confession- but not his way to it - expresses the fullness of faith: 'my Lord and my God' . 'Blessed rather are those who have come to the faith without having seen' (29); faith is the one way to 'life' (31)
