# John's vision on Patmos

Second Reading (Rev 1:9-13.17-19). The beginning verses of Revelation set the tone for the rest of the book. John, while exiled on the small island of Patmos, experiences the prophetic Spirit (1 Cor 14:1) and hears a voice commanding him to write down what he sees. Images abound in this selection a trumpet, golden lampstands, a figure like the Son of Man, a scroll - images that John utilises again in the text, to demonstrate the connection between this vision and all that follows. The trumpet often describes a theophany, the manifestation of the presence of God (Ex 19:16.19; 1 Thess 4 :16), and seven, a number signifying completion, reminds listeners of the universal dominion and authority of the Son of Man. Divine attributes and God-language describe Jesus because of the power of his death and resurrection. Long priestly robes symbolize Christ's sacrifice, and his words confirm his living presence among us. In addition, the one like the Son of Man recalls Daniel 7: 9-10.13-14 as well as the inaugural visions of the prophets (Is I; Jer 1). While the apocalyptic language and imagery tend to focus our attention on the drama of the end times, its deeper message concerns the present. John utilises prophetic insight to guide and challenge his community living within an oppressive Roman empire. He has this specific situation in mind when he later speaks of the beast, dragon, and Babylon and calls for endurance in distress and tribulation. John clearly understands God's graciousness in the victory of the Lamb, and he realizes that we continue to experience God's new revelation in Jesus, the Risen Lord. This reality is reason for hope for the early Church.
