# Signs and wonders

First Reading (Acts 5:12-16). This passage, like those provided for Low Sunday in the other two years (2:42-47 and 4:32-35) is a summary by Luke of the salient aspects of life in the earliest Christian community. Since it is clear that he considers this earliest community at Jerusalem to be a model for all Christian communities, these passages have a special importance as a sort of blueprint, making up the picture of the Christian community as it ideally should be. The first summary is the broadest, but if there is a special keynote it is unity and brotherhood, which expresses itself especially in common prayer and a shared Eucharist, and issues in joy and praise. In the second and keynote is more especially community of goods and generosity to the members of the brotherhood. The third concentrates more upon their external relations, their good repute - a point often noted also about individuals by Luke, who is very sensitive about a good name - and the miracles they work.
One important lesson taught by the miraculous cures effected by Peter and the others is that the Church carries on the work of Christ, and that his powers are present in the Church. The summary of miraculous cures given here is very similar to that given at the end of Jesus' first day of ministry at Capernaum (Luke 4:40-41) and elsewhere (e.g. Mark 6:56). The Church carries on Jesus' message in the persons of the apostles; and, like him, they confirm and prove their teaching by working miracles. In Luke's gospel the working of miracles often occurs especially in connection with Jesus' authority, so that his power in word and deed is seen to be a single manifestation of the presence in him of the Spirit and of the advent of the kingdom of God. So also in the Acts the miracles of the apostles serve particularly to give weight and authority to their message.
Miracles seem strange and unfamiliar in the modern age. The New Testament writers look at them chiefly from the point of view of fulfilment : the time of the Messiah was to he a time when all evil, sickness and unhappiness was to give way to an era of peace, prosperity and joy. The healing miracles, then, are signs that this era has arrived, and continues to spread in the apostolic age.
