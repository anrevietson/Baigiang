# Can a mother forget her child?

First Reading (Is 49:14-15). This passage is part of the larger complex of 49:14-26. Each segment begins with a complaint against the Lord: (1) God's people are forsaken (v 14); (2) they are bereft and barren (v 21); (3) they are booty and captives (v 24). In turn, each complaint ushers in a proclamation of salvation: (1) reconstruction on a grand scale (vv 15-20); (2) the return of sons and daughters (vv 22-23); (3) conquest of the people's enemies (vv 25-26). Writing during the exile, this anonymous prophet offers a message of hope that is calculated to relieve their despondency and despair.
The specific complaint in the passage in that Yahweh abandoned the people at the time of Jerusalem's downfall and subsequent exile. In reply, the prophet paints a picture of their God as a mother who feels unparalleled compassion for her children. "Can a mother forget her infant, be without tenderness for the child of her womb?" The expression "to be tender" comes from the Hebrew word for "womb" and thus catches up all those levels of concern that a mother necessarily has for her child.. If one can possibly imagine a mother forgetting her child, this can not hold true for the God of Israel. "Forget" is fundamentally the abandoning of all those instincts that bind mother to child. Yahwehas mother articulates the most profound dimensions of divine love.
