# Trusting people

Today's celebration calls on us to be trusting people. We are powerfully reminded that our God does not stand aloof from our problems and concerns. We are challenged to lead lives grounded in that sense of confidence.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Today's liturgy speaks of a God who is intimately involved in our lives, of a God who regards us as persons, not things. Realizing that we have not always responded to our God, with a vibrant trust, we make the following petitions:
Lord, you give us the capacity to recognize your providence in everyday life. Lord, have mercy.
Lord, you give us the ability to see that we must also reach out to you. Christ, have mercy.
Lord, you give us the strength to be your ministers of concern for our sisters and brothers. Lord, have mercy.

HEADING FOR THE READINGS
First Reading (Is 49:14-15). This passage speaks of the frustration of the exiles in the sixth century B.C. They contend that the God of Israel has neglected them. The prophet responds that Yahweh has motherly concern for her children and, hence, will not abandon them.
Second Reading (1 Cor 4:1-5). Here Paul describes a preacher as a steward of the mysteries of God. As such, he/she is responsible for the proper discharge of God's plan of salvation. It is only the Lord who is to pass judgment on the preacher.
Gospel (Mt 6:24-34). Without ruling out concern for material goods, Matthew has Jesus focus on God as the great provider. The God who provides for the things of this passing world will exhibit far more interest in the needs of his people.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware of our call to lead lives of trust and confidence, we pray to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): For all civil and religious leaders who have assumed the obligation of caring for others. May they see our God as their role model in providing generously for their charges.
For all parents who have taken upon themselves the task of nourishing both body and spirit. May they reflect the compassion and love of our deeply committed God.
For the depressed and neglected in our community. May they find in us believers who seek to alleviate their needs by imaging the God of mercy and concern.
For all those who have lavished their care on us over the years. May their example move us to do likewise and thus lead us to recognize the sisters and brothers of Jesus in our midst.
President: Father, we confidently bring these petitions before you. We know that in giving us your Son, you have expressed the greatest degree of involvement in our lives. We pray that this expression of love may be the springboard for our providing for others. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we call upon the Father to meet our needs. however, we also realize that this prayer also commissions us to meet the needs of others. To ask for help is to offer help.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Our sign of peace symbolizes our commitment to each other. we thereby express that we are members of one family of faith. To offer a sign of peace is to be willing to provide peace for our sisters and brothers in Church.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
We have shared the bread and the cup. Once again we have received nourishment and sustenance for the week ahead. At the same time we have shared the bread and the cup with the members of the community. By that action we show that we are to nourish and sustain them - we are to be bread and wine for them. Eucharist both challenges and empowers us to be the agents of God's providence.
