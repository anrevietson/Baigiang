# Get behind me, Satan

Gospel (Mt 16:21-27). What a sudden change from Peter's great profession of faith and its commendation, "Blessed are you Simon BarJona" to "Go behind me, Satan"! The Rock has become a stumbling-block. The contrast marks a watershed in Jesus' ministry. The beginning of a new phase is signalised also by the use of the same phrase, "From that time Jesus began ... "which Matthew used to introduce the public life of Jesus (4:17). Having reached the level of faith where Peter recognised the Master as "the Christ the Son of the Living God", the disciples can now be told the manner in which the Christ is to fulfil his mission. His Messiahship is not political. Rather, he must go to Jerusalem, the locus of sacrifice and access to God, where he will offer the definitive Passover sacrifice of his life and then be raised to victory on the third day. It is on the same path to God that he is to lead his followers.
