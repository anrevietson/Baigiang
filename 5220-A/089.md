# You seduced me, Lord

First Reading (Jer. 20:7-9). The prophet Jeremiah is traditionally the prophet of Passiontide, and by implication is linked naturally with the passion predictions of the Gospels, as is the case in today's Mass.
Of all the prophets Jeremiah most reveals his inner dispositions towards his divine calling. His vocation was 'to tear up and to knock down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant' (1:10). Perhaps he never realized how much uprooting he would need to do. Had he felt that his mission from God would somehow render him immune to barbs from the people he loved, who were after all God's people too? The hopes of earlier years were dashed when the reforming King Josiah fell on the battlefield in 609 BC. For Josiah's successor had neither the drive nor inclination to continue a renewal programme which was not without its unpopularity. Political intrigue was more in demand as Judab suddenly found herself sandwiched between two world powers after a political vacuum of several decades.
The reign of Jehoiakim was not only a time of crisis for Judali. Jeremiah was spiritually and emotionally depressed. The religious reform was discontinued or rather reversed, and in his own vocation he was experiencing all work and no play, all tearing down and no raising up. His prophecies of the time are studded with 'confessional' pieces in which he pours out his spirit against his sufferings. The present passage is the climax of these confessions. Even God himself is blamed for having seduced and enticed the prophet against his will. Yet the divine call is so powerful, constraining the prophet who remained constant in adversity.
The context of the outcry promotes something of its pain. God sent him to the temple with a symbolic action to perform. For performing what should have been recognized as God's word, Jeremiah is beaten and put in stocks. This is probably the first time physical violence is used against him. It is not the last in a worsening political Situation, but it shakes him to the roots.
