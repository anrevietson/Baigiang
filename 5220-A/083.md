# A living sacrifice

Second Reading (Rom 12:1-2). St Paul here bases the moral section of his letter on what is new in Christian worship, viz., its basis in living our everyday life as a response to God's treatment of us. The previous part of the letter had described loving mercy as the essential quality of God and the key to understanding his plan of universal salvation. It is not a mercy that is merely pitying or indulgent towards sinners, but, rather, one that resembles a mother who, in dedicating herself to the life and love of her child, overlooks faults and weaknesses. The same quality is required in our response to God, in what the Greeks called liturgy. Other religions, including the Jews, got people to offer God public work or sacrifices that were external to themselves, as substitutes (scape-goats) for themselves. For Christians, the offering must be their own bodies, by which he means their whole lives. "Present your bodies as a living sacrifice".
