# Jesus' rejection in Galilee

Gospel (Mk 3:20-35). Our Gospel reading is that part of Mark's Gospel in which he shows Jesus' rejection in Galilee. He begins this section with some stories of a positive response to Jesus' preaching. Some accept Jesus as a healer, yet they do not know or recognize his true identity. Mter the call of the Twelve in which Mark teaches that true discipleship means sharing Jesus' ministry, he presents in today's reading the negative responses to the ministry of Jesus. This rejection involves Jesus' own relatives. They seem to think he is embarrassing the family. The opposition, though, also includes those whom Mark calls scribes from Jerusalem. Jesus tries to show that their arguments are invalid and would mean that, if he were of Satan, the Kingdom of Satan would be battling itself. Instead he teaches that the Kingdom of God is breaking into people's lives. Jesus rejected those in his family who would keep him from his work. True discipleship, he says, is doing God's will. It is hard for us to imagine the strength of his argument. This was a society in which family was all important. Jesus taught that even this was insignificant in the ministry of the Kingdom. Mark thus emphasized the need of a total commitment to discipleship.
