# The fall

First Reading (Gen 3:9-15). The first twelve chapters of the Book of Genesis give us theological reflections on the period before we have history. These chapters give us pre-history and cannot be read or understood as history. The whole Book of Genesis is a compilation from four different traditions about the origins of the world and humankind, Genesis l:1-2:4a is the priestly account of creation and human sin, whereas our passage is part of the Yahwist account. The Yahwist tradition is the earliest of the sources of Genesis, written during the reigns of David and Solomon (about tenth century BCE), and presents God in very human terms.
Today's passage is in two parts: the first describes the fall in which Adam and Eve eat of the tree of knowledge of good and evil --a symbol of their desire to be autonomous, masters over everything. The second part consists in a poem that probably predates this section of Genesis and describes the consequences of sin: human suffering and death, and God's promise of redemption.
The second reading will show how a Christian approach to suffering can be redemptive, and the gospel will present Jesus' conquests of Satan as reversing the curse in Genesis.
