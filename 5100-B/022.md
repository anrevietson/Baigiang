# Jesus' family

The Gospel (Mark 3:20-35). The Greek text leaves us with a possible translation less injurious to the 'friends' (family) than the common account. In this context Jesus is actually in the house, trying to get a 'bite to eat' for his disciples and himself. People are forcing their way in. The friends try to control the crowd, saying:
'It is beside itself with excitement.' Nevertheless, we do know from John 7 :3 ff. that 'his own brethren did not believe in him', and the usual translation, 'He is beside himself' may prepare for the saying with which the Gospel reading ends today. We shall discover Jesus in a somewhat similar situation in the reading for the Sixteenth Sunday when 'he had no leisure even to eat' (cf. 6 :30-44). Yet, due to his compassion, he addresses the crowd and feeds them.
We take up our theme of 'The Two Standards'. Am I allied with the force for good, or have I joined the evil-doers? The crowd is receptive to Jesus. The scribes, on official investigation from Jerusalem, reject him. 'His magic throws out the smaller demons by means of the Great One.' They cannot deny his power, so they attack its origin. Jesus replies by means of two illustrations. Let those who hear him search the truth in the parables, if they genuinely seek the light. He does not explain further. This prepares us for 4:34 which we meet next Sunday.
The first image is the destructive effects of civil war, which many countries have experienced in their history. Satan would not dissipate his powers in this way. Jesus defeats the demons, due to his goodness which is opposed to them. Secondly, Jesus can plunder the house of the evil one, because he is stronger than the forces of evil which defend it (cf. further, Luke 11:20-23 and, by way of background, Is. 49:24-26).
The blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, which cannot be forgiven, runs parallel to the Jewish idea that whoever would speak in a contemptuous way against the law would perish. Now that Jesus has come whoever refuses to accept the holiness of his Spirit, and regards it rather as an unclean thing, puts himself outside the community of salvation. Such a man condemns himself through his obstinate deafness and blindness to the words and works of Jesus. He remains closed to the calling Spirit, entrenched in an attitude of total rejection of all that Jesus means.
We end with a scene in which membership of the community comes to be analyzed more deeply. Friendship with Jesus is something greater than being a blood-relation. Action, not status, constitutes the saving brotherhood. Obedience to the will of God decides a place in the good kingdom.
In the case of Mary, this attitude was already fully expressed in her answer, 'Behold the slave-girl of the Lord. With joy I want your word to be fulfilled in me.' For James, Joseph, Simon, Jude
... their cousinly scepticism would be changed to real acceptance after the resurrection.
Suggested Mass Theme. The key-idea of today's Mass is that of the two ways. Jesus, and all those who follow him, crush the evil one through their good influence on the world around them. We listen at the Communion reflection to the Spirit of Jesus, who reveals the will of God for us.
