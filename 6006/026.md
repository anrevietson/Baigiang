# The conclusion of the epistle

First Reading(1 John 5 :14-21). In this concluding section of the epistle John exhorts his readers to prayer, particularly to prayer for sinners, sin being the antithesis of that eternal life of which he has spoken so much. Prayer in accordance with God's will, he assures his readers, will always be successful. The 'deadly sin' ('mortal sin' of the RSV could be misleading, for Catholics at least) he mentions is much more than a mortal sin. He probably has the sin of the 'Antichrists' in mind, i.e. the rejection of Christ or the perversion of the Gospel message. This sin could be akin to the sin against the Holy Spirit mentioned by Christ. The New Testament period was one of special grace, and sins against the faith were looked on as especially heinous. John does not say that such sins cannot be forgiven; but neither does he say that they can. The Church would later make it clear that on the part of God and the Church there is no sin that cannot be forgiven. God's mercy is infinite.
John concludes his writing as he began it, with a reference to the eternal life brought to us by Jesus. This indeed is what the entire letter has been about.
