# Joy and confidence in God

The liturgy of the Christmas period ends today telling us to live in joy and in confidence in God and Jesus Christ. The spirit of John the Baptist, described in the Gospel passage, is put before us for our imitation. We need this bouncing joyful spirit in our own day, when Christ's religion is so often presented as something of a burden. And we need the spirit of confidence of which the first reading speaks. And let us not be put off by the paradoxes which are part and parcel of Christianity, facts noted in the first reading also. Everything in Christianity points to victory over sin. In one sense it is hard to see how a Christian, born of God, could sin at all. And yet experience bears evident witness to human frailty and to a multiplicity of sin, falling short of apostasy. All this is part of the human condition, within which the Christian lives out his or her existence. Let not sin deter us. Divine forgiveness is also a reality. Let us pray for others who fall into sin and seek forgiveness for our own, in all things living a faith that is founded on the awareness that Jesus is still the Lamb of God proclaimed by the Baptist, the one who takes away the sin of the world.
All in all, the liturgy of the Christmas period ends in a note in keeping with the celebration of the mystery of the incarnation begun at Christmastide.
