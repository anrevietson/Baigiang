# The word of God is living

Second Reading (Heb. 4 :12-13). This text forms a conclusion to the preceding passage. The author has already written of the word of God. It is spoken through his Son (Heb. 1:1-3), through the angels and through the Son (2 :1-4), as well as through Moses (3 :5). The voice of God is severally mentioned at the end of chapter three and the beginning of chapter four. To conclude this development, the author breaks into the hymn of praise for this word, and that hymn forms today's reading. What does the hymn mean? In the Bible, this word of God is a blanket term to cover a whole range of divine activities. It is the word which creates, 'God said: let there be light.' It is the revealing word which comes to the prophet: 'The word of Yahweh came to me . ..' It is a word of strength that overcomes the prophet and leaves him no opportunity to suppress it (Amos 3 :8). When God puts his word into the mouth of Jeremiah he receives power over peoples and kingdoms to uproot and tear down, to destroy and to ruin, to build and to plant (Jer. 1:10). Elsewhere, he says that the word of Yahweh is like fire, like a hammer that shatters rock (Jer. 23 :20). In Hebrews, this word judges and penetrates to the inmost recesses of human being and consciousness.
It is quite absurd to confine this word to the written text of the Bible. This flies straight in the face of scriptural usage. It is equally wrong to limit this word to the living preaching of the Church which is the contemporary expression of the word of scripture. Rather, is it the whole divine activity which is experienced in creation, the sacramental life of the Church, the written and spoken word, personal inspiration and grace. The use of terms like 'God said' and 'word of God' in such contexts centre attention on the verbal quality of divine activity in general. God's action is personal, revelational and only through it can we come to know him.
