# The hardest sayings of Christ

As we approach the end of the liturgical year we meet some of the hardest sayings of Christ. Today's gospel focuses on giving everything away to the poor, as a prerequisite to entering the kingdom of God.

PENITENTIAL RITE
As we ask for God's wisdom, a spirit of obedience to his word, and the grace to be disciples of Jesus, we recognise our failures and sins.
You call us to prefer wisdom to wealth. Lord, have mercy.
You pardon our neglect of God's word. Christ, have mercy.
You pardon our holding on to our possessions and neglecting the poor. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Wis 7:1-11). Nothing compares with the wisdom that comes from God.
Second Reading (Heb 4:12-13). The Word of God gets right to the heart of all that is within us, our minds, our hearts, our emotions and our values.
Gospel (Mk 10:17-30). One of the very hard sayings of Jesus. Keeping the Law of Moses is not enough. Selling one's riches and giving to the poor are conditions of discipleship of Jesus.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Conscious of the demands of discipleship we pray to the Lord.
Reader(s): For all the world, especially for the poor, at home and abroad.
For the Church, that it may a 'poor Church', always in the service of the poor.
For leaders of nations, that they may be empower the poor.
For the rich, that they may share their riches with the poor.
For the sick and those who are distressed.
For those who have gone before us, marked with the sign of faith.
President: God our Father, teach us to love your wisdom, to treasure your word above all riches, to share our riches with the poor, and to be faithful disciples of your Son, Jesus Christ, our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In God's kingdom everyone is rich, surrounded by God's wisdom and love. We pray for its coming, as Jesus taught us to pray.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Let us bring each other a sign of God's peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
We hold on tenaciously to our little treasures. Let us take courage from the example of Jesus, who though he was in the form of God, did not hold on to his equality with God, but emptied himself, making himself poor, that we might be rich.
