# The bronze serpent

First Reading (Num 21:4-9). During the wilderness wandering the complaints of the people provoke God's punishment, viz., snakes whose bite probably caused an inflammation. In view of the people's repentance Moses builds a bronze serpent that heals all those who look upon it. This practice reflects the belief that the best way to ward off an enemy is to display an exact image of such an enemy.
Second Reading (Phil 2:6-11). Paul cites a Jewish-Christian hymn to pro-mote charity in the Philippian community. Though Jesus should not have been subject to death and corruption, he opted to set aside his divine prerogatives and accept a life of suffering and pain. Though in nature he was the same as all other humans, he was still different since he did not need to be reconciled to God. Because he accepted death, indeed death on a cross, God superexalted him, conferring on him the title of 'Lord' that was previously reserved to God alone. Everyone must acknowledge him as such.
