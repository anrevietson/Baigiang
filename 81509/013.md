# The depth of Christ's suffering

The First Reading (Heb 5:7-9) is one of the most eloquent statements in the New Testament of the depths of Jesus' inner anguish before the mystery of suffering, 'who offered up prayer and entreaty alone and with silent tears to the one who had power to save him from death.'
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps 30) is a prayer of a just sufferer who commends his life to the Lord's keeping.
The brief Communion verse situates the mystery of the believer's present suffering as a participation in the sufferings of Christ.
Celebrants and planners of liturgy might note that the missal proposes the use of the Stabat Mater after the responsorial psalm. It is probably too long to use satisfactorily at that point, but could some portions, sung or spoken, in the original Latin or in a good translation, be used at other moments in the liturgy today?
