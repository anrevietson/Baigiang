# The superiority of Jesus' sacrifice

Second Reading (Heb 10 :11-14, 18). This section continues the argument about the superiority of Jesus' priesthood over that of the Old Testament. The Old Testament priests had to repeat endlessly the sacrificial ritual. Jesus, on the other hand, needed to sacrifice himself but once, and after that was raised up to sit on the right hand of the Father. Some authors argue from the contrasting postures of the priests (standing) and of Christ (sitting) that the sacrifice of Christ does not perjure in heaven. However, one must be aware that the author is in fact quoting Ps. 110 and has in mind the kingly status of Jesus in heaven, rather than his sacrificial role. Since Jesus is seated at the right hand of God, we have immediate access to the power of God, and this is the source of our confidence : 'let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith. . . . Let us hold fast the confession of Our hope without wavering' (10 :22 f.). The period between the exaltation of Christ and his parousia is described in terms of Ps. 110 :1. While the author does not indicate who the enemies of Christ are, perhaps he has in mind the stern warning he gives in vv. 26-31 to those in danger of sinning deliberately after they have received the knowledge of the truth. Christ, by his single act of offering himself, has accomplished more than generations of Old Testament priests could ever do. The author's insistence on the superiority of the sacrifice of Christ probably had an apologetic motivation. Perhaps the readers, who were probably Jewish converts to Christianity, had some doubts about the efficacy of the sacrifice of Christ, and were not quite convinced that his sacrifice rendered those of the Old Testament obsolete. Verse 18 concludes that since the sacrifice of Christ has caused sin to be blotted out, there is no longer any need to offer sacrifices for sin.
