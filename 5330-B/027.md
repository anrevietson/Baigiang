# The coming of the Son of Man

The Gospel (Mark 13 :24-32). The literary style used in Mark 13 is so unfamiliar to our generation that the chapter is one of the most difficult of the entire New Testament, at least for us. The central message of the chapter can be expressed in one simple sentence:
Jesus will come again : prepare! Such a bald statement, however, gives very little idea of how Jesus will come again, of when he will come, and by what signs we will know that he is about to come. The chapter is a gathering together of various sayings of Jesus concerning the end-time, and the language he uses is full of images, visions and poetic symbolism. Late Old Testament and intertestamental writings abound in the very same images. The teaching ought not to be examined with the closed mentality of either a nineteenth century historian, Or a twentieth century man of science. Today's section contains the nucleus of the whole chapter:
the coming of the Son of Man, and the need for watchfulness on our part. Jewish tradition had long predicted that, in the end, history would reach a triumphant consummation when the Lord would come to vindicate his people. This popular expectation was given expression in apocalyptic language, which tried to express a deep reality about God in the feeble words and images of human language. It was well known that the Day of the Lord would be preceded by wars (Mark 13 :7; cf. 4 Esdras 9 :3; 13 :31) and celestial phenomena (13 :7 f., 24-27; cf. 4 Esdras 5 :4-7). Jesus, in fore-telling his future coming, chose to use language that was very familiar to his contemporaries, although we have no reason to think that his contemporaries fully recognised the message in the medium. In any case, Jesus did foretell his second coming, and the apocalyptic discourse ends with a series of sayings and parables highlighting the necessity of vigilance. This exhortation has a perennial value. We do not know the hour (in fact, Jesus left this secret in the hands of the Father, v.32) : our watchfulness, therefore, must be a permanent disposition backed up by such an uprightness of life that we will be pleased to meet the Lord face to face when he does come.
