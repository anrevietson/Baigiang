# The Sunday introit

In the earlier Roman liturgy this Sunday was known from the opening words of the introit (entrance hymn) and of the epistle as Laetare Sunday, a day in which the Church called on her children to rejoice. Even in the new readings the theme of joy is still prominent in today's liturgy. Joy and peace, in fact, are central to the message of Advent (cf. Doctrine and Life, December 1967, pp.643-55).
First Reading (Is. 61:1-2, 10-11). In this reading we have the first two and last two verses of a hymn in which some unnamed person mysteriously declares that he has been sent by God to usher in the age of salvation for Jerusalem and God's people. In the opening verses he speaks in his own name while in the concluding ones ('I exult for joy...') he speaks in the person of the new Jerusalem. He is anointed with the Spirit of God to proclaim the gospel, to bear good tidings to the down-trodden and faint-hearted, to make known to them that God cares for them, that their persons are precious in his sight. Scholars may argue as to who this mysterious person was for the author of this hymn; whether the author was thinking of himself, the Servant of Yahweh or some future messenger as the bearer of the good tidings. What matters for Christians is that the prophecy is fulfilled in Jesus; what is more that Jesus solemnly announced in his home town of Nazareth that in his person and preaching these words of Isaiah were fulfilled (cf. Luke 4:16-21). His mission was to bring the message of hope to humanity, harassed and helpless like sheep without a shepherd (cf. Mat. 9:36). He takes the opening words of this song as the guiding principle of his life's work. His mission is to raise up to new life, not to break the bruised reed nor quench the smouldering wick (cf. Mat. 12:20). Jerusalem is represented as exulting for joy by reason of the good news brought to her. The life of the Church, the new Jerusalem, should also be characterised by joyful hope. Hope is always the measure of faith. Gaudium et spes, joy and hope, were indeed most appropriate words with which to begin the Council document on the role of the Church in the modern world. Her message must be one of hope and joy. Any other attitude would show unfaithfulness to her true nature.
