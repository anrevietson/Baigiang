# The humble remnant

First Reading (Zeph. 3 :1-2, 9-13). Zephaniah prophesied during the reign of king Josiah (640-609 BC), sometime before the ministry of Jeremiah. His message is mainly a prophecy of the 'Day of Yahweh', when judgement would be visited upon Judah and all the nations. In this vein our reading points to trouble for those in the city of Jerusalem who have practised and permitted all kinds of social injustice there. But it is verses 9-13 that are most striking. Therein messianic salvation is promised to a purified and repentant remnant. These are the poor in spirit, and there follows a classical description of what qualities of soul these people possess. They are humble and lowly, they rely on God's strength rather than their own, they are truthful, sincere and live at peace. They have, in short, been renewed inwardly and externally.
The poverty of spirit so beautifully described here is not just to be admired. It is something we can ill afford to do without. True, it is the ideal to be aimed at with the help of God's Spirit. But it is something too that we approach though ever so gingerly, when we accept the limitations of our humanity and the trials of our lives in the proper spirit. For the chastisement of God is but a reflection of his mercy and his love.
