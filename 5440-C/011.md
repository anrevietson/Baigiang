# A hymn to God's love

Second Reading (Rom. 5:5-11). The reading from Romans is a sort of hymn in which Paul is practically bubbling over with joy at his hope in Christ. His theme has been that we are reconciled to God not by any works of our own or by any advantages of the Jewish law but purely by faith in Christ, that is by attaching ourselves to Christ who has saved us by his death and resurrection. He now goes on to draw out the consequences of this : if we are' attached to Christ and Christ showed such incomparable love for us that he was prepared to die for us, this love must be something which endures and which will carry us through any number of difficulties and sufferings. It is inconceivable that he should love us enough to die for us when we were worthless in sin, and then desert us now that we have our worth through him and by living with his own life. Paul mentions, then, two human states with regard to the love of God. The first is when we are helpless in sin, and even then' God loves us and Christ loves us even to dying for us ; the second is when we have already accepted Christ's gift and, being members of his body, live with his life. In this latter state our union to Christ and to his love for us is the closest conceivable, and we enjoy also the love which God has for his own Son.
