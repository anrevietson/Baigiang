# God the shepherd

First Reading (Ezek. 34 :11-16). The reading from Ezekiel was written in a period of despair and disillusionment, when the bottom seemed to have fallen out of the Israelites' world. Ezekiel's hearers are exiles in Babylon, convinced that all their hopes for God's protection and of eventual rehabilitation are vain. To them
Ezekiel brings tile promise of renewed hope: their rulers, often called shepherds in the predominantly pastoral civilization of Mesopotamia and the Near East, have proved careless, selfish and corrupt; but the ultimate shepherd, God himself, does not abandon them. It is the same promise as that of the second part of Isaiah which is so prominent in the gospels. In this promise there is a significant ambiguity: the prophecy also announces: 'I mean to raise up one shepherd, my servant David ... and he will pasture them'. Is the shepherd God himself, or is it David, who stands for the perfect messianic king who is to come? Or is the David figure in fact Cod himself? The answer is given in St John: 'I am the good shepherd', where Jesus is both messianic shepherd and divine.
The Responsorial Psalm, the most loved of all the psalms, sings prophetically of the Lord's work for us. David looks to the Lord as his shepherd.
