# The lost sheep

'The Gospel (Luke 15:3-7). The passage of Luke which picks up this imagery is one of the parables of mercy and love which form the centrepiece of' Jesus' final journey up to Jerusalem,. Luke puts much of the teaching of Jesus on which he wants to lay special stress in the course of this journey to the climax at Jerusalem, and in this framework the parable's of forgiveness and divine search for man have pride of place. It is important to appreciate 'that the shepherd's pursuit of and care for this single sheep is so single-minded as to be almost unbalanced, certainly unreasonable, for5 the parable is intended to show the lengths to which God will go to 'save what has strayed, and so explain the depths of his love. The 'shepherds of Palestine nowadays normally count their sheep in the evening, and in the parable it must be after this that the shepherd leaves them to look for the singleton. Added to this, he leaves them in the desert, from where they may stray or be attacked by the wild beasts which abounded in the Palestinian deserts : and he goes on searching until he finds, no matter how long (a point stressed by Luke, for it is not in Matthew's version). The parable was spoken by Jesus to the Pharisees and scribes who complained that he consorted with the ritually unclean and moral outcasts, so it shows the very great value in his eyes of every individual. The expression 'What man among you ...?' with which it opens, may seem to suggest that such conduct is normal ; but in fact it appears to be Jesus' own formula - used by no one else - for provoking his listeners to thought and reaction. The self-righteous Pharisees and lawyers are to be jolted out of their cold respectability to see how different is the attitude of Jesus, how wholehearted and uncalculating his love for sinners.
