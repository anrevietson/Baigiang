# The woman who was a sinner

Gospel (Lk 7:36-8:3). Luke uses an incident in which a woman, known as a sinner, repents openly before Jesus by her act of homage. It becomes the occasion by which Jesus through a parable challenges his self-righteous host. If we remember that the Pharisees were those Jews who stressed the following of the Mosaic Law in all of its details, then we will realize that Luke is, like Paul, showing the importance of faith and repentance.
The whole story clearly proclaims the notion that recognition of one's sinfulness is balanced by God's love for sinners. The host Simon believed that Jesus became unclean by allowing a sinner to touch him. Jesus, in turn, shows that such uncleanness is not important in God's eyes when the real event is the sinner seeking forgiveness. Simon had done nothing wrong in his role of a host. He had shown correct hospitality. Yet unlike the woman who clearly had shown a feeling of generosity, Simon was more concerned for the proper attitude and was being self-righteous. Luke shows us by action and parable how Jesus stressed the need to be humble in God's presence and seek forgiveness when one has sinned.
