# Justified by faith in Christ

In the second reading (from Gal. 2:16, 19-21)' Paul is in full flight against men who taught that even for Christians observance of the Jewish law is necessary to salvation ; it is not the law but faith in Jesus Christ; he replies, that makes us acceptable to God. The legacy of Judaism died hard and it is clear that there were different

But Paul is inflexible that to salvation the law is irrelevant we cannot earn our salvation by our own observances, we must accept it as a free gift from Christ. By what is perhaps the very centre of his Christian thinking he explains how we are saved : the Christian somehow is Christ : he lives with Christ's life. The paradox is that though in one sense it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me, in another sense my life and personality are not diminished but intensified, and enhanced by the fact that I now live with Christ's life. This incorporation of the Christian into Christ affects not only the present but the past as well : I now take on as my own Christ's history, so that in Christ's death it is I who died; this is the meaning of 'being baptised into Christ's death'. Similarly for the future : my hope too is Christ's. But our incorporation into Christ and our need for this are proof enough of the inadequacy of the law.
