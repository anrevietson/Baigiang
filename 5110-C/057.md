# Justified by faith in Christ

In the second reading (from Gal. 2 :16, 19-21) Paul is inflexible that for salvation the Jewish law is irrelevant. We cannot earn our salvation by our own observances; we must accept it as a free gift from Christ. By what is perhaps the very centre of his Christian thinking he explains how we are saved: the Christian somehow is Christ; he lives with Christ's life. The paradox is that though in one sense it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me, in another sense my life and personality are not diminished but intensified and enhanced by the fact that I now live with Christ's life. This incorporation of the Christian into Christ affects not only the present but the past as well: I now take on as my own Christ's history, so that in Christ's death it is I who died; this is the meaning of 'being baptized into Christ's death'. Similarly for the future: my hope too is Christ's. But our incorporation into Christ and our need for this are proof enough of the inadequacy of the law.
