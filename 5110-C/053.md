# Nathan and David

In the first reading (2 Sam. 12:7-10, 13) the prophet Nathan who was, so to speak, David's 'domestic chaplain, is delivering God's sentence on him after his affair with Bathsheba, wife of Uriah. He had been attracted by 'her when seeing her bathing on the flat roof of her house (the houses of Jerusalem were built on the side of a hill, with David's on the height and the others below), had seduced her and then, to get her husband out of the way, had had him placed in the most dangerous part of the battle-line during a foolhardy advance against the Ammonites ; this had its desired effect. The story of Nathan's parable and of David's repentance is one of the most moving of the Old Testament, and must be read in full (2 Sam. 12:1-25). It shows David, who at times could he a very canny politician, at his most direct and genuine ; his warm simplicity and total submission to the Lord are a perfect illustration of the qualities of little children demanded by Jesus for entry into the kingdom of heaven.
