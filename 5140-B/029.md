# The call of Ezekiel

First Reading (Ezek 2:2-5). Taken into exile in Babylon following the fall of Jerusalem in 598 B.C., Ezekiel receives his call as prophet to the exiles unable to 'sing the Lord's song in a strange land'. His characteristic use of the term 'Son of Man' in contrast to the divine speaker emphasises not only his vocation but the source of rescue, as indicated in the repeated phrase, 'The Lord says this ... ' Ezekiel is commissioned to speak the word of the Lord in the midst of' as well as to, rebellious and hard-hearted people. The result of such rebellion is their present state, brought about through infidelity to the covenant to Yahweh. The task is hard, 'whether they listen or not' but in the listening and returning to former ways lie dignity and indeed deliverance. Just as God's judgement upon his people for their disobedience is revealed in their condition (with the Babylonians seen as acting as God's agents in this regard), so God's faithfulness will be revealed in their return to the demands of the covenant.
