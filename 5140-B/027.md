# The call of Ezekiel

First Reading (Ezek 2:2-5). This passage lies at the heart of the prophet's call and outlines his mission. He is called to speak a revelation of God in clear, unambiguous terms to a hard-necked stubborn people sure to raise a storm of opposition from the people and their leaders. This ministry is historically situated among Judean exiles in Babylon the prophet's tomb is, according to legend, near Hilla in Central Iraq and took place between 593 and 571 B.C. He preached a message that centered unequivocally on an image of God's reign at the heart of a faithful covenant people who had to choose between doom and consolation in stark socio-political terms. Even then the tension between faith and politics was evident; even then the choice was between a creative, God-respecting tension and a destructive God-rejecting one. To Ezekiel, infidelity to the covenant has historical consequences for the people in the form of political disaster. He is quite clear that people, both personally and collectively, generation by generation, are responsible for their own actions in real practical terms. He insists that choices always beget their own consequences. The begetting of good consequences, opening the way of consolation, requires the choice of integrity and justice in both social and religious terms. This means that God's holiness and grace are most clearly imaged in real acknowledgement of sinfulness and practical repentance. The other option, that of rejecting God, leads to doom.
