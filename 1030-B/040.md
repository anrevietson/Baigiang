# The testimony of John

Gospel (Jn 1:6-8.19-28). This gospel reading is the first of a list of testimonies (1:19-51) identifying Jesus for the community out of which this gospel came. John the Baptist was not only a powerful figure but, but the early Church found him, in some ways, to be an embarrassing figure. Groups founded (or inspired) by John seem to have persisted for quite some time after Jesus. Constantly the writers of the New Testament have to acknowledge John yet point out he is not the One, vv. 6 and 7. In v.19 John denies any messianic claims for himself.
This passage is loaded with nuances regarding Jesus and his mission. Already John's Gospel is setting the scene for debates with priests, levites, and Pharisees - each in their different ways. We also meet 'the Jews', an expression John later uses for the authorities who instigate opposition to Jesus.
Elijah: there was high expectation of the return of Elijah (Mal 3:1.23), the messenger sent to prepare the Day of the Lord. Only in the Christian tradition does Elijah become a forerunner of the Messiah rather than Yahweys day of judgement.
Verses 26-27: The synoptic traditions distinguish Jesus and John the Baptist by contrasting water baptism with the purification of the Spirit. The fourth gospel seems to have tempered this a little. Does John give a special christological insight in the sandal remark? Other gospels say 'I am not worthy' (Greek axios) whereas John says 'I am not able' (Greek hikanos) implying not simply a matter of worth or dignity but the intrinsic competence and ability.
