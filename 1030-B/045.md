# Good news to the poor

First Reading (Is 61:1-2.10-11). As this is an edited reading from a fairly complex chapter of the Book of Isaiah, it might be more easily understood if it is taken as spoken by two different voices (a suggestion which might be kept in mind in arranging the reading for the liturgy). The first speaker, the prophet, describes his anointing, not with oil like a priest or king, but with the Spirit of God. His mission is to proclaim good news (gospel) which comforts the poor and acts as healing balm on their wounded hearts. It will be heard like long-awaited news of release by those in prison. It will ring out like the ram's horn trumpet which announced the jubilee ('year of favour') when debts were remitted, those sold into slavery given freedom to return home, and the land itself would enjoy rest from the burden of ploughing and planting. The second voice is that of the people singing a song of joy for salvation. Salvation is seen as the wedding finery of bride and groom. Just as the land which has lain fallow in the jubilee will bear richer fruit, so Israel after the year of God's favour will bear the fruits of the covenant integrity (zedekah, the righteousness of the covenant) and praise.
Responsorial Psalm (Lk 1:46-50.53-54). The Magnificat takes up the Song of Israel which concluded the first reading. Mary's song is also a song celebrating the wonder of salvation. It is the act of God's gracious favour (strophe 1), the continuation of the favour shown in her people's past (strophe 2), bringing with it comfort for the poor (strophe 3).
