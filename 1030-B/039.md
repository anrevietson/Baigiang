# The testimony of the Baptist

Gospel (Jn 1: 6-8.19-28). Today's Gospel takes the verses relating to the Baptist from their context in the prologue and joins the with the account of the interrogation of John by the religious authorities of Judaism. The Fourth Gospel shows little interest in the content of the preaching of the Baptist, but at several points introduces him as a witness to Jesus. Two groups seem to be envisaged here, a point which the Jerusalem Bible translation leaves unclear but made better by the New American Bible's rendering 'Those whom the Pharisees sent questioned him further', and this has the support of many modern commentators. The first group consists of priests and levites representing the religious authorities of Jerusalem, the scene of major opposition to Jesus in the Gospel. Their questions concern John's identity and his reason for baptising. The second group representing the Pharisees is more interested in John's authority for baptising. In Jewish expectations Elijah the prophet who had been taken up to heaven, would return to prepare the Day of the Lord (Mal 3:1). 'The Prophet' likewise refers to the current expectation of a prophet like Moses which circulated in several circles, notably Qumran which expected a prophet and two Messiah figures that of Aaron and that of Israel. John identifies himself merely as 'the voice crying in the wilderness' who points to the hidden presence of 'one coming after'. Untying sandals was the work of a slave, and a pupil of a rabbi might be expected to render his master any service a slave would do, except that of taking off his sandals; it emphasises here the distance between John and Jesus. Unlike Mark's version of the Baptist's words (see last Sunday's Gospel), there is no reference to Jesus as one who baptises with the Holy Spirit.
