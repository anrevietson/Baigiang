# Ezra reads the Law

First Reading (Neh. 8:2-6, 8-10). This passage narrates an extremely important event : the reconstitution of the Jewish religion after Israel's return from exile in Babylon. It was a new beginning for Israel. For some time after the repatriation of the Jews - from 538 BA onwards - the rather small Jewish community living round about Jerusalem was in a precarious situation. The temple was rebuilt in 5 15 BA but the holy city was without walls and the civil status of the Jews was anything but satisfactory. There was need of both a civil and religious reform. The political reorganisation was carried out by Nehemiah, the religious by Ezra, both apparently in the second half of the fifth century. Ezra came from the Persian court (which then ruled Palestine and the Jews) with the task of reconstituting the Jewish religion in order to have it conform with the law of Moses, i.e. the Pentateuch as edited during and after the exile. This law-centred religion, or Judaism as it is known, began with that solemn convocation recounted in today's reading. It was a solemn occasion inaugurating a new age for the Jews, that final stage of their religion which would take them as far as the time of the Messiah. The law was read (possibly a only sections of it) and explained to them. It may also have been translated from Hebrew into the vernacular Aramaic, if this latter had already become the spoken language of the Jews. Generations of scribes and Pharisees would continue the work of Ezra, bringing a knowledge of Moses and the prophets to the people of Israel. This their work they did well, and thus prepared for the teaching of Christ. They also added other traditions of their own making, 'the traditions of the elders' as they are called, which in time came to smother God's voice in good part. In today's Gospel reading we read of another solemn occasion and proclamation when Jesus declared that the new age had come.
