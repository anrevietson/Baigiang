# One body, many members

Second Reading (1 Cor. 12:12-30). This is a continuation of last Sunday's reading in which Paul spoke of the unity in diversity which is a characteristic of Christ's Church. In the passage read today he illustrates this truth through the example of the human body and its diversity of organs. His illustration is clear and simple ; yet his teaching is profound and carries a much needed message for us today. As the human body without turning into something of a monster cannot be all mouth, ear or eye, so too with the Church. It cannot be all hierarchy, all 'charismatic', all theologians, all laity, etc. It has within it all these, but none of these groups can act as if it were the Church. Any particular office or group views itself aright only when it recognises and acknowledges the existence of the others. This vanity is to be accepted as willed by God, not merely tolerated as arising from human weakness. And the defence of multiformity within the Church is the affair of all. We are all members of the one body and the well-being of each and every office and divine gift is the concern of each and every group and member of the Church. An injury inflicted on the hierarchy is to the detriment of the entire body. So also is the violation of the rights of any other section within the Church, those of theologians, the laity and so forth. Theologians, laymen and others should be custodians of the rights of bishops. So also should the hierarchy, the laity, etc., uphold the rights and the liberty of theologians and so on. We are all but one body and we all suffer if any one office or group does not function as it should or if it is not allowed to do so. Respect fore the variety of gifts and offices within the Church means respect for the holy Spirit whose gifts they are.
