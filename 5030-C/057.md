# Today this scripture is fulfilled

The Gospel (Luke 1:1-4; 4:14-21). In the prologue to his gospel (1:1-4) Luke tells us that he had research work to do before composing it. He had Mark's gospel before him as he wrote. He also apparently knew of other attempts at 'gospels', works now lost. His purpose in writing hiss gospel is to relate the facts concerning Christ. Fact was very important for the early Church, and of the central facts there were attested witnesses in the persons of the apostles and others. Luke says she intends to give 'an ordered account' of these events. Order there is in his gospel, but not always the chronological order we would desire. He is more interested in a theological order, containing both the event and its religious significance. In order to achieve this he will not scruple to change the chronological order. This is particularly clear in Luke's account of Christ's visit to Nazareth. He places it at the beginning of Christ's public ministry because he sees in it a solemn proclamation of all that Christ's coming means. Furthermore, in this narrative he combines accounts of more than one visit of Jesus to Nazareth, events with very different outcomes. As background to this reading we may note that an essential part of the synagogue liturgy was the reading of the law (the Pentateuch) and the prophets. This was followed by a homily. During the reading the readers stood. The homily Was given seated, this being the accepted posture for a teacher (cf. Mark 4:1 etc.). In Palestine the Bible was read in the original Hebrew. Where the spoken language was Aramaic (as it certainly was in Galilee) the text was translated into the vernacular in a free and interpretative rather than in a literal fashion. Luke gives the opening words or the substance of Jesus' homily which was that the text he had read was there and then being fulfilled in his own person and preaching. The 'acceptable year of the Lord', the time of redemption, had come. Many prophecies had been made concerning the Messiah and the nature of his kingdom. The one preferred by Christ himself as a description of his work was that of Isaiah 60:1-2 which speaks of one anointed by God and sent to preach the good tidings of salvation to the poor, the down-trodden and faint-hearted, to those rejected by society. Luke in his gospel will lay special emphasis on these aspects of Christ's work.
