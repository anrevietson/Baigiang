# The reconstitution of Jewish religion

First Reading (Neh. 8 :2-6, 8-10). The reconstitution of the Jewish religion after Israel's return from exile in Babylon. It was a new beginning for Israel. There was need of both a civil and religious reform. The political reorganization was carried out by Nehemiah, the religious by Ezra. Ezra came from the Persian court (which then ruled Palestine and the Jews) with the task of reconstituting the Jewish religion in order to have it conform with the law of Moses, i.e. the Pentateuch as edited during and after the exile. This law-centred religion, or Judaism as it is known, began with that solemn convocation recounted in today's reading. It was a solemn occasion inaugurating a new age for the Jews, that final stage of their religion which would take them as far as the time of the Messiah. The law was read (possibly only sections of it) and explained to them. Generations of scribes and Pharisees would continue the work of Ezra, bringing a knowledge of Moses and the prophets to the people of Israel. They thus prepared for the teaching of Christ, but also added other traditions of their own making, 'the traditions of the elders' which in time came to smother God's voice in good part. In today's Gospel reading we read of another solemn occasion and proclamation when Jesus declared that the new age had come.
The Responsorial Psalm sings in praise of the Law. It is God's Word, the bearer of life and salvation to his people.
