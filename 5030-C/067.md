# The reading of the Law

First Reading (Neh 8.2-4.5-6.8-10). The greatest crisis in the Old Testament was the destruction of Jerusalem and Judah in 587 B.C. Every structure of their religion was dismantled and removed and a large number of the people were exiled to Babylon. When this terrible period ended in 539 B.C. and some stragglers returned to start over again in the holy city, nothing seemed to go right. Only in 458 B.C did a real renewal take hold under the leadership of Ezra, a religious leader whom the Persians permitted to establish reforms for the Jewish faith. The single most important event in that reform is read today in its threefold drama:
the proclamation of the first five books of the Old Testament, the Pentateuch; Ezra's interpretation of its demands upon the community; and their total acceptance of it as a binding covenant in their lives.
In humbling themselves and doing penance, the people of Judah recognized how far they had strayed from God's commands given in the holy books, and Ezra's policy that it should be embraced with joy was the new jump start their despairing faith needed. God's spirit was at work again.
