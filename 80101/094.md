# God sent his Son, born of a woman

Second Reading (Gal. 4:4-7). In the epistle to the Galatians Paul presents in brief themes which are more fully developed in the epistle to the Romans. The general theme is that we are now saved not through the Law but through faith in Jesus Christ, not through membership of the Jewish race, carnally descended from Abraham, but through spiritual descent.
The small section of the epistle read today -treats of the coming of Christ and its effect. The appointed time (lit. 'the fullness of time') signifies the messianic age, the time longed for and desired for centuries. It has come. In this passage we have the only reference made by Paul to Mary and here it is implied. Paul is emphasising that the Son of God became truly man, but at the same time he may have in mind the virgin birth, which seems to have been fully accepted by Christians from the beginning. Jesus br6ught the Law, which in itself was good, to fulfilment. God sent him with authority and with a mission. This mission had a positive and negative aspect to free from slavery to the Law and to enable us to be adopted as sons. It seems more in keeping with Paul's teaching elsewhere to regard our adopted sonship as a consequence of the sending . of the holy Spirit than vice versa. One is constituted a son of God by receiving the Spirit of the Son and thus becoming one with him. The Spirit gives us an intimate interior experience of God as Father. We can address God as Jesus himself did, Abba, in a way which Jews would never have dared. The Christian's law in the Spirit working within him giving knowledge of God's will and at the same time giving him strength to fulfil it. As sons, Christians have a right to the inheritance and we share in the inheritance of Christ, the glorious life in communion with the Father.
