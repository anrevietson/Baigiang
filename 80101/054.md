# The octave of Christmas

Today is the octave of Christmas, the end of Christmas week and we remember the divine motherhood of Mary whom we proclaim to be the Mother of God, the Mother of Jesus Christ and the Mother of the Church. Mary has been in the centre of the Christmas story and today we especially celebrate her role in the Incarnation.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Let us begin by asking God's mercy in our lives for all our sinfulness and failure.
Lord Jesus, you are mighty God and Prince of peace: Lord, have mercy.
Lord Jesus, you are Son of God and Son of Mary: Christ, have mercy. Lord Jesus, you are Word made flesh and splendour of the Father.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Num 6:22-27). This day also celebrates the name of Jesus. Moses is told to call upon the sacred name and be blessed.
Second Reading (Gal 4:4-7). St Paul reminds us that Mary's destiny was to be mother so that we might become adopted children of God.
Gospel (Lk 2:16-21). The shepherds found the new horn King with Mary, his mother. The gospel also gives the account of the circumcision and naming of Jesus

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Let us call upon the name of the Lord in our prayers for all in need.
Reader(s): We pray for the Church throughout the world that the name of Jesus may be made known to all people.
We pray for governments of nations that they may work together for the cause of world peace.
We pray for mothers, that the Mother of God may be their protection and their support in the bringing up of their children.
We pray that Mary may be honoured by all Christians and bring us to unity in her Son.
President: Almighty God, your Son Jesus Christ worked the first of his signs in response to the prayers of Mary. Hear our prayers this day through her intercession and the merits of Jesus Christ, our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
God sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts; the Spirit that cries 'Abba, Father'. With confidence then, let us pray as Jesus taught us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
That the Lord may uncover his face to us and bring us peace, let us greet each other with a sign of reconciliation.

COMMUNION REFLECTION.
The child to whom Mary gave birth is the King of heaven and earth.
He is called the Prince of Peace.
He comes to us today to fill us with his farewell gift of peace.
In the power of his Holy Name
let us become peace makers wherever we may find ourselves.
In doing so we shall indeed give glory to God.
