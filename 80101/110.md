# The Aaronic blessing

First Reading (Num 6:22-27). This passage contains the Aaronic blessing which the priests used when they blessed the people. The blessing given in this passage is very ancient. It is in poetic form and consists of three lines, each with two parts. The first half line invokes a personal act of God upon the people, which is followed in the second half line by a consequence. To 'bless' means to 'praise, glorify or extol' someone, but it also means that the person is consecrated. The consequence is that God will 'keep you', that is, he will protect the recipient of the blessing. The metaphor in the first half of the second line describes God's benevolence and good pleasure, the consequence of which is the unreserved favour he shows to the recipient. In the final line God turns to the recipient in friendship, the consequence of which is to bring 'peace', that is, wholeness and well-being, whereby the recipient is in harmony with God's will. This blessing, which was probably meant to show God's response to acts of voluntary devotion, has passed into Christian usage and has special relevance when applied to Mary and her response to the angel's message at the annunciation.
