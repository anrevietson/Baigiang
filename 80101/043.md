# The shepherds at Bethlehem

The Gospel (Luke 2 :16-21). This reading coincides with the Gospel of the Dawn Mass on Christmas Day except that it begins and ends one verse later. It is the last verse which relates to the circumcision and the naming of Jesus which is commemorated today. The earlier verses recall to us Mary's motherhood and the faith she practised while the meaning of events was still veiled, a faith on account of which she was blessed (cf. Luke 1:45). The law of circumcising a male child on the eighth day after birth comes from Lev. 12 :3. Circumcision was not confined to the Jews but for them it had taken on importance as a sign of belonging to the covenant. Gen. 17 :10 shows us circumcision as a sign of the covenant between God and Abraham. By his circumcision Jesus became a member of the Chosen People and an heir to the promises made to Abraham and the patriarchs. These promises were fulfilled in himself. Circumcision received its full prominence only during the exile and afterwards when Judaism, which was inward-looking and somewhat narrow-minded, arose. As circumcision was a sign of the old covenant, so circumcision of the heart would characterise the new covenant (cf. Dt. 10 :16; Jer. 4 :4). In the New Testament the law has become interiorised. St Paul teaches that true circumcision which is the mark of the true Israel is that of the heart (Rom. 2 :25-29). The account of Jesus' circumcision recalls that of John the Baptist (Luke 1:59 ff.). Circumcision was the occasion for naming a child. The name Jesus (Yeshua) was a common name at the time but in this case it has a special significance, since it had been decreed by God himself before the conception of Jesus. It means 'God saves' or 'God is salvation' and expressed the mission of Jesus (cf. Matt. 1 :21). Jesus received from the Father the mission of saving all men. A change of name meant a change of destiny (cf. Gen. 17 :5; John 1:42).
