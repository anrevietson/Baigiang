# The prophet Malachi

anonymous. He has not given us his name. That given to him, that is "Malachi" (which in Hebrew means "My messenger"). may only be an
appellation based on 3:1 the opening words of today's reading. In the course of his prophetic addresses to Israel Malachi speaks of God's love for his people (1:1-5), a love which has received as response only the neglect of
God and of their solemn vocation on the part of the priests; by divorce and lack of marital fidelity among the people. The Lord says that his people has wearied him (2:17). This divine complaint is immediately followed by the passage selected for today's liturgical reading. God wants a holy and dedicated people. In today's reading he promises to intervene personally, beginning with the Temple and the priesthood. ("the messenger of the covenant in whom you delight", is probably another title for God himself; cf. Ex 3:2; 23:20); he is "the Lord whom they seek", and distinct from the special messenger to be sent before the great day of the Lord.
On various occasions through her history Israel had been alerted to prepare for the coming of a great divine intervention, often referred to as the
-ay of the Lord. Already in Second Isaiah ('sa 40:3) there is mention of a herald solemnly proclaiming the advent of this day, there referred to as a day of salvation. In other contexts this day of divine intervention could be spoken of as one of purifying for the just and of the destruction of sinners. How in practice matters would work out was to remain a divine secret. In this reading from Malachi there is emphasis on purification by fire. John the
Baptist appears to have been influenced very much by the vision of the
prophet Malachi and had consequently some difficulties in reconciling Jesus' gentle approach to sinners with it (sec Mat 11:2-6).
In the context of today's liturgical celebration Malachi's prophecy reminds us once again of the holiness God requires from his people and from his priesthood. His purifying fire comes in different ways. But we are also
reminded that the purification which has now come is not through destroying fire but in the person of the gentle Jesus, the messenger of the covenant, carried to the Temple as a child in the arms of his mother.
