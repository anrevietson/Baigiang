# Persecution of the early Church

First Reading (Acts 5 :27-32, 40-41) (H.W.). Persecution is an important element in the life of the early Church, and will be a constant feature of Acts. It does not seem to take the apostles by surprise, for they are conscious that it was foretold by Jesus in his instructions to them: 'They will hand you over to the synagogues and to imprisonment, and bring you before kings and governors because of my name - and that will be your opportunity to bear witness' (Luke 21 :12-13). This prophecy is fulfilled to the letter: here they have been brought before Jewish meetings and have undergone imprisonment; Paul will be brought before 'kings and governors', and on each occasion they bear witness in the power of the Spirit - Paul even when he is actually in prison at Philippi (16 :25-34).
There is a vital connection between the Christian who suffers for Christ, Christ himself, and the rest of the Church, which is all the body of Christ, and is sanctified by the sufferings of one of its members (Col. 1:24; Phil. 2 :17). In the Acts one of the major themes of the book is the continuance in the Church of the life of Christ. This theme comes to expression in the complex of ideas which centres round the 'name' of Jesus. One is baptised into the name of Jesus; Christians are those who call upon the name of Jesus, or over whom the name of Jesus has been called; they can act, even miraculously, in his name, and are persecuted and preach in his name. At that time a name was considered to have a real connection with the person or thing named, so that one might almost say that the expressions mentioned above are equivalent to being baptised into Jesus, acting and preaching in his own power, and being persecuted as Jesus.
The central point of Peter's witness is, on this occasion as always, the resurrection of Jesus. In speaking to the Jewish authorities here, Peter stresses especially that Jesus has been raised to the position of leader, an expression which designates Moses in contemporary Jewish literature. The Jewish rulers no doubt prided themselves on obedience to the old law of Moses; Peter replies that true obedience must be to Christ, the new Moses.
