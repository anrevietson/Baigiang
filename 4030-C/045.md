# The heavenly liturgy

Second Reading (Rev 5:11-14). John has a vision of a heavenly liturgy, of which the earthly worship of the Church is a reflection, involving angels, the living creatures and the elders worshipping God seated upon his throne. They all celebrate 'the lamb who was slain" a symbol of Jesus as the Messiah who, through his death, has entered into God's presence and now participates in God's glory as a gift. The liturgy widens to embrace the whole of creation resounding with a hymn of praise to God and the Lamb. This vivid display of sight and sound assures John's readers that Jesus is Messiah not despite but because of his suffering and death and, at the same time, gives meaning to their own present suffering. It also shows that the Church's worship, symbolised by that of the elders, is directed to Jesus himself as enjoying God's glory.
