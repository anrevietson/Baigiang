# The genealogy of Jesus

Gospel (Mt 1:1-17). Matthew, writing for a Jewish audience, stresses the true pedigree background of Jesus: his origins he back in the mists of Jewish history, with Abraham. The genealogy, of course, is not historically accurate. Consistent with Jewish literary style and with a predilection for the figure seven, the family tree is divided into three sets of fourteen generations. Such a neat division illustrates God's careful planning of history right down to the great event of the birth of Jesus. An unusual feature of the reading, considering the absolute nature of male dominance in Matthew '5 world, is the mention of four women as ancestors of Jesus. Their presence, scattered throughout the genealogy, orientates the reader towards the role of the Virgin Mary, in Gods plan. In mentioning Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and Bathsheba (Uriah's wife), Matthew anticipates the crucial role of Mary as the immediate parent of Jesus.
