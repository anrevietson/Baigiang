# Peter's last discourse

Second Reading (Acts 10:34-38). This last discourse of Peter's to be recorded in the Acts is an example of the early Church's preaching to non-Jewish converts. Such an audience had to be convinced that the God of Israel had no favourites and that he extended his salvation to Gentile as well as Jew Peter declares that anyone who fears God, that is, anyone who has a desire to serve God and to love him, is the object of his favour. Even if the people of Israel had God's message preached to them before others, Jesus is in reality 'the Lord of all men'. He came to announce the 'good news of peace' (cf. Is. 52:7) that the salvation promised by God is now made available to all men.
Luke, who all through the Gospel and the Acts stresses the role of the Spirit in the life of Christ and of the early Christians, refers to Jesus' baptism as an anointing with the Spirit. In the resume of Jesus' ministry which he places on Peter's lips in the present text he pictures Jesus as going about Palestine under the guidance of the Spirit doing good to all who needed his assistance. In saying that God was with Jesus in performing his good deeds the author wishes to express the idea that God was present in him, in his preaching and in his work, manifesting the divine power to the world and offering salvation to all.
It is worth noting) that this passage in the Acts is the only text outside the Gospels which refers to the baptism which inaugurated the mission of Jesus.
