# Suffering as a Christian

Second Reading (1 Pet 4:13-16). The first letter of Peter is an encouraging, confident, and pastoral document written to communities suffering persecution. The author appeals to Christ's sufferings to in stil hope and confidence in those believers who lack understanding about the significance of their situation.
This particular passage brings together themes that will recur in the gospel readings, namely suffering and glorification. Christians share in the sufferings of Christ, particularly when they suffer for their faith. The persecution that comes from being a Christian and witnessing to different values is the quality of suffering that leads to joy, goodness, and glorification. This emphasis resonates with the perspective of Acts as well, since Luke realistically promises suffering as well as joy to ministering disciples. If the disciples await the new presence of Jesus in the first reading, the second reading reveals the mature Christian response to the cost of discipleship.
