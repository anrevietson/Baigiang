# Sharing Christ's sufferings

Second Reading (1 Pet. 4:13-16). This reading repeats in a way what was said in the second reading last Sunday. Since suffering for the Christian means participation in the suffering of Christ we
should rejoice in it because our reward will be the greater. If we realise that Jesus regards as done to himself what is done to us, his members, we shall not be discouraged or disheartened by suffering or persecution. To be insulted because one is a Christian is a reason for happiness as Jesus himself said : 'Happy are you when people abuse you and persecute you and speak all kinds of calumny against you on my account. Rejoice and be glad for your reward will be great in heaven' (Mat. 5 :11). The Spirit of God had rested on Jesus at his baptism. So, too, he rests on Christians. He is regarded as the pledge of future glory resting on the suffering Church. In a similar way in the Old Testament the glory of God had descended on the assembly of Israel and filled the temple. The Christian community is the true temple, the spiritual house of God (1 Pet. 2:5). Again there is repeated the distinction between suffering deservedly for a crime and suffering innocently simply because one is a Christian. The former is something to be ashamed of, something unworthy of any follower of Christ; the latter is not only nothing to be ashamed of but rather an honour and glory, something for which to thank God. The actual title 'Christian' used here is found elsewhere in the New Testament only in Acts 11:26 and 26:28.
As Christians we have in us the Spirit of Christ, the Holy Spirit, who convinces us of the truth of Jesus and the error of those who do not accept him. This conviction must strengthen us in our faith and commitment when we are persecuted or mocked for refusing to swerve in our loyalty to Jesus. Courage is needed even here in a Christian country where virtue and fidelity to Christ are sometimes scorned as being unmanly or cowardly.
