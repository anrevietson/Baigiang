# Suffering as a Christian

Second Reading (1 Pet 4:13-16). Living as a minority in a predominantly non-Christian society, Christians may not only have to render an account of their religious conviction (1 Pet 23:15); they may also experience persecution and suffering because of it. This suffering is to he seen as a participation in the suffering of Christ. Being a Christian means following Christ in every detail of the pattern of his life, death and resurrection. Suffering and, even, dying for the sake of Christ is therefore a way of being conformed more closely with him. Christians do not simply benefit from Christ's saving death and resurrection; they are called to co-operate actively in this salvation, especially through suffering as a consequence of their commitment to Christ. In this way, the sacrament of baptism, representing a participation in Christ's life, death and resurrection becomes a reality in their daily lives. Far from being a cause of deep sorrow, therefore, suffering for Christ should be the motive for profound spiritual joy. It is a sign that the sufferer is destined to share in Christ's resurrection.
