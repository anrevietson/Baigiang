# Called to be a disciple and witness

Today we hear God's call to be a disciple and witness of the Gospel to the world. There are great similarities between the commission of the prophet Isaiah, the summons of the twelve apostles, and our own baptismal charge to follow Christ.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
God called Isaiah to the office of prophet, and Jesus called Peter as an apostle. Both knew and confessed their unworthiness to act in such a role, but God sanctified both for their tasks. Let us confess our own sinfulness and prepare our hearts to serve God.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 6:1-2.3-8). Isaiah experienced his own unworthiness before God's majestic holiness, but humbly accepted what God asked of him. His call prepares us for our own mission as disciples of Christ.
Second Reading (1 Cor 15:1-11 or 15:3-8.11). Paul was called to be an apostle only after the resurrection of Jesus. He relies on the testimony of those who had experienced the risen Jesus before himself to show his converts how central knowing the risen Christ is to their faith and ours.
Gospel (Lk 5:1 - 11). The call of the first apostles emphasizes both how Jesus made an offer that could not be refused, and how readily Peter, James and John said 'yes.' At the same time, the story of the great catch of fish promises rich reward to those who join Christ's ministry.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: As ready servants of Christ, let us offer our prayers for the needs of the Church and the world.
Reader(s): For our holy father, Pope John Paul II and all the bishops:
that they may be worthy successors of the apostles and sanctified in their service to the Church.
For all priests who share in the apostolic office as mediators of Christ's care for the entire people of God; and for all deacons, religious, and those who minister in the Church.
For vocations to the priesthood and religious life: that God may bless the Church with many dedicated young people willing to follow Christ's call unselfishly in the apostolic ministry.
For all the ministries of the Church that work for the poor, the helpless, the cold and hungry: that we may never fail to minister to the physical needs of people as well as to their spiritual hunger.
For peace and justice throughout the world, especially in lands where religious hatred or persecution is a source of violence and death.
President: We give thanks to you, 0 God, because when we call, you answer us and build up strength within us to serve you faithfully in all we do. Grant what we ask through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
As Jesus taught his first disciples how to pray when they asked, we now join their prayer by repeating the words he gave them.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Christ our Lord has called us to be witnesses of his peace and of Gospel joy. Let us, then, offer one another the sign of peace.
