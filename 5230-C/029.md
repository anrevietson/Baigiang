# A hymn in praise of Wisdom

First Reading (Wis. 9 :13-18). Chapters 6-9 of the Book of Wisdom constitute a hymn in praise of Wisdom which the author - who wrote sometime in the first century B.C. - puts on the lips of Solomon, the proverbially wise king of Israel. Solomon is represented as recognizing that he is a mere man (7 :3-6) and that Wisdom which belongs to God alone can only be acquired from God through prayer (7 :7f; 8 :21). In 9 :1-18, from which today's reading is taken, the author presents his version of Solomon's prayer for Wisdom. The prayer as formulated here is a development of Solomon's appeal to God for Wisdom and for the ability to rule well as recorded in 1 Kings 3 :6-9.
Today's reading begins with an expression of admiration and awe at the greatness of God's wisdom and at the mysterious nature of the divine plan for governing the world. By comparison man's intelligence appears very limited and his decisions fickle. However, God, the sage par excellence, does not leave man in his ignorance and uncertainty but gives him the gift of knowledge (cf. Job 32 :8; Prov. 2 :6). He sends his holy spirit (which the author here identifies with Wisdom) so that man, whose limited knowledge is due to the fact that he is compounded of clay (cf. Job 4 :19), might know something about God and his ways. Unless he receives the gift of Wisdom man cannot know what way of life pleases God and leads to salvation.
The manner in which our reading associates the spirit with wisdom and connects both with man's spiritual destiny prepared the way for St Paul's teaching (1 Cor 2 :6-16) that the Spirit communicates to spiritual men a wisdom that is not of this world and that enables those who accept it to understand and appreciate the divine plan. The tendency of our author to personify Wisdom
- a tendency which he shared with the authors of other Old Testament Wisdom books, cf. e.g. Prov. 8 and Job 28. - prepared the way for the New Testament writers who presented Christ as the incarnate Word and Wisdom of God (cf. Jn. 1:1-18; 1 Cor. 1:24).
