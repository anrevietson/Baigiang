# The visit of the Magi

Gospel (Mt 2:1-12). In his opening chapter Matthew used a genealogy, a dream revelation to Joseph including a typical Old Testament annunciation of birth pattern, and a number of scriptural references in order to declare the identity and significance of Jesus for the Jewish world of faith.
It is possible that the Magi story was also addressed to those in Matthew's audience who came from the Jewish world of faith. That audience might have picked up his allusion to the Balaam story (in Numbers 23-24), with its wicked king who tried to use a "seer" from the East to curse Israel but who ends up blessing God's people and uttering the famous vision of the "messianic star" which would arise from Jacob (cf.Num.24: 17).
Without this kind of specialised Old Testament knowledge one might not appreciate the subtle scriptural resonances that are part of Matthew's way of showing that Jesus is the fulfilment of the scriptures. However, one might still be able to appreciate other levels of meaning which the story has to offer.
In the pagan world of his time it was common for people to believe that the births of great men were marked by unusual signs in the heavens. There was a growing influence of eastern religious movements and wisdom. Perhaps Matthew intended to use his "star" and his Magi as a way of signalling to that gentile world just how significant Jesus was for them. The central players in the story are gentiles who follow their own particular path to revelation and who become true believers. They come out of the story much better than the privileged members of God's chosen people (the king, the chief priests and scribes), who despite their role as possessors and interpreters of the scriptures fail to be guided by them to the light of faith in Christ.
