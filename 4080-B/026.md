# The Spirit of Jesus

Second Reading (1 Cor. 12 :3-7, 12-13). This passage shows how the Spirit is the Spirit of Jesus and how it unites all who are endowed with it. As the Spirit of Jesus it cannot be the Spirit which moves those who reject Jesus. On the other hand no one could confess the divinity of Christ unless God's Spirit enables him. This teaching of Paul reassures the Corinthians.
All the charisma, despite their diversity, have a common origin, God, the Triune God - the Spirit, Jesus and the Father. Paul almost always Uses the term 'God' in reference to the Father. Once (Rom. 9 :5) or possibly twice (Tit. 2 :13) he applies it to Jesus. To explain his teaching Paul introduces the image of the body and its members. This is a classical analogy but no doubt Paul's concept of the body of Christ arises from his own experience on the way to Damascus (Acts 9 :4 ff.) when Jesus said to him: 'I am Jesus and you are persecuting me.' He realised from this that Christians are identified with the risen Jesus. The image of the body is developed and extended in the captivity epistles. Here stress is put on the unity of the body despite its various members, and the identification of the body of Christ with the Church.
