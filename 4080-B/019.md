# The birth of the Church

First Reading (Acts 2 :1-11). The event described in this reading might be called the birth of the Church. It describes the fulfilment of the promise made by Jesus to his apostles (Jn. 14 :15, 25-26; Acts 1 :5 etc.). Their mission to carry on his work is inaugurated. They are now united with the risen and glorious Christ as his body.
The images used, wind and fire, show that the author saw a parallel with the events of Sinai where the law was given, the covenant sealed and Israel became God's people. Now there is a new law, the law of the Spirit, a new covenant and a new Israel.
On this occasion is fulfilled the prophecy of Joel (Joel 3 :1-5; cf. Acts 2 :17). The era of the Church is seen as the final stage in God's plan of salvation before the parousia. It is possible that on Pentecost the apostles were not actually understood by foreigners but became ecstatic in their speech (as speaking 'in tongues' means elsewhere in the New Testament) and that Luke later interpreted the event and saw it as the beginning of a universal mission which would overcome every division of mankind. The apostles preached the marvels of God, principally the fact that Jesus was man and had now poured out his Spirit.
Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 103). God creates and gives life through his Spirit. The work of creation and life-giving is continuous. For this we thank and praise God.
