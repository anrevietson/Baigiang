# Charismatic gifts

Second Reading (1 Cor 12:3-7,12-13). Ecstatic and charismatic activity were present in many ancient religions and were common for the early Christians, Evidently a disproportionate preoccupation with some, of the charismatic gifts was disrupting the order of the liturgy. There also seems to have been some concern that evil spirits could be causing the disruptions. For this reason the author focuses on the common features of authentic Christian charisms: Despite their diversity all the charisms arc gifts (cnarismata) from outside the individual which form some service (diakonia) for the welfare of the whole Church, and it is God who is working and acting through each gift.
Although the early Church did not yet have a clear understanding of the Trinity the author associates each of these aspects of the charismatic gifts with one of the persons of the Trinity. The 'gifts' are attributed to the Spirit who is the Gift sent by the Son and Father. As 'ministry' the gifts are attributed to Jesus who was sent to serve. As the inner 'working' of God, the gifts are attributed to the Father as the source of all being and activity. All the gifts which reveal the presence of the Spirit are for the common good, namely the building up of the one Body of Christ, Just as the human body is one body, those who believe in Christ form one body of believers in spite of the diversity of the component members,
