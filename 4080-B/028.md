# A new stage in sacred history

First Reading (Acts 2:1-11). Luke introduces a new stage in sacred history with the opening words 'when the day was fulfilled.' The notion of fulfilment is an overarching concept in the Book of Acts just as it was in Luke's gospel. The fire and the wind, visual elements of ancient theophanies, repeat a time-honoured association between Ascension and Pentecost: Moses ascended to God and returned with the gift of the Law; Jesus ascended to heaven and sent the gift of the Spirit. Nevertheless, the description of the wind and fire emphasizes the disproportion between the wind and the fire and the heavenly realities they represent. T he apocalyptic comparatives 'tongues as if of fire' and 'a sound like the wind try 10 convey the meaning of a primarily interior experience of the thyne presence. In this passage the Spirit becomes the principle divine presence much like Yahweh in the earlier Scriptures and Jesus in the Gospel narratives, The visible and lasting effect of this felt presence of Gods Spirit is the universal preaching that transcends every boundary of nationality, speech and religious status.
