# Jesus appears in the midst of the disciples

Gospel (Jn 20:19-23). This passage describes Jesus appearing in the midst of the disciples even though the doors are locked for fear of the Jews. The opening lines serve the twofold purpose of describing the nature of Jesus's resurrected body and setting the scene for the resurrection account, Nothing seems to have been further from the mind of the disciples than the reappearance of their teacher. They seemed to fear a fate similar to his. The element of the unexpected adds credibility to their witness of an actual resurrection event. After greeting them, Jesus shows them his hands and side. This portion of the resurrection testimony focuses attention on the historical Jesus of Nazareth whom the disciples knew so well. The emphasis on details like the empty tomb and wounds of Jesus points to the continued existence of the historical Jesus after the crucifixion event.
Jesus wishes the disciples 'peace', a kind of peace the world cannot give them. For Jesus offers them the gift of salvation, the fulfilment of messianic expectations. Jesus solemnly sends the disciples as messenger's (apostobi) of this peace borne of forgiveness, thereby commissioning them to perpetuate the work of salvation accomplished through Christ. Jesus breathes on the disciples in an action that recalls the creation account where God breathed on Adam and gave him life. The action of Jesus breathing on the disciples is a sign, or sacrament, of the new creation, of the new life that comes from Jesus as the Spirit is breathed into those who are being sent forth. The giving of the Spirit is specifically related to the power to forgive. The sign of the Spirit's presence is compassion, mercy, forgiveness, reconciliation and peace. The power of Pentecost is in the hands of those who choose to love and forgive and share the peace that is borne from above.
