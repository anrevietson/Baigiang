# Pleading on their behalf

First Reading(Jer. 18:18-20; Mt. 20:17-28). For New Testament writers it was our Lord's passion-rather than, as in later times, his resurrection-that was his vindication, the Christian apologetic: 'this was the way God carried out what he had foretold, when he said through all his prophets that his Christ would suffer' (Acts 3:18). The murderous alliance which our Lord foretells in today's Gospel (recalled in Acts 4:27) fulfils the forecast of Wisdom 2:12 f., and especially of Isaiah 53:8-9. Yet those are anonymous figures. No historical personage in the Old Testament more vividly pre-enacted the fate of our Lord than Jeremiah: in the hostility accorded his preaching of God's will; in his rejection as a trouble-maker (cf. John 11:50); in his redemptive 'standing in God's presence to plead on their behalf' (cf. Luke 23:34).
