# The discipline of the Lord

Second Reading (Heb. 12 :5-7, 11-13). We continue on this Sunday the exhortation to the suffering Christians addressed by the writer of this letter. He reminds them of the statement of the Book of Proverbs (3 :12) that the Lord disciplines him whom he loves. He interprets the hardships of this life as a training into Christian maturity. Even though they may be unpleasant, they lead towards 'heavenly' living. We share God's very holiness (wholesomeness) as a result.
Next he encourages his readers with two images: the body, and the way. Let them lift up their hanging hands, and strengthen their weak knees. Let them carve out a straight and smooth path, so that the lame, halting, Christian may not fall by the wayside, but continue securely on his journey.
One might recall here the sufferings of Paul through which the Holy Spirit worked in him all the more fully. Cf. 2 Cor. 6 :3-10 'is having nothing and yet possessing everything', and 2 Cor 11 23 - 12 :10... 'for when I am weak, then I am strong'.
I would suggest that our controlled attitude towards the pressures of modern life may be quite an effective way into the fullness of Christian response, e.g., the interminable ringing of the telephone, the arrival of the postman with the problem letters, the noise of the jet-plane overhead, the insatiable desire of people in our day to discuss everything at length. . . . This may prove a more meaningful image of penance than that of a Father God actually causing our suffering, in order to test us. Secondary causes abound a-plenty.
