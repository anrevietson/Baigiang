# God's gift of salvation

Today we are invited to meditate together on and celebrate God's gift of salvation to the whole human family. We are challenged to respond in faith made concrete in the reality of our lives to this loving invitation, to renew our covenant commitment to be disciples who are able to handle the consequences of being Christians in western society today.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Aware of our own weakness and pain let us stand now in God's presence and allow his compassion to teach us and heal us, renew us and strengthen us in our resolve to live lives open to the challenge of the Kingdom.
Lord Jesus, you call us to faith and obedience. Lord, have mercy.
You have commanded us to support one another on our journey to God. Christ, have mercy.
You invite us to offer one another forgiveness, encouragement and support. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Is 66:18-21) Today's first reading is taken from the third and last part of the Book of Isaiah which forms the finale to the whole of the text. It is set in Palestine rather than Babylon and opens a vision of a glorious future in which the Covenant is opened to the whole world, to the whole human family.
Second Reading (Heb 12:5-7.11-13). The writer of Hebrews turns, in chapters 11 and 12, to address issues of Christian spiritual discipline set against the background of obedience and disobedience and offers an invitation to perseverance and commitment.
Gospel (Lk 13:22-30). In the Gospel the clear next for repentance, rethinking, reviewing, for change and renewal, is stressed by Jesus as part and parcel of a life left open to salvation, to a life radically directed by the teachings of Jesus; part and parcel of facing the paradoxes and polarities of existence as a disciple.

PRAYERS OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware of the challenge of the Good News to live lives radically open to Gospel values and aware of the universal quality of divine love, let us pray for the whole human family.
Reader(s): For world leaders in Church and state: that compassion and truth be guiding principles and priorities.
For the Christian community: that in realistic hope and optimism it may help people on the way to a just and peaceful world.
For all who have lost hope, who have fallen into despair and see nothing to live for: that they may see a ray of light, a hint of new possibilities.
For our own parish and local community: that we find authentic ways of helping each other live in Christian joy and action.
For our departed relations and friends, and for all who have died, especially those who have died tragically: may they have light, happiness and peace.
President: Yahweh-Shalom, God of universal compassion, draw us ever more deeply into the heart of covenant love. In this Eucharist, correct us and renew us, strengthen 115 and heal us for the week to come. Grant this through Christ our Lord.

INVITATION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Aware of the challenge of discipleship, of the challenge to be authentically Christian let us pray in the way Jesus taught us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Peace in biblical terms is a positive force committed to wholeness and harmony. It is the most ancient of blessings, a desire to touch and be touched by God's own life. Open to this touch, aware of this love, let us bless each other with Shalom-Peace.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
Lord Jesus, you teach us in the Gospel that people from east and west, from opposite poles of the earth, will sit at table with you in the feast of the kingdom. We share now in this hope made present. Lead us gently to the narrow door, hidden in the reality of our own lives, and strengthen us for the crossing of the threshold to truer life.
