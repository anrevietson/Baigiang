# Abraham's hospitality

First Reading (Gen. 18 :1-10). The reader of the Bible is often struck by the tendency of Old Testament writers to describe God in purely human terms. Abraham was relaxing in front of his tent during the hottest part of the day, when he was surprised by the sudden appearance of three strangers who seemed to materialize from nowhere. Acting the part of deferential host he rates at their lowest the refreshments he intends to offer. The richness of the fare offered is in sharp contrast to the invitation to accept a little water and bread. Abraham addresses the man whom he regards as leader with the title 'my lord'. But since he does not yet recognize his visitor as God we must understand the title as a conventional mark of respect directed to a guest. It is not easy to establish who the three 'men' were. But it seems probable that it was Yahweh himself who appears with two angels. The Bible often speaks of the appearance of an angel when it wishes to refer to a manifestation of God to men; cf. Genesis 16 :3; 22 :11 ff.; Exodus 3 :2. The purpose of the visit is to announce to Abraham that, due to a special divine favour, a child will be born to him in his old age. The transcendent God deals familiarly with his friend and watches over even the details of his life. Such intimacy between God and man foreshadows the extreme condescension of God who became man among men.
The Responsorial Psalm sings of friendship of God. The just man is he who d listens to the Lord and heeds his commands.
