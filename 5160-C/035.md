# Abraham's hospitality

First Reading (Gen. 18:1-10). The reader of the Bible is often struck by the tendency of Old Testament writers to describe God in purely human terms and to attribute to him such faculties as eyes, ears, mouth, heart. This practice of representing God in human form is known by scholars as anthropomorphism. Some of the most anthropomorphic passages in the Bible are to be found in Genesis, and the delightfully homely description of God's visit to Abraham which we read today is perhaps the most vivid and picturesque of all these passages. The old patriarch was relaxing in front of his tent during the hottest part of the day, when he was surprised by the sudden appearance of three strangers who seemed to materialise from nowhere. His immediate reaction was to prepare to treat his unknown visitors with the lavish hospitality that is characteristic of the dwellers of the desert. Acting the part of deferential host he rates at their lowest the refreshments he intends to offer. The richness of the fare offered is in sharp contrast to the invitation to accept a little water and bread. Abraham addresses the man whom he regards as leader with the title 'my lord'. But since he does not yet recognise his visitor as God (verse one, which informs us that it was God who visited Abraham, is a late addition to the story) we must understand the title as a conventional mark of respect directed to a guest. It is not easy to establish who the three men were. But it seems probable that it was Jahweh himself who appears with two angels. The Bible often speaks of the appearance of an angel when it wishes to refer to a manifestation of God to men; cf. Genesis 16:3; 22:11 ff.; Exodus 3:2. The purpose of the visit is to announce to Abraham that, due to a special divine favour, a child will be born to him in his old age. The transcendent God deals familiarly with his friend and watches over even the details of his life. Such intimacy between God and man foreshadows the extreme condescension of God who became man among men.
