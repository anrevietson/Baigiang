# Fretting like Martha

Like Martha in today's Gospel story we fret and worry about the many things that occupy us each day. But like Mary in the same story we realise the importance of coming into the presence of the Lord and of listening to his word. May our liturgical celebration enable us to return to our daily duties with new peace of mind and with new courage.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
Lord, your word is a light for our faith and a guide for our lives. Lord, have mercy.
Lord, you are with us when we try to show our love for you as we serve our neighbour. Christ, have mercy.
Lord, you forgive us when we forget you because of the duties and distractions of life. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Gen 18:1 - 10). The hospitable Abraham welcomed three guests, one of whom turned out to be the Lord himself. When we do something for one of our brothers or sisters we do it for the Lord himself
Second Reading (Col 1:24-28). The passion of Christ has atoned for the sins of the world, but his victory over evil is not yet complete. The efforts and the sacrifices of Christians help to build up his kingdom.
Gospel (Lk 10:38-42). Jesus gently rebukes Martha who frets and worries about her duties. He praises Mary who takes time to listen to his teaching. The ideal Christian is one who can serve others generously, without forgetting to pay attention to the Lord and to his word.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Christ lives among all his followers, and he lives in each one of us. Through him as our mediator let us offer our prayers to the Father.
Reader(s): We pray for the Pope, for the Bishops, for all church leaders, that they may lead God's people in the ways of true wisdom.
We pray for all Christians, that they may reverently listen to the word of God, and that all their actions may be guided by that word.
We pray for all who suffer in any way, that the Lord may send them healing and strength, and that they may see that their pain completes what is lacking in Christ's suffering.
We pray for refugees and for the homeless. May Christians show them hospitality and generosity as they would to Christ himself.
We pray for the faithful departed, now that their journey of faith is over, may they enjoy the full revelation of Christ's glory.
President: Lord, hear our prayers as we celebrate this act of worship. Give us the grace to listen to your word, and give us the strength to put it into practice in the week ahead. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
Having listened in this liturgy to the word of God, let us now ask that his will may be done in our lives, as we pray together in the words our Saviour gave us.

INVITATION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
Martha's agitated words in today's Gospel show that the peace of the household was broken for a moment. Jesus' words restored peace. May his presence in the Eucharist establish peace in our hearts ,and may we be able to bring that peace to others. And now, let us share with each other a sign of peace.
