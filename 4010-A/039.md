# The Cornelius story

First Reading (Acts 10:34, 37-43). The Cornelius story as told in Acts 10 1-11:18 is central to the unfolding of Luke's history of the spread of the gospel. Cornelius is a Roman centurion stationed at Caesarea who is interested in the Jewish faith and is a man of prayer. At this point in the life of the early Church, Gentiles are still considered with suspicion so it is a cause of amazement to both Peter and Cornelius that their paths should cross under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. Peter, on arriving at Cornelius' house, is warmly welcomed and proceeds to preach about Jesus, giving a synopsis of the recent events and emphasizing the meaning of the resurrection - Jesus risen from the dead is the source of the forgiveness of sins for all who believe in him. The way is prepared for the Christian faith to reach out to the whole world. This is indeed the message of Easter.
