# The discovery of the empty tomb

Gospel (Jn 20:1-9). This resurrection narrative includes traditional material regarding the discovery of the empty tomb by women (verses 1-2), and the visit of Peter and the disciples to the empty tomb (verses 3-10). While it does not include any appearances of Jesus to his followers, the meaning of these events is clearly related to the theme of faith.
Mary of Magdala, mentioned in all four gospels, comes to the tomb very early in the first day of the week, probably with the other women, to anoint the body of Jesus. John focuses only on Mary here and in verses 11-28. She tells of her experience, identifying Jesus as "the Lord" a term commonly used by Luke, but reserved by John for the period after the resurrection.
Peter and the Beloved Disciple run to the tomb, concerned about this report. Although the disciple arrives first, he waits for Peter who enters ahead of him. Peter sees the linen cloths, an indication that the body has not been stolen, but rather that Jesus has risen from the dead. Peter observes the evidence offering no comment, and then John enters the tomb. His response, noted by the gospel author, is a powerful one "he saw and believed." The Beloved Disciple believes in Jesus' resurrection without the benefit of an appearance. He believes without a major sign
so important in this gospel, that the writer will later comment in the form of a beatitude: "Happy those who have not seen and yet have believed" (Jn 20:29). This belief, a model for John's community and for the Christian, is repeated again in John 21:4,7. The Beloved Disciple detects the presence of the Lord, understands in faith the meaning of the events, and believes simply on what he has seen in the tomb. When John writes for his community, their faith is in "the Lord" and they understand more completely the resurrection as a fulfilment of scripture (Jn 20:9. This account draws on the signs in the beginning of John's gospel, particularly the Lazarus account in chapter 11. Just as signs led to belief in Jesus during his earthly ministry, so too, will they lead to the fullness of faith in the post-resurrection period. However, this account indicates a quality of faith that goes beyond the sign. Perhaps, the love of the Beloved Disciple gives him an insight into the meaning of the empty tomb. While Peter is leader of the Twelve, another disciple believes before him. The same evidence elicits different responses in Mary of Magdala, Peter, and the Beloved Disciple.
