# A turning point in Acts

First Reading (Acts 10.34,37-43). Chapters 10, 11 and 15 represent a turning point in Acts for in these sections Peter and the Twelve legitimise the Gentile Christian Church. Peter addresses the household of Cornelius and the theological core of his speech is the Old Testament insight that God shows no partiality, but accepts every nation that acts justly. In the selection for today's Easter celebration, Peter recalls Jesus' ministry in Galilee and the events in Judea. Then he identifies himself and those with him, as witnesses to these events. In Luke's writings witnesses to the resurrection were also witnesses to the events of Jesus' ministry and death, a point he makes at the choice of Matthias in Acts. However, the heart of the reading is the basic proclamation of faith or kerygma of the early Church, that Luke repeats in all the speeches in Acts. These witnesses proclaim the death and resurrection of Jesus, his presence among them, the fulfilment of prophecy, the testimony of witnesses, and the call to believe in Jesus for the forgiveness of sins. This proclamation of belief by enthusiastic witnesses has the power to move people to open their hearts in faith to the Lord. The experience and presence of the Risen Lord impel Peter and other witnesses to understand the death of Jesus differently and to expand their mission to the Gentile community. They allow Gentiles to respond directly in faith to the Lord, by by-passing the Jewish rituals regarding food and circumcision.
