# Peter's speech in the house of Cornelius

First Reading (Acts 10:34, 37-43). This reading is part of Peter's speech in the house of the Roman centurion Cornelius which led to the conversion of the centurion and of his entire household. The discourse gives a summary of the public life of Jesus, ending with the narrative of his resurrection. The reader will notice the emphasis placed by Peter on the fact that he and his companions were eyewitnesses of the public life of Jesus. He also stresses the fact that he and certain others were select witnesses of the resurrection, the reality of which is proved by the fact that they partook of food with the risen Christ. They are witnesses of all this, so that they can be sent to preach redemption, the forgiveness of sin.
The risen Saviour continued to be with his Church after the resurrection. And it was because Christ had risen again from the dead that the apostles could preach the reality of redemption, of the forgiveness of sin. Because God was with Christ during his 1)public life Jesus went about doing good. God is present in a very special way with his people in the person of the risen Christ. In him the very fullness of God dwells, the plenitude of divine power and goodness. This, however, is an overflowing plenitude, bringing new divine life to all who believe in Christ. This is the power of Christ's resurrection of which Paul speaks in his letter to the Philippians (Phil. 3:10).
Peter could speak with confidence of this divine power, having experienced the advent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost. But the lapse of almost two thousand years makes no difference to the Church's confidence in this regard. For faith the resurrection has removed chronology. Each successive generation of Christians can proclaim, and must proclaim, the reality and the significance of the resurrection as much as Peter did. The Church does so unceasingly, but in a special manner at Easter. And down through the ages the opening words of the Easter Mass have brought us Christ's reassuring message 'I have arisen and am with you still, alleluia.' The passage of the years has made no difference. The risen Christ is the same yesterday, today and will be so forever. (On this first reading see also scripture IN CHURC~ no.1, pp. 57 f.)
