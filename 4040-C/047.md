# My sheep hear my voice

The Gospel (John 10 :27-30) (K.C.). The general context is that of the good shepherd. In ancient times - before the rise of the modern 'democratic ideal' - men looked for happiness and security to the gifted and righteous ruler. In the Greek world such a beneficent king was often given the title 'saviour' (so ter), in Israel he was a pastor Or shepherd (for the ideal, read Ezek. 34, and Ps. 72 - both messianic, and both with good preaching material). Because the shepherds of Israel were unfaithful to their charge, the Lord would himself raise up a shepherd: 'my servant David, and he shall feed them' (Ezek. 34 :23; cf. Jer. 33 :14-16). This messianic shepherd is first and foremost a king, one who would rule justly and benevolently. The New Testament (particularly John and 1 Peter) spirituals the idea with the result that the good shepherd appears more as a priestly than a royal personage.
Christianity does not mean a freedom for man to decide for himself; this is the very cause of his insecurity. Christianity is a submission of faith: 'My sheep hear my voice.' It is a superior knowledge, a transcendent awareness: 'I know mine and mine know me' (v.14; cf. v.27). It is a discipline: 'My sheep follow me.' And it is the only absolute security man can have: 'I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish, and no one shall snatch them out of my hand.' Fundamentally, it is the security of the immutable God, because 'I and the Father are one. '
