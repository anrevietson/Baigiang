# My sheep hear my voice

The Gospel (John 10:27-30). Context and content : The general context is that of the good shepherd. In ancient times - before the rise of the modern 'democratic ideal' - men looked for happiness and security to the gifted and righteous ruler. In the Greek world such a beneficent king was often given the title 'Saviour' (sótér), in Israel be was a pastor or shepherd (for the ideal, read Ezek. 34, and Ps. 72 - both messianic, and both with good preaching material). Because the shepherds of Israel were unfaithful to their charge, the Lord would himself raise up a shepherd : 'my servant David, and he shall feed them' (Ezek. 34 :23; cf. Jer. 33 :14-16). This messianic shepherd is first and foremost a king, one who would rule justly and benevolently. The New Testament (particularly John and 1 Peter) spiritualize the idea with the result that the good shepherd appears more as a priestly than a royal personage.
John 10:1-18 embodies two distinct parables, both of which are exploited in the elucidation given in the gospel : first (1-5) is that of the good shepherd (as distinct from the hireling). Here the idea is that of community of knowledge and love between the shepherd and his sheep. He knows his own, and they know him; he gives his life for them. The other (7-9) is that of the gate by which one goes into and out from the fold. Jesus alone is the gate; only by him can one enter and be saved. In both parables, however, there is an underlying common motif - that of confidence and security. This could be a suitable theme on which to centre a sermon. One hears a lot today about 'alienation'; men are out of joint with themselves and with their environment; they need support, they need a life-line. And John gives the answer in verses twenty-seven to thirty.
