# Joseph sold by his brothers

First Reading(Gen. 37:3-4, 12-13, 17-28; Mt. 21:33-43, 45-46). Today's readings like Wednesday's, remind us that we are preparing to celebrate the mystery of Christ's passion. Joseph is a striking Old Testament figure of Christ: in being surrendered by his brothers to virtual death; in the fulfilment, after affliction, of his prophetic dreams of greatness; above all in the lesson of Genesis 50:20-'The evil you planned to do me has by God's design been turned to good ... the deliverance of a numerous people.' Today's parable, like Wednesday's Gospel, is a prediction by our Lord of his passion. But it is given almost unbearable dramatic intensity in that it is spoken only a few days before his death; in Jerusalem, the place of his death; and to the very enemies the chief priests and scribes, who would have him put to death. We may well remind ourselves by verse forty-three that we too must 'bear fruit' if we are to inherit the kingdom.
