# Sayings about following Jesus

In the second half of the Gospel reading (Luke 9 :51-62) we have a series of sayings of Jesus about following him. Here again the absolute demands of Christ's call are emphasized, but in an even stronger manner than in the passage about Elisha's call by Elijah, for in the gospel the disciple is not allowed even to say goodbye to his family. The synoptic evangelists omit the disciples' psychological preparation; they give only the bare bones of what they consider to be significant, and this is the absolute, uncompromising call of Christ, and their immediate and unconditional response, leaving family and all their means of livelihood without a backward glance.
In the earlier part the gospel passage shows yet another incident where the disciples misunderstood the nature of the kingdom which they are so soon to start preaching. They still think that the methods of the kingdom can be aggressive, the destruction of opposition by force, after the manner of those among the Jews who hoped for a material and triumphalistic world empire. But Jesus came to bring a healing and saving, not a destructive, rule. The second part of the passage, however, shows that it was not a weak kingdom of compromise, but that its gentleness is allied to an uncompromising firmness.
