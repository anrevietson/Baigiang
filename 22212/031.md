# The Magnificat

The Gospel (Luke 1:40-56). The Magnificat, whatever its origin, sums up divers themes of the Old Testament hope. The promise to Abraham which is its backbone is fulfilled, and now, instead of Abraham's name being a blessing to all nations, Mary's will be for all generations. Fulfilled now also are the themes of the exodus, when God formed to himself a people; it was then first that he showed to Israel 'the power of his arm', 'exalted the lowly' people from their miserable plight in Egypt, and 'filled the hungry' in the desert. Strong also in the hymn is the thought of the poor of Yahweh. Since the prophets had castigated the rich, and especially since the exile had deprived Israel of everything and left the nation again destitute to the Lord, the theology had been continually developing of a poor remnant, faithful to the Lord in and because of that poverty. Mary was the spearhead of this remnant.
