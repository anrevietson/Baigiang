# The towering figures of the early Church

Today's celebration brings together in one feast the towering figures of the early Christian Church, Peter and Paul. They were quite different in their temperaments and talents. But they shared a common belief in Jesus that eventually led to their martyrdom. As we recall both their weaknesses and their strengths, we are reminded that in the believing community there is room for differences, provided we focus on Jesus, the central figure of Christianity. In Eucharist we gather around that same Jesus, although our gifts and our backgrounds may be different.

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We humbly acknowledge that, like Peter and Paul, we too have made mistakes. Aware of those failings, we confidently ask our God and our community to forgive us.
You call us to make you the central person in our lives. Lord, have mercy.
You bring us the courage to proclaim your message in our daily lives. Christ, have mercy.
You inspire us always to respect others though they are different. Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Acts 12:1 - 11). The escape of Peter from prison shows that God will indeed provide for his people in times of persecution. Without any effort on his part Peter is miraculously delivered.
Second Reading (2 Tim 4:6-8, 17-18). The author exhorts his audience to remain faithful by citing the example of Paul. Paul's steadfastness in the midst of dangers is to encourage the perseverance of Church leaders.
Gospel (Mt 16:13-19). Matthew shows Jesus acknowledging Peter's perception of who Jesus really is. Jesus then confers on Peter the grace of leadership in his community with the power to bind and loose.

PRAYER OF THE faithful
President. Realizing our God's invitation to share the Good News with others, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son.
Reader(s): We pray for all religious and civil leaders. May they have the courage of Peter and Paul to announce and live out the gospel message.
We pray for the members of our local faith community. May we imitate Peter and Paul by using our variety of gifts to sustain and encourage each other.
We pray for all those, both living and dead, who professed their faith in the midst of trying circumstances.
We pray for all those who in one way or another yearn to hear the Good News. May the example of Peter and Paul help us to respond to those deepest yearnings.
President: Gracious and loving God, we give you thanks for the common gift of Peter and Paul. We beseech you to help us lead lives founded on their example of faith and courage. Enable us to be bearers of the Good News in our time and place. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we profess that we share a common faith and a common destiny. Implicitly we acknowledge that this faith and destiny rest on the witness and example of many others, such as Peter and Paul. As we offer this prayer, we are challenged to recall their self-giving ways and make them contagious for our own witness.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
In sharing the sign of peace, we realize that there are many different people in our community with many different gifts and talents. On this feast of Peter and Paul we are urged to accept those differences and make them the means for promoting peace, not disunity. To offer the sign of peace is to acknowledge the multiplicity of God's goodness in others. To offer that sign is also to seek to enriched by such goodness.

COMMUNION REFLECTION
On this special feast we may recall the teaching of Paul about the Eucharist - we are to proclaim the death of the Lord until he comes (1 Cor 11:26). Jesus self-giving culminating in death is to be the way of life for all Christians. In this Eucharist we are asked to reflect on our manner of Eucharist outside the place of worship. We are to proclaim the death of the Lord by reaching out to all those who have a claim on our time and energy. Eucharist and concern for others go hand in hand.
