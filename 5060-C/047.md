# The resurrection of the dead

Second Reading (1 Cor 15:12, 16-20). In chapter 15 of 1 Corinthians Paul is making a detailed argument to support Christian belief in the resurrection of the dead. It is very likely that some of his readers have doubts about the fate of those Christians who have died. Two opinions might lie behind this position. There were those in the community who put an exaggerated emphasis on the possession of the Spirit. They believed they possessed eternal life now, so the death of others cast doubt on their true status as saved. Secondly some people - perhaps the same people held the body in low esteem. For them only spiritual realties mattered. Paul's answer is not based on a philosophical argument but on the heart of Christian proclamation: Christ is risen. The opening sentence of this reading is the kernel of his answer. If Christ has been raised from the dead, then there is a resurrection of the dead. If one denies the latter,
then one is denying the former as well. So these people are in fact denying the heart and totality of the Christian message.
Paul is both atheologian and a pastor. He not only with this speculation but draws out the practical points. To question the resurrection has serious implications for all dimensions of Christian faith. Deny the resurrection, and one denies the forgiveness of sin. Deny the resurrection and one denigrates the body and devalues Christian action in this life. Perhaps too often we use the sacrifice of Jesus on the Cross as the motivation for Christian living. We urge people to live in imitation of Christ who gave up everything for us. When we think about it, it is very difficult to let go of everything for us. When we think about it, it is very difficult to let go of everything: self-preservation impels living is the Resurrection. It supplies the true horizon against which we can make sacrifices. Now, in imitation of Christ, we can give of ourselves completely and know that we will receive anew. Paul's insistence on the Resurrection as the fundamental Christian belief reminds us that it, not so much the Cross, should stand at the centre of our teaching.
