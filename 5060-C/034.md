# The beatitudes

The Gospel (Luke 6 :17, 20-26). Today's reading with the beatitudes is the opening section of 'the great discourse' of Christ, which is more generally known as the Sermon on the Mount, a title it gets from Matthew's gospel. Luke's form of the discourse is much shorter than that of the First Gospel. In his presentation Matthew is interested in the religion of Christ as the fulfilment, the bringing to perfection, of 'the law and the prophets'. The theme of the discourse in Luke is love and forgiveness. God brings the poor and lowly into his kingdom; the followers of Christ should love and forgive their enemies. In Matthew's gospel Jesus, as the new Moses, gives his new law on a mountain. For Luke the mountain has come to have a symbolic significance. It is the place where Jesus communes with his Father or reveals himself to his chosen disciples. The discourse is given not on the mountain, but on level ground where he converses with humanity.
On the mountain Jesus prayed and chose the twelve apostles. He then descended to the poor who are below, to the sick and afflicted from Palestine and from pagan territory (Tyre and Sidon) who have come to him. In this crowd we have an image of the universal Church. Jesus has descended to bring these the message of redemption. Matthew's form of the beatitudes is also generic: 'Blessed are the poor in spirit'. Luke is more specific and direct:
'Blessed are you poor'. In Matthew the beatitudes are 'spiritualized'; in Luke they are addressed to a specific social class, to individuals in unfortunate social conditions. To these Jesus promises eternal bliss. Those, on the contrary, who enjoy the good things of life here below will suffer hereafter. Not that there is any contradiction between the presentation as found in Luke and Matthew. The poor in Luke represent the pious and the God-fearing; the rich are those who deny them their rights. It is not the social condition as such that counts for Luke. Yet Luke's direct address to the 'poor' and the 'rich', and his reminder of God's concern for and favour towards those neglected by society, has a lasting message.
