# The sermon on the plain

Gospel (Lk 6:17,20-26). Luke's sermon on the plain is shorter than the better-known Sermon on the Mount of Matthew's Gospel. The same can be said about the Lucan version of the Beatitudes. It is important to pay close attention to what the Lucan Jesus says, forgetting Matthew's version for a moment.
The Lucan Jesus addresses a mixed group of disciples and people: his focus shifts between the two so that it is often not clear if the teachings are for committed disciples or uncommitted listeners. This suggests that the division between the two groups is not fixed. Discipleship must constantly be renewed by listening and responding to Jesus' word. It is we, the listeners, who drift from one category to the other.
Like the blessing and curse of the first reading, Luke's beatitudes and woes place a choice before the would-be disciples. Jesus is preaching to those who would belong to the Kingdom. Who are they, then? Those who admit their need for salvation and so belong to the 'poor'. They accept that Jesus is the Son of Man who brings the Kingdom. The 'poor' of the beatitudes is not a social class. They are any person, impoverished or well-to-do, who becomes a disciple. The woes are addressed to the same would-be disciples. They are cautionary remarks warning against a superficial understanding of discipleship. The 'rich' do not commit themselves to Jesus nor accept the Kingdom. They are content with their present comfortable existence. Luke is not banning the well-to-do from the Kingdom but warning all people of the need to check our attitudes. It is clear that Luke has well-to-do members among his readers. In the sermon he is showing how Jesus' teaching applies to them. They are to imitate the God of the Kingdom by lending, forgiving debts and giving generously without a thought to recompense. The important things in life are not wealth, gourmet foods, rest and relaxation or social respectability. These things are ephemeral in comparison to follow Jesus and his Kingdom.
