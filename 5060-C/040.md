# Trust in the Lord

First Reading (Jer 17:5-8). This reading is one of a series of short passages from the Book of Jeremiah which bear a close resemblance to the teachings of the sages. Such wisdom teaching can be compared to passages from the Books of Proverbs and Ecclesiasticus. But the closest parallel is Psalm I, today's Responsorial psalm. Both make use of the image of the watered tree and the dry scrub to contrast the person who trusts in God with the person who trusts in humans. The use of contrasting blessing and curse is also typical of wisdom teaching.
This teaching observes human behaviour and seeks to guide the pupil toward happiness and fulfilment. Note that this type of instruction is not prescriptive - no 'do this' or 'don't do that'. It is descriptive and seeks to influence indirectly. It describes alternatives in such a way that one is faced with a choice; but one option is obviously more attractive! Here the question is: where does one's security come from? What or who grants true peace of mind? Jeremiah's choice is marked by the language of blessing and curse. Happiness in this life depends on where we commit our heart.
