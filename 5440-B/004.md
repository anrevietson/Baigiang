# The heart, symbol of compassion

Today's celebration focuses on the heart as the great symbol of compassion and love. Specifically we are reminded of the Sacred Heart of Jesus, of God's Son who loves so intensely and deeply that only his death on the cross for us could be the adequate expression of that love. We are left with the lingering question: how are we to respond to such?

INVITATION TO REPENTANCE
We must candidly admit that through our sinfulness we have not properly responded to God's immense love. Aware of these failures, we humbly ask both God and our community for forgiveness.
You call us to have love and compassion for our sisters and brothers:
Lord, have mercy.
Your limitless love is to be the norm for dealing with sisters and brothers: Christ, have mercy.
You call us never to refuse to forgive our sisters and brothers: Lord, have mercy.

HEADINGS FOR READINGS
First Reading (Hos 11:1,3-4,8-9). The prophet announces the Lord's overwhelming love for his son Israel. Though Israel has not responded in love and concern, God's wrath will not he uncontrollable because God is God, not a human.
Second Reading (Eph 3:8-12,14-19). The author prays that God's unfathomable love may become a reality among his audience. The challenge of the audience is to grasp fully the breadth, length, height, and depth of Christ's love.
Gospel (Jn 19:31-37). John links the flow of water with Jesus' prophecy that from within him there would flow rivers of living water. In the death of Jesus new life comes to the community because of Jesus' release of the Spirit.

PRAYER OF THE FAITHFUL
President: Aware that God has revealed the mystery of overwhelming love in the Sacred Heart of Jesus, we make these petitions to the Father through the Son:
Reader(s): We pray for all religious and civil leaders who have demonstrated the reality of the Sacred Heart of Jesus through justice and concern.
We pray for all the members of our local faith community who have made the Sacred Heart of Jesus their norm in showing love and compassion to their sisters and brothers.
We pray for the depressed and lonely of our world. May they experience the joy of community through believers committed to the Sacred Heart of Jesus.
We pray for those who have lost the capacity to love. May our imitation of the Sacred Heart of Jesus energize them and reawaken their ability to give of themselves.
President: Gracious and loving God, the symbol of your Son's sacred heart assures us of your concern for us. We ask you to strengthen us so that we may demonstrate concern for you by showing concern for our sisters and brothers. We ask this through Christ our Lord.

INTRODUCTION TO THE LORD'S PRAYER
In this prayer we ask that our Father's Kingdom may come. While we acknowledge that the Kingdom has begun, we also admit that it is not yet a full reality. In offering this prayer, we commit ourselves to imitating the Sacred Heart of Jesus. When we practice compassion and concern, we contribute to the final coming if that Kingdom.

INTRODUCTION TO THE SIGN OF PEACE
The sign of peace is implicitly our willingness to make the Sacred Heart of Jesus a reality in our community. To offer such a sign is to impose upon ourselves the obligation to make compassion and love something genuine and meaningful. Peace and the Sacred Heart of Jesus go hand in hand.

COMMUNION REELECTION
As we participate in Eucharist, we recall that the self-giving of Jesus is to be norm for living Christian lives. On this feast we attest that the heart of Jesus captures the depths of that self-giving. Having shared the bread and the cup, we recommit ourselves to self-giving through imitation of the Sacred Heart. When we demonstrate compassion and concern, we make that heart the norm of our actions. Eucharist and the feast of the Sacred Heart powerfully challenge us to be ever willing to give of ourselves.
