# Always beginning anew

Second Reading (Phil 3:8-14). Paul wrote to the Philippians around 58-59, a decade after he founded this first European community during his first missionary journey in 48 or 49. Paul had a very positive relationship with this community, but the letter indicates he has concerns about dissension in the community and divisive influence of travelling missionaries who are taking advantage of Paul's absence to advocate teachings that provoke problems among the faithful.
Our passage, part of a synthesis of Pauls apostolic preaching in the letter, summarises Paul's views on justification and the nature of salvation. It is a moving testimony to his own spirituality that is dominated by deep commitment to Christ. Paul cuts through the superficial preaching of his opponents and stresses that nothing is more important for the Christian than the development of a personal relationship with the Lord. This commitment is not something that is easily over and done with but rather the daily effort of struggling to build the relationship with the Lord, forgetting the past and always beginning anew.
