# The Lord who makes a way in the desert

First Reading (Is 43:16-21). The Lord who promises to bring the exiles out of Babylon is the one who liberated their ancestors from the slavery of Egypt. The exiles should understand their return from exile not simply as part of a secular political policy but as an act of the Lord, as a new Exodus, with the focus being no longer on the Lord's power to destroy past enemies but on his giving of life to his creatures, including the non-human animal world. As in the first Exodus, the Lord saves the exiles and forms them into his people specifically so that they may praise him, which means that they are called not only to enjoy a new life but also to ascribe it to their Lord in their worship, which is a witnessing to his saving power.
