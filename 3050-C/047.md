# Knowing Christ

Second Reading (Phil. 3:8-14). The apostle Paul (formerly Saul) was at first a Pharisee who persecuted the Church. The religious and legal traditions of the group to which he belonged had led the Jewish people to reject Christ and his redemptive work. The emphasis they placed on their own traditions practically closed their minds to the demands made by God's law on the conscience. They had further concentrated their attention so much on external observance that redemption was almost made to depend on human effort, on the external works demanded by their law. When the risen Saviour appeared to Saul on the road to Damascus the
zealous Pharisee saw how wrong he had been. Salvation could only come through Christ, through his cross and resurrection; by acceptance, through faith in Christ, of that salvation that came from God. Saul now saw that there could be no compromise between the Christian and the orthodox Jewish position. With this revelation Saul viewed Jewish religion in its proper perspective and judged it as useless, as so much refuse or rubbish to be discarded. On the cross Christ died to the earthly order of things. He arose to a new order, to that of the Spirit. With his resurrection he was constituted Lord, head of his Church and giver of the holy Spirit. Through this Spirit the power of Christ's resurrection is present in the Church. All Christian life is a living out of the paschal mystery, of the dying and of the resurrection of Christ. By putting to death in themselves everything that is contrary to Christ's law, and by the trials and sufferings inherent in Christian living, believers share in Christ's death. In so doing, in overcoming in themselves the power of sin, they also share in his triumph; they experience the power of his resurrection. Through the work of the holy Spirit they are changed into the image of Christ, from one degree of glory to another as Paul elsewhere puts it (2 Cor. 3:18), a process to be completed only at the resurrection when their earthly body will be transformed into a heavenly one. This is the goal of all Christian life, for every believer as much as for the apostle of the gentiles. Made Christ's own in baptism Christians must press on towards this goal without any moral relaxation. This teaching of Paul sums up what the Church has being saying right through the entire lenten period.
