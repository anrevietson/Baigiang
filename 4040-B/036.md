# No other name

First Reading (Acts 4 :8-12). The Church has met opposition and undergone persecution from the very beginning of its existence. Today's first reading gives us Peter's speech to the Great Sanhedrin of Jerusalem. He and John had been arrested on the previous day at the instigation of the Sadducees because they preached the resurrection of the dead which the Sadducees denied. Their preaching had followed the cure of the cripple at the entrance to the Temple and it is to this cure Peter refers. He gives a short sermon on the saving power of the name of Jesus (which means 'God saves ' or 'God is salvation'). The cure of the cripple was physical but Peter uses it to teach that no one can attain salvation, eternal life, except through Christ, through the power of the risen Jesus. Peter is 'filled with the Holy Spirit'. Here we see fulfilled a promise made by Jesus to his disciples: 'But when they hand you over do not worry about how to speak or what to say; what you are to say will be given to you when the time comes; because it is not you who will be speaking; the Spirit of your Father will be speaking in you' (Mt. 10 :19-20). The cure of the cripple in the naine of Jesus also recalls another promise of Jesus to his disciples: 'Whatever you ask for in my name I will do' (Jn. 14:13). Possibly the formula 'Jesus Christ the Nazarene ' was used frequently in early professions of faith, especially, for example, at baptism Or in exorcisms. The contrast between the rejection and crucifixion of Jesus by the Jewish leaders and his acceptance and glorification by God is found many times in the Acts and Epistles. Psalm 118 :22 expresses the triumph of the risen Christ over his enemies and the fact that he is the solid foundation of the new community, the new Israel. Possibly this verse, cited here by Peter, was one of a collection used by the apostles in preaching to show how the Old Testament was fulfilled in Jesus Christ.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 117) is a hymn of thanksgiving and trust in God who has saved us through Christ rejected by men but raised up by God.
