# Thiên Chúa thanh tẩy, tha thứ và che chở dân Người

First Reading: The alternative reading (Is. 4 :2-6). The twin ideas of the passage seem to be those of purification and pardon. These are familiar biblical themes especially in the prophets. The historical background appears to be the Fall of Jerusalem (v. 4), which in our reading is aligned with and prefaced by a messianic poem referring to a future restoration and salvation (vv. 2-3). There is no agreement as to what the phrases 'the branch of the Lord' and 'the fruit of the earth' refer to. Some take them as indicating the Messiah; others prefer the remnant of the people which will escape the impending judgement; and still others see a parallelism between the two phrases which suggests an unusual fertility of the land (and therefore a gift from God) in the new era of restoration.
In any case the pastoral import of the reading is clear. God may allow his people to suffer and undergo trials. But chastisement is never his final word; that is always salvation. And so it is that after the cleansing trials God will again come to be with his people to protect them as of old. Verses 5 and 6 are a very vivid description of this latter aspect. The cloud and the fire were well-known symbols of the Lord's presence to his people during their weary wanderings in the wilderness. From the midst of unfaithfulness and evil, then, there are always the faithful few the remnant' - who return purified and pardoned. Because of their knowledge of the ultimate goodness of God they continue to affirm that life is good despite all its sorrow and that there is always to-morrow! It is a basic biblical attitude - out of death comes life.
