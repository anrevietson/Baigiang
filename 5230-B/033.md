# Partiality

Second Reading (James 2 :1-5). This follows on immediately from last Sunday's second reading. 'Partiality' (v.1) towards the rich is the wisdom of the 'world' (1:27), whereas God's wisdom (that is, his way of acting) is partiality towards the oppressed and poor (cf. Is. 35 and Ps. 145 :7-9). The Christian's judgement is to be modelled on God's judgement, otherwise he is a 'judge with evil thoughts' (v.4), that is, with a judgement based not on God's behaviour but on man .Wealth is no more an evil in itself than poverty is a blessing in itself. All the emphasis which the Bible puts on God's love for the poor does not imply that God loves them simply because they are poor, as if poverty were a virtue. The original setting of the judgement motif, involving the humiliation of the rich and the exaltation of the poor, is the law court in which justice was perverted and the poor were abused by the wealthy. The Bible looks at the rich man and the poor man as the oppressor and the oppressed respectively. When it speaks about humiliation and exaltation in this regard it is always a question of God's justice being revealed. Just as the social law of Israel was an expression of God's will for his people, so social justice is a reflection of God's justice.
