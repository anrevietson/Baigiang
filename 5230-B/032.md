# The return from exile

First Reading (Is. 35 :4-7). The whole of is. 35 is a poetical, idyllic description of the return from the Babylonian exile. It provides the pendant to chapter thirty-four which describes, in equally imaginative terms, the punishment which God will eke out to the nations hostile to Israel (especially Edom). The main theme running throughout these two chapters is that of judgement, which involves the reversal of situations : the situation of Israel visa-virsa the other nations, the situation of the different elements of creation enumerated in chapter thirty-five. The key to understanding these chapters is that God's judgement is at the antipodes of man's judgement, since he comes in judgement precisely to vindicate the oppressed. When he judges he turns things upside down and inside out. Thus the reversals of chapters thirty-four/thirty-five are poetic expressions of God's judgement, the signs that the judgement has come, that God's rule has been inaugurated.
In 35 :5-7 the prophet does not predict actual miracles. The picture he paints is of the impossible happening, the natural order of things coming to an end. This, he maintains, is what it will be like when the rule of God finally comes.
The Responsorial Psalm (Ps. 145 :7-10). This psalm contrasts faith in the Lord who saves: 'Happy is he whose help is in the God of Jacob' (v.5) with confidence in man: 'Put not your trust in princes' (v.3). It is the Lord alone 'who executes justice' (v.7), that is, who reverses the lot of the hungry, the prisoners, the blind, etc.
