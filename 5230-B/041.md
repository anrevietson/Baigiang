# No partiality

Second Reading (Jas 2:1-5). It is likely that James, in the fellowship to which he belonged, had experienced some form of discrimination. He possibly exaggerated that situation in order to make the point that faith in God was the criterion which Christians should use when judging a person. The rich man wearing a gold ring may have belonged to the Equestrian Order, a second rank order in Roman society. This order often lead to high office in the civil service e.g., Procurator, or in the tax system. They were influential people in society. James reminds the readers that discrimination between people is against the law of God. (Lev 19:15, Ps 132:2, Mal 12:9) God himself doesn't discriminate; therefore we must not. (Job 34:19, Ecclus 35:13). Worldly possessions are of no value in the Kingdom of God.
