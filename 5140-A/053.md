# Life in the Spirit

Second Reading (Rom. 8 :9, 11-13). In this passage Paul contrasts 'life in the Spirit' with life 'in the flesh'. 'Flesh' for St Paul signifies man in his frailty and transience (2 Cor. 12 :7; 4 :11; Phil. 1:22, 24). It also designates man's being and attitude as opposed to and in contradiction to God and God's Spirit. To he 'in the flesh' means to he turned in upon oneself, to rely on ones own resources to the rejection of the power of God. To be 'in the Spirit' on the other hand means to be open to the invasive power of God in human life. It means that our lives are directed from a source beyond ourselves, and at the same time that this source now lies within us. The Christian, therefore, who is 'in the Spirit', is not merely told how to live his life, he is given the inner strength to do so.
This passage tells us something very important about God. He is the one who raised Jesus from the dead, who turned Christ's failure into triumph. When the same God 'gives life to our mortal bodies' he gives meaning to our frustrations too, hope to our hopelessness.
