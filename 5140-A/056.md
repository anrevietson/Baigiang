# Life in the Spirit

Second Reading (Rom 8:9.11-13). Paul is under no illusion about human nature; he records his own fall into the great gulf that exists between choosing good and actually doing it (cf Rom 7:15-20); in this reading, though, the outlook is far more optimistic. Old excuses are no longer valid ("Ah, we're only human"), for the Spirit of God now lives within Christians. Now Christians are no longer confined to the merely human; they can even aspire to the spiritual, since it is really the Spirit of God who is at work.

This Wisdom stands in contrast to the wisdom of "the learned and the clever", and is pure gift which Jesus can bestow on all of those who come to him. This passage and the demand to become children in order to enter the kingdom (which is only found in Mt 18:3) shed light upon each other:
The Wisdom of God must be embraced without the encumbrance of contrary wisdoms (which can be little more than justifications for a hodge-podge of selfishness, bitterness, bigotries, hurts, ambitions and the like), so that the lighter burden which Jesus offers can then be taken up instead of the heavy loads which contrary wisdoms often impose.
