# Christ crucified

Second Reading (1 Cor. 1:22-25). Father Raymond Brown, the American scripture scholar, once said: 'Christianity would be quite another religion if Jesus had died of a heart attack on the shores of the Sea of Galilee.' This is precisely the point Paul makes in this reading. Christianity was the message of the cross of Christ and he let nobody forget it. He preached fearlessly of a crucified saviour, knowing full well that this would shock the Jews and scandalise the Greek torrid of his time. For the Jews, awaiting impatiently the advent of a Messiah who would inaugurate the sovereignty of Israel over the Gentiles with great power and glory, it was incredible that someone who had been crucified as a criminal could be the Messiah. Didn't their own law state clearly: .... one who has been hanged is accursed of God ...' (Deut. 21:23)! The fact of crucifixion automatically disqualified Jesus from being the Messiah. The Greek mind, constantly seeking for a philosophical system that would satisfactorily explain man and the world, found the idea of God becoming man repugnant. The incarnation, let alone the crucifixion, was 'madness .
But it seems to be characteristic of God to confound human wisdom. He accomplishes by 'foolishness' what the greatest human wisdom cannot achieve. And his wisdom and saving action are, paradoxically, revealed in Jesus Christ, crucified and risen. Those who 'have been called' i.e. hear and heed the divine invitation see in Christ 'the power and the wisdom of God'. For in him is salvation and resurrection; and his life and mission reveal the salvific designs of God for man.
The cross of Christ is hard to accept, until we reflect on the fact that it is but the practical expression of 'God so loved the world'. In the Old Testament era man knew that God loved him - there was plenty of evidence to substantiate it. What he did not even guess at was how much God loved him. Only the cross of Christ would tell that. It was also a strangely fitting way of doing it.
'Greater love than this no man has ...' The thought will give us strength in the sufferings and troubles that beset us. Any attempt to explain them will ultimately sound like Job's 'empty-headed words', and tend to obscure rather than clarify the love of God at work in our lives. For we are holding forth on matters we cannot understand. Acceptance - though not explanation - will come only in the strength of our belief that 'God's foolishness is wiser than human wisdom and God's weakness is stronger than human strength.
