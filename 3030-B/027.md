# The cleansing of the temple

The Gospel (John 2 :13-25). The first thing to be clear about in this incident is that Jesus did not say that he would rebuild the physical, material temple in three days, in the event of its destruction. What he did indicate by his action was that he didn't think much of the whole Jewish sacrificial system which had made a 'market' of the temple. It had become both irreverent and irrelevant, and no longer helped man to come closer to God. So by word and deed Jesus was, in the fashion of the Old Testament prophets (Jer. 7 :11; Mal. 3 :1), protesting against the profanation of God's house and signalling that its messianic purification was at hand. He was the Messiah and he would carry it out.
The second thing to note is the reactions of the onlookers. The Jews seemed to recognise that his action was that of one who was a prophet and claimed to be the Messiah. They asked him for a sign to justify himself. They take his reply literally and will later distort it and use it against him at his trial. The disciples. too seem to recognise Jesus' action as befitting the Messiah. The words of Psalm 69 10 come to mind: 'Zeal for your house will devour me ' and it was a psalm that referred to the Messiah. Further the kind of action that he had performed had been predicted of the messianic age by Zech. 14 :21 .... there will be no more traders in the temple of Yahweh Sabbath when that day comes.' But it is clear from what the evangelist says (vv. 21-22) that they did not understand the full meaning of the event until after the resurrection.
Finally, there is the significance of what Jesus did and said. His coming had marked the end of an era. The curtain had come down over its symbol - the elaborate temple worship. Jesus, the risen Messiah, will take the place of the old temple and all it stood for. The centre of God's presence among his people would no longer be a place; it would henceforth be a person. The new sanctuary would be the risen body of Jesus. In this new temple would dwell all the fullness of the Spirit. And that Spirit would come to those who believed, and dwell with them so that they too in their turn would become temples of God. This is a deep thought. But it has very practical implications. The Jews by their actions were destroying' the temple i.e. they were emptying of all value the sign of their special relationship with God. We who are ourselves temples of God by virtue of our life in the risen Christ, should take note of the words of Jeremiah 'Put no trust in delusive words . like these. This is the sanctuary of Yahweh... If you amend your behaviour and your actions, if you treat each other fairly, if you do not exploit the stranger, the orphan and the widow . . . then here in this place I will stay with you' (7 :3-7).
