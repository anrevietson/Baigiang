# The Ten Commandments

First Reading (Exod. 20 :1-17). This reading is one of the forms in which the Decalogue has come down to us - the other is in the book of Deuteronomy 5 :6-21. Both texts are based on the original Sinaitic decrees, and it is possible that each of the commandments was originally as short as the fifth, sixth, seventh and eighth. Additions were made later according to the necessities of time and place.
The Ten Commandments set forth the fundamental principles underlying all moral and religious obligations. They contain, in summary, the will of God for all mankind, and outline the inescapable duties of man to God and to his neighbour. In them is to be found the real meaning of life, apprehended in faith. We tend to look on the Ten Commandments as an isolated piece of legislation, and consequently as a code of laws to be observed. This is not the full story. The Ten Commandments should always be looked on in their original setting at Mount Sinai. There they are clearly part of the covenant God made with his people. In fact they are an expression of the profound and abiding nature of the alliance. Thus they are not so much a set of laws to which God expected and exacted obedience. They are declarations of willing loyalty offered to God by his people. They constitute, in sum, a way of life.
The fact that Moses put these declarations of loyalty to God in terms of the normal evil law of the ancient Near-East of his day should not deter us. The Ten Commandments are still valid. Jesus appealed to them and added the evangelical counsels as seals of -perfection. Like the Israelites in the Sea of Reeds and -in the barren reaches of the desert, we too have seen the power and love of God at work in our lives. We therefore owe a loyal obedience to him. The Ten Commandments are its universal expression.
